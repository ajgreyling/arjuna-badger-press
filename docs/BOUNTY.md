## Why the reward shrinks (read this first)

When a book is fresh to new eyes, the mistakes are dense — so the **first** people to dig get paid
the most. As each kind of fault gets found and fixed, the next one of that kind is rarer and harder
to spot, so its reward steps **down**. Keep going long enough and you reach the **long tail**: the
deep, rare catches that still pay, just less.

**This is deliberate. It is the whole point.** The total purse is set at roughly what professional
proofreading and sensitivity reading this library would have cost. We're paying that to *you*, the
sharpest readers in the country, instead — but only for finds that are real, and most to the people
who get there first. **Read early. Dig early. Earn most.**

## What we pay for — and the declining scale

Three kinds of find, weighted by how much they matter to the promise. The reward for each is set by
**how many of that kind have already been accepted** (see the live count on
[Fixes &amp; Finders](FINDERS.md)).

### 🔴 Factual error — *high weight*
A claim about the real world — history, science, geography, a real person — that is simply **wrong**.
(Or a *contested* claim we presented as settled fact, with only one side. That still counts here.)

| Accepted so far | You earn |
|---|---|
| First **5** | **R750** |
| Next **10** (6–15) | **R500** |
| Next **20** (16–35) | **R300** |
| Next **40** (36–75) | **R150** |
| Long tail (76+) | **R100** |

### 🟠 Cultural / sensitivity issue — *medium weight*
A people, custom, language, or belief **misrepresented** — handled without the dignity it deserves,
or simply gotten wrong.

| Accepted so far | You earn |
|---|---|
| First **5** | **R400** |
| Next **10** (6–15) | **R275** |
| Next **20** (16–35) | **R175** |
| Next **40** (36–75) | **R100** |
| Long tail (76+) | **R75** |

### 🟢 Continuity fault — *low weight*
An **internal contradiction** — a timeline that doesn't add up, a prop that changes, a name that
slips, an eye colour that moves.

| Accepted so far | You earn |
|---|---|
| First **10** | **R150** |
| Next **20** (11–30) | **R100** |
| Next **30** (31–60) | **R60** |
| Next **50** (61–110) | **R40** |
| Long tail (111+) | **R25** |

> Why these weights? A wrong fact can launder a falsehood into a reader's head — the worst thing a
> "grounded" book can do — so it pays the most. A cultural misstep wounds the people whose stories
> we're trying to honour — mission-critical, so it pays well. A continuity slip is a craft bruise:
> real, worth fixing, but it doesn't break trust the way the other two do.

## Everything is public, on the record

There is no private inbox. **Every submission, and every decision, is posted publicly** on the
[Fixes &amp; Finders](FINDERS.md) board — accepted, rejected, or duplicate, with the reasoning. This
is the point: you can see exactly what's been found, what the current reward for each category is,
who's been paid, and that we are fair. Transparency is the trust contract.

## The rules (short, and in your favour)

1. **First valid report of an issue wins it.** Submissions are timestamped and public. If two people
   find the same thing, the earlier one is paid; the later one is logged as a duplicate.
2. **Tell us *where* and *why*.** Point to the book and the place (chapter, line, a quote). For a
   factual or cultural find, say **why** it's wrong — a source, a correction, the real story. "I
   didn't like it" is not a finding; "this ceremony is shown wrong, here's how it actually works" is.
3. **Contested ≠ wrong — and it still pays.** If something is genuinely *disputed* and we told only
   one side as if it were fact, that's a **both-sides failure** and it pays at the **factual** tier.
   We would rather know.
4. **The reward is whatever the scale says when your find is accepted** — based on the live accepted
   count for that category. Get there early; the rate only goes down.
5. **One reward per distinct issue.** A list of ten typos is kind, but it's one continuity-tier
   reward, not ten. Genuinely separate findings each pay.
6. **We adjudicate, in the open.** Every find is judged on the public board with our reasoning. The
   credit — your name or handle on the fix — is half the reward.
7. **Good faith.** We read every report from a person who wants the book to be *right*. We ignore
   bad-faith nitpicking, AI-generated spam, and "gotchas" that aren't.

**Who decides?** We do — the press makes the call, and shows its working on the public board.
Disagree with a call? Say so on the record; we've reversed ourselves before.

## Who can take part (eligibility)

We can only run a prize we can actually **pay**. So:

- **Open to readers we can legally and easily remit a cash prize to.** When you submit, you tell us
  the country you'd be paid in and your payout method.
- **South Africa first** (instant EFT / PayShap). We are widening from there to other countries we can
  pay without friction — tell us where you are and we'll confirm.
- **We cannot pay** where money transfer is sanctioned, blocked, or impractical for us — this
  includes (non-exhaustively) **Russia, Belarus, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Cuba**, and anywhere our
  payment providers won't settle. Finds from there are still welcome and credited by name — we just
  can't send a cash prize.
- You must be old enough to be paid (18+, or have a guardian receive on your behalf).
- One person, one identity. No sockpuppets to farm the early-tier rates.

If you're unsure whether we can pay you, ask in your submission — we'll tell you before you spend
hours hunting.

## Why we're doing this

Because the honey badger doesn't let go until it's right. Because we built tooling that *measures*
the work — a continuity gate, a fact-and-balance check, a machine-tell scanner — and the honest
next step is to let the sharpest readers in the country test it against the one thing tooling can't
fully see: lived truth, and local knowledge. You know KwaZulu-Natal, the Free State veld, isiZulu,
the real history, better than any model ever will.

So: read the books free. If you catch us out, we pay you on a scale that rewards getting there first,
we fix it, and we thank you by name — in public. That is the whole deal.
