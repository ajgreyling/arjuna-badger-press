# The African Worlds Study Bible

### *Africa in Scripture and the Early Church*

> **Status:** Development proposal — August 2026  
> **Working title:** *The African Worlds Study Bible*  
> **Campaign line:** **See the biblical world whole.**

---

## The proposal in one sentence

**An Africa-centred, evidence-led study Bible that restores Africa's lands, peoples, textual
traditions, interpreters, and earliest churches to the ordinary reading of Scripture — without
replacing European distortion with unsupported counterclaims.**

The Bible did not begin as a European story. It arose chiefly through Jewish history in the
Levant, inside an **Afro-Asiatic world** joined by the Nile, the Red Sea, deserts, ports, empires,
trade, refuge, war, and migration.

Egypt is not outside Africa. Cush is not a poetic abstraction. Cyrene is not a European city.
Alexandria is not merely a footnote. African readers are not late guests in a faith delivered to
them by modern Europe.

Africa is part of the map, the text, the transmission, the argument, and the earliest history of
the church.

---

## Why this edition should exist

For centuries, popular biblical art and teaching often rendered the ancient world through a
European lens. North Africa was detached from Africa. Cush became a vague "Ethiopia." Darker
Arabian and African peoples were lightened, marginalised, or removed. Christianity in Africa was
frequently narrated as though it began with European missions.

That account is historically incomplete.

This edition will recover what the biblical text and surviving history can establish:

- **Egypt** appears throughout Israel's memory as refuge, oppression, neighbour, empire, and
  intellectual world.
- **Cush/Kush** was a powerful Middle Nile civilisation, principally in present-day Sudan and
  southern Egypt.
- **Libya, Put, Cyrene, and Alexandria** place North Africa inside the Bible's Mediterranean
  world.
- **Ebed-melech the Cushite**, **Simon of Cyrene**, the **official of the Kandake**, **Apollos of
  Alexandria**, and others will be treated as people with real geographies rather than colourless
  names.
- **Alexandria, Carthage, Numidia, Hippo, Aksum, and the Christian kingdoms of Nubia** belong to
  the formation of Christian theology, biblical transmission, monasticism, and church life.
- African and diaspora communities have interpreted, sung, contested, survived, and reclaimed
  Scripture for centuries.

The purpose is not to make Africa decorative. It is to make the biblical world complete.

## Four truths the edition will state plainly

### 1. Africa is structurally present in Scripture

The most defensible claim is not that every biblical person was African. It is that Africa is
**indispensable** to the biblical world.

The biblical lands also include the Levant, Mesopotamia, Arabia, Persia, Anatolia, Greece, and
Rome. Christianity began as a Jewish movement in Roman Judea. Ancient Israel emerged chiefly in a
Levantine and Canaanite environment while remaining connected to Egypt, Cush, Arabia, and the wider
eastern Mediterranean.

**Central does not mean exclusive. Recovery does not require annexation.**

### 2. Ancient Arabia was not a pale or uniform world

Modern media often imagines ancient Arabians through a narrow, light-skinned template. The evidence
supports a much broader range.

A 2024 ancient-DNA study of four people from Tylos-period Bahrain, dated approximately 300 BCE to
600 CE, predicted brown eyes and brown or black hair for all four. Two were predicted to have
**dark skin pigmentation with greater than 90% probability**. The remaining predictions were less
certain. This is direct evidence for those ancient eastern Arabian individuals — not proof that
every Arabian looked the same.

The phrase "original Arabs" also needs care. Early imperial writers used labels related to "Arab"
for varied desert and steppe populations. People across pre-Islamic Arabia more often identified
through particular kingdoms, peoples, cities, and tribes: Sabaean, Himyarite, Nabataean,
Hadramite, and many others. There was no single original Arab phenotype.

The responsible conclusion is still important:

> **Ancient Arabian peoples included people with brown through very dark complexions. Biblical
> illustration should show that range instead of treating European appearance as the unmarked
> default.**

Sources: [ancient genomes from eastern Arabia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10943591/)
· [the historical development of Arab identity](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/843/BAR27-10-Webb-reduced_0.pdf)
· [long-term Red Sea population connections](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.21522)

### 3. Genesis opens with a plant-fed world

Genesis 1:29–30 gives seed-bearing plants and fruit to humans, and green plants to other living
creatures. The scene precedes animal fear, slaughter, and permission to consume flesh. Many
biblical scholars therefore describe the creation account as presenting a **vegetarian or
nonviolent dietary ideal**.

That claim must be stated precisely. Genesis 1 says, "I have given" rather than issuing a modern
dietary manifesto. Genesis 9:3 later permits animal food after the flood, with blood prohibited.
The Torah subsequently regulates meat, sacrifice, and festival meals. The whole biblical canon
therefore does not impose universal veganism.

What it does preserve is a meaningful theological sequence:

1. Creation begins with plant food.
2. Violence enters the human story.
3. Animal consumption is later permitted and bounded.
4. Prophetic visions of peace repeatedly imagine the ending of predation and harm.

The study Bible will let readers see that sequence without pretending the later texts do not
exist.

Source: [The Vegetarian Ideal in the Bible](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/Vegetarian%20Ideal%20in%20the%20Bible%201.pdf)

### 4. Climate, diet, and skin colour require science rather than racial mythology

Humans were flexible omnivores before entering Ice Age Europe. In colder regions, seasonal plant
scarcity often increased reliance on animal and marine foods, but archaeological evidence also
shows plant processing and consumption among Neanderthals and early modern humans.

Lighter pigmentation did not evolve simply because people became cold or carnivorous. It emerged
through different combinations of:

- reduced ultraviolet-B exposure at higher latitudes;
- vitamin-D and folate biology;
- migration and population mixture;
- inherited pigmentation variants;
- diet and cultural practice;
- natural selection acting differently in different populations.

Some ancient Europeans retained darker pigmentation for thousands of years. Some Arctic peoples
retained comparatively darker skin while adapting through diet and vitamin-D metabolism. A
vitamin-D-rich animal or marine diet can reduce, rather than create, selection pressure for lighter
skin.

The edition will not turn human adaptation into a moral hierarchy. Pigmentation is an adaptation,
not a measure of civilisation, divine favour, intelligence, or spiritual identity.

Sources: [human pigmentation and ultraviolet radiation](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK210015/)
· [plant foods in Neanderthal and early modern human diets](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/98c7c5df-9438-4176-a2ec-ab550bf371bd/content)

## The editorial maxim: restore without annexing

This project will be bold about evidence and conservative about certainty.

It will not:

- remove Egypt or North Africa from African history;
- depict every biblical person as northern European;
- claim that every Israelite, Judean, or biblical figure was African or modern Black;
- use biblical genealogies as modern genetic pedigrees;
- turn skin colour into proof of nationality, tribe, covenant, or moral character;
- call living Jewish communities impostors or erase the Jewish identity of Jesus and the earliest
  Jesus movement;
- treat a modern religious testimony as archaeological evidence;
- collapse the continent's peoples into one timeless "African culture";
- replace Eurocentrism with a mirror-image ethnocentrism.

It will:

- name African locations and people directly;
- distinguish ancient geography from modern borders;
- separate text, material evidence, historical reconstruction, tradition, and modern reception;
- show uncertainty where evidence is incomplete;
- commission scholars from across Africa and its diasporas;
- include Jewish review wherever claims concern Israel, Judaism, ancestry, or antisemitism;
- maintain a public bibliography, corrections record, and revision history.

## Evidence readers can see

Every contested historical annotation will carry two labels.

| Source | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TXT** | Explicit wording or literary structure of the biblical text |
| **MAT** | Archaeological, epigraphic, numismatic, or manuscript evidence |
| **HIST** | Critical historical reconstruction from multiple sources |
| **TRAD** | Later Jewish, Christian, or community tradition |
| **REC** | Modern reception, interpretation, or direct testimony |
| **GEN** | Population-genetic evidence, used within strict methodological limits |

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Broad evidentiary agreement |
| **Probable** | Best-supported reconstruction with remaining uncertainty |
| **Disputed** | Several credible interpretations remain |
| **Unsupported** | Evidence is insufficient or weighs against the claim |

The reader should never have to guess whether a note describes what a biblical text says, what an
archaeologist found, what a church remembers, or what a modern community believes.

### Africa in the Text

Notes on people, places, rulers, institutions, migration, trade, warfare, and political
relationships.

### Words and Maps

Ancient place names, translation choices, changing boundaries, disputed identifications, and maps
that use modern borders only for orientation.

### Ancient Worlds

Archaeology, inscriptions, manuscripts, coins, agriculture, foodways, routes, climate, and
material culture.

### Africa's Early Churches

Alexandria, Egyptian Christianity, Carthage, Numidia, Hippo, desert monasticism, Aksum, and the
Christian kingdoms of Nubia.

### Africana Readings

Continental and diaspora interpretation: inculturation, oral and mother-tongue reading,
African-Initiated churches, liberation, Black theology, womanism, African women's theology,
postcolonial and decolonial work, and ordinary community reading.

### Abuse and Repair

The "curse of Ham," slavery, colonialism, racism, antisemitism, gender violence, and the history of
texts used as weapons — followed by responsible interpretation and repair.

### Claims Audit

Popular historical, racial, and genealogical claims tested openly against textual, archaeological,
historical, and genetic evidence.

### Across Traditions

Clearly attributed Orthodox, Catholic, Protestant, Pentecostal, African-Initiated, and Jewish
perspectives where traditions differ.

### Living Voices

Contemporary testimony represented accurately as testimony. The
[Penuel Show interview with Sar Ahmadiel Ben Yehuda](https://youtu.be/VCvH3FbROJg?si=6Z8JqZAPpqxBLl_c),
for example, documents the African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem's present self-understanding. It
does not independently prove ancient tribal descent.

## Sample study notes

### Genesis 1:29–30 — The first table

God gives seed-bearing plants and fruit trees to humanity and green vegetation to land animals and
birds. Within this creation account, dominion does not begin as permission to kill for food. Human
and animal life share a plant-fed world. Read beside Genesis 9:1–4, this scene has often been
understood as an original ideal of nonviolent provision rather than a description of the world as
later experienced.

Genesis does not use the modern term "vegan," and other biblical texts permit animal food. The
sequence nevertheless matters: Scripture's opening food economy is abundance from the ground, not
the fear of animals.

**Evidence:** TXT — established. Vegetarianism as the passage's moral ideal — probable and
interpretively debated.

### Genesis 9:3–4 — Permission with a boundary

After the flood, God permits humans to eat moving creatures "just as" green plants had previously
been given. The permission is immediately restricted: flesh with its lifeblood may not be eaten.
The narrative therefore acknowledges a changed relationship marked by animal fear and human
violence while placing a boundary around consumption.

Readers may understand this as concession, adaptation, or expanded provision. It cannot honestly
be used to claim that Genesis never presented plant food as the original arrangement.

**Evidence:** TXT — established. "Concession" as theological interpretation — probable but not
grammatically explicit.

### Jeremiah 38:7–13; 39:15–18 — The Cushite who trusted and acted

Ebed-melech, whose name or title means "servant of the king," is explicitly identified as a
Cushite in Jerusalem's court. Here Cush is best located in the African Middle Nile world of
Kush/Nubia, south of Egypt, rather than automatically in the modern Ethiopian state.

During Jerusalem's siege, Judean officials leave Jeremiah to die, but Ebed-melech challenges the
king and organises a careful rescue. God later promises him survival "because you have trusted in
me." His African origin is neither incidental nor to be translated away: a foreign courtier
embodies courage, mercy, and trust when powerful insiders fail.

**Evidence:** TXT — established. Middle Nile identification — probable to established.

### Acts 8:26–40 — An African royal official at the widening frontier

Luke calls the traveller an *Aithiops* and places him in the service of the Kandake, the dynastic
title used by queens or queen mothers of Meroitic Kush. In this setting, "Ethiopia" points
principally to the Nile kingdom centred in present-day Sudan, not automatically to modern Ethiopia
or Aksum.

The official is powerful, literate, and agentic: he invites Philip to interpret Isaiah and then
requests baptism. His baptism places African participation inside the apostolic-era expansion
described by Acts, centuries before modern European missions.

Later traditions connecting him directly to the Ethiopian church belong in reception history.
Acts does not say he founded a church; securely documented royal Christianity at Aksum belongs to
the fourth century.

**Evidence:** TXT/HIST — Meroitic setting and Kandake title established. Religious status —
disputed. Church-founding claim — TRAD, historically unverified.

## A new edition, not an erased lineage

This project follows important predecessors.

The [Original African Heritage Study Bible](https://archive.org/details/originalafricanh00feld),
edited by Cain Hope Felder and first published in 1993, recovered African presence and Black
heritage through the King James Version.

The [Africa Study Bible](https://oasisinternationalpublishing.com/africa-study-bible/) brought
together more than 350 contributors from 50 countries and connected the New Living Translation to
African life and application.

*The African Worlds Study Bible* should not duplicate either achievement. Its distinct task is to
combine:

1. a complete licensed Bible text;
2. African geography and peoples inside the biblical worlds;
3. African manuscript, translation, and canon histories;
4. early Christianity in Egypt, Roman North Africa, Aksum, and Nubia;
5. continental and diaspora reception;
6. visible sourcing and explicit treatment of disputed claims.

The preferred translation for publisher discussion is the **NRSVue**, including feasibility for
the Apocrypha/Deuterocanonical books, because of its scholarly and ecumenical fit. No translation
has yet been licensed. The study apparatus should remain reference-keyed and portable so that it
can support another authorised text or, if necessary, first appear as a verse-keyed companion.

## Who should build it

The project requires a smaller, accountable contributor body rather than a symbolic list of names:
approximately **60–90 commissioned contributors** supported by strong section editors.

Governance should include:

- an Africa-based editor-in-chief or genuinely Africa-led co-editorship;
- at least two-thirds African or African-diaspora representation on the executive board;
- at least half of that board based at African institutions;
- regional representation from North, West, Central, East/Horn, and Southern Africa;
- Coptic, Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo, Catholic, Protestant, Evangelical,
  Pentecostal/Charismatic, and African-Initiated Church voices;
- Jewish scholars of ancient Judaism, African Jewish communities, and Black Jewish experience;
- specialists in Hebrew Bible, New Testament, archaeology, Egyptology, Nubian studies, Arabian
  history, patristics, manuscripts, genetics, food history, and Africana hermeneutics;
- gender parity and meaningful participation by younger scholars and ordinary readers.

No contributor should be asked to represent a continent, race, region, or denomination alone.

## Development path

A credible full study Bible is a four-to-five-year publishing project, not a fast content drop.

| Window | Work |
|---|---|
| **Months 0–6** | Physical competitor audit, reader interviews, translation-rights inquiries, publisher meetings, charter, and sampler |
| **Months 6–12** | Contracts, content matrix, source policy, visual plan, editorial system, and typeset prototype |
| **Months 12–30** | Notes, essays, maps, art, and introductions commissioned in waves |
| **Months 24–40** | Historical, regional, theological, Jewish, and scientific review; fact-checking and revision |
| **Months 38–48** | Copyediting, permissions, indexing, accessibility, digital conversion, and typesetting |
| **Months 46–54** | Proofs, manufacturing, institutional orders, coordinated African and diaspora launch |

The first funded development phase should produce ten finished sample spreads:

1. Genesis 1 — creation and the first table
2. Genesis 9 — permission, blood, and violence
3. Genesis 10 — a family map, not a race chart
4. Exodus — Egypt as both African civilisation and imperial oppressor
5. Jeremiah 38–39 — Ebed-melech the Cushite
6. Matthew 2 — refuge in Egypt
7. Acts 2 — Egypt, Libya, and the diaspora at Pentecost
8. Acts 8 — the official of the Kandake
9. Acts 13 — Cyrene inside the Antioch church
10. One map, one visual timeline, and one transparent claims audit

Full commissioning should begin only after a publisher, translation-rights pathway, editorial
leadership, development funding, tested page model, and reader validation are in place.
