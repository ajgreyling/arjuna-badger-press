# DECISION — *Stranger in a Strange Land*: retelling vs. original

> **Status:** decision-grade brief. Read this before doing anything with the Eleanor Wood contact.
> **Author goal that prompted this study:** read *Stranger* in full, absorb its craft and arcs (the
> way the public-domain Sherlock canon was ingested for `books/modern-sherlock`), then decide whether
> the next book is a **retelling** of *Stranger* or an **original** in Heinlein's tradition.
> **The ingestion is done** — see `00-CRAFT-BIBLE.md` and `01..05-part-*.md`. This file is the *decide* step.

---

## 1. The one fact that governs everything: *Stranger* is in copyright

| | Sherlock Holmes canon | *Stranger in a Strange Land* |
|---|---|---|
| Author died | Conan Doyle, 1930 | Heinlein, 1988 |
| Copyright status | **Public domain** (the canon; the last few US stories cleared ~2023) | **In copyright until ~2058** (US: life + 70) |
| Can you ingest the craft/arcs? | ✅ Yes | ✅ **Yes** (technique, structure, theme, voice are NOT copyrightable) |
| Can you publish a *derivative* (same characters / "grok" / Church of All Worlds / plot)? | ✅ Yes — that's exactly what `modern-sherlock` is | ❌ **No** — needs a license from the estate |
| Who controls that license? | nobody (PD) | **Eleanor Wood / Spectrum Literary Agency** — the exact person you found |

`books/modern-sherlock/build/BOOK.md` states the model on its own copyright page:
> *"Characters created by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle (public domain). This modern adaptation is an original work by the author."*

That sentence is **only legal because Doyle is public domain.** The identical sentence about Heinlein would be a false claim — his characters and his coinages are *not* free to use. So the Sherlock method (ingest → keep arcs → write original prose → label the derivation) **transfers in full to the LEARNING step and not at all to the OUTPUT step.** The craft is yours to take. The expression is not.

**What is protected (do not reuse without a license):** Valentine Michael Smith, Jubal Harshaw, Jill, Ben, the specific plot beats, "grok," "discorporate," "water-brother(hood)", the Church of All Worlds / the Nest, "Thou art God" as the book's load-bearing creed, the Fosterites, the Martian Old Ones — and of course any actual sentences.

**What is free (this whole study is built on it):** the outsider-as-redescription engine; the messiah arc built by elimination; the mentor-with-a-heckler; the deficit-scorecard picaresque; the satirized-institution-as-prototype move; the triumphant-sacrifice mechanics; register-shift as POV cue; "embody don't state"; the deceleration-into-detonation finale shape. Ideas, methods, structures, and themes are not ownable. **That is exactly what `00-CRAFT-BIBLE.md` distils.**

## 2. The Eleanor Wood reality (why the original plan would backfire)

The plan as first stated — *"present Eleanor Wood with an unpublished modern retelling of Stranger in the Arjuna Badger House voice"* — would **damage the exact relationship you've worked to find.** Three reasons, now confirmed by research:

1. **She is the estate's rights-gatekeeper, not an acquiring agent for new authors.** The public guidance is that she is the contact "for all copyright queries regarding all works of Robert A. Heinlein." She prefers **mail or fax**. This is estate administration, not an open submissions desk. A finished, unauthorized retelling lands on her desk as *"someone reproduced our client's crown-jewel novel without asking"* — i.e., closer to an infringement notice than a manuscript. Worst possible first impression with the one person whose goodwill the whole idea depends on.
2. **The estate actively licenses derivatives — through controlled channels.** A SyFy/Paramount TV adaptation of *Stranger* was announced in 2016 (it lost momentum by ~2023). That tells us two things: (a) they *do* deals, so a *licensing* approach is not hopeless; (b) they manage screen/derivative rights deliberately, so an unsolicited competing prose adaptation is unwelcome noise against channels they control.
3. **You cannot sell or self-publish it anyway.** Even if she liked it, an unlicensed retelling can't go on the Arjuna shelf, can't go to KDP, can't be optioned. It is unshippable until a license exists. Writing the whole book *first* inverts the order of operations.

**Conclusion:** never send a finished retelling cold. If the retelling path is pursued at all, the artifact you send is a **short licensing proposal + a single sample chapter**, asking permission — not a manuscript presenting a fait accompli.

## 3. The fork — two clean paths (and a recommendation)

### Path A — Original "spiritual successor" (LEGALLY CLEAN, ship anytime) ✅ recommended default
Write a **new, original** novel that runs the *Stranger* **engine** (from `00-CRAFT-BIBLE.md`) with entirely original characters, world, coinages, and plot. No Smith, no "grok," no Church of All Worlds. The DNA — outsider-as-redescription-machine, the messiah-by-elimination arc, the satirized-institution-as-prototype, the triumphant sacrifice — is *yours to use*. This is precisely how the trilogy already carries Crichton/Weir/Taylor without infringing any of them.

- **Pros:** zero rights risk; ships on the Arjuna shelf and KDP immediately; sits naturally beside `modern-sherlock` and the trilogy; the craft bible is a *complete* build spec for it; and it becomes a far stronger thing to eventually *show* Eleanor Wood — "here is an original Heinlein-tradition novel I wrote," which is a credential, not a liability.
- **Cons:** doesn't carry the *Stranger* brand/title recognition.
- **Arjuna fit:** Priya Ellis (or a new lead) as the *reader* of the alien thing is already the house's signature; the outsider-who-reads-machines is one substitution away from the outsider-who-reads-humanity. The "embody don't state" doctrine is the *exact fix* for Heinlein's biggest fault (§9 of the craft bible).

### Path B — Licensed retelling (REQUIRES PERMISSION FIRST)
Pursue an authorized modern retelling/adaptation of *Stranger* — but **license first, write second.** The deliverable to Eleanor Wood is a **proposal**, not a novel:
- a 1–2 page pitch (concept, why-now, your track record, the Arjuna house, the respect-for-the-estate framing);
- **one** sample chapter in the Arjuna voice (enough to show craft, not a derivative of the whole);
- an explicit ask for licensing terms.
- **Pros:** the *Stranger* brand; a genuine relationship with the estate if it lands.
- **Cons:** most estates decline prose re-tellings of a flagship title; slow; gated on a deal you don't control; the craft bible can inform the *pitch* but you must not write the *book* until a license exists.

### Path C — Hybrid (the pragmatic real-world sequence) ✅ recommended overall
Do **A now**, and use it as the **calling card for B later**. Write the original spiritual-successor first (clean, shippable, proves you can do Heinlein-tradition SF at a high level). *Then*, if you still want the *Stranger* brand, approach Eleanor Wood with (i) the published original as evidence of craft and (ii) a short licensing proposal. You arrive as an accomplished author asking permission, not a stranger presenting an infringement. This is the order of operations that maximizes both legal safety and the odds with the estate.

## 4. Recommendation

**Path C.** Concretely:
1. **Now:** build the original Heinlein-tradition novel from `00-CRAFT-BIBLE.md`, with the §10 Doctrine Cross-Walk as the guardrail (it tells you exactly which Heinlein moves to take and which faults to fix — especially: dramatize the lectures, give the antagonist a mortal want (L-07), give the lead one costly un-excused mistake (L-06), end on hope-not-certainty (L-08)). Original cast, original world, original coinages.
2. **Do not** send Eleanor Wood anything yet. If/when you want the *Stranger* brand, the artifact is a **licensing proposal + one sample chapter**, by mail/fax, asking permission — never a finished retelling.
3. **Hold the retelling impulse** as the §3-B path, gated on a license. The craft bible serves both paths; only Path A is shippable today.

> This keeps you 100% inside the law, keeps the Arjuna self-publishing rights clean, honors the Sherlock precedent (which works *because* of public domain), and turns the rare Eleanor Wood contact from a risk into an opportunity you approach from strength.

---

## 5. If you choose Path B/the licensing pitch — what NOT to do

- ❌ Do **not** write the full retelling first.
- ❌ Do **not** reuse "grok," "discorporate," "water-brother," the Church of All Worlds, the Nest, or any character name in anything you send.
- ❌ Do **not** frame it as "I've made a modern *Stranger*" — frame it as "I would like to license the right to."
- ✅ **Do** lead with respect for the estate and a clear, single ask.
- ✅ **Do** keep the sample to original prose that demonstrates *voice and craft*, carrying the *engine* (legal) without the *expression* (not legal).
- ✅ **Do** expect a "no" and have Path A already shipping so the contact costs you nothing.
