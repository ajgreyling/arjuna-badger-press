# Atlas: African Places and Peoples in Scripture

*The African Worlds Study Bible* · Geographic companion

Modern borders are orientation aids only. Ancient names rarely match today’s states one-for-one. For method and labels, see [how-to-read.html](how-to-read.html). For contested popular claims, see [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html).

This atlas does not relocate Abraham to Napata or Jesus to Cyrene. It restores African names that popular maps faded, while keeping the Levant, Mesopotamia, Arabia, Anatolia, Greece, and Rome on the same table. **Indispensable is not exclusive.**

## Egypt (Miṣrayim / Miṣraim)

**Biblical names:** Hebrew *Miṣrayim*; Greek *Aigyptos*; often “Egypt” in English Bibles. Related place language includes Pathros (Upper Egypt), Zoan/Tanis, Memphis, On/Heliopolis, Rameses, and Goshen. “Going down to Egypt” and “coming up from Egypt” are stock movements in Israel’s memory.

**Modern geography:** The Nile Valley and Delta in the modern Arab Republic of Egypt, in **northeast Africa**. Detaching Egypt from Africa is a modern racial and curricular habit, not an ancient fact. Egypt’s Africanity does not erase millennia of Mediterranean and Near Eastern entanglement; those links are how Afro-Asiatic worlds work.

**Key texts:** Genesis 12; 37–50 (famine, Joseph, settlement); Exodus 1–15 (oppression and departure); Deuteronomy 5:15; 6:21 (memory of slavery as ethics); 1 Kings 3–11 (alliance and trade horizons); Isaiah 19; Jeremiah 42–44; Ezekiel 29–32; Matthew 2:13–15, 19–21; Acts 2:10; Acts 6:9; Acts 18:24 (Apollos of Alexandria); Acts 27–28 (Alexandrian shipping).

**Evidence note:** Egypt as African Nile civilisation and as Israel’s memory of refuge and oppression — **TXT/HIST — Established**. Precise locations of “Goshen,” store-cities, and the Exodus route — **HIST — Disputed**. Egypt as intellectual and diaspora centre by the Hellenistic–Roman period — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Egypt as “not African” — **Unsupported**.

## Cush / Kush / Nubia

**Biblical names:** Hebrew *Kûš* (Cush); people called Cushites; Greek often *Aithiopia* for the same broad southern Nile horizon. Related names in lists and oracles include Seba and, in wider classical geography, Meroë. English “Ethiopian” in older Bibles frequently translates Cushite language without meaning the modern Ethiopian state.

**Modern geography:** Principally the **Middle Nile** — northern Sudan and southern Egypt — the world of Kerma, Napata, and Meroë. This was a powerful African polity interacting with Egypt, Assyria, and Judah. It is not a vague “south,” and it is not automatically Addis Ababa.

**Key texts:** Genesis 10:6–8; Numbers 12:1 (Moses’ Cushite wife — identity details debated); 2 Samuel 18:21–32 (Cushite runner); 2 Kings 19:9 / Isaiah 37:9 (Tirhakah of Cush); Psalm 68:31; Isaiah 18; Jeremiah 13:23; Jeremiah 38–39 (Ebed-melech); Ezekiel 30:4–9; Zephaniah 3:10; Acts 8:26–40.

**Evidence note:** Cush as a powerful African polity south of Egypt — **TXT/HIST/MAT — Established**. Equating every “Cush” occurrence with modern Ethiopia — **HIST — Unsupported**. Ebed-melech as Cushite courtier in Jerusalem — **TXT — Established**; Middle Nile identification — **HIST — Probable to Established**. Public orientation to Nubian civilisation: [The Met — Nubia](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/nubia/hd_nubia.htm).

## Ethiopia / Aithiopia

**Biblical and classical names:** Hebrew *Kûš* is often rendered “Ethiopia” in older English Bibles; Greek *Aithiops* / *Aithiopia* (in Greek ethnographic language, roughly “burnt-face” land) usually points to the Nile south of Egypt in biblical-period usage. Later Christian Ethiopia — Aksum and its successors — is a related but distinct horizon in the Horn.

**Modern geography:** In New Testament-era notes, prefer **Meroitic Kush (Sudan)** unless the text or later history clearly shifts to the northern Horn. Modern Ethiopia and Eritrea enter securely in the **Aksumite** Christian story (fourth century onward) and in long Geʿez scriptural traditions.

**Key texts:** Psalm 68:31; Isaiah 18; Acts 8:27; and, for reception, liturgical and national readings in Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Christianity.

**Evidence note:** Acts 8’s *Aithiops* + Kandake title → Meroitic setting — **TXT/HIST — Established**. Acts 8 as founding charter of the Ethiopian church — **TRAD — historically unverified** (see [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html)). Aksumite royal Christianity in the fourth century — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Psalm 68 as diaspora and continental hope anthem — **REC — Established as reception**.

**Reading tip:** Honour Aksum without forcing Luke to write Aksum’s conversion narrative. Honour Meroë without erasing Horn churches.

**See also:** [spreads/08-acts-kandake.html](spreads/08-acts-kandake.html) · [essays/early-african-christianity.html](essays/early-african-christianity.html)

## Libya / Put (Phut)

**Biblical names:** Hebrew *Pûṭ* (Put/Phut); Greek and Latin sources use Libya for much of North Africa west of Egypt. Related: Lubim / Libyans in some historical books and prophets, often as military partners or neighbours of Egypt.

**Modern geography:** Broader **North Africa west of Egypt** — not only the modern state of Libya. Exact borders of Put remain debated; the biblical cluster places Put with African partners of Egypt and Cush in Genesis 10 and in prophetic oracles.

**Key texts:** Genesis 10:6; 1 Chronicles 1:8; Jeremiah 46:9; Ezekiel 27:10; 30:5; 38:5; Nahum 3:9; Daniel 11:43; Acts 2:10 (“the parts of Libya about Cyrene”).

**Evidence note:** Put/Libyans as African neighbours and sometimes military partners of Egypt — **TXT/HIST — Probable to Established**. Pinning Put to one modern province or ethnicity — **HIST — Disputed**.

**Reading tip:** When Acts specifies “Libya about Cyrene,” Luke narrows from the broad classical “Libya” to a known Cyrenaican zone — see the next entry.

## Cyrene

**Biblical names:** Cyrene; Cyrenians; Simon of Cyrene; Lucius of Cyrene.

**Modern geography:** Ancient Greek–Libyan city on the coast of **Cyrenaica**, in modern eastern Libya, North Africa. A major Mediterranean port with a documented Jewish diaspora in the Hellenistic and Roman periods. Cyrene is African and Mediterranean at once — as Alexandria is.

**Key texts:** Matthew 27:32; Mark 15:21; Luke 23:26 (Simon compelled to carry the cross); Acts 2:10; Acts 6:9 (Cyrenians in synagogue dispute); Acts 11:20 (men of Cyprus and Cyrene evangelising Greeks in Antioch); Acts 13:1 (Lucius of Cyrene among prophets and teachers).

**Evidence note:** Cyrene as North African city with Jewish population in the Roman period — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Cyrenians as active in Jerusalem and Antioch Christianity — **TXT — Established**. Simon’s personal ancestry beyond “of Cyrene” — **TXT — not specified**. Simeon called Niger in Acts 13:1 may indicate dark complexion — **HIST — Disputed / possible**.

**See also:** [spreads/07-acts-pentecost.html](spreads/07-acts-pentecost.html) · [spreads/09-acts-cyrene.html](spreads/09-acts-cyrene.html)

## Alexandria

**Biblical names:** Alexandria; Apollos “born at Alexandria” (Acts 18:24). The city is more often the **world behind** New Testament Judaism and early Christianity than a frequent narrative place-name.

**Modern geography:** Mediterranean capital of Hellenistic and Roman Egypt — an **African** port city, intellectual hub associated with the Mouseion/Library tradition, home to a vast Jewish community and later to formative Christian teachers (the Alexandrian tradition, Coptic Christianity’s deeper roots, monastic invention inland).

**Key texts:** Acts 6:9 (Alexandrians in Jerusalem); Acts 18:24–28; Acts 27–28 (Alexandrian grain ships); indirect horizon for Greek Scriptures (Septuagint traditions) and for Alexandrian Christian theology after the New Testament page.

**Evidence note:** Jewish Alexandria and Greek scriptural translation traditions — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Alexandria as a cradle of early Christian theology and biblical interpretation — **HIST — Established**. Full details of Septuagint origins remain partly traditional — **TRAD/HIST — Disputed in detail**.

**See also:** [essays/early-african-christianity.html](essays/early-african-christianity.html) · [essays/egypt-in-scripture.html](essays/egypt-in-scripture.html)

## The Red Sea world

**Biblical names:** Hebrew *Yam Sûph* (Sea of Reeds / Red Sea in many English traditions); trade and kinship language involving Sheba, Dedan, Ophir (locations debated), Midian, and Cush; New Testament “Arabians” in Acts 2 beside African names.

**Modern geography:** The **Red Sea corridor** joining northeast Africa and Arabia — Egypt, Sudan, Eritrea, Djibouti, Saudi Arabia, Yemen — a zone of boats, incense, gold traditions, military campaigns, and population movement long before Islam or modern race categories.

**Key texts:** Exodus 13–15; 1 Kings 9–10 (Red Sea shipping under Solomon’s horizon); Isaiah 18; Acts 2:11; Acts 8 (the Gaza–Jerusalem road toward a Nile kingdom); wider prophetic oracles pairing Egypt, Cush, and southern partners.

**Evidence note:** Long-term Africa–Arabia connection across the Red Sea — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Uniform racial identity for “Arabs” or “Africans” in antiquity — **Unsupported** (see [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html)). Ancient eastern Arabian pigmentation diversity is illuminated, not exhausted, by samples such as the 2024 Bahrain genomes ([Cell Genomics / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10943591/)). Arab identity as historical development rather than a single original phenotype: [Peter Webb, British Academy](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/843/BAR27-10-Webb-reduced_0.pdf). Related long-term Red Sea population connections: [DOI 10.1002/ajpa.21522](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.21522).

**Reading tip:** Illustrate range — brown through very dark complexions — rather than a pale unmarked default or a single counter-stereotype.

## Aksum (later horizon)

**Biblical names:** Not a standard Old or New Testament place-name. Aksum belongs to **early African Christian history** that this edition treats as part of Scripture’s African afterlife and transmission.

**Modern geography:** Highland kingdom centred in northern Ethiopia and Eritrea; Red Sea port access via Adulis; Christianised in the fourth century under Ezana’s horizon. Geʿez Scriptures and liturgy carry biblical life forward for centuries.

**Key texts for reception:** Psalm and prophetic “Ethiopia/Cush” passages later read through Aksumite and Ethiopian Christian identity; New Testament canon and liturgy in Geʿez translation traditions; honour traditions around Acts 8 in some church memories.

**Evidence note:** Fourth-century royal Christianity at Aksum — **HIST/MAT — Established**. Direct narrative continuity from Acts 8’s official to Aksum’s conversion — **TRAD — not established as history**. Nubian Christian kingdoms (Nobatia, Makuria, Alodia) form a parallel Middle Nile Christian story — **HIST/MAT — Established**.

## How to read this atlas with Scripture

1. Prefer **ancient names** first; add modern states second.
2. When English says “Ethiopia,” ask whether the Hebrew or Greek points to **Cush/Nubia** or to later Horn traditions.
3. Keep **Jewish Levantine** settings intact while restoring African connections — Jesus and the apostles are not relocated by this map.
4. Use sample spreads for narrative depth: Genesis 10 ([spreads/03-genesis-10.html](spreads/03-genesis-10.html)), Exodus Egypt ([spreads/04-exodus-egypt.html](spreads/04-exodus-egypt.html)), Pentecost ([spreads/07-acts-pentecost.html](spreads/07-acts-pentecost.html)), Kandake ([spreads/08-acts-kandake.html](spreads/08-acts-kandake.html)).
5. When a viral claim outruns the map, open [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html).

Africa is indispensable to the biblical world. Indispensable is not the same as exclusive. See the biblical world whole.
