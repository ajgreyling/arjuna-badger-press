# Africa in the biblical worlds

### Essay

**Read with:** [Atlas](../atlas.md) · [How to read](../how-to-read.md)

---

The Bible’s world is Afro-Asiatic before it is European. That sentence is geography and history, not a racial takeover.

Israel’s story is rooted in the Levant. Its neighbours and partners repeatedly include **Egypt** and **Cush**, with Libya, Put, and later Cyrene and Alexandria pulling North Africa into view. Trade and empire moved people along the Nile, the coastal road, and the Red Sea. Refugees fled to Egypt; armies marched from Kush; Jewish communities lived in Alexandria and Cyrene; an African official reads Isaiah on a desert road.

### What “worlds” means

Africa was never one culture. Nile Egypt, Kushite kingdoms, Libyan groups, Cyrenaican Jews, and later Aksumite and Nubian Christians are distinct. “African Worlds” in the title resists flattening.

### What recovery requires

Popular art often lightened faces, moved Egypt into a vague Orient, and treated African Christianity as a nineteenth-century import. Recovery puts Egypt back on the African map, restores Cush as Middle Nile power, and names Cyrenians as New Testament actors.

### What recovery forbids

It forbids claiming every Israelite was modern Black, erasing Jewish peoplehood, or treating Genesis genealogies as DNA tests. **Restore without annexing.**
