# Sample Spread 09 — Acts 13:1–3 (with 11:19–26)

## Cyrene inside the Antioch church

*The African Worlds Study Bible* · Sampler

---

## Passage context

In Antioch, prophets and teachers include:

> “Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.” (Acts 13:1, KJV-style)

Earlier, men of **Cyprus and Cyrene**, scattered by persecution, speak the Lord Jesus to Greeks at Antioch (Acts 11:20). A great number believe. Barnabas fetches Saul; for a year they teach; the disciples are called Christians first in Antioch (11:26). From the leadership circle of chapter 13 the Spirit sends Barnabas and Saul on mission (13:2–3).

North African diaspora believers help midwife the church that first wears the name “Christians” and that sends the mission toward Asia Minor.

---

## Notes

**Cyrene on the map.** Cyrene stands in Cyrenaica, modern eastern **Libya**, North Africa — a Mediterranean port city with a Jewish community. Simon of Cyrene appears in the passion narratives (Matthew 27:32 and parallels); Cyrenians appear in Jerusalem debates (Acts 6:9) and at Pentecost (Acts 2:10). See [atlas.html](../atlas.html) · [spreads/07-acts-pentecost.html](07-acts-pentecost.html).

**Lucius of Cyrene.** Named among Antioch’s teachers. Luke offers no further biography; silence is not licence for legend. What is clear: a Cyrenian helps lead the multi-ethnic sending church of Acts 13. African geography is not only “mission field”; it is leadership address.

**Simeon called Niger.** *Niger* is Latin for “black.” It may describe dark complexion, be a cognomen, or both — **Disputed**. This edition notes the possibility without building a racial system on a nickname. Even a cautious reading keeps colour visible in the leadership list rather than bleaching the cast of Acts. Do not invent a biography; do not erase the name’s possible sense.

**African initiative, Jewish centre.** Antioch’s story still runs through Jewish believers scattered from Judea and through Saul/Paul the Pharisee. African and Cypriot speakers widen the audience to Greeks; they do not replace Israel’s story. Mission from Antioch is not Europe saving Africa; it is a Levantine–Mediterranean church with African teachers sending messengers into Asia Minor.

**Against the late-arrival myth.** When popular history implies Christianity entered Africa only with modern colonial missions, Acts 11–13 is a quiet refutation. North African Jews and converts are already teaching and sending. See [essays/early-african-christianity.html](../essays/early-african-christianity.html).

**Antisemitism watch.** Some online readings cast Jewish believers as villains of a story they began, then crown African names as racial replacements. That violates this edition’s covenant. Lucius does not need Jewish erasure to be important.

**Teaching move.** Trace Cyrene across Passion week, Pentecost, Acts 6, Acts 11, and Acts 13. One city keeps returning. Ask why curricula forget it.

## Africana reading note

Antioch models shared leadership without erasing difference. For African Independent and Initiated churches that know how the Spirit raises local teachers, Lucius is an early cousin — not in institutional succession, but in pattern: periphery names at the centre of sending. Read this spread against any curriculum that jumps from Paul to Europe and forgets Libya. Then refuse antisemitic readings that cast Jewish believers as villains of a story they began. Pride in Cyrene and honour for Jerusalem’s Jewish remnant can sit in one church meeting — as they did in Antioch.
