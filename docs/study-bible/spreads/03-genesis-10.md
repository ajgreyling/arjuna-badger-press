## Passage (context)

Genesis 10 lists peoples as descendants of Noah’s sons. Among Ham’s line appear **Cush, Miṣrayim (Egypt), Put, and Canaan**, with further names tied to cities, coasts, and regions. The chapter personifies lands and peoples as kin.

---

## Notes

### Africa in the Text

Egypt and Cush sit inside the Bible’s first ethnographic map. That is recovery: Africa is not an afterthought. Canaan (Levant) and Mesopotamian city names also appear under Ham — so the chapter **crosses later continental boxes**. It is not “Ham = Black Africa.”

### Words

English “races” and nineteenth-century colour hierarchies are foreign to this text. Genesis 10 is ethnogeography and kinship imagination.

### Abuse and repair

The racial “curse of Ham” misreads Genesis 9–10. Noah curses Canaan. Enslavers and colonial theologians invented a skin-colour curse. This edition names that history to refuse it.

### Africana readings

Pride that Egypt and Cush are named early is legitimate. Turning the table into a modern racial passport system is not.
