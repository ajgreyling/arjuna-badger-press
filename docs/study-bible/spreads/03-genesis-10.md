# Sample Spread 03 — Genesis 10

## A family map, not a race chart

*The African Worlds Study Bible* · Sampler

---

## Passage context

Genesis 10, the so-called Table of Nations, lists the descendants of Noah’s sons — Shem, Ham, and Japheth — as a map of peoples known to Israel’s storytellers. Among the lines of Ham appear **Cush, Miṣrayim (Egypt), Put, and Canaan** (Genesis 10:6). From Cush the list names Seba, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah, Sabteca, and Nimrod (10:7–8). From Miṣrayim come peoples associated with Egypt and neighbours, including the Pathrusim and Caphtorim in the wider list (10:13–14). Japhethine and Shemite lines map other neighbourhoods toward Anatolia, the Aegean horizon, Mesopotamia, and Arabia.

The chapter is geography and kinship in the idiom of genealogy. It is not a modern genetic test, not a skin-colour key, and not a charter for enslaving Africans.

---

## Notes

**What kind of text is this?** Ancient genealogies organise memory, politics, and neighbourhood. They explain who borders whom, who trades with whom, and how Israel locates itself among nations. Treating Genesis 10 as a scientific race taxonomy imports nineteenth-century categories into a Bronze–Iron Age literary form. Horizon 1 is a family map; Horizon 2 is ancient Near Eastern ethnographic imagination; Horizon 3 includes both African retrieval and racial abuse.

**Africa on the family map.** By placing **Cush, Egypt, and Put** in the same Hamite cluster as Canaan, the text binds northeast Africa into Israel’s imaginative world from the start. That is restoration material: Egypt and Cush are not afterthoughts. It is not annexation material: the chapter also maps peoples far beyond Africa. Central presence is not exclusive ownership. See [atlas.html](../atlas.html).

**Cush and Egypt.** Cush here aligns with the African Middle Nile world; Miṣrayim is Egypt. See [essays/cush-nubia-ethiopia.html](../essays/cush-nubia-ethiopia.html) and [essays/egypt-in-scripture.html](../essays/egypt-in-scripture.html). Nimrod’s Mesopotamian associations show how fluid these lists can be — another reason not to freeze them into racial destinies or modern passport offices.

**The Ham abuse.** Enslavers and racial theorists twisted “Ham” into a curse on Blackness. Genesis 9 curses **Canaan**, not African peoples as such. Genesis 10’s inclusion of African names was conscripted into that abuse and into later “Hamitic” race science. This edition marks that history under Abuse and Repair and rejects it as exegesis. Full labels: [claims-audit.html](../claims-audit.html).

**Jewish identity remains Levantine.** Shem’s line leads toward peoples among whom Israel narrates its closer kinship. Affirming African names in the Table does not relocate Abraham’s family to Napata or deny Jewish continuity. Jesus’ people are not rewritten by this chapter into a modern colour category. Restore without annexing.

**Teaching move.** Place three columns on a board: What the text lists; What archaeology knows of Cush/Egypt; What enslavers claimed. Keep the columns from leaking into each other without labels.

## Africana reading note

For many African readers, seeing Cush and Egypt named in Torah’s opening geography is a quiet correction of Sunday-school maps that began at Rome and ended at Galilee. Receive that correction with joy. Then refuse the temptation to reverse the harm by declaring every biblical hero “ours” alone or every Jew an impostor. The Table of Nations is a shared neighbourhood sketch. Read it as hospitality to complexity — Nile and Levant, Africa and Asia — under one creation God. In diaspora settings where Genesis 10 has been preached as racial destiny, this spread is repair work: name Cush loudly, curse the curse mythology louder.
