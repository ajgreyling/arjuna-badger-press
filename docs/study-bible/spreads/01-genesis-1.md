## Passage (context)

God gives seed-bearing plants and fruit trees to humanity for food, and green vegetation to land animals and birds. Within this creation account, dominion does not begin as permission to kill for food. Human and animal life share a plant-fed world.

Short wording (public-domain style): *“I have given you every plant yielding seed… and every tree with seed in its fruit; you shall have them for food… to every beast… I have given every green plant for food.”*

---

## Notes

### Africa in the Text

Genesis 1 is not set on a modern map of Africa. Its force for this edition is **theological geography of peace**: the world begins with abundance from the ground. Later biblical history will run through Egypt, Cush, and the Afro-Asiatic world; the creation table still frames what “very good” provision looked like before fear and slaughter dominate the narrative.

### Words

The Hebrew emphasises gift (“I have given”) more than a modern dietary manifesto. Still, the speech is the longest food instruction in the chapter — the camera lingers.

### Ancient worlds

Comparative ancient Near Eastern creation stories often assume predation or divine combat. Genesis 1’s plant table is distinctive in imagining a nonviolent food economy for humans and animals together.

### Africana readings

African and diaspora communities have long connected land, harvest, fasting, and justice. Reading Genesis 1 as an ideal of shared provision can critique extractive meat economies without erasing Genesis 9 or Torah practice. Keep horizons distinct: ancient ideal ≠ modern mandatory veganism for all Christians.
