# Sample Spread 07 — Acts 2:5–11

## Egypt, Libya, and Cyrenians at Pentecost

*The African Worlds Study Bible* · Sampler

---

## Passage context

Luke lists the nations represented among Jews staying in Jerusalem at Pentecost. The catalogue includes Mesopotamia, Judea, Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia, Phrygia and Pamphylia, **Egypt**, and **the parts of Libya about Cyrene**, as well as strangers of Rome, Jews and proselytes, Cretans and Arabians (Acts 2:9–11). They hear the mighty works of God spoken in their languages.

Africa is not waiting offstage for a later missionary age. African Jewish diaspora voices are inside the first Christian Pentecost narrative.

---

## Notes

**Who is present?** Luke emphasises **Jews and proselytes** from the diaspora (2:5, 10–11). The African names here are not yet a Gentile mission report; they are diaspora Judaism’s African reach — Egypt and Cyrenaica — gathered for the feast. Gentile inclusion unfolds across Acts; erase neither stage. An Africa-centred reading that skips the Jewish identity of these hearers annexes the scene.

**Egypt.** Points to the massive Jewish population of Hellenistic–Roman Egypt, with Alexandria as intellectual centre. Scripture in Greek, philosophical exegesis, and later Christian teachers all grow in that soil. See [atlas.html](../atlas.html) · [essays/egypt-in-scripture.html](../essays/egypt-in-scripture.html).

**Libya about Cyrene.** Cyrenaica in modern eastern Libya; Cyrene as Greek–Libyan city with documented Jewish community. Cyrenians reappear as synagogue disputants (Acts 6:9), as evangelists to Antiochenes (11:20), and in the leadership circle of Antioch (13:1). Pentecost’s list foreshadows that network. See [spreads/09-acts-cyrene.html](09-acts-cyrene.html).

**Arabians.** The list’s “Arabians” sit beside African names, reminding readers of the Red Sea and desert corridors joining Africa and Arabia. Do not paint that world in one skin tone; see [claims-audit.html](../claims-audit.html) and ancient eastern Arabian pigmentation evidence summarised in [essays/pigmentation-climate-diet.html](../essays/pigmentation-climate-diet.html).

**Translation and power.** Pentecost reverses Babel by gift, not by empire’s single language. For African mother-tongue theologies, this scene authorises hearing God’s works in local speech — a rebuke to colonial insistence that faith must arrive in a European tongue. The miracle is comprehension across diaspora languages, not the erasure of Jerusalem as centre of this particular day.

**Against the late-arrival myth.** When popular history implies Christianity entered Africa only with modern colonial missions, Acts 2 is already a quiet refutation — and Acts 8 and 13 will shout louder. See [essays/early-african-christianity.html](../essays/early-african-christianity.html).

**Teaching move.** Read the list slowly, pausing at Egypt and Cyrene. Ask congregants what they were taught about “when Africa became Christian.” Then keep reading Acts rather than jumping to the nineteenth century.

## Africana reading note

When congregations recite “ends of the earth,” begin the map at Acts 2’s African lines. Egypt and Cyrene are not decorative exoticism; they are synagogue geography. Let this spread tutor both pride and patience: pride that Africa is present at the outpouring; patience that Luke still has Samaria, the Kandake’s official, Cornelius, and Antioch’s African teachers ahead. Mother-tongue Bible translation projects can claim Pentecost as cousin, not as colonial leftover. Hold Jewish names in the list with honour; African presence here is Jewish African presence before it is anything else.
