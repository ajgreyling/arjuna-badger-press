# How to Read This Edition

*The African Worlds Study Bible* · Editorial front matter

---

## Editorial covenant

This edition exists to help readers **see the biblical world whole**. Africa belongs on the map of Scripture and of the earliest churches. Egypt is African. Cush is not a poetic blur. Cyrene is not a European city. Alexandria is not a footnote. African readers are not late guests who arrived only when modern European missions opened a door.

We restore what evidence can support. We refuse annexation.

**Restore without annexing** means:

- We name African lands, peoples, routes, and interpreters where the text and history place them.
- We do not claim that every Israelite, Judean, or biblical figure was African or modern Black.
- We do not use biblical genealogies as modern genetic pedigrees.
- We do not turn skin colour into proof of covenant, tribe, nationality, or moral worth.
- We affirm the Jewish identity of Jesus of Nazareth and of the earliest Jesus movement in Roman Judea.
- We do not call living Jewish communities impostors, “fake Jews,” or racial substitutes.
- We do not replace a Eurocentric picture with a mirror-image ethnocentrism.
- We do not collapse Egypt, Kush, Libya, Aksum, Carthage, and modern African churches into one timeless “African culture.”

The Bible arose chiefly through Jewish history in the Levant, inside an **Afro-Asiatic world** joined by the Nile, the Red Sea, deserts, ports, empires, trade, refuge, war, and migration. Central does not mean exclusive. Recovery does not require ownership of every name on the page.

When a note concerns Israel, Judaism, ancestry, or antisemitism, this edition privileges careful Jewish review and plain rejection of conspiracy. When a note concerns African geography or African Christianity, it refuses the habit of treating North Africa as somehow “not Africa.” Both commitments stand together. Anyone who demands hatred of Jews as the price of loving Africa has left this covenant. Anyone who demands the bleaching of Cush as the price of honouring Judea has also left it.

## What this edition is

This is an **Africa-centred, evidence-led study apparatus** designed to sit beside a licensed Bible text. The preferred translation for publisher discussion is the NRSVue, including feasibility for the Apocrypha/Deuterocanonical books; no translation has yet been licensed. Notes are reference-keyed so they can travel with another authorised text or appear first as a verse-keyed companion.

The apparatus aims to:

1. Place African geography and peoples inside ordinary reading, not only in specialist essays.
2. Trace African manuscript, translation, monastic, and early-church histories as part of biblical transmission.
3. Host continental and diaspora interpretation without pretending all African readers speak with one voice.
4. Mark sources and confidence so readers can tell text from archaeology, history from tradition, and testimony from proof.
5. Confront abusive receptions — the “curse of Ham,” colonial mission myths, antisemitic genealogies — under Abuse and Repair rather than in embarrassed silence.

It continues a lineage that includes Cain Hope Felder’s *Original African Heritage Study Bible* and the multi-contributor *Africa Study Bible*, while adding visible sourcing, a public claims audit, and explicit treatment of disputed racial and genealogical claims. See also [atlas.html](atlas.html), [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html), and [essays/africa-in-biblical-worlds.html](essays/africa-in-biblical-worlds.html).

## What this edition is not

- **Not a new revelation.** It does not invent verses, lost tribes on demand, or secret genealogies sealed in the editor’s drawer.
- **Not a race Bible.** It does not grade peoples by colour or assign salvation by phenotype.
- **Not anti-Jewish.** It rejects replacement myths, “synagogue of Satan” weaponising, and claims that contemporary Jews are impostors.
- **Not a vegan manifesto or a carnivore gospel.** Genesis opens with plant food; later texts permit and regulate animal food. Sequence matters; sloganising does not.
- **Not a museum of one culture.** Northeast Africa, the Maghreb, the Horn, and diaspora Atlantic worlds are related by history and geography, not identical.
- **Not finished dogma.** Contested notes carry confidence labels. Corrections will be public. A sampler is not yet a complete study Bible.

## Evidence labels

Every contested historical annotation carries **source** and **confidence** tags. The reader should never have to guess whether a sentence reports what Scripture says, what a dig uncovered, what a church remembers, or what a living community believes today.

### Source labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TXT** | Explicit wording or literary structure of the biblical text |
| **MAT** | Archaeological, epigraphic, numismatic, or manuscript evidence |
| **HIST** | Critical historical reconstruction from multiple sources |
| **TRAD** | Later Jewish, Christian, or community tradition |
| **REC** | Modern reception, interpretation, or direct testimony |
| **GEN** | Population-genetic evidence, used within strict limits |

### Confidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Broad evidentiary agreement |
| **Probable** | Best-supported reconstruction with remaining uncertainty |
| **Disputed** | Several credible interpretations remain |
| **Unsupported** | Evidence is insufficient or weighs against the claim |

A note may combine labels. Example from Acts 8: **TXT — Established** that Luke calls the official an *Aithiops* serving the Kandake; **HIST — Established** that Kandake is a Meroitic royal title pointing chiefly to the Middle Nile; **TRAD — historically unverified** that he founded the Ethiopian church; **HIST/MAT — Established** that Aksumite royal Christianity is securely documented in the fourth century.

**GEN** never stands alone as identity proof for a biblical person. Ancient DNA samples populations and probabilities; it does not baptise tribes, cancel peoples, or adjudicate covenant.

## The three-horizon method

Each major note is written across three horizons. Keep them distinct; then let them speak to one another.

### Horizon 1 — Text

What does the passage say in its wording, genre, and literary place? Paraphrase carefully. Do not smuggle later race theory into a genealogy list, or modern diet politics into a creation blessing. If Genesis 9 curses Canaan, do not report that it curses “Hamites” as a colour caste. If Acts does not say the official founded a church, do not make Luke say it.

### Horizon 2 — World

What can geography, languages, archaeology, inscriptions, and critical history establish about the ancient setting? Here Egypt stays in Africa; Cush is chiefly the Middle Nile; “Ethiopia” in Greek sources often means the Nile south of Egypt rather than the modern Ethiopian state. Modern borders appear only for orientation. Jewish Levantine settings remain Levantine even when African connections are thick.

### Horizon 3 — Reception

How have Jewish, Christian, African, and diaspora readers received, contested, sung, or weaponised this text? Reception can be life-giving or abusive. Ethiopianism singing Psalm 68, enslaved communities hearing Exodus, colonial preachers inventing Hamite slavery, antisemitic street charts — all belong here as **history of interpretation**, not as Horizon 1 facts unless the text itself says so.

Worked example: Acts 8. Horizon 1 — an *Aithiops* official of the Kandake is baptised after reading Isaiah. Horizon 2 — Kandake is Meroitic; the setting is Kush/Nubia in present-day Sudan. Horizon 3 — later tradition links him to Ethiopian Christianity; that tradition is real as tradition, not as Acts’ own claim. See [spreads/08-acts-kandake.html](spreads/08-acts-kandake.html).

## How to use the apparatus

1. **Read the passage first.** Notes serve the text; they do not replace it.
2. **Check the labels.** If a claim you love is marked Unsupported, sit with that discomfort before arguing with the footnote.
3. **Use the atlas** for place-names that feel vague: [atlas.html](atlas.html).
4. **Use the claims audit** when a social-media genealogy outruns the evidence: [claims-audit.html](claims-audit.html).
5. **Treat Living Voices as testimony.** A recorded interview documents self-understanding (**REC**). It is not a dig report (**MAT**).
6. **Hold Jewish and African presence together.** Restoring Africa does not require erasing Judea, the Levant, or Jewish continuity.
7. **Move from sampler to essays.** Short spreads train habits; essays widen the field without abandoning labels.

---

## A word on tone and humility

Popular European art often lightened, marginalised, or removed darker African and Arabian peoples. Correcting that habit is justice, not fashion. The remedy is evidence, not counter-mythology. Where we are wrong, we will say so. Where the evidence is thin, we will not shout louder. Confidence words — Established, Probable, Disputed, Unsupported — are a mercy for communities harmed by charismatic certainty that later collapses into shame or violence.

**See the biblical world whole — and keep the labels honest.**
