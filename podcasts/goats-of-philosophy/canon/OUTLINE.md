# The GOATs of Philosophy — Full Episode Outline

**Runtime shape:** ~90 minutes of countdown (8 philosophers, ~10 min each, with cold open and
transition) + a genuinely long, loose 2.5-3 hour guest conversation with Alex O'Connor. Total episode
length: a real 4-4.5 hour JRE-style unit, which is exactly the show's actual normal range.

## Cold Open (~5 min)
Joe and Jamie, no guest yet. Joe pitches the premise on-air: he's been wanting to do a "GOATs" list
for philosophy the way people do it for boxing or basketball, and had Jamie spend the week building
the list and pulling research. Banter about how you'd even rank philosophers (kills the "who's #1"
framing early, reframes it as "the guys everyone else had to respond to"). Sets up: eight names,
ten minutes each, then today's guest for the real conversation.

## Part One: The Countdown — 8 GOATs, ~10 min each (beat-sheet only, not scripted)

Each beat below is what a real 10-minute Joe/Jamie riff would actually hit: the one big idea, the
one weird biographical detail Joe would fixate on, and the one line that sets up why it still matters
today (feeding into how modern philosophy, and Alex's segment, treats it).

1. **Socrates** — never wrote anything down; the whole method is *admitting you don't know*
   (Socratic ignorance) as the actual power move; died by hemlock rather than stop asking questions.
   Joe-hook: "the guy invented podcasting by just asking people questions in the street and pissing
   off the wrong people."
2. **Plato** — the Cave, and the idea that what we call "real" might just be shadows on a wall; his
   own teacher's execution is *why* he distrusted democracy and public opinion. Joe-hook: the Cave as
   the original "what if we're in a simulation" (ties directly into what Alex will later push back on
   re: idealism vs. simulation theory as different claims people conflate).
3. **Aristotle** — the anti-Plato: reality is in the actual physical things, not perfect forms
   somewhere else; basically invented formal logic and about six other fields by accident. Joe-hook:
   tutored Alexander the Great — "imagine your philosophy professor also built an empire."
4. **René Descartes** — "I think, therefore I am": the one thing you can't doubt is that you're
   doubting; started modern philosophy by trying to doubt literally everything first. Joe-hook: the
   evil-demon thought experiment as the 1600s version of "what if this is all a simulation."
5. **Immanuel Kant** — never left his hometown, ran like clockwork, and still produced the idea that
   morality has to work as a universal rule for everyone, not a one-off excuse for you. Joe-hook: the
   most disciplined human who ever lived also basically invented modern ethics from a desk.
6. **Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel** — history isn't random, it moves through conflict and resolution
   (thesis/antithesis/synthesis) toward greater self-understanding; famously, almost willfully,
   difficult to read. Joe-hook: "so is Hegel saying the internet arguing with itself is like... the
   engine of progress?" (sets up a real callback later with Alex).
7. **Friedrich Nietzsche** — "God is dead," and what do you do with a universe that doesn't hand you
   meaning; the Übermensch as "build your own values since nobody's coming to hand you a script";
   went mad at the end, hugging a horse in Turin. Joe-hook: the most quoted and most misquoted
   philosopher in history, usually by people who never read past the one line.
8. **Ludwig Wittgenstein** — philosophy's problems are often just language confusing itself; gave
   away his family fortune, worked as a schoolteacher and a hospital porter mid-career, came back and
   changed his own mind about his own famous book. Joe-hook: "the guy solved philosophy, got bored,
   gave away his inheritance, and came back to unsolve it."

**Closing beat of Part One:** Joe, to camera/Jamie: "Alright — that's the history. Today's guest
is somebody actually doing this, right now, live, in real time, in front of an audience our age.
Let's bring him in." Alex O'Connor walks into the studio.

## Part Two: The Long Conversation — Alex O'Connor (2.5-3 hrs, outline only except the excerpt)

Structured the way a real JRE episode actually drifts: not clean chapters, but a loose arc with
recognisable centres of gravity. Roughly:

**Hour 1 — God, and why the argument never actually lands.** Starts predictably (does God exist),
but Joe pushes past the standard new-atheist script because Alex isn't running it — his honest,
public "increasingly drawn to the figure of Jesus" position surprises Joe, who expected a debate and
gets a genuinely undecided, still-searching guy instead. Touches the problem of evil, briefly, then
Joe pivots to "okay but do you think people believe for the argument or for the community," which
is the real question Alex actually cares about answering.

**Hour 1.5-2 — Free will, and whether any of this conversation is actually "us" doing it.** The
free-will-skeptic position, explained properly: not "everything is random" and not "everything is
fated," but that *authorship* doesn't survive either option. Joe gets stuck, genuinely, on the
practical version: "so should we still put people in jail?" Alex separates *deserving blame* from
*needing consequences* — you can keep the second without the first. Callback to Kant's segment (does
morality even work without a free chooser behind it?).

**Hour 2-2.5 — Consciousness, idealism, and effective altruism as "ethics you can actually do
something about."** Alex's real shift away from strict materialism, the hard problem, why panpsychism
and idealism keep getting taken more seriously by people who used to mock them. Then a turn toward
practical ethics: effective altruism, the factory-farming discussion (his honest former-vegan
position — critical of the system, unconvinced boycotts fix it, favors legislative reform), Joe
pushing back with his hunting/whole-animal-use position, genuinely good-faith disagreement, nobody
"wins."

**Hour 2.5-3 (THE EXCERPT LIVES HERE) — DMT, and whether a trip is data or noise.** Joe's actual
bucket-list line finally lands: "you ever done DMT?" Alex, careful, philosophically precise rather
than mystical-larper: the real question isn't "did you see something real," it's "what would even
count as evidence either way." Ties back to the idealism conversation from the previous block, and
to Plato's Cave from Part One, without being cute about it. This is the fully-scripted excerpt (see
`build/chapters/`).

**Closing (last ~15-20 min).** Wind-down, Jamie pulls something up, Joe asks where people can find
Alex's podcast (*Within Reason*), genuine mutual respect, no neat bow — ends mid-thought the way
real long episodes do, not with a summary.
