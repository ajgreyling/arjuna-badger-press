# Project — *The Unnumbered* *(working title)*

> **Type:** original commercial crime novel · **Lineage:** the Sherlock Holmes *engine* (public
> domain), deliberately **unfaithful** · **Status:** scaffolding · **Length:** one novel, ~110k,
> series potential · **Substrate:** present-day Stellenbosch, the Cape winelands, and the labour
> corridors north to the Namibian border.

## What this is, and what it is not

This is **not** [`../modern-sherlock/`](../modern-sherlock/) — *The Scarlet Thread*, the house's
canon-**faithful** London retelling (Reichenbach Files Book One). That book keeps Baker Street,
Mrs Hudson, Afghanistan, the lot. **This one is its heretic twin.** Same press, opposite doctrine.

What we take from Doyle: **the machine.** A man who reads the physical world faster and more
literally than the people around him; a second voice who narrates him because he cannot narrate
himself; a case that opens domestic and turns out to be structural. That's it.

What we throw out: the name, the canon, London, the violin, the cocaine, Mycroft-as-government,
Moriarty-as-mastermind, and — the big one — **deduction as a superpower with no bill attached.**

## The two mandates (this is why the book exists)

**Mandate #1 — the autistic detective, written from the inside, with the cost attached.**

The genre is thick with the neurotype as a party trick: brilliant, rude, unbothered, effortless. It
is almost entirely written by people who are not autistic. This book is not. Piet Buys does not have
a superpower; he has an **intolerance for the unexplained detail**, a memory that files everything
without being asked, and no working mechanism for letting go. That combination solves murders. It
also costs him: masking is a billable professional skill he performs all day and pays for all night;
a mortuary is easier than a shopping centre; being right and not being believed is the *ordinary*
condition of his life, not a plot device. The disability and the plot problem are the same problem.
**If a scene shows the gift without ever showing the bill, that scene is wrong.** See
[`canon/STYLE_GUIDE.md`](canon/STYLE_GUIDE.md) § *The bill*.

**Mandate #2 — indict the machine, not the man.**

House law (`all_sides_framing_law`). The serial killer is the *symptom*. The antagonist of the series
is the arrangement that let him work for eleven years: an economy where **safety is a purchased
commodity**, where the private-security industry is larger than the police service, where a missing
wine-farmer's daughter is a funded national project and fifty missing seasonal labourers are nobody's
file. Piet's whole business model *is* the indictment — he funds the dead women with the paranoia of
the rich. Never let the book become a tour of one monster's interiority. The monster is boring. The
arithmetic of who gets looked for is not.

## Premise

Pieter Buys was the second-to-last profiler in a police unit that has since been hollowed out. He
left, or was left; the answer changes depending on who is telling it. Now he works out of a room
above a bicycle shop in Stellenbosch, writing threat assessments for wine estates and armed-response
contracts for people who can afford to be frightened.

Charmaine Adonis was an FCS detective in Mitchells Plain until her back and the service parted
company. She brings him a file no one is paying for: a woman from a Rawsonville labour crew, gone
eight months, never entered as missing — because a seasonal worker who does not come back is not
missing, she has **gone for work**.

Piet finds the second one in an afternoon. He finds the ninth by Friday. They are strung along the
harvest calendar and the transport routes like beads on a wire, north through the Cederberg and the
Northern Cape toward a border where the dockets stop being anybody's.

They were never reported. They were never counted. They were never numbered.

## Provenance & legal (binding — read before drafting a word)

- **Doyle is public domain** in South Africa and internationally; the engine is free to use. We use
  **no canon names, no canon plots, no canon text.** Nothing in this book needs Doyle's permission
  and nothing in it should read as pastiche.
- **Real serial killers are NOT characters in this book.** Per the author's decision (2026-07-25),
  every killer is a **fictionalised composite** — method, geography and social conditions drawn from
  the real South African case record, names and acts invented and recombined. No living convicted
  offender is named as a character. No real victim's death is dramatised. Real victims have living
  families in Gauteng and the Cape *right now*.
- **The real cases are credited honestly in an appendix**, not smuggled in. See
  [`canon/SOURCE_NOTES.md`](canon/SOURCE_NOTES.md), which becomes the book's afterword.
- **The real investigators are credited, not portrayed.** Piet Buys is invented. He owes his patience
  and his confession-room method to the late **Piet Byleveld** (SAPS, d. 2017), and his profession to
  **Dr Micki Pistorius** (founded the SAPS Investigative Psychology Unit, 1996) and **Dr Gérard
  Labuschagne** (headed it 14½ years; now in private practice). An author's note says so plainly. He
  is none of them and does none of their cases.
- **Fact-check gate:** every factual claim in SOURCE_NOTES and every procedural detail in the
  manuscript goes through the house fact-checker (Perplexity) **before** the drafting run. The
  scaffold's factual assertions are flagged `[VERIFY]` until then.
