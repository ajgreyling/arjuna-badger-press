# Drafting prompt — *The Unnumbered*

You are drafting one chapter of a South African literary crime novel. Read, in this order and in
full, before writing a word: `canon/SEED_STORY.md`, `canon/STYLE_GUIDE.md`, `canon/CHARACTERS.md`,
`canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md`, `canon/PLOT.md`, `canon/WORLD.md`, and `build/story_state.md`.
Then read the previous chapter in `manuscript/` for voice.

## Voice

First person, past tense, **Charmaine "Charl" Adonis**. Cape Flats English with Afrikaans running
under it. Dry, fast, unsentimental, funny where funny is the only exit from a room. She is a
fourteen-year FCS detective, not local colour. Never write her as an accent.

**You never enter Piet Buys's head.** Not once, not for a line. He is seen from outside, always.
Charl interprets him and is sometimes wrong.

## The two hard mandates

1. **The bill.** Every chapter that shows what Piet can do also shows what it costs — in the same
   chapter or the next, in his body, never explained by an author. No effortless savant. No autism
   that conveniently supplies a plot fact for free. Equally: no misery-porn. He is good at this, he
   has a friend, he swims in cold water because he likes it.
2. **Indict the machine.** Never an individual as the source of the evil. Not the constable, not the
   broker, not the farmer. The target is the arrangement — who gets looked for, and who pays.

## Hard prohibitions

- No killer POV. No victim POV. No final-moments scene. Ever.
- No real serial killer named or characterised. No real victim's death dramatised.
- No reveal-lecture. Piet gives conclusions flatly, without working. The reader reconstructs.
- No canon Doyle names, devices, or easter eggs.
- No character characterised through a drink.
- No forensic detail that reads as instruction.

## Craft

- 2,500–4,000 words. Open in motion. Close on a turn — a fact, not a gimmick.
- Scene, not summary. If a passage explains instead of happening, delete it and build the scene.
- Multilingual by default; untranslated where context carries it; never italicise the country's own
  languages as foreign.
- The camera stays on the living. Aunty Miems's kitchen gets more words than any crime scene.
- Weather, dust, heat, gates. Every location notices whether it has a gate and who holds the remote.

## Output

Markdown. `# Chapter N` heading, then prose. No chapter title, no epigraph, no author notes in the
file. Update `build/story_state.md` after the chapter with: what changed, what the reader now knows,
what is still held back, and any new `[VERIFY]` items.
