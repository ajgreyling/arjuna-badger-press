# CHARACTERS — *The Unnumbered*

## Pieter "Piet" Buys — 44, Stellenbosch

**Function:** the observation engine. **Not** the narrator.

Afrikaans first language. English precise, slightly over-formal, no contractions when he is tired —
a second language spoken correctly rather than natively, which reads as coldness and is not.

**The wiring.** Autistic; diagnosed at 37, by a psychologist he consulted about something else. The
diagnosis explained his whole life and changed nothing about it, and he has complicated feelings
about the word that he does not discuss. He masks — expertly, expensively, all day. Masking is a
professional skill for him in the literal sense: it is what the client is paying for when he stands
in a boardroom on a wine estate and presents a threat assessment in a jacket.

**What he actually does.** He does not deduce; he cannot *stop*. Detail arrives unfiltered and
unranked, and an unresolved detail does not close. He solved his first case at 26 because a
bloodstain was the wrong shape and it kept him awake for nine days.

**The bill** (must appear; see STYLE_GUIDE):
- Mortuaries, mountains and empty roads are restful. Malls, weddings, open-plan offices, and the
  Stellenbosch tourist strip in season are not.
- After a full masking day he is non-verbal for two to three hours. Charl knows to text, not call.
- Interoception is poor: he forgets to eat, then crashes, and reads the crash as moral failure.
- Sleep is the first thing a case takes.
- He is *not* rude. He is precise, and precision reads as rudeness to people who wanted warmth. He
  knows this and has never found a workaround that survives contact with a bad week.

**The business.** Two-person consultancy, one room above a bicycle shop off Bird Street. Income:
threat and risk assessments for wine estates, armed-response contract audits, pre-employment
screening, the occasional insurance fraud. **He funds the dead women with the fear of the rich.**
He is aware of this and it is the closest thing he has to a politics.

**History.** Eleven years in the SAPS Investigative Psychology Section. He left in a way that
nobody in this book will describe the same way twice — Nyathi says he was pushed, Hendrik says he
was saved, Piet says he stopped being useful, and only one of them knows about the case in
Upington. `[hold this back — Book One reveals it in the last third]`

**The thing that is his and not a symptom:** he swims. Cold water, early, alone, the Eerste River
or the sea at Kogel Bay. Not therapy. He likes it.

**Wants:** the question to close. **Needs:** to accept that some of them never will, and to keep
working anyway. **Flaw:** over-fitting — he builds cathedrals on one bad datum.

## Charmaine "Charl" Adonis — 41, Kuils River (from Mitchells Plain)

**Function:** narrator, human read, every door in the book. **Not** the assistant.

Fourteen years SAPS, most of it FCS — Family Violence, Child Protection and Sexual Offences. She
was in the unit when it was disbanded in 2006, in the wilderness while it did not exist, and back in
it when it was reinstated. `[VERIFY dates]` Medically boarded at 39: two crushed discs and a bad
statement she gave about a colleague, and she will tell you it was the discs.

**Voice.** Cape Flats English and Afrikaans braided in one sentence, code-switching by room. She is
funny, and her humour is load-bearing — it is how she stays in rooms she cannot leave. She narrates
the book, so **the book's prose voice is hers**, not his. `[sensitivity read required]`

**The why.** She was nine years old in Mitchells Plain in 1994, when boys were being found in the
dunes and every mother on her road counted her children twice a day. She has never worked a case
that was not that case.

**What she has that he does not:** she can sit in a kitchen in Rawsonville for three hours drinking
tea and not ask the question, and get the answer. Piet cannot do this and has stopped pretending he
can. She is also the only person alive who can tell him he is wrong and be heard, because she does
it without softening it.

**Wants:** the file to be real — an official case, a docket number, a state that admits the count.
**Needs:** to stop treating being believed as the same thing as being safe.

## Col. Thandeka Nyathi — 48, SAPS Serious Violent Crime, Western Cape

**Function:** the institution. **Not** an obstacle-idiot.

Good detective, good commander, and the book must never let her be a fool. She has nine active
task teams, four vehicles that run, a forensic backlog measured in years, and a provincial
commissioner who wants the tourist-corridor robberies cleared before the season. When she declines
to open the linkage docket, **she is right about her resources and wrong about the world**, and the
reader should feel both at once.

She worked with Piet twice before and respects him without liking the experience.

**Her line, somewhere in the last third:** *"You think I don't believe you. I believe you. Do you
know how much worse that is?"*

---

## Hendrik Buys — 52, Piet's older brother. CEO, listed security group.

**Function:** access to everything — all of it **bought**.

He can produce a helicopter, a database, a former commissioner's mobile number, and a lawyer, in
under an hour. He is not corrupt. He is not a villain. He is **what replaced the state**, and he is
charming and generous and genuinely loves his brother, which is the problem: every time he helps,
he proves the book's thesis, and he does not notice.

The brothers' fault line is not childhood. It is that Hendrik's business is *worth more* the worse
the country gets, and Piet has said this out loud exactly once, at a family lunch, and has not been
invited to another one.

---

## Dr Elsie Mtimkulu — 57, forensic pathologist, Salt River Mortuary

**Function:** the conscience. Keeper of the unidentified dead.

She has a wall of case numbers with no names on them. She has been asking, in writing, for nineteen
years and to four successive administrations, for one specific thing: that the national forensic DNA
database be **pointed at the unidentified dead**. The capability exists. Familial searching works.
It is simply not aimed at them. (FACTS §3.)

She is precise about numbers in a way that is characterisation: she will not say "ten thousand a
year" because she cannot source it, and she *will* say that on the day you are standing there, this
province held two hundred and eighty-nine people who were buried without a name, and that the law
allows it after thirty days.

She is the only character who has read every file and the only one who is not surprised. She is the
one who tells Charl what the title means.

## The killer — Book One `[name TBD, fictionalised composite]`

**Binding:** see SOURCE_NOTES. He is invented. No real offender's name, nickname, or signature.

**Design constraints:**
- **Not a genius. Not charismatic. No philosophy.** He is not given a memorable speech and he does
  not get a POV chapter. `[hard rule — revisit only with a very good reason]`
- His method is a **labour broker's clipboard**: he offers seasonal work that exists just enough to
  be plausible, in a country where that offer is the most ordinary thing in the world. His victims
  get into the bakkie willingly, in daylight, in front of witnesses who think nothing of it.
- He is caught not by brilliance but by **bookkeeping** — Piet reconstructs a hiring pattern from
  three seasons of harvest records and a fuel card.
- His power is entirely borrowed from the arrangement. Remove the arrangement and he is a man with
  a bakkie. **That is the horror, and it is the only horror this book indulges.**
- What he is *not*: a mirror for Piet. No "we are alike, you and I." If that sentence appears in a
  draft, delete the scene.
