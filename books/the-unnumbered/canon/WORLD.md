# WORLD — *The Unnumbered*

> Present day. Real country, real geography, invented institutions where an invented one is safer
> than a real one. The world is not backdrop — **the world is the antagonist.**

## The thesis, in one fact

South Africa has more private security officers than police. Safety is a **product**. It is sold to
people who can pay, on a subscription, with a panic button and a four-minute response time — and it
is not sold to anyone else, and there is no public equivalent to fall back on.

Everything in this book follows from that. Piet is a *vendor*. Hendrik is a *market leader*. The
women in the harvest crews are not customers, and therefore they are not covered, and therefore when
they disappear there is no one whose job it is to notice.

## The geography (the spine of the plot)

**Stellenbosch** — the base. Oak-lined, moneyed, tourist-thick in season, ringed by farms whose
labour lives somewhere the tourists do not drive. The gap between the town and the farm road is
about eleven minutes and about a century. Piet's office is above a bicycle shop off Bird Street;
his clients are up the R44 behind gates.

**De Doorns / Rawsonville / Worcester — the Breede Valley.** Where the first file comes from. Table
grapes; roughly half the province's farm workforce is seasonal, mostly women, hired through brokers,
living off-farm in rented rooms with short contracts or none (FACTS §4). **De Doorns is the centre
of the broker economy** and the only rural site of the 2008/09 xenophobic violence — 14–17 November
2009, ~3,000 Zimbabwean workers displaced in three days, camped on the Hexvallei sports field
(FACTS §5). Nobody in the valley calls it anything but *2009*, and they say it the way you say a
year, not an event. **Handle with the machine-not-the-people law.**

**Northward, the corridor:** the N7 up the West Coast, the Cederberg, Vanrhynsdorp, Springbok, the
Northern Cape scrub, and the border posts at the top. Rooibos, table grapes, citrus — each with its
own season, each pulling crews in and letting them go, six weeks here, eight weeks there. **A person
moving along that calendar is invisible by design**, and so is a person hunting along it.

**Kogel Bay / the Eerste River** — where Piet swims. The only places in the book where he is easy.

## The institutions

**SAPS** — real, and written with respect and without flattery. Under-resourced, backlogged, staffed
by people like Nyathi who are competent and losing. **The book's target is never an individual
officer.** It is the arithmetic they are handed.

**The labour broker** — the true hinge of the book. A man with a clipboard and a bakkie assembles a
crew for a farm, takes a cut, and is the only record that the crew existed. Many workers are
undocumented, cross-border, or both. A woman who does not come back has *gone for work.* That
sentence is the murder weapon.

## The texture rules

- **February heat, the south-easter, dust on a farm road, the specific grey-green of a rooibos
  field.** The Cape is not postcard. Write the working country, not the wine route.
- **Money is always visible.** Who paid for the helicopter. What Piet's invoice says. What the crew
  earns a day. `[VERIFY current seasonal rates]`
- **Nobody in this book is a symbol.** Every farmer, broker, constable and mother is specific enough
  to argue back.
- **The gates.** Every scene notices whether the place has a gate, who holds the remote, and what
  the response-time sticker on it says. It is the quietest way to keep the thesis running under the
  floor without a single speech.
