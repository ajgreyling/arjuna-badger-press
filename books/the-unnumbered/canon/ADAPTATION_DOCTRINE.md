# ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE — *The Unnumbered*

> How much Doyle, and where. The short answer: **the engine, and nothing else.** This book is the
> house's *unfaithful* Sherlock — the deliberate opposite of [`../../modern-sherlock/`](../../modern-sherlock/),
> which keeps everything. If a reader finishes this book and says "that was a Sherlock retelling,"
> we have failed. If they say "that was the same *machine*, aimed at something that matters here,"
> we have won.

## What we keep (four things, that's all)

1. **The observation engine.** A mind that reads the physical world faster, more literally, and more
   completely than the people beside it — and that reads *people* through objects rather than
   through faces. This is the whole borrowing.
2. **The two-hander.** One who sees, one who tells. The narrator is not the solver, and the solver
   cannot narrate himself. The friction between the two voices is the prose style.
3. **The escalation shape.** A case that arrives domestic and private, and turns out to be
   structural and public. Doyle did this constantly; it is the most useful thing he built.
4. **The consulting position.** Outside the institution, brought in by it, resented by it, without
   its powers. In South Africa this is not an eccentricity — it is **an industry**, and that fact
   converts Doyle's whimsy into the book's thesis.

## What we throw out (name them, so no one drifts back)

| Doyle | Why it goes |
|---|---|
| Baker Street, London, the fog, the hansom | Wrong hemisphere, wrong century, already done in *The Scarlet Thread* |
| The name Holmes / Watson / Lestrade / Mycroft / Moriarty / Hudson / Adler | No canon names anywhere. Not as jokes, not as easter eggs, not as street names |
| Deduction as magic | **The core heresy.** See below |
| The savant who pays no price | See PROJECT.md Mandate #1 |
| Moriarty, the criminal genius | A single evil mastermind is a *comforting* explanation. We do not comfort |
| The violin, the cocaine, the dressing gown, the Irregulars | Costume. If we want texture we invent our own |
| Victorian class comedy | Replaced by South African class *fact*, which is not comedy |
| The reveal-lecture ("You will observe, Watson…") | Replaced — see below |

## The core heresy: deduction is not magic, it is intolerance

Doyle's Holmes performs a miracle and then explains it, and the explanation is the pleasure. That
trick is a lie about how minds work, and it is specifically a lie about **this** mind.

Piet Buys does not deduce. He **cannot stop noticing**, and he cannot leave a noticed thing alone.
The mud on a boot does not announce a conclusion to him; it announces *an unresolved question*, and
the question will not switch off, and it will still be running at four in the morning while he lies
awake with the fan on for the white noise. He solves crimes for the same reason he cannot get
through a Woolworths on a Saturday: **the filter that lets other people discard information was
never installed.**

Therefore, in prose:

- **No reveal-lectures.** He does not perform the chain for an audience. He gives the *conclusion*,
  flatly, with no working — because to him the working is not interesting, it is what he was trying
  to get away from. Charl (and the reader) reconstruct the chain afterwards. This is more fun and
  it is true.
- **He is wrong sometimes,** and wrong in a specific way: over-fitting. He builds a beautiful
  structure on one bad datum and cannot abandon it until something physically contradicts it.
  Charl's job — the *real* Watson function in this book — is to be the person who can say "or she
  just liked those shoes," and be listened to.
- **Every deduction has a body-cost in the same scene or the next.** No exceptions.

## The Watson inversion

Charl Adonis narrates, but she is not the admiring lesser. She has fourteen years of FCS work, a
register Piet cannot produce and cannot fake, and every door in the book. She is the one mothers
talk to. She is the one who knows when to stop asking.

The book's engine is that **neither of them can do it alone, and both of them know it** — no
mystique, no worship. If a passage has her marvelling at him, cut it.

## The Moriarty rule (binding, house law)

*All-sides framing law: indict the machine, not the people.*

Book One has a killer, and he is a man, and he is caught. He is not a genius. He is not charismatic.
He is not given a philosophy. He is an ordinary man with a bakkie, a labour-broker's clipboard, and
a country that made him invisible by making his victims invisible first.

The **series** antagonist is the arrangement: the arithmetic of who gets looked for. Every book in
this series ends with a man in custody and the arrangement still running. That is the honest ending
and it is also the reason there is a Book Two.
