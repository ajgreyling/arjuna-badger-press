## 1. The thesis: safety is a purchased commodity ✅ VERIFIED

- **PSIRA-registered security officers: over 2.6 million.** Of those, **~586,000 are actively
  employed.** (PSIRA / Portfolio Committee on Police, 2023/24.)
- **SAPS personnel strength: 184,106** as at 31 March 2024.
- South Africa has the **largest private security industry per capita in the world** — more active
  private security officers than SAPS and the SANDF **combined**.
- Police-to-population ratio cited at roughly **1:427**.

**This is the whole book.** Roughly three private officers for every police officer, sold by
subscription to whoever can pay. Hendrik's business is not a metaphor; it is the fourth-largest
employer of armed men in the country's imagination and it answers to shareholders.

## 2. Charl's biography: the FCS gap ✅ VERIFIED — dates now exact

- FCS units were **broken up in late September 2006**, decentralised to station level under then
  National Commissioner **Jackie Selebi**.
- The Minister of Police ordered re-establishment in the **budget vote of 6 May 2010**; units were
  **re-established in June 2010**, with a Forensic Social Work Section added.
- Pre-2006 national footprint: **67 FCS units, 1,261 members** (and 32 Serious Violent Crime units,
  909 members).

**Use:** Charl joined FCS young, was in it when it was dismantled, spent 2006–2010 at station level
watching those cases go to whoever was on the desk, and came back in 2010. She has a sentence about

## 3. The title: the unidentified dead ✅ VERIFIED

- **~3,186 unclaimed bodies** in state mortuaries nationally as at **August 2024**; KZN and Eastern
  Cape worst affected. Some held **up to six years**.
- **Western Cape: 289 pauper burials in 2024**, of which **279 in Cape Town**.
- Pauper burial may proceed after **30 days** unidentified.
- **National forensic DNA backlog exceeded 140,000 cases.**
- The **National Forensic DNA Database (NFDD-ZA)** supports familial searching, but this capability
  is **underutilised for humanitarian identification** — i.e. the tool to match the missing to the
  unidentified exists and is not being pointed at them. **This is Elsie's nineteen-year letter.**
- A published retrospective exists on **unidentified decedents at Salt River Mortuary, Cape Town,
  2010–2017**.

⚠️ **CANON CORRECTION:** Elsie Mtimkulu works at **Salt River Mortuary**, not Tygerberg. Salt River
is the Cape Town forensic pathology institute and carries the actual published record of
unidentified decedents. Update CHARACTERS.md.

⚠️ One source gives "approximately 10,000 unidentified individuals buried each year" nationally —
that is an order of magnitude above the 3,186 figure, which counts bodies *currently held*, not
annual burials. **Do not put either number in a character's mouth as a flat fact.** Elsie is careful
about exactly this, and her carefulness is characterisation.

## 4. The murder weapon: the seasonal-labour system ✅ VERIFIED — and better than the scaffold guessed

- **The calendar.** Western and Northern Cape grape farms: vines prepared **August–September**;
  harvest and packing run through to **March–April**. A worker's year is a sequence of six- and
  eight-week engagements in different districts.
- **De Doorns** (Breede Valley municipality — with **Worcester, Rawsonville, Touwsrivier**) is the
  table-grape centre.
- **In 2012, of ~121,000 Western Cape farm workers, slightly more than half were seasonal, mostly
  women, employed via labour brokers.**
- The structural shift: away from on-farm permanent workers, toward **off-farm seasonal and casual
  workers with short-term or no contracts** — explicitly because it lets farmers avoid labour-law
  obligations.
- Seasonal workers face documented **seasonal food insecurity** (studied in the Northern Cape).
- **National minimum wage from 1 March 2026: R30.23/hour** (up from R28.79) — farm workers included
  at the same rate. ~**R241.84 for an eight-hour day**, ~R4,974/month full-time.

⚠️ **CANON UPGRADE — use De Doorns.** The scaffold put victim #1 in Rawsonville. Keep Rawsonville as
Aunty Miems's home, but the **crews and the broker economy centre on De Doorns**, and De Doorns
carries the other load-bearing fact below.

**The mechanism, stated plainly:** a woman who leaves for a six-week engagement in another district,
arranged by a broker, paid in cash, living in rented rooms off-farm, with no contract — has no
employer of record, no fixed address for that period, and no one whose job it is to notice she did
not arrive. **The book's killer does not need to hide bodies. The system hides the absence.**

Sources: [PMC — Seasonal food insecurity among farm workers, Northern Cape](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6683081/) ·
[M&G — Sour grapes for farmers, workers](https://mg.co.za/business/2013-01-18-sour-grapes-for-farmers-workers/) ·

## 5. De Doorns, November 2009 ✅ VERIFIED — the book's deepest floor

- **14 November 2009:** first wave of xenophobic attacks in **Ekuphumleni**, De Doorns. **68
  Zimbabwean nationals displaced.**
- **17 November 2009:** violence intensified, spreading to **Stofland** and **Hasie Square**.
  **~3,000 Zimbabweans displaced.**
- The displaced first went to **De Doorns police station**, then were moved on **18 November** to
  the **Hexvallei Sportklub** sports field, which became an IDP camp. PASSOP was the first
  organisation on site.
- **De Doorns was the only *rural* site of xenophobic violence in the 2008/09 national wave.**

**Why this is the floor of the whole book:** three thousand people, in this valley, in this labour
system, moved off a map in three days — and the state's own records of who they were begin and end
with a sports field. A cross-border workforce lives inside the seasonal-crew economy here, on the
record, at scale. Its members can leave a district and leave no trace of having been in it.

**How it is used:** never as a set piece, never as a lecture, and *never* as a scene the book
dramatises for effect. It is the reason a count can be more than thirty while the state confirms
four. One character — probably the broker, possibly Aunty Miems — refers to it the way people refer
to a year rather than an event: *"after 2009."* That is all. The reader who knows, knows; the reader
who does not is told plainly once, in the afterword.

**House law check:** indict the machine, not the people. The violence in De Doorns came out of
people competing for six-week jobs inside a system designed to make them compete. If a draft ever
lets a single community carry the blame for 2009, that draft is wrong and gets cut.

Sources: [Freedom House — De Doorns community case study](https://freedomhouse.org/sites/default/files/De-Doorns_ZA_Community_Case_Study_FINAL.pdf) ·
[SAHO — Xenophobic violence in democratic South Africa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xenophobic-violence-democratic-south-africa) ·
[JSAS — The dilemma of anti-xenophobia discourse after De Doorns](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03057070.2013.825133)

## 5b. How a missing person is actually reported ✅ VERIFIED — this is chapter 3

- **There is no 24-hour rule.** SAPS is explicit: no 24- or 48-hour waiting period exists, and a
  missing-person report **must be opened and investigated immediately**.
- The form is the **SAPS 55(A)**. The complainant fills it in; it guards against hoax reports and
  authorises circulation of the photograph and details.
- Details go into the **National Bureau for Missing Persons** database (a Crime Stop project).
- An investigating officer is assigned; the complainant should take the detective's name and
  contact details.
- **And yet:** the Portfolio Committee chairperson has issued a media statement expressing concern
  at **non-adherence to the directive** — i.e. members continue to turn people away for 24 hours.
  There are contemporary reports of exactly this happening.

**This is the most quietly devastating fact in the file, and it is chapter 3.** Aunty Miems was told
three times to come back tomorrow. Every one of those three times was against standing instruction.
Nobody broke a law. Nobody will be disciplined. There is no docket because a man behind a counter
was tired and said a thing that is not true and that everybody believes.

**Charl's Movement II argument rests on this**, and she must state it exactly right, because she is
the one person in the book who has stood on both sides of that counter: *the rule was always that
you open it. The rule was never the problem.*

Sources: [SAPS — Report a missing person](https://www.saps.gov.za/services/report_missing_person.php) ·
[Parliament of SA — concern at non-adherence to the directive](https://www.parliament.gov.za/press-releases/media-statement-chairperson-concerned-non-adherence-directive-reporting-missing-person) ·
[Cape Town Etc — SAPS continue waiting 24 hours](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/concern-as-saps-continue-waiting-24-hours-to-file-missing-persons-report/)

## 6. Method sources — mechanism only, no names on the page

**The fake job offer** ✅ VERIFIED. In the 1994–95 Gauteng series, the offender ran a **shell
organisation posing as an anti-abuse charity**, walked around townships **handing out
official-looking forms**, presented himself as a businessman who wanted to help, and offered
**desk jobs that did not exist** to women aged roughly 18–45. Convicted of 38 murders and 40 rapes.

> **Our transposition:** not a fake charity — a **real-looking labour placement**. Our man has a
> clipboard, a crew list, a bakkie, and an arrangement with a packhouse that exists. The offer is
> *not fictional*, which is worse: the work is real, the wage is real, and only the destination is
> not. Women get in willingly, in daylight, in front of witnesses who think nothing of it, because
> **that is exactly what getting a job looks like here.**

**The transport corridor** ✅ VERIFIED. The Namibian **B1 Butcher** case: at least five women
murdered 2005–2007, remains found near National Road **B1**, which runs north–south across the
country. Three identified, two never identified. Bodies showed signs of **freezing/refrigeration**.
One man arrested and acquitted for lack of evidence; another implicated after suicide in 2008;
**the case remains unsolved.**

> **Our transposition:** the corridor and the unsolved-across-a-border shape. **Not the
> refrigeration signature** — that is distinctive enough to be recognisable, and we do not borrow
> signatures. Ours is the harvest calendar, and the northward drift is the crews', not a dumping
> route.

## 8. The northern corridor ✅ VERIFIED — **and it corrected the draft twice**

**Vioolsdrif / Noordoewer.** The N7's northern end; connects by road bridge to **Noordoewer**,
Namibia, where it becomes the **B1**. Busiest land crossing between the two countries, open 24
hours, the main Cape Town–Windhoek road.

⚠️ **CORRECTION 1 — there is no filling station at Vioolsdrif.** The last fuel on the South African
side is **Steinkopf, about 67 km short of the border**. The nearest pump is **across the line, at
Noordoewer, in Namibia.**

> The draft of ch. 16 had the fuel card's last transaction at Vioolsdrif. That is impossible.
> **The corrected version is better:** the last *South African* transaction is Steinkopf — and in
> two windows the next transaction on that card is at **Noordoewer**. A South African fleet card,
> on a bakkie belonging to a dead man's deregistered CC, buying diesel in another country. Nobody
> in this book has to say the word "border"; the card says it. `[VERIFY: do SA fleet cards
> transact in Namibia, and on which networks?]`
>
> Bonus the research handed us: the road they are following **is called the B1 on the other side.**
> The book never remarks on this. Readers who know the real unsolved Namibian case will feel the
> floor move; readers who don't lose nothing. We borrow no signature and name no one. (FACTS §6.)

**Aussenkehr.** Agricultural settlement, **Karas Region**, on the Orange River **immediately
adjacent to the South African border**. Table grapes, exported to Europe.

- **Harvest: early November to the second week of January** — genuinely weeks ahead of the Cape's
  December–April season. The draft's core geography holds: **the Cape's dead shoulder is the
  river's moving season.**
- One producer alone: ~**2,000 seasonal** and ~300 permanent workers for that harvest.
- Population **4,522** (2011 census); a non-seasonal core of ~**5,000**, rising to as many as
  **20,000** in harvest.
- Living conditions are extensively and critically documented (Daily Maverick / CCIJ / Windhoek
  Observer / The Namibian). **Report, do not gawp.**

⚠️ **CORRECTION 2 — the seasonal workforce is predominantly Namibian internal migrants**, largely
Oshiwambo-speaking people from the north of the country. **No source found for a substantial
Zimbabwean workforce at Aussenkehr.**

> The ch. 16 draft says "substantially not South African and substantially undocumented." **Cut
> that.** It is unsourced and it quietly imports the De Doorns story into a country where it does
> not belong — exactly the kind of borrowed grievance the house law forbids.
>
> **The true version is stronger and it must replace it:** a settlement of five thousand that
> becomes twenty thousand for nine weeks, on a river, against a border, where the seasonal
> workforce comes from a thousand kilometres *north* — so a South African woman who goes up there
> is the foreigner, in a place where nobody knows her face, that empties again in January. She does
> not vanish into a lawless zone. She vanishes into a **census that only exists in the off-season.**
