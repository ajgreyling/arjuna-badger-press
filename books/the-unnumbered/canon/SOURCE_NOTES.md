# SOURCE_NOTES — *The Unnumbered*

> **SSOT for the real record.** What actually happened, what we take from it, and the hard line
> between the two. This file becomes the book's **afterword** — the reader is told exactly what is
> real and exactly what is invented, because a crime novel that blurs that line is doing something
> dishonest to people who are still alive.
>
> **Every line marked `[VERIFY]` must clear the house fact-checker before drafting.** The scaffold
> is written from working knowledge; working knowledge is not a source.

## The binding rule

**We take method, geography and social conditions. We do not take people.**

No real offender appears as a character or is named in the fiction. No real victim's death is
dramatised, renamed, or re-staged. Where a real case supplies a *mechanism* — how a man got women
into a car, why nobody looked for them — we use the mechanism and invent everything else around it.

Rationale, stated plainly for anyone who picks this file up later: the men are alive and in prison,
and inventing acts for a named living person is defamation. More importantly, the victims are real,
recent, and survived by mothers and sisters who did not consent to be in a novel. And naming the
killers hands their names another book. They have enough.

## What we do NOT do

- No offender's real name, alias, nickname, prison number, or signature detail on the page.
- No real victim named, and no invented victim given a real victim's circumstances closely enough
  to be recognisable to a family.
- No "based on a true story" marketing. The book is fiction that **knows** its ground.
- No forensic or procedural detail that functions as instruction.
- No scene that invites the reader to enjoy the killer. Method is shown where it indicts the
  system that permitted it, and nowhere else.

## The afterword promise (drafted early on purpose)

> *This is a novel. Piet Buys never existed, and neither did the man he hunts. The country they
> move through does. The women in this book are invented; the reason nobody looked for them is not.
> I owe the shape of Buys's patience to a detective named Piet Byleveld, who died in 2017, and his
> profession to Micki Pistorius and Gérard Labuschagne, who built and kept a unit this country has
> since allowed to thin out. They are not in this book. Their work made it possible to imagine.*
