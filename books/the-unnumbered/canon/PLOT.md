# PLOT — *The Unnumbered*

> Four movements, ~32 chapters, ~110k. Beat sheet is a **skeleton, not a contract** — the drafting
> run may improve it, but the movement turns and the four hard rules below are fixed.

## The four fixed points

1. The book opens on a woman nobody reported, and closes on a number nobody can produce.
2. Piet is **badly wrong** at the top of Movement III, and the wrongness hurts a living person.
3. The killer is caught by **bookkeeping**, not brilliance, and the arrest is anticlimactic on
   purpose.
4. The arrangement is still running on the last page. Nothing is fixed. Someone is holding a list.

## Movement I — *Gone for work* (ch. 1–8)

✅ **DRAFTED 2026-07-25 — the beats below are the actual drafted ones and are now canon.**

- **1.** Wine-estate threat assessment. Piet reconstructs a fourteen-month staff theft from stock
  dates alone (it left on Tuesdays; the count is Wednesday), refuses to name the driver, walks out.
  Forty minutes later he cannot answer a question about food. The machine and the bill in one
  chapter. The gate stickers start here and never stop.
- **2.** The office above Riaan's bicycle shop; the empty wall. Charl hands over the Checkers packet
  — and confesses she carried it four days because she knew he would not be able to put it down.
  Piet: *"Not lost. Lost is a thing that happens to a docket. There is no docket."* They go, and he
  leaves the jacket, because a jacket in a kitchen says who is being interviewed.
- **3.** Aunty Miems's kitchen. Two hours eleven minutes; Piet says nothing after *"Miems."* The
  three refusals, every one against standing instruction. *"Ek het nie meer 'n storie gehad nie."*
  Charl tells her the truth about the 24-hour myth, and it is worse than a lie, because a lie has
  somebody standing behind it. **Curtain: the C4 slip out of the Quality Street tin** — nobody had
  ever asked her for it. Piet institutes the Friday call.
- **4.** The method chapter. A public labour page; forty-one women posting their names and numbers
  under an ad, because speed is the whole strategy. *"There is nothing wrong with that page."*
  Bernadine Klaasen found in one morning — *"Anyone could have found her. Nobody was looking."*
  **Doctrine on the page:** he never explains the chain — *"it is not a card trick, it is a tap I
  cannot close."* Wednesday is the bill.
- **5.** De Doorns. **Ashwin Damon**, broker — courteous, cooperative, thirty calls a day, keeps no
  records because why would he. **The refinement that makes the whole book work: the farm is real,
  the wage is real, the work is real. Only the destination is false, and only sometimes** — which is
  why nobody ever phones to check. A waitress says *"veral ná 2009"* the way you say a year. Piet:
  everybody is bad at keeping a list of people and excellent at keeping a list of money.
- **6.** Nine names go up — onto the empty wall. Piet refuses the word *serial* except once, to one
  person, when it will move something. The bill inverts: he is **lit**, and knows the only thing
  that has ever fitted him exactly is a floor covered in dead women. The Friday call, and why Aunty
  Miems is not told there are nine.
- **7.** The map is wrong for four days — pins on the N7 make a corridor, and *"I have made a map of
  agriculture."* Charl cracks it from the top of the pass: **not where — when.** The **shoulder**:
  the six-to-nine days between engagements when no one on earth expects to hear from her. The
  disappearance is eight days; the silence is engineered by the calendar, free, twice a year.
- **8. Turn — bigger than planned.** Charl opens the drawer on a Sunday: a pencil estimate on a bank
  envelope (`≥22 in 4 yrs. ≥30 if from '19`) and a maroon lever-arch of **thirty-one absences begun
  three years and four months before this case.** He was not deducing in ch. 2 — he was
  **confirming.** Also in the drawer, folded soft from handling: an eleven-year-old occurrence-book
  page stamped **Upington**, which she does not read, because she is frightened. His defence: *"You
  would have believed me. On nothing."* Her closing judgement — one hand, one pencil, three years,
  no second opinion: **"a room with no windows in it, and I was about to walk in and close the
  door."**

## Movement II — *No docket* (ch. 9–16) ✅ **DRAFTED** — actual beats below, now canon

- **9.** Nyathi gives them forty minutes and listens for thirty-one. She believes them and cannot
  act: nine task teams, four vehicles that go, a 140k national DNA queue. Piet says *serial* once,
  then tells her to pull the taxi-conflict team — and she answers with his own book: *"that's called
  every decision that has ever been made in this country about who gets looked for."* She has been
  trying since six a.m. to find a way. Her ask: not nine absences — **two dockets in one district
  with a common denominator.** Her method: **bring the mothers**, because taking a 55(A) is already
  the instruction and nobody can tell her no. Her reason for believing them: *"I have never had
  someone give me a number of victims that was lower than the one they actually believed."* Curtain,
  in the corridor: **"There is nobody to replace. That is the part I did not want to know."**
- **10.** Six mothers at De Doorns station. **Thirty-eight minutes** for a 55(A) that took eight
  months to be allowed to make; *"Dis al?"* Piet drives strangers four hours to Upington and will
  not enter the town. **Grieta** arrives uninvited: her daughter has had a docket for two years and
  it produced exactly as much as no docket at all — *"the reporting is the first wall. There are
  four."* Then the mothers do what the state didn't: they tell each other. 206 calls in nine days,
  two of them ours, and every one a woman who had needed to tell somebody. Nyathi opens the series
  and points them at Salt River.
- **11. Elsie Mtimkulu.** The R355 unidentified. A sample is not a search. The familial-search
  capability exists and is aimed at crime, not at the missing. She refuses to say a number she
  cannot source (289 provincial / 279 metro, and no more). **The title lands:** they are among the
  most thoroughly *numbered* people in the country — DR number, case number, plot number, page in a
  book — and they are never **counted**, because nobody was given the job of adding them up.
- **12.** Hendrik Buys, Vantage Security Group — comes down eleven floors himself. 11,400 members,
  1,100 dots, **AVG RESP 04:11**. Piet asks for three things; Hendrik grants all three before he can
  finish. The lunch: the risk register lists *"material improvement in state policing capability"*
  under downside risks. Piet: *"You are the proof of the thing that killed these women, and you are
  the only reason I am going to find him."* Hendrik: **"Neither do I."** The state contract, eleven
  seconds, on a phone face-down.
- **13.** The archive lands in **ninety-one minutes**; SAPS has a vehicle on the eleventh. The swabs
  — four seconds, eleven rand, and every mother experiences it as *participation*: **"So nou is ek in
  die ding."** Charl's crisis: the money came, and it went straight past the state, and nobody stole
  it. Piet's answer to why he never asks his brother — *"because he would have given me anything"* —
  and the warning Charl flags in advance: *the one thing I have is what is in my head.*
- **14.** The reconstruction. Four days, 411 pages, the floor no one may walk on, Riaan's plates
  inside the door and never a question. Wednesday: no language, no distress — *"there is no line
  free"* — and Charl texting him from four metres away. **The method, stated plainly: he is not
  looking for a murderer, he is looking for a vehicle that moves when no work is happening.** He
  deliberately refuses to open the fuel data first, and says why: *"I will find a vehicle I like and
  then look for the movement that fits it."* Nineteen → two.
- **15.** Kaap-Wes Arbeid BK: deregistered, member dead since 2021, licence renewed, fuel card still
  running. **A ghost with a full tank.** And **Neville Arendse**, driver-for-anyone, one fifteen-
  year-old suspended sentence. Charl says it out loud — *"half the men in this country have one"* —
  and Piet agrees, sincerely, and puts the page on the left-hand side of the desk, and that side
  gets thicker for six weeks. **Four true facts, no false brick, wrong house.** Charl names her own
  failure: she stopped auditing around the tenth.
- **16. Turn.** The fuel card goes north — five windows to **Steinkopf**, the last pump in the
  Republic, which on its own means nothing. Then the thing Piet sat on overnight: in two windows the
  *next* transaction, forty minutes later, is at **Noordoewer — on the other side.** A dead man's
  deregistered CC buying diesel in Namibia. **Nobody in the book says the word *border*; the card
  says it.** (And the road stops being the N7 and becomes the B1, and the book never remarks on it.)
  Nyathi cannot follow: her series is provincial, MLA is Justice, and she has waited two years four
  months on one *with a body and a confession*. She places the workaround on the table without
  touching it, and one hard condition: **do it properly or you burn it — up there you are nobody.**
  The river: Aussenkehr ripens November to mid-January, so the Cape's dead shoulder is the river's
  moving season, and *sy's Oranjerivier toe* is the most believable sentence in the valley. **And
  Charl's correction of her own assumption — it is not a lawless border camp.** It is five thousand
  people becoming twenty thousand for nine weeks, and the fifteen thousand who arrive come down from
  a thousand kilometres *north*, so a Rawsonville woman up there is the foreigner in a town whose
  only census is of the version that exists in the off-season. Piet: *"The window is a place. It is
  where nobody's records are."* **Curtain: *"If I become certain, will you tell me." "Ja, Piet."***
  She doesn't.

## Movement III — *Over-fitting* (ch. 17–25)

- **17–19.** The corridor: Vanrhynsdorp, Springbok, the border. The country opens out and the
  procedural gets harder — no forensics, no warrants, no standing.
- **20.** Piet builds a beautiful structure on one bad datum. He is certain. Charl says *"or she
  just liked those shoes,"* and he does not hear her.
- **21–22.** **The cost:** a wrong man — a broker's driver — is exposed, loses work, is nearly
  killed by a community that has had enough. Piet did that. The book does not let him off.
- **23. Upington.** Why he really left the unit: the same failure mode, years earlier, with a worse
  outcome. Hendrik tells Charl, not Piet. `[the reveal that recontextualises Movement I]`
- **24–25. Turn:** Charl walks. Not a tantrum — a professional judgement that he is dangerous when
  he is certain. She takes the file and works it alone for two chapters, and gets further than he
  did, in a kitchen, with tea. **This is the book's argument in dramatic form.**

## Movement IV — *The count* (ch. 26–32)

- **26–27.** She comes back, on her terms, with a rule: he does not get to be certain out loud
  anymore without her. He accepts it. That is the whole character arc and it is one paragraph long.
- **28–29.** **Bookkeeping.** A fuel card, a hiring register, and three seasons of dates. The name
  falls out of a spreadsheet. It is not a revelation; it is a Tuesday.
- **30.** The arrest. Undramatic, procedurally correct, Nyathi's team, Piet standing outside a gate
  he has no right to walk through. The man is ordinary. He says nothing memorable. **No speech.**
- **31.** The count. They can prove eleven. They believe it is more than thirty. The state can
  confirm four. Elsie's wall does not get shorter.
- **32.** Last beat: Aunty Miems gets a docket number. One. Charl writes it down for her on the back
  of a till slip because there is no form for this. Piet, at Kogel Bay, in cold water, with the
  second list still in his jacket on the rocks. **Someone is holding a list. Nothing is fixed.**
