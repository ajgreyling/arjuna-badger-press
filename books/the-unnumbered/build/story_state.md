# STORY STATE — *The Unnumbered*

**Drafted:** Movements I and II complete — **ch. 1–16, ~23,700 words.** Next: ch. 17 (the corridor).

## Where we are

They are on the N7 heading north, out of Nyathi's province and toward a border. Eleven women on the
wall, five new dockets, one provincial series reference, six DNA swabs in a private fridge in
Century City, and a white Nissan NP300 that moves in the shoulder and belongs to a dead man's
deregistered close corporation.

And a driver called **Neville Arendse**, whose pile on the left-hand side of Piet's desk has been
getting thicker for six weeks.

## What the reader knows

- **The vehicle finding is correct.** Charl says so outright in ch. 15, at the top, before the
  wrongness starts: everything that follows, including the arrest, comes out of that NP300.
- **The man is wrong**, and Charl has told us so in advance, and told us why: four true facts, no
  false brick, one person in the room for three years and four months, and a partner who **stopped
  auditing around the tenth of the month** because being the one who says *or she just liked those
  shoes* to a man who is right nine times in ten makes you feel like the stupidest person in the
  building nine times in ten.
- **Piet asked her, unprompted, 400 km before it mattered:** *"If I become certain, will you tell
  me."* She said yes. She didn't.
- The state is not villainous and not replaceable. Nyathi got in at six and read it twice; she has
  nine columns and four vehicles that go; her MLA request on a case *with a body and a confession*
  has been pending two years four months. Piet, in the corridor: *"There is nobody to replace. That
  is the part I did not want to know."*
- Hendrik delivered four years of tracking data in **ninety-one minutes**; SAPS had a vehicle
  available on the eleventh. The help works and the help is the thesis. Hendrik knows — *"neither do
  I"* — which is why he can't be hated.
- **The title landed** (ch. 11, Elsie): they are not nameless and not un-numbered. Every one has a DR
  number, a case number, a plot number and a page in a book. **They are never *counted*.** There is
  no total anybody has to answer for, because nobody was given the job of adding them up.
- The familial-search capability exists, works, and is simply not aimed at the missing. Six mothers
  have given swabs that may never be run — and every one of them experienced it as *participation*,
  not grief. *"So nou is ek in die ding."*

## Voice notes (now canon, do not drift)

- Charl narrates from **after** — she flags her own errors before they happen, refuses to reveal
  ahead of order (*"I am not going to put it here just because I know it"*), and repeatedly corrects
  the public version of events. This is the book's honesty engine.
- **Piet never explains the chain** (ch. 4, stated). **We never enter his head** (ch. 1–16, held).
- Piet's fixed tells: hands knees → flat on table; the left-leg tremor; two cups always; the coaster
  she never uses; goes still in car parks; **texts from four metres away in the same room**; *"there
  is no line free."*
- Afrikaans runs untranslated where context carries, glossed only when Charl herself would gloss it.
