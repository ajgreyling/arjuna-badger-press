# Chapter 3 — The List of Necessary Things

Aristotle began with lentils because they could be counted.

The pantry shelves reported quantities when he touched their labels. Kilograms, litres, dates expressed in a calendar that still made him feel as though he had awakened inside someone else’s arithmetic. He copied figures into his stitched notebook and divided by the meal Mateo had described.

“For how many?” he asked when Amina found him.

She wore no coat despite the cold efficiency of the house. Her wristband was blue, though she carried no medical equipment. “That depends on diet, duration and whether you mean this pantry or the supply network.”

“Good.”

“Good?”

“You did not pretend the question contained its answer.”

She looked at the columns in his notebook. “You have counted oils as food and vinegar as medicine.”

“Provisionally.”

“Provisionally, you will ruin lunch.”

In the briefing room, she gave him no image of fire. Instead she showed maps, instrument traces and nested ranges. A fictional name—Asterion—had been placed over a region beneath distant ice because institutions required a noun before they could fear a process.

“This line is observed movement,” Amina said. “This one is a modelled pathway. They are not the same thing.”

“And the great destruction?”

“One conditional scenario. Not the only one.”

Mara sat at the end of the table, watching the clock. Noah Vale, the council recorder, typed without looking at his hands.

Aristotle pointed to the diagram. “What produces the event?”

Amina described pressure, heat, fracture and material moving where it had not moved before. The translation signalled over several modern terms. Aristotle did not ask it to make them ancient.

“Those are changes by which it may occur,” he said. “What brings the plan into being?”

“Governments, engineers, money, fear,” Amina said.

“And what is the plan for?”

“Survival,” Mara answered.

“That is not yet sufficient. A knife and a physician may both preserve life by cutting. Their purposes distinguish what the hand does.”

Noah paused his typing. “You’re describing four causes?”

Aristotle disliked the satisfaction in the young man’s voice, as if a specimen had performed its label. “I am asking different questions which your picture has folded together. What is changing? By what agency? Into what organised form? For the sake of what end? If you call them all cause, the word must work harder than one meaning.”

Amina leaned forward. “Modern physical science will not use all four as causes.”

“Then do not use them so. But your shelters are not mountains. They are things made by choice.”

Amina’s gaze sharpened. She changed the display from the earth system to a shelter schematic. Air, water, stored energy, seed, sanitation, tools, medicines, people.

Aristotle began another list. At first it pleased everyone. Knowledge of cultivation. Care of bodies. Building and repair. Governance. Memory. The teaching of children. Conflict without dissolution. Practices that trained courage without worshipping war, temperance without starving joy, justice without mistaking equal numbers for equal claims.

“You have moved from skills to virtues,” Amina said.

“A person may know how to grow food and still hoard it. A settlement is not preserved by expertise alone.”

“And who judges the virtue?” Noah asked.

“Those capable of judging well.”

“Which will be identified by those already judged capable?”

Aristotle met his gaze. Noah’s hands had left the keyboard. “That danger exists.”

“Do philosophers go on the list of necessary persons?”

Silence tightened around the table. Mara flashed Noah a warning too quick to be accidental.

Aristotle examined his own writing. He had made columns for practices and kinds of knowledge. Without noticing, he had begun a third column: persons.

“A good community needs deliberation,” he said.

“That is not an answer.”

“It may need a farmer who deliberates well more than a man who only deliberates about farming.”

Amina’s respect did not vanish. It narrowed. “People do not remain examples once you put them underground.”

Mara stood. “We are ending the session.”

Noah closed the display, but not before Aristotle saw the administrative layer beneath it. Most lines were grey. One had been obscured by a black bar.

**RESIDENT CONTINUITY: 19**

Then the screen went blank.

Aristotle wrote the phrase at the bottom of his taxonomy. He did not yet know what it counted.
