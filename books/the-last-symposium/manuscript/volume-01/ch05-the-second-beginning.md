# Chapter 5 — The Second Beginning

First there was pain, although *first* already claimed more order than René could justify.

Pressure beneath his ribs. Cold along his right side. Light without a source he could locate. A woman’s voice reached him from inside his own hearing while her visible mouth moved at the wrong instant.

“Do not try to stand.”

He closed his eyes. The voice remained.

Dream, fever, deception: the alternatives arrived before the room did. None explained the others away. A dream could contain pain. Fever could contain reasoning. A deceiver could supply the thought that named deception.

“Can you hear me?” the woman asked.

He opened his eyes and found her beside the bed. Dark hair, blue band at the wrist, hands held where he could see them.

“I experience a voice.”

“It is translated through the device in your ear. My name is Samira. You are in medical care.”

The rendered words were smooth; the movement of her lips was not. He covered the device. Her actual speech became rapid and incomprehensible. He uncovered it and meaning returned.

“A mechanism may give false meaning as easily as true.”

“Yes.”

The agreement interrupted him.

“You do not deny it?”

“No. But before we decide what the mechanism proves, I need to know whether you can breathe comfortably.”

She did not argue him into trust. She offered tests. He could choose which finger she touched while his eyes were closed. He could watch water poured into a clear cup, refuse it, smell it, wet his lips, wait. He could ask her to leave an object in one place and return it after a measured interval. None proved the world. Each established a regularity within whatever world this was.

When she asked to examine his chest, he demanded the purpose. She explained. He consented. The body embarrassed him by shivering throughout the discussion.

“The room is cold,” he said.

“It is warmer than you feel it is. Retrieval disrupts temperature regulation.”

“Or you say it does.”

“Or I say it does.” She placed another blanket within his reach rather than over him. “You can use this regardless.”

Later a woman named Mara Voss explained time. Her account was extraordinary but internally tidy: his remembered century had continued; he had died; a machine had retrieved or continued or reconstructed him according to principles its operators themselves could not settle. Beneath distant ice, a system threatened catastrophe. He had been summoned to advise the living.

“Summoned from where?” René asked.

“That is disputed.”

“By whom?”

“Physicists, philosophers, theologians, lawyers.”

“None of whom entered the machine?”

“No.”

The honesty did not make the claim true. It made Mara a more sophisticated candidate for deceiver.

She brought another man into the observation bay. He appeared older, broad through the chest, plainly dressed, with the calm proprietary air of someone receiving a visitor in a house he had occupied for one day.

“I am Aristotle,” he said.

René studied him. The name came with stories older than any remembered acquaintance. “You report that you are.”

“The people here report it also.”

“Then I have two reports produced inside one controlled place.”

The older man’s mouth tightened. “You may observe me yourself.”

“I may observe a man suited to my expectation of Aristotle.”

“What observation would not be suited to some expectation?”

At last, an objection rather than reassurance. René turned towards him despite the ache in his neck.

Mara repeated the mandate in a shorter form. This time she ended with the decision: humanity needed principles for survival before governments fixed their own by force.

“Suppose the disaster is false,” René said.

“Preparation still has consequences.”

“Suppose the history is false.”

“You are still here.”

“Suppose the body and its pain are false.”

Samira said, “Then the false pain still requires care.”

René looked at his hands. Sensation could be fabricated. Memory could be fabricated. The room, the century, Aristotle, the catastrophe—all might have been arranged by a power whose motives he could not infer. But the act of doubting did not disappear with the things doubted. Something was occurring. Thought could not be removed from the present act without the removal itself being thought.

He withheld the sentence they seemed to expect. A private foothold ceased to be private when offered for applause.

“What proposition,” he asked Mara, “must remain true if every person presenting this mandate is false?”

She answered too quickly. “That a decision is being demanded.”

“Demanded of what?”

“Of you.”

“That is precisely what has not been established.”

Fatigue pressed upon him with the force of weather. Samira ended the interview. He requested a room alone, with no camera and no voices transmitted to him.

At the bedroom door, she showed him the visible absence of a lens and the charging cradle for the earpiece. She let him remove it himself.

The quiet was immense.

René entered, locked the door, and stood with his back against it. If hidden mechanisms remained, solitude could not reveal them. If another person waited behind the wall, his inability to perceive them did not make him alone. Even the category he had requested depended on testimony about the room.

He had asked for solitude and discovered he could not verify that he possessed it.
