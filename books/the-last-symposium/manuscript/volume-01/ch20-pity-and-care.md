# Chapter 20 — Pity and Care

Samira closed the treatment-room screen before Aristotle could see what she was doing.

The communal camera had a line into the medical observation bay, though not into active treatment. A technician had angled it toward the bed when Nietzsche was brought in. Samira stepped between lens and patient and pointed to the floor.

“Move it back to the marked position.”

The technician obeyed.

Aristotle sat outside with Kant. Through the screen came brief questions, then Nietzsche’s answers, thinned by pain. Samira never supplied a word he could supply himself.

Kant held the glass of water in both hands.

“You may set it down,” Aristotle said.

“It is due when she permits him to leave.”

“The glass will remain due on the table.”

Kant set it down.

Aristotle had watched animals injured in field and courtyard, students made foolish by fever, men in public assemblies whose hunger shortened every argument. Yet the house’s briefings proceeded as if deliberation belonged to a creature without bowels, nerves or sleep. Nietzsche had been asked to decide what made life worthy while light itself injured him.

Samira emerged. “He can return to his room. He should eat a little first. One person may accompany him if he wants.”

“No procession,” Nietzsche said behind her.

He walked slowly, refusing the wheeled bed. Kant offered the water.

“It is time according to the support rule,” he said.

Nietzsche looked from the glass to Kant’s face. “Must you polish even kindness until you can see your law in it?”

“No. But the rule prevents your dislike of me from making you miss medicine.”

“And if I refuse?”

“I return at the next agreed time.”

Nietzsche took the glass. His hand trembled against it.

Aristotle walked on his other side without offering an arm. At the dining room Nietzsche stopped.

“Do you also have a moral account of this?” he asked.

“Several. None improves the corridor.”

For the first time since waking, Nietzsche’s smile held no blade.

Hume had taken possession of the kitchen. He questioned Samira about what Nietzsche had tolerated before the retrieval, accepted that she could not know much, then built a meal from rice, broth and a little salt. He tasted each stage and discarded one portion after a spice proved too strong.

“You are acting from inclination,” Kant observed.

“Entirely,” Hume said. “You may add duty at the table if you find the broth deficient.”

“I did not criticise it.”

“Then we are both improving.”

Nietzsche sat where the overhead light was weakest. Hume placed the bowl before him without announcing its gentleness. Aristotle sat nearby and spoke to Kant about the table joints until Nietzsche had eaten four spoonfuls.

Care changed shape according to the giver. Samira protected a boundary. Kant returned at the promised hour. Hume remembered appetite. Aristotle made company that did not demand gratitude. None of these acts proved the theory claimed for it. The same broth could be sentiment, duty, habit, friendship, vanity—or merely broth.

When Nietzsche pushed the bowl away, Aristotle asked, “Will you object if I remain while you sleep?”

“Yes.”

“Then I will sit in the corridor.”

“That is worse.”

“What would be better?”

Nietzsche studied him, perhaps suspecting a trap. “Leave the door open. Do not look in.”

Aristotle did exactly that.

Later, when artificial night lowered over the garden, Kant brought the second dose and waited outside until Nietzsche took the glass. Hume covered the remaining broth. Descartes checked the medical corridor’s newly revealed wall and found no visible seam.

At half past ten, Aristotle entered the kitchen to clean the supper things.

The bowls were gone.

The counters had been wiped beneath objects he knew nobody had lifted. Waste had vanished from the bin. The scorched pan Hume had left soaking three days earlier stood dry in the repair tray, blackened but scrubbed. Rule Sheet 1.0 had been moved away from moisture and squared beneath a ceramic weight.

“Did Mateo’s staff enter?” Kant asked.

Mara answered through the wall display. “Night sanitation is routine.”

“Routine since when?” Aristotle said.

“Since before the first retrieval.”

He fetched his notebook. On a clean page he made columns: time, task observed, task inferred, person visible, access used. He wrote *23:00 — waste removal likely*.

“You cannot observe a likely event,” Descartes said.

“No. But I can mark where an effect lacks an actor.”

They waited in the darkened dining room. At five-twenty, the machinery below the house began its low vibration. Retrieval six.

At five twenty-seven, the kitchen panel opened two hand-widths. A person in gloves reached through, gathered a rubbish sack and froze on seeing the four men watching.

The gloved hand withdrew. The panel slid shut.

Aristotle wrote the time before he looked at the person.
