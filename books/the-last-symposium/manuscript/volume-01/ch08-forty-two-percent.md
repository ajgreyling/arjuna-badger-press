# Chapter 8 — Forty-Two Percent

The number occupied the entire wall.

**42%**

Aristotle waited for the rest of the proposition. Nothing came.

Amina stood beside the display with her arms folded. “This is the executive bulletin that went to participating governments forty minutes ago.”

“Forty-two percent of what?” he asked.

“Exactly.”

Mara touched the control. Smaller text appeared: a time horizon, an event threshold, a reference to one conditional model family. It was still not enough to show which assumptions had entered or which alternatives had been excluded.

René held the printed briefing rather than the tablet. “Who removed them?”

“The communications group compressed the result,” Mara said.

“Compression that discards the conditions is alteration.”

“It is also how a minister reads a briefing while managing twelve other emergencies.”

Amina’s anger made her voice more exact. “The physical observations did not change this morning. The model output did. That distinction is absent.”

Mara brought up the question requiring decision. Agricultural reserves currently assigned to ordinary markets could be redirected into protected seed systems and controlled stores. Immediate diversion would improve long-duration preparation. It would also raise prices now, remove food from existing distribution, and reveal through market behaviour that governments expected disaster.

“I need one recommendation in six hours,” she said. “Begin diversion or do not.”

“On the authority of a number whose production we cannot inspect?” René asked.

“On the basis of all available information.”

“Available to whom?”

Aristotle disliked the number. More troubling was the way René’s question threatened to become a shelter from answering.

“If a danger concerns the common good,” Aristotle said, “the prudent action is not always postponed until knowledge is complete. We act in medicine, navigation and war where outcomes are uncertain.”

“We act on judgements whose grounds may be examined.”

“Then let us examine what we have.”

“The source selects what we have.”

“And hunger will select consequences whether or not our source is honest.”

René stood and approached the wall. “The number commands because it appears precise. Remove it.”

Amina did. The room felt no less endangered.

They made three columns on the display.

**KNOWN. INFERRED. UNKNOWN.**

Observed movement beneath ice went in the first, though René required the amendment *reported observations*. Existing shelter inequality went there too; governments were already buying stores and restricting information.

That the signals indicated increasing instability went under inferred. That early preparation could preserve future food systems went there, followed by Aristotle’s addition: *if the stores remain usable and governed toward that end*.

Under unknown they placed timing, maximum severity, hidden national models, concealed private capacity, and the political uses already being made of fear.

“Now,” Aristotle said, “the question is which error is worse and which can be corrected. Grain diverted too early may be released. A season not planted cannot be recalled.”

Amina shook her head. “Some diversion is not reversible. Price shocks kill at the margins. Land contracts displace farmers. Seed held in the wrong conditions dies while officials report it preserved.”

“Then the means must be specified with the end.”

“You are still assuming the institution can carry out your specification,” René said.

“You are assuming no institution can be trusted enough to act.”

“I am saying trust must be built from something other than urgency.”

“While it is built, what do people eat if the danger comes?”

“And if it does not come, what do they eat after we remove their reserves?”

Mara watched them worry at foundations while her six hours diminished. Noah’s cursor waited under **RECOMMENDATION**.

Aristotle proposed a limited diversion with public conditions and scheduled review. René refused to attach certainty to data he could not independently test. René proposed demanding raw model assumptions before authorisation. Mara told him the governments might proceed before answering.

“Then our recommendation is theatre,” he said.

“It may be leverage,” Mara said.

“With an audience we cannot see and evidence we cannot verify.”

Aristotle rubbed his throat; it had begun hurting again. Reluctantly, he recognised what René supplied: a refusal to let organised purpose certify its own evidence. René, in turn, had begun using Aristotle’s columns and distinctions while objecting to every conclusion drawn from them.

Neither man could produce the requested action alone. Together they had made their paralysis harder to disguise.

“No recommendation delivered,” Noah said at last.

Mara looked at the clock. “Six hours means six hours, not the duration of your comfort.”

Then Aristotle smelled burning.

It came through the briefing-room door: fat, smoke, something rich and animal. A warning began to pulse amber along the ceiling.

They followed it into the kitchen.

A third man stood at the induction hob in medical trousers, a blanket draped grandly around his shoulders. He was broad, pale and entirely composed. In one hand he held a spatula; in the other, an egg from which he had already taken a bite.

The pan smoked beneath another egg whose edges had gone black.

Samira arrived behind them, breathless. “You were meant to remain in observation.”

“I observed that I was hungry,” the man said.

René stared at the bitten egg. “You do not know whether that is safe.”

“No,” the stranger agreed. He ate the rest. “But it resembles food, smells customarily like food, and has so far behaved with tolerable regularity.”

The alarm chirped louder.

He looked from René to Aristotle, then toward the blank recommendation still visible through the open door.

“David Hume,” Samira said, with resignation rather than ceremony.

Hume turned the smoking egg over. “Tell me,” he said, “what usually happens after breakfast?”
