# Chapter 6 — The Sky Has a Seam

Aristotle began the tour with the table.

“Its arrangement encourages common deliberation,” he said.

René rested one hand inside his coat against the house’s chill. “Or encourages the appearance of it for the cameras.”

“Both may be true.”

“Then you do not know its purpose.”

“Purpose is learned from organisation and repeated use, not from suspicion alone.”

“Yesterday it was used by one man. Today that man reports its social purpose.”

Aristotle looked toward Mara, who had accompanied them only as far as the lounge. “He has been awake two hours.”

“And already recognises weak inference,” René said.

Mara left them with Mateo. The facilities lead demonstrated the kitchen, the library tablets and the marked private rooms. René touched nothing until he had watched someone else touch it. In the confession room, he stood outside the camera’s view.

“The cloth is gone,” Aristotle said.

“What cloth?” Mateo asked.

“The one covering that device.”

Mateo checked his handheld screen. “Maintenance must have restored it.”

Aristotle glanced at the floor beneath camera 4B. Whatever he sought there was absent.

The garden’s warmth relieved René’s body before his judgement could object. Damp earth gave off a smell both familiar and impossibly displaced. A bee moved among flowers beneath a sky with no sun.

“A garden,” Aristotle said.

“A room containing plants.”

“What more do you require?”

“An exterior.”

“If every condition necessary to the plants is supplied, in what respect is it not their proper place?”

“In the respect that the sky is an image and the weather an instruction.”

“Cultivation always arranges conditions.”

“A glasshouse does not pretend its roof is heaven.”

Mateo suppressed a smile.

Aristotle saw it. “You find this useful?”

“I find it familiar. People argue about whether artificial daylight counts as daylight. The plants mostly continue growing.”

René looked upward. The blue surface was too uniform to provide depth. “And we infer from their growth that the conditions are what you claim.”

“What would satisfy you?” Aristotle asked.

“A criterion not supplied by the same system being judged.”

The sky stopped.

It did not go dark. A small white cloud at the far edge froze, then the blue vanished into a black plane lettered with pale diagnostic text. For less than a breath the garden displayed its machinery: clock, render state, environmental overlay, an error code. Then harsh work lights came on above the panels.

The bee struck the transparent ceiling and fell into the rosemary.

Mateo touched his earpiece. “Garden control, confirm environmental systems independent.”

Aristotle crouched to find the bee. René kept his eyes on the diagnostic clock. 09:17:42.

Léa Fournier arrived through a door concealed beside the vine seam. Her wristband was green. She carried a controller and the look of someone pulled from a more soluble problem.

“The display process stopped,” she said. “Air and plant systems are separate. You were not in danger.”

“At what time?” René asked.

She consulted the controller. “09:17:51.”

“The display showed forty-two.”

“The display clock can lag during failure.”

“Or your record can.”

“Yes,” she said, with the same infuriating discipline as Samira. “I’ll preserve both logs.”

Aristotle stood with the bee cupped in his palms. “One false appearance does not render every perception false.”

“No. It demonstrates that an appearance we were invited to take as natural was controlled.”

“We already knew the sky was artificial.”

“We knew because they told us. Now we know because the deception failed.”

“You call it deception after being told what it was.”

“A labelled illusion remains an illusion. More importantly, it shares its operators with the labels.”

Aristotle opened his hands beside a flower. The bee clung to his thumb, recovered, and flew.

“And yet,” he said, “the creature navigates by what is sufficient.”

“We are being asked to decide who lives, not where to find rosemary.”

The garden remained offline. Under the work lights, every camera looked newly visible.

That afternoon Léa showed René the archive playback at his insistence. The official clip began before the failure. He watched himself enter the garden, heard the rendered disagreement, saw the sky become diagnostic.

“Again.”

On the third viewing he found it—not in the failure but before it. The archive timestamp advanced nine seconds while the image changed discontinuously from his hand at his side to his hand touching his coat.

“Compression,” Léa said, then shook her head. “No. Not in the raw view.”

“Do you remember those seconds?”

She did not answer for him.

René searched his memory: artificial warmth, Aristotle speaking, Mateo amused. A gap could not be experienced as a gap. It could only be inferred from the join.

The house had preserved nine seconds he did not remember—or failed to preserve nine seconds he had lived.
