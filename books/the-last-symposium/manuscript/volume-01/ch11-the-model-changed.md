# Chapter 11 — The Model Changed

The number became sixty-one while Amina was brushing pastry flakes from her keyboard.

No tremor shook the control room. No new plume darkened a satellite image. The plots of ground deformation had not leapt. One moment the conditional scenario on her left-hand display read forty-two; after the scheduled build finished and the dashboard refreshed, it read sixty-one.

Three messages arrived before she could open the change log.

**CONFIRM ESCALATION.**

**NEED TOP LINE FOR MINISTERIAL CALL.**

**HAS EVENT WINDOW CHANGED?**

“Nothing has happened,” Amina said.

The junior modeller beside her looked alarmed. “The build completed.”

“That is something the model did.” She pointed at the observation feeds. “What happened under the ice?”

“No major data ingestion since 03:00.”

“Then do not write *risk increased*.”

The change log was dense but not mysterious. The team had revised how several precursor patterns were weighted, widened one class of analogue event, and corrected an interaction that had been suppressing high-impact branches. Defensible changes. Unvalidated in combination. The new output could be better than the old one and still be unfit for a minister’s mouth.

Mara entered without greeting. “I need your confidence level.”

“In which claim?”

“That sixty-one supersedes forty-two.”

“The dashboard superseded it. Science has not yet.”

Mara checked the clock. “They know the number exists.”

“How?”

“Because every consortium contains a person who believes urgency is transparency.”

Amina brought up the data layers. “The physical observations are substantially unchanged. The event definition is unchanged. The time horizon is unchanged. The interpretation changed.”

“Can the interpretation be more accurate?”

“Yes.”

“Can you prove it before the call?”

“No.”

Mara exhaled once through her nose. “Brief the residents. I want the distinction on record.”

The four men waited in the briefing room beneath a display deliberately stripped of the large percentage. Amina began with the ice, not the number. She showed the physical measurements as separate families: movements, gases, heat, patterns of small seismic events. She told them what each instrument observed and what it did not.

Then she showed two identical stacks of observations feeding two different versions of JANUS. Forty-two appeared over the first. Sixty-one over the second.

“So the mountain did not alter,” Descartes said. “Your judgement did.”

“Both may have altered. We have no relevant new measurement showing the first.”

“Then sixty-one is constructed.”

“All model outputs are constructed. That does not make them arbitrary.”

He leaned forward. “It makes the number dependent on decisions hidden inside the instrument.”

“Yes.” Her anger sharpened the word. “Which is why I am showing you the decisions.”

Hume studied the paired diagrams. “Did the earlier design have reasons?”

“Yes.”

“And the revision?”

“Yes.”

“Then a reasonable person may alter confidence though Antarctica has been courteous enough to do nothing during luncheon.”

Amina heard the lightness and disliked him for making simple what she would spend the evening defending to hostile committees. “A reasonable person may also wait for validation.”

“Of course.”

“You do not carry the consequence if that waiting is quoted as reassurance.”

His expression changed. Not wounded—attentive. “No. I do not.”

Aristotle tapped the table beside the two outputs. “Which measures are prudent under either interpretation?”

“Some reserve expansion. Distributed crop trials. Ventilation upgrades. Supply audits. Measures with value even if the largest event does not occur.”

“And which depend upon sixty-one?”

“Forced diversion at the proposed scale. Emergency allocation powers. Accelerated shelter seizure.”

“Then recommend the first kind now.”

Descartes said, “That evades whether the model is true.”

“It separates actions by how much truth they require,” Aristotle replied.

Hume nodded. “And how much harm follows if expectation fails.”

Amina held his gaze. “Do not turn this into a general permission to be approximately right.”

“I intended a particular permission to avoid being catastrophically late.”

Mara entered as the argument narrowed. “I have ninety seconds.”

Amina dictated the recommendation herself. “The revised JANUS build produces a higher conditional output. That output is not yet validated and does not reflect a comparably large change in physical observations. We recommend immediate no-regret preparations robust across model versions. We do not recommend using the unvalidated headline figure as sole justification for coercive allocation.”

Mara repeated it back without changing a word. Then she sent it.

For twelve minutes Amina believed the qualifiers had survived.

Noah arrived with the executive summary on his tablet. “We’ve made it readable.”

The first sentence said: **JANUS NOW ESTIMATES A 61% PROBABILITY OF A CIVILISATION-DISRUPTING EVENT.**

The time horizon had vanished. So had *conditional*. The second paragraph described “the council’s recommendation for immediate preparation.” It did not distinguish no-regret measures from seizure.

“Put the qualifiers back,” Amina said.

“They’re in the technical annex.”

“The annex is not the claim.”

“Ministers need the decision signal first.”

“Then tell them the decision. Do not disguise a model output as an observation.”

Noah turned to Mara. She read the summary once. “Restore unvalidated and the time horizon.”

“That weakens the opening.”

“It makes the opening true.”

Noah stepped away to edit. Hume remained by the display.

“You resent me,” he said.

“At present.”

“Because I can tolerate the uncertainty.”

“Because you can leave the room after tolerating it.”

He considered that. “Then my ease is evidence about my position, not about the model.”

“Yes.”

“I shall try not to confuse them.”

The public news feed activated on the wall before Noah returned. It was normally delayed and silent, a curated strip intended to orient residents without exposing reactions to them. A red banner now crossed it.

**ASTERION ERUPTION RISK JUMPS TO 61%.**

Amina did not move.

Below the banner ran an image of the Antarctic ice. Nothing in the image had changed.
