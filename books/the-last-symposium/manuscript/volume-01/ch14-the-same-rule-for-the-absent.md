# Chapter 14 — The Same Rule for the Absent

Kant placed five empty chairs at the table and discovered immediately that no one agreed whom they represented.

“Future residents,” he said.

Aristotle moved one chair slightly back from the table. “They cannot all have the same function.”

“They have the same standing as persons.”

Hume lowered himself into his seat. “The chair nearest the coffee might represent a person with stronger inclinations.”

Descartes inspected the blank cards Kant had set before each empty place. “A symbol cannot consent.”

“It can remind those present that absence is not permission.”

The fifth chair stood for Nia Bako and outside claimants like her. Kant disliked the category even as he wrote it. It gathered innumerable persons beneath one name and then congratulated itself for inclusion. Still, the visible vacancy was better than the effortless disappearance that had preceded it.

Mara arrived with Noah and stopped at the crowded table. “We need a council procedure by seventeen hundred. Not a household constitution.”

“A procedure is a constitution in miniature,” Kant said.

“A procedure is something people can use before dinner.”

They began with rules of speech, disclosure and decision. Each participant would state the action proposed, the principle authorising it, who bore the burden and what uncertainty remained. No official summary would call disagreement consensus. No person would be discussed as a resource without acknowledging that person’s standing as an end in herself.

Aristotle objected to the equal rota. “The physician should not clean the kitchen while a patient requires her. Different roles create different obligations.”

“A public exception can be justified,” Kant said. “An invisible privilege cannot.”

“You make equality the form of every justice.”

“No. I make equal standing the condition under which differences may be justified.”

Hume rocked an empty chair with one foot. “Has any society ever arisen from people seated nowhere, choosing rules without history, dependence or habit?”

“That is not required.”

“Then why speak as though the rule arrives clean?”

“Because the origin of a practice does not alone justify its continuation.”

“Agreed. Nor does a principle’s cleanliness prove people can live in it.”

Descartes pointed to the line reading **No participant shall lie in formal deliberation**. “The translation system marks your word for lie as uncertain.”

Léa, observing from the wall, opened the semantic trace. “The source term separates deliberate false assertion from error. The English output is broader.”

“Then the rule may condemn failure of knowledge as dishonesty,” Descartes said.

Kant revised it: **No participant shall knowingly assert as true what that participant judges false. Material uncertainty and relevant withholding must be declared.**

Mara read the second sentence. “I cannot accept an unlimited duty of disclosure.”

“Then identify the limit publicly.”

“Security classifications, personal medical information, active negotiations, technical vulnerabilities, names of staff at risk—”

“Those are categories. Who decides whether information belongs inside them?”

“The accountable authority.”

“Accountable to whom?”

“Governments, auditors, courts, the programme board.”

“Not those in this room.”

“Also those in this room, within the mandate.”

Kant felt the temptation to call her evasive. It would have been easy and not wholly true. Mara was not hiding because she loved darkness. She was maintaining a machine built from promises to parties with unequal power, and she believed collapse would injure more people than compromise. That made her a serious opponent.

“Will you promise,” he asked, “that no information materially affecting a resident’s judgement will be withheld from that resident?”

Mara’s answer took long enough to be honest. “No. I will promise to disclose the categories of withholding and provide reasons when I am permitted.”

“Then the institution reserves the right to use our uninformed judgement.”

“The institution reserves the right to continue existing.”

Hume looked around the empty chairs. “Our absent friends are not finding this reassuring.”

By noon they adopted the rules provisionally. Descartes signed beside a note that translation logs formed part of the record. Aristotle signed after adding that relevant differences could justify differentiated duties. Hume signed and wrote, **Custom reserves appeal**. Kant signed last.

Mara did not sign. She took Noah’s stylus and wrote across several provisions in firm block letters: **NON-BINDING ON STAFF OPERATIONS**.

The phrase crossed privacy, disclosure, common resources and access.

“Then there are two houses,” Kant said.

“There is a residence and a facility.”

“Occupied by persons subject to different permissions.”

“Because they perform different functions,” Aristotle said, though without his earlier certainty.

Kant turned to him. “Do functions permit staff to observe residents without reciprocal observation?”

“Not by function alone.”

“To enter while residents may not leave?”

Aristotle turned towards the corridor.

As if summoned by the question, the grey panel beside the pantry opened. Mateo entered carrying a replacement filter. His amber band unlocked the panel with a soft tone. He nodded to the table, crossed to a wall vent and began work.

Kant walked to the panel before it closed. It did not open for his white resident band. Through its narrow window he saw the hard-lit service corridor, the back of the kitchen wall and a row of doors without handles on his side.

Mateo glanced over. “Residents don’t have corridor clearance.”

“Are you bound by the household privacy rule?”

“Mara would have to answer.”

“I am asking you.”

Mateo put down his tool. “I’m bound by staff policy, my contract, safety rules and what I can live with.”

It was a better answer than the form provided and a worse condition than the rules admitted.

Behind Kant, the empty chairs remained at the table. They no longer represented only future philosophers and distant claimants. One seemed to represent Mateo, who could enter their room but not their government. Another stood for whoever cleaned before dawn.

On the refrigerator, **NON-BINDING** cut across Rule Sheet 1.0 like a second law written by the people with keys.
