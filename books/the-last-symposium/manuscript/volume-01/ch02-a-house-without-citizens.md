# Chapter 2 — A House Without Citizens

The corridor had no windows, yet every twelve paces the light changed as though they had passed beneath a tree.

Aristotle stopped at the third change.

Mateo stopped too. He carried no physician’s instruments, only a green band on his wrist and a ring of flat tokens at his belt. “Are you dizzy?”

“No. Why does the light pretend to move?”

“It helps the body keep time.”

“Then this corridor is a clock.”

Mateo smiled. “Among other things.”

He had introduced himself as facilities lead. The translated phrase arrived with a faint ambiguity: keeper of the place, leader of servants, steward of mechanisms. Aristotle’s throat hurt too much to pursue all three.

The house opened around a long room. At its centre stood a table large enough for a council, though only one place had been set. Beyond it were low seats, blank walls and doors distinguished by symbols rather than words.

“What is this room for?” Aristotle asked.

“Meals. Meetings. Whatever you make of it.”

“Who decided the table should dominate it?”

Mateo glanced at the table as if seeing the decision for the first time. “Architects.”

“Will I meet them?”

“Probably not.”

They moved into the kitchen. It was a clean puzzle of surfaces. Aristotle opened each cupboard, grouping objects by material and apparent use. Bowls were easy. A whisk resembled a small instrument of punishment until Mateo demonstrated it in water. Aristotle assigned the electric kettle to medicine because of its precise measures. Mateo returned it to the cups.

The black cooking surface defeated him. There was no ash, brazier or opening for fuel.

“It makes heat here,” Mateo said, setting down a pan.

Aristotle placed his palm above it.

“Not until there’s suitable metal on it.”

“The pan causes the heat?”

“The system recognises the pan.”

“Recognition without perception.”

“You’re going to enjoy the manuals.”

Mateo touched a mark. The pan warmed. Aristotle watched the empty surface and then crouched to inspect the cabinet beneath. One hinge shifted. A small screw lay loose in the corner. He pocketed it before Mateo noticed.

“Food appears here?”

“Stores are through that door. Deliveries come through service access.”

“Delivered by whom?”

“Facilities.”

The word included Mateo and concealed everyone else. Aristotle let it remain, provisionally.

In the lounge he found the first camera. It occupied the upper corner in a dark glass dome.

“That watches.”

“Yes.”

“Always?”

“Communal rooms are recorded. Not bedrooms, bathing rooms or active medical treatment.”

“Who watches the recording?”

“Archive staff. Security when necessary. Some material may later be shown publicly.”

“Publicly to whom?”

Mateo spread his hands. “Potentially everyone.”

Aristotle looked from the camera to the single place at the table. “Do these unseen people belong to the household?”

“No.”

“Then do they rule it?”

“I maintain the building. Dr Voss can answer governance questions.”

“A person who knows how a thing is used may answer more truly than the person who says why it was built.”

Mateo’s expression closed—not anger, exactly, but the withdrawal of a warmth Aristotle had mistaken for deference.

The garden repaired the mood. Real soil lay beneath paths of pale stone. Leaves trembled in controlled air. Above them, a blue sky stretched without cloud or sun, its brightness perfect and therefore slightly dead.

Aristotle knelt beside a herb bed. He crushed a leaf between his fingers, smelled it, tasted the smallest edge. “This is alive.”

“Yes.”

“The sky is not.”

“No.”

He laughed, and the laugh hurt his throat. “Then the garden has a truthful earth and a lying heaven.”

He walked the perimeter. A vine climbed one concrete wall. Behind it his fingers found a hairline join. Cool air breathed through the seam.

“Another passage.”

“Service cavity.”

“For the facilities.”

“For pipes, wiring, maintenance.”

Again the role without the people.

The final room contained one chair facing one lens. A line of text brightened on the wall; the earpiece read it aloud.

**SHARE YOUR TRUTH.**

Aristotle looked at Mateo. “Whose words are those?”

“The engagement team’s.”

“Are they here?”

“No.”

“Everyone important in this house appears to be absent.”

Mateo rubbed a thumb over his green band. “I’ll wait outside.”

When the door closed, another prompt appeared.

**WHAT DO YOU FEAR?**

Aristotle considered answering. Not because the room had earned truth, but because a future audience might contain a person capable of hearing it. Yet the command disguised itself as an invitation. It wanted a private thing while displaying the machinery for taking it.

He stood on the chair. His body protested. He removed the folded cloth from his shoulder and draped it over the camera marked 4B.

The room dimmed, but did not go dark.

Behind the cloth, a small red status light remained on.
