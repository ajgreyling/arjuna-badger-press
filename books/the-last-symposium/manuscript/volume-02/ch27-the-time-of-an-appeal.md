# Chapter 27 — The Time of an Appeal

At ten-oh-four, the printer began producing the labour the contracts had omitted.

Pages slid into Elena's hands warm and faintly curled. **Environmental technician** expanded into containment integrity, pathogen-control response, air-quality intervention, waste-stream isolation and emergency seal repair. Mateo's title grew from facilities lead into a list long enough to threaten the second page.

“It knew,” Marx said.

Léa stood beside the printer, checking source fields. “Different systems knew different parts.”

“The institution benefited from their unity and paid according to their separation.”

“Yes,” Elena said. “Now staple.”

Marx stapled.

Husserl observed the certification table until Elena handed him a stack. His task was to compare the machine-generated function list with each worker's spoken correction. He asked them not what their role was but what they did when something went wrong.

A sanitation contractor described a blocked clinical waste transfer. A kitchen worker described feeding residents during a seal alert without cross-contaminating zones. A translation reviewer explained how a wrong imperative could turn medical guidance into an order not to breathe.

The earpiece flagged *responsibility* differently in three accounts.

“The same word?” Husserl asked Léa.

“Not quite. Assigned duty, answerability, and the thing you take charge of because nobody else has noticed.”

“Do not merge them.”

“Public summaries prefer one term.”

“Then public summaries prefer the institution to experience.”

At ten-forty, Mara entered carrying a refusal from the agricultural allocation bureau. Mnemosyne was not an accredited issuer for vocational evidence. The new records could enter general appeals but could not reopen the preliminary cohort.

Elena read the message once. “Who can accredit us?”

“A national continuity authority.”

“The one using language from this house?” Kant asked.

“One of them.”

Mara had already requested an emergency recognition. She said this before anyone could accuse her of treating the refusal as final.

“Expected response?” Elena asked.

“After noon.”

Husserl looked at the certification pages. His method had made hidden work describable. The system had answered by changing which descriptions counted.

He requested access to the communications chamber.

Mara refused. “Formal external transmissions require an authorised council position.”

“You accepted our request for certification.”

“An internal administrative act.”

“Then the boundary between thought and action is wherever your access control places it.”

“At present, yes.”

He seemed almost pleased by the precision.

The council reconvened at eleven. Husserl proposed that the session describe the allocation process without yet endorsing a selection principle. Elena objected until he specified the object: not Nia, not deserving agricultural workers, but the structure by which a person's lived competence disappeared between employment, certification and survival classification.

“And after we describe it?” she asked.

“We transmit the description before noon.”

Noah drafted. Elena deleted *skills gap*. Mateo replaced it with *evidence gap produced by incompatible systems*. Marx deleted *unintended*. Mara restored *whether intended or not*. Hume removed a claim that the problem affected all states because they did not know that. Descartes inserted the names of the documents they had actually seen. Kant added that no person should bear exclusion merely because institutions failed to recognise the work they had required.

At eleven-thirty-eight they had a statement everyone could tolerate and no authority to send it.

The workers resumed the stoppage of public editing.

No footage left the house. Translation safety and medical queues continued. A scheduled educational package showing Husserl's arrival remained dark in broadcast systems across Europe.

Mara's wrist display began receiving calls.

“This is coercion,” she said.

Elena answered, “It is the only communication chamber we can enter.”

Mara walked out.

For fourteen minutes, the house waited. Husserl stood in the garden beneath an artificial morning and watched droplets gather on a leaf after the irrigation cycle. The droplet was not less real because the rain had been scheduled. Nor did its reality make the ceiling a sky.

At eleven-fifty-five, the communications chamber unlocked.

Mara waited beside the open door. “One transmission. The council statement, unedited. Attribution includes the worker representatives. This does not establish permanent release authority.”

“Standing argued tomorrow,” Elena said.

“Everything is argued tomorrow.”

They sent the document at eleven-fifty-seven.

At twelve-oh-one, Nia's status remained expired.

At twelve-oh-six, the bureau acknowledged receipt of a material procedural challenge and froze final confirmation of the agricultural cohort pending review. It did not restore any application. It did not promise to.

Elena printed the acknowledgement and placed it beside her daughter's rejection.

The two pages described the same future differently.

Neither was allowed to replace the other.
