# Chapter 32 — Where the Body Begins

Nietzsche wanted the cup repaired again.

Mateo set the pieces on the workshop bench and said the ceramic had become more resin than trust. Nietzsche, pale and wrapped in a blanket despite the controlled temperature, told him this was an argument for better resin.

“It is an argument for another cup,” Mateo said.

“That is what people say about damaged men.”

Samira looked up from the doorway. “The cup is not my patient.”

Nietzsche permitted the repair.

While the resin cured, the council met without him in the medical-suite waiting area. It was the only resident-accessible space near treatment with no direct camera view, though an audio safety pickup remained disclosed above the door. Chairs made a rough circle around a trolley of unused dressings.

Samira had brought the three records and a fourth account dictated by Nietzsche after the event. His account contradicted each of them in at least one detail. He remembered asking for time before the medication, not refusing it. He believed he had pointed to pain travelling from his stomach, not located it in his chest. He had held Kant because the floor seemed to tilt.

“Memory under pain is not a master record,” Samira said. “Neither are sensors. Neither is translation. We keep the differences.”

Mara wanted an operational rule. Bunker triage systems already relied on translated self-report, observed behaviour and automated capacity scores. In crowded shelters, there would not always be a physician with seven minutes to let meaning settle.

“Then the systems will be wrong faster,” Merleau-Ponty said.

“Sometimes speed keeps people alive.”

“And sometimes it decides what their body meant before they can inhabit the decision.”

Elena placed her amber band on the dressing trolley. “A capacity score already says what a body is for.”

Marx added, “For whom.”

Husserl asked them to distinguish necessary emergency action from the record later used to justify it. Samira agreed. A clinician might act on incomplete signs; the archive must not convert that practical necessity into certainty about intention.

Kant proposed a right of later correction wherever a person survived to give it. Hume asked what happened when they did not. Descartes demanded preservation of source data so no institutional summary became irrebuttable. Léa warned that raw data were not raw experience and could expose intimate information indefinitely.

Nobody solved the body.

They produced four rules instead.

Automated interpretations of gesture could not establish consent or refusal by themselves. Translation uncertainty during pain had to remain visible in clinical and allocation records. Emergency action required later human review. The subject's own account had standing without becoming infallible.

Noah read the last sentence twice. “People will find that untidy.”

“People are untidy,” Hume said.

“Records need not pretend otherwise,” Husserl added.

Mara authorised the rules for Mnemosyne. Adoption elsewhere would require negotiation with medical agencies, bunker operators and software vendors.

“What changes tomorrow?” Elena asked.

Léa answered. “I can stop gesture intention from entering the resident risk score.”

The room went still.

“Resident what?” Merleau-Ponty asked.

Léa's face showed that she had spoken one layer too deep. Mara closed her eyes briefly.

There was a health and conduct model, Léa explained. It did not determine allocation. It estimated support requirements, environmental compatibility and security load for possible shelter transfer. Every retrieved resident had one. So did staff, under different employment systems.

“Show us,” Elena said.

Mara refused immediate access on medical-privacy grounds.

Samira did not support her. “Patients may see their own records.”

“This is programme modelling, not the clinical chart.”

“It uses the body I treat.”

Merleau-Ponty looked through the waiting-room glass toward the treatment corridor. “You brought us here as minds to advise humanity. Yet before we spoke, you had already modelled our bodies as loads.”

“Bodies require air, medicine, food and space,” Mara said. “Ignoring that would be negligence.”

“Choosing which body to retrieve was not neglect.”

The sentence landed before its full implication did.

Husserl asked, “Were alternative retrieval moments medically modelled?”

Mara stood. “That question concerns selection records outside the present council scope.”

Elena picked up her amber band. “Scope argued tomorrow.”

This time nobody smiled.

In the workshop, the repaired cup waited beneath a clamp. Two pale seams crossed its surface now, neither following the other. Merleau-Ponty turned it carefully after Mateo released the pressure.

His earpiece described the object as restored.

He removed it.

From the medical suite came the sound of Nietzsche laughing at something Kant had said, followed by a cough and Samira telling both men to stop being difficult.

Merleau-Ponty handed the cup to Mateo with both hands.

At five-thirty, the arrival corridor lights remained dark. The next retrieval had been delayed pending review of bodily selection parameters.

For the first time since Aristotle woke, the house did not receive a new philosopher at dawn.

It received a file-access demand signed by every person at the table.
