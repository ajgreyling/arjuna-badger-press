# Chapter 40 — The Coefficient

The model did not call it a death list.

It called it the Minimum Viable Continuity Scenario.

Asterion’s forecast had shifted—not enough for certainty, enough for governments to shorten construction schedules. The new request came from a coalition managing twelve shelters. Ventilation components would arrive late. Fourteen thousand planned places had to be reduced to eleven thousand eight hundred or the system would exceed safe load.

The coalition wanted the council to recommend a principle.

Not names. A principle.

On the wall, every principle became names.

Prioritise expected life-years: older applicants vanished first. Prioritise recovery skills: children, carers and people outside recognised occupations fell. Prioritise equal lottery: medically unsuitable placements increased. Prioritise least support load: disabled people disappeared in clean descending order. Prioritise family unity: large families consumed places faster. Prioritise historical disadvantage: the model’s data became least reliable exactly where injustice was greatest.

Mara locked the doors to prevent interruptions. Elena made her unlock them.

“Nobody deliberates about sealed doors behind a sealed door.”

Levinas moved among the projected cohorts. Each was represented by a small human outline. The interface let him touch one and reveal a profile: name, age, work, medical forecast, dependants, language, location. It was meant to humanise the exercise. It also made persons available one at a time to his choosing finger.

He pulled his hand back.

“If you refuse every criterion,” Amina said, “the coalition will use its current one.”

“Support load.”

“Weighted by survival probability.”

“The vocabulary improves nothing.”

“It tells us where to intervene.”

This was their recurring wound. Levinas heard every system as a risk of totalising the Other. Amina heard refusal of systems as permission for inherited power to operate unseen. Neither was wrong enough to dismiss.

Samira entered from medical with a sheet of engineering options. “The reduction is not a single number. Three sites can preserve capacity if they accept louder ventilation, less privacy and higher maintenance staffing. Two can convert archive rooms. One has a private executive wing excluded from the load calculation.”

Marx stood. “There is our missing principle.”

“No exclusion before luxury and hierarchy are counted,” Elena said.

Mara warned that executive control agreements might collapse if private suites were requisitioned. Hume asked whether the executives’ threatened withdrawal was evidence of operational necessity or bargaining power. Descartes requested the contracts. Mateo requested the ventilation specifications. Nietzsche, white with pain, asked whether a continuation built on compulsory gratitude deserved its arithmetic.

They worked for six hours.

The first recovered places came from executive exclusions, archive compression and revised safety margins that engineers—not philosophers—certified could be changed. The second came from funding additional maintenance crews rather than excluding high-support applicants. The coalition still faced a shortfall of eight hundred and twelve.

There the philosophy could no longer expose a hidden room large enough to save everyone.

Kant proposed a constrained lottery among those who could survive in the available sites, with equal moral standing and public reasons for every medical constraint. Aristotle insisted that site-specific fit mattered. Marx demanded that class-made vulnerability not become a penalty. Hume wanted uncertainty ranges made visible. Heidegger warned that their combined procedure might perfect the ordering it criticised.

Levinas listened, then said, “Use the lottery.”

The room turned.

“You accept chance?” Kant asked.

“I accept no innocence in choosing. The lottery does not declare the selected more worthy. But first bind it: family and chosen support relations may enter as linked units; historical disadvantage receives access correction before the draw, not moral scoring within it; medical exclusion must be appealable; and every person left out remains owed material protection outside the shelters.”

Amina asked, “What protection, if the catastrophe exceeds surface survival?”

“Everything possible without pretending possible means sufficient.”

Elena added worker oversight of the draw, public code, independent replication and no protected category for the council.

Mara looked at the last clause. “That question is outside this request.”

“It is inside every request,” Elena said.

Heidegger spoke from his temporary seat beyond the voting line. “A lottery can still treat persons as interchangeable units.”

Levinas faced him. “And choosing by philosophical depth would treat them as what?”

Heidegger did not answer.

They sent a conditional recommendation. It did not solve the eight hundred and twelve losses. It prohibited calling them deserved. It required the coalition to publish every capacity decision, fund surface and alternative shelters, maintain appeal, and reopen the draw whenever capacity changed. It named dependency not as deviation but as the condition of every survivor.

The coalition acknowledged receipt without commitment.

At twenty-one hundred, Noah displayed the council’s own protected allocations. Nineteen tokens, each backed by transport, medical support and a place in a hardened site. Heidegger’s token glowed amber under review. Levinas’s had activated the moment his vital signs stabilised.

He stared at his name.

“I did not consent.”

Elena said, “Neither did the nurse yours displaced.”

The sentence had no spectacle in it. That made it worse.

Levinas requested immediate transfer of his place. Mara refused; beneficiaries could not unilaterally reassign security allocations. Nietzsche laughed from the doorway.

“The honour has found another creditor.”

“And still cannot pay,” Levinas said.

They ended without a vote on their own places. Outside, the coalition began running simulations using the council’s conditions. Inside, eleven protected people ate beneath cameras while workers changed filters that allowed them to breathe.

Before artificial night, Levinas wrote one sentence for the charter draft:

**No calculation of survival may convert the person it excludes into a remainder without claim.**

Amina read it and added beneath:

**A claim without a mechanism is not yet protection.**

They left both sentences on the wall.
