# Chapter 36 — The Record

Legacy access opened at nine.

Noah did not let Heidegger discover it alone on a child-safe tablet. The council had fought for that much after Nietzsche’s archive had taught them what private revelation became under public surveillance. The briefing room cameras remained on, but the public feed was cut. Raw archive only. Heidegger could leave at any time.

On the wall appeared a restrained chronology: his university career, his philosophy, his political affiliation, the rectorate, public acts and addresses, later explanations, scholarly disputes about texts and conduct. Where evidence or interpretation remained contested, the display said so. The central fact did not require embellishment.

He had joined the Nazi Party.

He had held the rectorship under the regime.

He had used its language and authority rather than merely suffering its arrival.

Husserl sat two chairs away. Heidegger read the screen once, then again. The earpiece could translate the modern historical summary. It could not make the decades fit inside the man who had not yet lived them.

“This is fabricated,” he said.

Descartes shifted but did not intervene.

Noah opened the evidence panel. Membership documentation. University records. Contemporary publications. Later correspondence and testimony. No single source was offered as omniscient. Together they left little refuge.

Heidegger looked at Husserl.

“You are named.”

Husserl’s tablet lay face down before him. The archive described exclusion, rupture and the broader persecution of Jews under the state Heidegger had joined. It carefully distinguished documented action from later claim. Care did not soften it.

“Yes,” Husserl said.

“Did I—”

“The record should answer only what the record can answer.”

Heidegger stood, struck the table with his thigh and remained upright by gripping the chair. Samira rose. He waved her away, then saw what the gesture did and lowered his hand.

“I would not.”

Elena’s voice came from across the table. “It says you did.”

“That man knew events I do not know.”

“Yesterday you objected when the model divided a person into useful slices. Today you want to detach the thinker we heard from the actor you have not yet become.”

“Am I guilty before an act?”

Kant answered, “Not of an act you have not chosen here.”

Marx said, “But neither are you entitled to the authority his career produced while refusing the history attached to it.”

Mara placed four institutional questions on the wall. Should Heidegger retain council standing? Should his sessions remain in the educational archive? Should his framework be used in the charter? Should he retain a protected bunker allocation?

“You prepared these before showing me,” Heidegger said.

“Yes.”

“Then this revelation is also administration.”

Elena slapped her palm against the table. “Do not turn the machinery of your exposure into an escape from what was exposed.”

Silence followed.

Heidegger sat.

The council heard arguments until lunch went cold. Kant opposed retroactive punishment of the retrieved man but insisted that institutional trust could not ignore foreseeable character and public meaning. Marx wanted no individual purity tribunal; he wanted Heidegger stripped of unearned authority and confronted by those his politics endangered. Nietzsche rejected cleansing insight by biography, then turned on Heidegger for seeking innocence in metaphysical vocabulary. Hume distinguished evidence of affiliation from uncertain claims about motive, without pretending motive could erase affiliation. Aristotle asked what role could be assigned without making honour appear endorsement.

Husserl spoke last.

“He was my student,” he said. “That is not a defence. It is not an accusation. It is a relation whose meaning this knowledge changes. His work does not become unintelligible because he acted shamefully. It becomes more difficult to trust where the work asks us to follow.”

Heidegger stared at the closed notebook beneath Husserl’s hands.

“What do you ask of me?”

“Do not ask the injured to design your absolution.”

They voted on interim measures. Heidegger retained residence, medical rights and the right to speak. His council vote was suspended pending a hearing with external historians and representatives of communities targeted by Nazism. His educational archive would include the critique, the record and the challenge, not a clean excerpt. No policy could cite him as sole authority. His protected allocation entered immediate review under the same rule as everyone else’s.

Heidegger abstained, though abstention had no status while his vote was suspended.

At dusk he went to the garden. The artificial sky dimmed on schedule. Someone had removed the promotional print from Elena’s desk and pinned it beneath the garden camera. Across **WE ARE NOT INVENTORY**, a hand had written: **WHO IS WE?**

Noah found him there at twenty-one thirty.

“Tomorrow’s retrieval has requested the full record before he enters the house,” Noah said.

“Requested?”

“The orientation protocol was changed. We can show incoming residents selected material before council contact.”

“Who is he?”

“Emmanuel Levinas.”

Heidegger recognised the name only as a young student whose face belonged to lecture rooms and possibility.

“What does the record say I become to him?”

Noah considered the question.

“Not the centre of the story.”
