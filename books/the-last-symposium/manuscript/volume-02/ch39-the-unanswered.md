# Chapter 39 — The Unanswered

At eleven, the council met the people it had excluded in yesterday’s exercise.

Not all of them. That fiction ended first.

Bandwidth, translation, time zones, state permission, disability access and simple exhaustion reduced millions to fourteen delegates. Two connections failed. One government replaced its elected delegate with an official. A remote island coalition sent audio because its satellite video allocation had been rationed. Group H’s children were represented by three adults and no child, after a safeguarding dispute the house had no time to resolve.

Levinas looked at the communication wall and said, “This is inadequate.”

Elena replied, “Yes. Begin there, then listen.”

The elder-care cooperative’s delegate was Dr Sofia Mensah, a geriatrician who had stopped accepting the language of “bed blockers” long before Asterion. She asked why continuity planning treated old people as consumers of the future rather than bearers of memory, relationship and skill.

The island coalition’s audio delegate asked why agricultural expertise was scored by current caloric output when salination had forced them to develop crops and water practices richer countries would soon need.

The private enclave’s spokesperson defended self-selection. They had financed their shelter. Removing wealth as an allocation mechanism after construction, he argued, was confiscation disguised as ethics.

Marx took that one.

Levinas listened for the temptation to sort speakers into innocent face and guilty system. The enclave spokesperson had a face too. Responsibility did not mean every demand became just. The Other could command him not to murder; it did not command him to preserve a deed of ownership over stolen possibility.

“If ethics comes before judgement,” Amina asked during the break, “how do you ever judge?”

“Before does not mean instead.”

“Then what does it change?”

“The judge does not begin innocent.”

Amina rubbed her eyes. “That is morally serious and operationally vague.”

“Yes.”

“You say that as though vagueness were humility.”

“Sometimes it is failure.”

She looked at him then, more willing to continue.

They returned to the model. With delegates watching, its variables changed character. “Dependency years” no longer sat quietly in a column after Sofia described a ninety-year-old teaching six teenagers how to maintain an insulin refrigerator from scavenged parts. “Relocation feasibility” stopped looking neutral after the island delegate explained that relocation meant surrendering sovereignty to the state offering bunker access.

Heidegger spoke once. “The model gathers each world into availability for a single plan.”

Sofia answered from the screen. “And what did your politics gather people into?”

Noah had briefed delegates on Heidegger’s status. The question was not a surprise. Its force was.

Heidegger did not reach for the difference between his retrieved self and historical acts. “A movement that made some lives disposable and called the disposal destiny.”

“Your membership helped it.”

“The record says yes.”

“Do you?”

The room waited.

“Yes.”

It was not confession in the ordinary sense. He could not remember the choice. It was an acceptance that the name and authority he inhabited could not be separated from its consequence whenever convenient.

Levinas did not thank him.

Near evening the island audio dropped entirely. Mara wanted to continue because the transmission window was closing. Levinas objected. The enclave spokesperson accused the council of letting one absent party veto everyone’s time.

Hume surprised them by supporting delay. “We have more than enough experience of decisions made in another person’s convenient silence.”

The worker council proposed a narrower action: preserve yesterday’s no-ranking stance, publish the failures of representation, and appoint paid delegates with recall mechanisms rather than pretending this single hearing fulfilled encounter.

“Paid by whom?” Marx asked.

“The project,” Elena said. “From the public communications budget currently used to edit our clips.”

Mara’s expression hardened. “That budget is contractually restricted.”

“Then publish that constraint beside the empty island window.”

They did.

That night, the public archive carried no triumphant vote. It carried fourteen partial testimonies, two failed connections, one substituted official, a list of missing groups and a declaration that no ethically legitimate model could manufacture consent from absence.

Levinas found Heidegger in the workshop sanding the sharp edge from a wooden tablet stand. Wittgenstein had not yet arrived; repairs still accumulated without a resident patron saint.

“Was the answer enough?” Heidegger asked.

“Which answer?”

“To Dr Mensah.”

“If you make me your measure, you will use me.”

Heidegger set down the sandpaper.

“Then there is no completion.”

“Not while the person remains beyond what you can contain.”

Heidegger looked at the stand in his hands. “How does one live under an accusation that cannot be completed?”

Levinas thought of families the archive could name and those it could not; of a teacher both present and historically absent where he had been needed; of his own desire to make one man bear the whole century because a face was easier to confront than a continent.

“You answer again,” he said.

In the corridor, the night crew arrived. The house did not clear.
