# Chapter 38 — The First Word

The house’s first word each morning was not philosophical.

It was usually *clear*.

Clear the medical corridor. Clear the retrieval load. Clear yesterday’s correction queue. Clear the breakfast surfaces before council. In the service layer it meant move a body, a cart, an error or a trace of use so that the visible house could begin again.

Levinas learned this from Mateo at five forty-five.

He had woken before artificial dawn and followed the sound of wheels to the amber door. His access band would not open it. Mateo found him waiting.

“I want to see the work.”

“Everyone wants to see the work after Marx makes it morally interesting.”

“Then I can leave.”

Mateo studied him. “Or you can carry linen.”

They spent an hour replacing medical bedding. Levinas was clumsy with fitted corners and worse with the sealed laundry system. Mateo corrected him without reverence. No metaphysical lesson emerged from the sheets. They still had to be folded.

At breakfast, the council received MODEL TRIAGE EXERCISE B. It was presented as a test, not an active policy. Ten representative population groups competed for four thousand places. Each group came with survival probability, skills distribution, food burden, reproductive projection, disease risk and recovery value.

The names were hidden.

Group C had high medical need and low projected labour contribution. Group F had high agricultural value and a politically destabilising internal conflict score. Group H included many children but few specialised workers. Group J offered strong technical continuity and low cultural diversity.

“No selection until names are restored,” Levinas said.

Mara replied, “The masking tests whether irrelevant identity cues distort allocation.”

“You have decided the names are irrelevant.”

Amina leaned forward. “Blind review can reduce racial, national and class bias.”

“And blindness can become a virtue precisely when seeing would make the decision unbearable.”

They tested both claims. Half the council ranked the anonymised groups. The other half received community histories and testimony but not model scores. The results diverged violently. The blind group favoured J and penalised C. The testimony group favoured communities whose delegates spoke persuasively, and nearly ignored a remote population unable to establish video contact.

Hume examined the two lists. “We have discovered two ways to be unfair.”

“Good,” Amina said. “Now we can design against both.”

Levinas resisted the language of design, then caught himself. Resistance could become another form of purity performed while Amina did the work.

Group C was a network of elder-care homes and disability cooperatives. Its members included experienced carers, pharmacists and administrators, but the skills model counted only occupations currently licensed in the host country. Group J was a private technology enclave whose residents had selected one another before submitting the cohort. Its low diversity was not a demographic accident; it was prior exclusion disguised as group cohesion.

Marx laughed without humour. “The blind model begins after property has already seen.”

Kant proposed constraints: no cohort could select itself through wealth or private access; every person must have equal standing; characteristics irrelevant to legitimate purpose must be masked.

Levinas asked, “Who stands beside the person whose need cannot be made equal to another’s?”

“Equal standing is not identical treatment,” Kant said.

“Then we agree more than your procedures permit you to enjoy.”

Across the room, Nietzsche made a small appreciative sound.

The exercise forced a choice anyway. Four thousand places. Ten groups. The screen would not accept a refusal.

Elena unplugged the voting interface.

Mara stared at the dead panel. “That does not create capacity.”

“No. It stops a rehearsal being mistaken for consent.”

“The exercise is intended to improve the system.”

“Then the system can accept our conditions before it receives our rankings.”

They wrote five: restore names after a bias-control stage; provide independent advocates for groups without communication access; count care as capability rather than pure cost; expose preselection by wealth and border; display investments that could expand capacity beside every exclusion.

Levinas added a sixth: every final selection panel must meet, directly or through accountable representation, people it would exclude.

Amina objected to scale. “Millions may be excluded.”

“Then representation must remain answerable to them.”

“That is a political institution, not a model feature.”

“Yes.”

It was the simplest answer he gave all day.

At dinner Heidegger approached the worker seats but did not sit.

“May I?” he asked Elena.

“It isn’t my chair.”

He chose an empty one.

Levinas watched him receive soup from the same pot, under the same cameras, in a room whose equality lasted only as far as its walls. Ethics before ontology did not tell him whether to forgive Heidegger, expel him or hear him. It did not remove the need for law, evidence or allocation. It removed one excuse: none of them could say the person had arrived too late for thought.

Mateo passed Levinas the bread.

“First word tomorrow?” he asked.

Levinas thought of the corridor.

“Not clear,” he said.

“Good. Because it’s your turn to strip the beds.”
