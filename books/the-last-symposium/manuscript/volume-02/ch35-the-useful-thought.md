# Chapter 35 — The Useful Thought

The proposal arrived from a northern bunker consortium at breakfast: replace disability penalties with a universal support-load index.

At first glance it looked like victory. The new model did not mark bodies as defective. It calculated hours of care, power, equipment, food, mobility assistance and medical supervision for every applicant. Infants carried high loads. So did most people over seventy. So did Nietzsche on a bad week, Kant during respiratory illness, and every resident if the house lost ventilation.

“Dependence universalised,” Kant said.

Samira read the annex. “Dependence monetised.”

The consortium had kept the capacity ceiling unchanged. It had merely distributed the stigma more elegantly. Families whose members supported one another received no credit because informal care was classified as unreliable. Paid clinical labour counted; daughters did not. Community networks vanished unless contracted.

“They obeyed the words,” Elena said. “Not the correction.”

Heidegger stood at the briefing wall while the model rearranged thousands of lives in pale blue. Yesterday’s critique had travelled outward, been translated into policy and returned as a better instrument.

Nietzsche watched him recognise the trap. “Congratulations. You have become useful.”

“Use is not the measure.”

“Tell that to your admirers.”

Mara requested a response by seventeen hundred. This time no countdown appeared. The deadline still organised the day.

Heidegger proposed they refuse to improve the index until the consortium answered a prior question: what kind of community treated care only as depletion? Marx supported the refusal but demanded a material counterproposal. Amina warned that if they supplied none, the old disability coefficient would remain active.

They split into working groups.

In the garden, Heidegger, Husserl, Elena and Amina sat around Alina Petrescu’s case file. Artificial noon held steady above them. Husserl tried to make the group describe what the model actually presented before assigning motives. Heidegger kept returning to the way presentation itself narrowed the person.

“Martin,” Husserl said, “one cannot correct an appearance by announcing a deeper destiny whenever description becomes inconvenient.”

The old intimacy in the rebuke silenced the others.

Heidegger’s reply was quiet. “And one cannot suspend the world while others are sorted within it.”

Elena pushed the file between them. “You can settle which of you owns the road to reality after Alina gets an answer.”

Amina opened a map of three facilities. One lacked reliable backup power. One could support Alina medically but had already reserved all accessible rooms for senior administrators with mobility limitations. The third had power and space but no water engineer because its skills model preferred civil engineers with lower projected care loads.

“There,” Marx said when they reconvened. “Scarcity produced by allocation, not merely found.”

Aristotle traced the facilities with one finger. “Move two administrators to standard rooms. Transfer an accessible-room retrofit team from Site Four. Assign Petrescu where her knowledge answers the site’s need.”

“Do not justify her place only by usefulness,” Kant said.

“Do not deny that where we place a person matters,” Aristotle replied.

Samira added a fourth intervention: ask Alina. The room went still in the particular way it did when ten elaborate systems encountered an omitted telephone call.

Mara authorised contact through the communications chamber.

Alina appeared on the wall at fourteen twenty. Her image froze twice. She wore a headset and sat beside a window full of ordinary late-summer light. Noah explained the council’s access limits and asked where she preferred to be placed.

“You have spent a day deciding whether I am expensive,” she said.

“Yes,” Elena answered. “They have.”

“And now?”

“Now we ask what the model should have asked first.”

Alina wanted the third site. She knew its water design because the consortium had licensed part of her team’s work without hiring them. She would bring two assistants already trained in her daily support. Neither assistant currently qualified for a place. She would not go without them.

“The model calls that three loads for one skill,” Amina said.

“The model can learn arithmetic after it learns theft,” Alina replied.

Heidegger moved nearer the screen. “Your refusal discloses—”

“Please don’t,” she said.

He stopped.

“I have read the clips,” Alina continued. “You say the system makes us into resources. Fine. But I am also a resource. I have knowledge people need. I am a burden. I am a person. I am not liberated when a philosopher chooses the noblest of those words and forbids the others.”

Heidegger bowed his head.

The council’s response proposed linked applications chosen by applicants themselves, recognition of unpaid care, audit of inaccessible reserved rooms, and investment options displayed beside every alleged capacity limit. It also stated that no index could replace encounter with the people affected.

Mara called the last clause unenforceable.

“Then make a procedure,” Elena said. “Every rejected cohort gets representatives with access to assumptions and appeal power.”

By seventeen hundred they had a policy less elegant than the one they received and more difficult to automate. Noah sent it.

After dinner Heidegger found a printout outside his bedroom. No envelope, no sender. It was a public promotional image from Germany: his face beside the sentence **WE ARE NOT INVENTORY**. Beneath it, a survival party demanded national control of bunker places and exclusion of “non-contributing dependants.”

His critique had been turned against the people it was meant to rescue from calculation.

He carried the page to Elena.

“What do I do with this?”

She read it. “First, stop imagining distortion only happens to good ideas from outside. Tomorrow we find out what your own history did with yours.”
