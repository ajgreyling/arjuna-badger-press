# Chapter 25 — The Things Themselves

Edmund Husserl returned to the world coughing.

The cough folded him against the restraint straps before he had understood the table, the white light or the woman telling him not to sit up. Something cold pressed beneath his ribs. A tone sounded inside his right ear half a breath after her mouth moved.

“You are safe. My name is Doctor Samira El-Khoury.”

The tone came again, softer, as though admitting uncertainty about *safe*.

Husserl opened his eyes. Above him, a dark glass circle held a lens. To his left, numbers altered without any visible hand. His throat tasted of metal.

“What is given,” he said, “is a voice, pressure at my side, light, nausea and a claim.”

Samira removed her hand from his shoulder. “Good. Tell me which part hurts.”

The machine translated his answer into a body diagram. He corrected it twice. The pain was not in the place the screen coloured red; it spread toward it. Samira made the screen subordinate to his description.

Outside the active treatment zone, Mara watched the seventh retrieval become twenty-three minutes late.

When Samira finally admitted her, Husserl was sitting beneath a blanket, examining the earpiece between two fingers. He had been retrieved as an older man, moustache greying, shoulders narrower than the formal coat waiting in his room suggested. His gaze moved between the device and the people with the patient severity of someone checking a student's proof.

Mara delivered the prepared account: the historical continuation, the forecast, the charter, the house.

He stopped her at *brought back*.

“That is already a theory.”

“It is the programme description.”

“Then describe the programme.”

She tried again. A machine had produced his living body from information associated with a specified historical moment.

“Associated by whom?”

“The retrieval team.”

“And identity?”

“Clinically and historically verified.”

“You have named two procedures. You have not given me my identity through either.”

Mara looked at the clock. “There are eleven people waiting to meet you.”

“That is something I can understand.”

They gave him shoes. He refused the tablet until he could walk without holding the wall.

The enlarged council had not slept. Bulk-food lids, notebooks and allocation instruments covered the dining table. Elena occupied the chair added beside Mara. Mateo and Léa sat opposite the philosophers. Two worker representatives had gone off shift and been replaced according to a rule written at three in the morning. On the wall, Noah's draft heading read: **PROVISIONAL SCOPE OF WORKER STANDING**.

Below it, six people had proposed six meanings of provisional.

Husserl entered while Marx was asking who possessed power to end the provision.

Every person stood except Nietzsche, who raised two fingers from beside a cup.

Aristotle approached first. “Edmund Husserl.”

Husserl regarded the name as another claim. “That is what they tell me.”

Descartes smiled for the first time since dawn.

Mara began introductions. Husserl stopped her after the fourth.

“Please. I cannot yet distinguish what each of you is saying from what I am told each of you is.” He turned to Noah's heading. “What occurred here?”

Everyone answered.

Translation latency climbed. The wall transcript assigned one of Mateo's sentences to Marx and split Elena's interruption into three incomplete statements. Kant tried to restore order by invoking the overnight rule. Marx objected that the rule itself was under dispute. Hume asked whether breakfast might qualify as a neutral institution. Nietzsche said neutrality was what appetite called itself after eating.

Husserl lifted both hands.

“One thing. The thing itself, before your account of why it occurred.”

Elena said, “We stopped nonessential work.”

Mara said, “The project admitted worker representatives.”

“Those are different things,” Husserl said. “Good. Write both.”

Noah did.

“What can everyone here presently confirm?”

The room resisted. Confirmation felt smaller than justice.

Mateo said comfort service had been reduced and restored. Léa said translation corrections had paused. Elena said workers had entered through the resident door and had standing recorded in the minutes. Mara said no final scope had been authorised. Kant said the stoppage was suspended, not settled.

Husserl asked Noah to move every explanation to a second column.

For forty minutes, the room became almost peaceful. Claims about motive, legitimacy and consequence did not disappear; they waited visibly beside what had been experienced, recorded or done. Descartes objected that an observation could still be deceived. Hume replied that at least the deceptions were now in useful little boxes. Aristotle began dividing events from interpretations in his notebook.

Elena watched a deadline approach on her wrist display.

At seven-fifteen she interrupted.

“My crew's provisional allocation review closes at noon.”

Husserl turned toward her.

“Is that in the first column?”

“It is in the column where people lose places while you teach us to describe losing them.”

The calm remained, but its innocence did not.

On the wall, Noah had written two headings.

Husserl took the stylus and added a third.

**WHAT CHANGES BEFORE NOON.**
