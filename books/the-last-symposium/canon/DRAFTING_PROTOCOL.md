# Full-Chapter Drafting Protocol

Use this file immediately before and after writing every chapter.

## Pre-draft card

Copy and complete:

```text
Chapter:
Dominant POV:
Clock / duration:
Opening physical condition:
Concrete want:
House problem:
Philosophical question:
Claim enacted before named:
Strongest objection:
Relationship movement:
Institutional pressure:
Objects / props entering:
Existing continuity obligations:
Research gates cleared:
Irreversible change by chapter end:
End image or pressure hook:
```

Do not begin prose if “irreversible change” is empty. A chapter may end quietly, but the serial cannot
return to exactly the previous state.

## Scene card

```text
Scene number and location:
POV enters believing:
POV wants:
Other person wants:
Material activity occurring:
Philosophy expressed through action:
Information asymmetry:
Camera / audience condition:
Turn:
What carries into next scene:
```

## Chapter composition constraints

- Open on an action, perception, bodily condition, or institutional demand—not a philosophy summary.
- Let at least one ordinary object carry the abstract problem.
- No more than one uninterrupted explanatory passage before another person acts on, resists, or misunderstands it.
- A philosophical term should arrive after the reader has experienced the problem whenever possible.
- Give the strongest available objection to a competent character.
- Do not close by announcing which philosophy won.
- End with changed access, trust, obligation, knowledge, bodily state, object, rule, or relationship.
- Preserve humour and domestic texture under pressure.

## Dialogue tests

For each major exchange:

1. Could the names be removed while the speakers remain distinguishable?
2. Is each speaker trying to achieve something besides explaining an idea?
3. Does somebody misunderstand for a credible reason rather than to cue exposition?
4. Is the modern character permitted to know more than the philosopher?
5. Has translation changed or flagged a term that matters?
6. Is the camera affecting honesty, performance, or silence?
7. Is anyone saying a famous quotation because the story earned it—or because the author recognised it?

Delete ornamental quotations.

## Exposition budget

A chapter may explicitly explain:

- one primary philosophical distinction;
- one secondary complication;
- one relevant modern fact cluster.

Everything else should be seeded, enacted, or deferred. The series has room. Do not make every philosopher
complete their syllabus on arrival day.

## POV discipline

- Narration may use the POV character's conceptual habits but should remain readable without prior study.
- A philosopher cannot recognise modern mechanisms by name before learning them.
- A modern POV cannot know private historical motives absent evidence.
- Avoid free indirect textbook prose: if the narration sounds like an encyclopedia, return to body, object,
  social aim, or uncertainty.
- No head-hopping inside a scene.

## Believability pass

After draft, verify:

- food, sleep, medication, hygiene, clothing, pain, and elapsed time;
- who prepared and cleaned the space;
- doors/access permissions and staff availability;
- what the resident has learned about modernity so far;
- earpiece latency and untranslatable terms;
- tablet access restrictions;
- camera sightlines and privacy;
- which catastrophe data version the character saw;
- whether any character gained information without a route.

## Philosophy pass

- Identify each philosophical claim in margin notes.
- Cite the source or research note supporting it.
- Write the strongest objection beside it.
- Check whether the retrieval-date person could make the claim.
- Remove any line whose only purpose is to show the author researched it.
- Check that the principle helps before it harms, or harms before it helps; avoid predetermined prosecution.

## Character pass

- Give each philosopher one unphilosophical desire in the chapter.
- Give each modern character an independent task and risk.
- Replace generic anger with the character's specific pressure behaviour.
- Check that bodily vulnerability is not symbolic punishment.
- Let agreement coexist with dislike and disagreement coexist with care.
- Record the relationship delta in the continuity ledger.

## Series pass

- What question opens that the series can answer later?
- What earlier seed pays off?
- What physical trace remains?
- Which faction or pair has shifted?
- Does catastrophe pressure advance without swallowing the domestic episode?
- Does the chapter make the next arrival necessary or merely scheduled?

## Post-draft handoff block

Append to working notes, not published prose:

```text
Canon established:
Objects moved/damaged:
Knowledge gained:
Promises/debts:
Relationship deltas:
Health/sleep state:
Open questions:
Research debt introduced:
Next-chapter pickup:
```

## Red flags requiring revision

- “As you know” exposition.
- A philosopher lectures uninterrupted for more than a page.
- One resident always supplies the author's preferred answer.
- Modern staff exist only to be impressed.
- The catastrophe conveniently validates a philosophy.
- A rule changes without leaving institutional residue.
- Cameras disappear when privacy would complicate a scene.
- Historical prejudice appears only when the plot wants to discredit someone.
- Everyone adapts to tablets, plumbing, food, medicine, or gender norms at the same speed.
- The reader could swap the domestic problem for a seminar prompt without changing the chapter.

## Definition of chapter-ready

A chapter is ready for full prose when:

- its research gates are cleared;
- its scene sequence is causal;
- every present character has a want;
- philosophy is first enacted;
- the material activity is specified;
- location/access/camera logic is known;
- prior continuity inputs are listed;
- the chapter creates a durable output;
- the next chapter has something concrete to inherit.
