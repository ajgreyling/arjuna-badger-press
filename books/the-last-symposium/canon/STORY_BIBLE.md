# Working Story Bible — *The Last Symposium*

Status: exploratory scaffold, v0.1  
Format: expandable philosophical ensemble series  
Genre: philosophical science fiction / locked-house ensemble / institutional thriller  
Primary setting: a purpose-built residence beneath or adjacent to CERN  
Story clock: philosophers are initially retrieved one per day; bunker construction and a disputed
catastrophe forecast create an elastic horizon rather than a conventional thriller countdown

## The promise

An international forecasting system predicts a civilisation-ending super-eruption. Its exact date,
severity, and probability remain contested, but governments are already building and seizing bunkers.
A machine at CERN can retrieve a human consciousness from one moment in history and instantiate it in
a living body. Rather than leave the selection of survivors and the design of the aftermath entirely to
states, billionaires, militaries, or contemporary AI, the project resurrects nineteen canonical
philosophers and confines them in a filmed house. Their task is to produce a blueprint for those who live.

The experiment asks two questions at once:

1. Can humanity reason its way through an existential crisis?
2. Who gets to define the humanity being saved?

## The dramatic engine

The philosophers must produce a **Survivors' Charter** before the bunker doors close.
It must state:

- what a human being is;
- what humanity values;
- who, if anyone, has the authority to choose the survivors;
- whether places should be allocated by lottery, need, skill, age, wealth, power, family, or moral claim;
- what the living owe those knowingly left outside;
- whether coercion, secrecy, reproductive control, rationing, exile, or execution can ever be authorised;
- which knowledge, seeds, tools, languages, histories, beliefs, and institutions must be preserved;
- which claims are known, inferred, contested, or unknowable.

Every new resident reopens earlier agreements. Governments and bunker operators may adopt the charter,
ignore it, weaponise it, or falsely claim to follow it.

## The house

The house is comfortable but visibly instrumental: glass, concrete, cameras, private bedrooms,
communal kitchen, library without books, garden under an artificial sky, confession room, medical
suite, workshop, and a sealed communications chamber.

Each resident receives:

- a translation earpiece that renders speech but not always concepts;
- a child-safe tablet locked to a frozen copy of Wikipedia;
- clothing without status markers;
- a private notebook that cameras cannot read while closed;
- a daily briefing containing curated eruption forecasts, bunker inventories, population models, crop
  projections, political reports, and requests from the outside world.

The house records everything. Residents are told the archive may become humanity's final testament.

## Retrieval rules

- The machine retrieves a person from a specific bodily and intellectual moment, not necessarily
  the moment later scholars call their philosophical peak.
- The retrieved person remembers life only up to that instant.
- The body is biologically alive and vulnerable. Chronic illness and disability are not erased.
- Each resident is told that the original historical timeline continued without them.
- No retrieval can be repeated. Death in the house is final.
- The machine's account of what it does is institutional testimony, not established metaphysical fact.
- Whether these are transported originals, exact continuations, or reconstructions remains unresolved.

## Information rules

- Wikipedia is frozen several weeks before the first retrieval.
- Pages may be incomplete, edited, politically shaped, or deliberately withheld.
- Residents may read their own biographies, works, influence, criticism, and historical consequences.
- Search histories are recorded and can become evidence or leverage.
- The translation system handles ordinary meaning well but flags some untranslatable terms rather
  than flattening them: *Dasein*, *Aufhebung*, *epoché*, *conatus*, *différance*, and so on.
- The predicted catastrophe is never directly knowable. Forecasts, probabilities, and timelines are
  mediated by modern institutions with unequal information and interests.

## Core cast and house function

### Plato — the architect

Seeks the just ordering behind appearances. Builds models, ranks souls and competences, and is
tempted by rule of the wise. His ideal state starts to resemble the logic of a bunker.

### Aristotle — the classifier

Observes before declaring. Creates taxonomies of residents, necessary skills, virtues, and regimes.
His practical usefulness wins authority before his historical prejudices cost it.

### Anselm — the believer

Tests whether reason can reach beyond available evidence. His ontological argument becomes a comic
and serious template for arguments about the necessary existence of superior intelligence.

### Thomas Aquinas — the synthesiser

Attempts to reconcile revelation, natural reason, hierarchy, and prudence. Becomes an institutional
bridge until triage forces a conflict between natural law, just authority, and killing by exclusion.

### René Descartes — the doubter

Suspects simulation, false memories, manipulated forecasts, and a deceiving machine. His paranoia is
epistemically responsible until it becomes socially corrosive.

### Baruch Spinoza — the stabiliser

Interprets fear as bondage and understanding as freedom. Refuses providential explanations for disaster.
Becomes the person others seek at night, while quietly challenging the premise of free choice.

### David Hume — the puncturer

Demands proportional belief, distrusts causal stories, and uses humour to dissolve grandeur. He is
excellent at exposing bad evidence and poor at satisfying the house's hunger for certainty.

### Immanuel Kant — the legislator

Creates schedules, rules, and universal tests. Insists that people outside the bunkers must not be treated
merely as means, but struggles when duty appears to endanger every possible survivor.

### G. W. F. Hegel — the historian

Reads every conflict as a movement through contradiction. Believes the encounter may transform the
meaning of humanity itself. Other residents suspect that his explanations make every outcome inevitable.

### Karl Marx — the materialist organiser

Asks who owns the machine, controls the feed, cleans the house, and benefits from the emergency. Turns
domestic labour and rationing into politics; treats the selection of the canon as class power made flesh.

### Friedrich Nietzsche — the incendiary

Attacks herd consensus, resentment, metaphysical comfort, and survival at any price. Learning how his
work was appropriated enrages and wounds him. He may prefer a worthy end to a servile continuation.

### Edmund Husserl — the method keeper

Tries to bracket every assumption about catastrophe, resurrection, and world. Runs disciplined description
sessions that help until the clock makes suspension of judgement look like evasion.

### Martin Heidegger — the compromised seer

Asks what mode of being the machine imposes on persons and whether technology has already framed humans,
the dead, and the planet as resources. His insight is inseparable from the moral fact of his Nazism.

### Ludwig Wittgenstein — the breaker of false problems

Interrogates how words such as *survival*, *merit*, *necessary*, and *civilisation* are being used. Repairs objects,
rejects praise, and can end an argument by revealing that its opponents do not share a grammar.

### Hans-Georg Gadamer — the mediator

Treats understanding as a fusion of horizons rather than extraction of neutral facts. Becomes vital to
interpretation, yet may underestimate power and deliberate deception.

### Maurice Merleau-Ponty — the embodied witness

Distrusts the fantasy that translated propositions exhaust understanding. Studies gesture, space,
fatigue, touch, and the bodily effect of surveillance. Periodically removes the earpiece.

### Jean-Paul Sartre — the voluntarist

Insists the house cannot escape responsibility by blaming history, the machine, or human nature. Reads
roles as bad faith, while performing his own public identity for the cameras.

### Albert Camus — the lucid dissenter

Accepts uncertainty without surrendering solidarity. Opposes murder justified by perfect futures. His
plain speech attracts the house and irritates systems-builders.

### Emmanuel Levinas — the ethical interruption

Argues that responsibility begins in encounter with the Other before knowledge or reciprocity. He makes
every anonymous population model answer to the face of someone being left outside.

## The absent chairs

The initial list is overwhelmingly male and European. This is not repaired invisibly; it becomes plot.
The house discovers unused capacity, rejected candidate files, or a selection committee's rationale.
Potential later retrievals include Hannah Arendt, Simone Weil, Simone de Beauvoir, W. E. B. Du Bois,
Frantz Fanon, Confucius, Nāgārjuna, and Ibn Sina. Their absence is evidence about who built “humanity.”

Recommended reveal: the first nineteen must vote on whether to spend scarce retrieval capacity on a
twentieth thinker whose presence may make consensus harder but the charter more legitimate.

## Long-form movement

The project is designed as an expandable series, not a single closed novel. The movements below describe
the initial pressure arc only. See `SERIES_BEATS.md` for the recurring episode engine, extended movements,
and the first run of instalments.

### Initial movement — Resurrection and classification

Daily arrivals turn novelty into hierarchy. The residents test the house, tablets, translation, and
one another. They form provisional camps around evidence, duty, survival, and otherness. The first draft
of the charter fails over whether a bunker place can ever be *deserved*.

### Developing movement — Legacy and contamination

Residents learn what history made of them. Private searches leak. Heidegger's record splits the house;
Nietzsche's legacy destabilises him; Marx discovers the project's labour structure; Sartre and Camus
re-enter their quarrel with posterity watching. Evidence suggests the briefings are curated. The house
must decide whether to trust the institution that resurrected them.

A major escalation: a government publishes bunker criteria that echo a phrase spoken only inside the house.

### Long-term movement — The definition becomes the weapon

The fallback authority is revealed. The residents learn that their charter may train or constrain
the systems that select survivors and govern bunkers. Consensus becomes dangerous: a clean definition can
exclude. The final solution is not perfect agreement but an honest protocol for acting amid disagreement.

## Ending test

Any eventual ending should force a cost, not reward clever synthesis. A strong candidate: the charter
protects the excluded, labels its uncertainties, binds bunker authority, and prevents any single
institution—including the philosophers—from controlling selection. Passing the test requires the council
to surrender its own privileged places or authority.

## Recurring domestic arenas

- **Kitchen:** labour, appetite, property, ritual, hierarchy.
- **Dining table:** formal deliberation and status theatre.
- **Garden:** embodiment, solitude, informal alliances.
- **Workshop:** Wittgenstein's practical refuge; philosophy judged by what it repairs.
- **Confession room:** performance for an imagined audience.
- **Tablet alcove:** private encounters with legacy and shame.
- **Medical suite:** dependence, dignity, pain, and the lie of the disembodied mind.
- **Communications chamber:** the only room where words may become irreversible action.

## Tone laws

- No philosopher is merely a doctrine delivery system.
- No school receives the author's final-word privilege.
- Intelligence does not cancel vanity, desire, pain, prejudice, humour, or fear.
- Philosophical claims alter chores, friendships, sex, sleep, discipline, and risk—not only debates.
- The catastrophe forecast remains genuinely uncertain long enough for each response to expose the human.
- Comedy comes from character and incompatible habits, never from making old accents or disability the joke.
