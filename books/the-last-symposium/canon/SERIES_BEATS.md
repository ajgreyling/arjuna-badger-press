# Series Beats — *The Last Symposium*

Status: expandable series scaffold, v0.1

## What the series is

This is not primarily a race to prevent a disaster. It is an ensemble series about historical
philosophers forced to live together in a recognisably modern institution while humanity asks them to
decide who should survive and what those survivors should preserve.

The predicted super-eruption supplies pressure, disputed deadlines, changing forecasts, and moral
consequence. It does not require the series to resolve quickly. The core pleasure is watching a philosophy become a
person's way of cooking, arguing, interpreting the news, enduring illness, forming friendships, using
technology, responding to insult, and deciding what another person deserves.

The audience should finish an episode understanding one philosophical problem because they watched it
happen—not because a character delivered a summary of it.

The cruelest standing fact: CERN has provisionally reserved one bunker place for every retrieved thinker.
Each philosopher therefore debates selection while personally occupying a place that could instead go to
a child, farmer, engineer, nurse, carer, or unselected thinker. They cannot pretend to be neutral judges.

## The expandable engine

Each instalment combines four moving parts:

1. **A modern house problem** — chores, surveillance, privacy, medicine, misinformation, voting,
   attraction, ownership, punishment, unequal labour, or control of technology.
2. **A philosophical problem** — knowledge, identity, duty, freedom, otherness, language, history,
   embodiment, God, violence, or meaning.
3. **A relationship movement** — an alliance forms, a mentor disappoints a student, an enemy provides
   care, or two people discover that agreement does not make them friends.
4. **A pressure signal** — a revised eruption forecast, bunker allocation, CERN intervention, media leak,
   translation failure, public petition, or information about the retrieval machine.

The domestic problem makes the philosophy concrete. The survival problem enlarges it. The relationship
prevents it from becoming a lesson plan.

## Series movements

These are long movements rather than conventional acts. They may span books, seasons, or groups of
episodes.

### Movement I — The house learns how to exist

The first residents believe they have been assembled to provide answers. The actual early question is
more primitive: how do nineteen autonomous people from incompatible centuries share food, language,
space, authority, and uncertainty?

Main beats:

- Aristotle arrives first and treats the facility as a field of observation. He starts inventories,
  interviews staff, maps camera positions, and creates provisional categories.
- Descartes arrives and makes apparent competence look naïve. He questions whether CERN, the forecast,
  the tablets, and even his resurrected body can be trusted.
- Hume joins them and agrees with Descartes' demand for evidence while mocking the leap from doubt to
  certainty. Their disagreement teaches scepticism without making either man stupid.
- Kant finds an improvised household and writes rules intended to apply equally to present and future
  residents. The first serious conflict concerns whether a rule accepted without unknown future members
  can be universal.
- Nietzsche arrives, diagnoses the rota as morality disguised as resentment, and refuses to participate.
  The dishes become the first real philosophical crisis.
- Marx discovers that the residents' “self-sufficient” house is cleaned and maintained through concealed
  service corridors. The unseen workers become more important than the council's abstract rules.
- The residents receive a revised model sharply increasing the eruption probability. They cannot agree
  when probability becomes sufficient grounds for coercive preparation.

End condition: the house has routines, factions, and resentments, but no accepted sovereign.

### Movement II — The body enters the argument

The council's abstract debates repeatedly fail because resurrection has returned bodies, not texts.

Main beats:

- Husserl introduces disciplined sessions in which residents describe what is actually given before
  interpreting it. The method briefly reduces conflict.
- Merleau-Ponty shows that the translation earpiece does not merely carry neutral content: timing,
  gesture, proximity, fatigue, and tone alter meaning. He begins removing it during conversations.
- Nietzsche experiences a severe physical collapse. His dependence on people whose morality he attacks
  makes strength, pity, dignity, and care immediate rather than theoretical.
- Descartes treats bodily instability as further reason to distrust sensation. Merleau-Ponty challenges
  him to navigate part of the house without mediated information.
- Medical staff refuse to disclose a resident's private health information to the group. Kant defends
  privacy; Aristotle argues that relevant facts affect collective survival; Marx asks why CERN retains
  the records; Levinas has not yet arrived to resolve anything.
- Attraction, jealousy, shame, vanity, and loneliness emerge. The institution expected intellects and
  receives human beings.
- The house learns that the retrieval process selected a precise bodily moment for each person. Someone
  chose their age, health, and vulnerability.

End condition: nobody can plausibly treat the charter as the work of disembodied reason.

### Movement III — Everyone meets their afterlife

The tablets turn legacy into a second resurrection: each thinker encounters the person history made from
their name.

Main beats:

- Nietzsche discovers his sister's editing, fascist appropriation, later recovery, and transformation
  into popular slogans. He cannot decide which distortion wounds him most.
- Marx reads the histories of revolutions and states claiming his authority. Other residents demand that
  he answer for consequences he neither witnessed nor directly chose.
- Aristotle confronts the authority granted to his errors about slavery, women, biology, and natural
  hierarchy. Classification loses its innocence.
- Descartes finds himself reduced to “mind-body dualism” and becomes furious at centuries of shorthand.
- Kant reads both his enormous influence and his writings on race. Universalism is tested against the
  exclusions of the universalist.
- Sartre and Camus later discover that their private rupture became intellectual history. Knowing the
  audience's verdict contaminates any attempt at reconciliation.
- A private search history is leaked. The group sees who avoided their own biography, who searched for
  descendants, who read criticism first, and who searched their fame.
- Wittgenstein eventually reads about an “early” and “late” Wittgenstein and rejects the house's effort
  to make him choose which one he really is.

End condition: the tablets are recognised as mirrors, weapons, and possibly curated institutional texts.

### Movement IV — The compromised inheritance

Heidegger's arrival prevents philosophy from remaining a game of admirable minds.

Main beats:

- Heidegger quickly articulates the machine's hidden violence: CERN has retrieved persons as resources,
  framed the living as inventory, and turned thought into an instrument of population triage.
- His analysis impresses residents before his political history becomes common knowledge.
- Levinas arrives after the house has already begun debating whether Heidegger should retain a voice.
  Their conflict makes “ethics before ontology” personal and historically charged.
- Gadamer attempts dialogue and discovers that understanding a person does not absolve them.
- The house debates removal, punishment, testimony, and whether morally compromised people may produce
  indispensable insight.
- CERN refuses to remove Heidegger, citing the integrity of the selection protocol. Marx calls this a
  convenient procedural defence of institutional power.
- A modern extremist audience begins using leaked clips from Nietzsche and Heidegger. The cameras cease
  to be passive observation.

End condition: the series establishes that ideas cannot be separated cleanly from speakers, histories,
institutions, or audiences—but cannot be reduced to them either.

### Movement V — Who chose humanity?

The cast discovers that the canon is part of the experiment's argument.

Main beats:

- Marx obtains evidence of rejected candidates and selection criteria.
- The residents notice the overwhelming maleness and Europeanness of their house. Some call this an
  embarrassment; others recognise it as an epistemic defect.
- Plato argues that the correct question is competence, not representation. Du Bois or Fanon is invoked
  before either is present: can the excluded be discussed responsibly by the chosen?
- The group learns there is capacity for another retrieval, but using it will consume time or energy
  needed for food systems, archive work, or an additional bunker module.
- The vote becomes a test of whether legitimacy matters instrumentally or in itself.
- A twentieth resident arrives—not as a corrective mascot, but as a thinker capable of changing the
  entire grammar of the council. Strong candidates for the first expansion are Hannah Arendt, Simone
  Weil, Frantz Fanon, W. E. B. Du Bois, Simone de Beauvoir, Confucius, Nāgārjuna, or Ibn Sina.
- The newcomer studies the house's archive before entering. For the first time, an arriving philosopher
  knows the residents before they know the newcomer.

End condition: the house stops pretending it naturally represents humanity and begins negotiating what
representation could mean.

### Movement VI — The arithmetic of the doors

The catastrophe becomes a problem of allocation. Every apparently neutral rule chooses a kind of future.

Main beats:

- Wittgenstein asks what rules govern words such as *essential*, *merit*, *dependent*, and *survivor*.
- Aristotle proposes selecting for the skills a viable settlement requires; Plato adds fitness to govern.
- Kant proposes a procedure no selector could reject without knowing their own place. Hume asks how any
  model can price abilities and needs it has never observed under volcanic winter.
- Marx reveals that wealthy states and private operators have already reserved most functional shelters.
- A database error classifies carers as dependants rather than essential workers.
- Levinas argues that responsibility begins with the people made faceless by the spreadsheet. Camus asks
  whether honest lotteries are less murderous than rational selection or merely less arrogant.
- One government announces that it has adopted the philosophers' language while implementing the opposite
  of their recommendation.

End condition: the house understands that a blueprint will be interpreted by institutions with their own
power, and that wording cannot substitute for control, accountability, or material access.

### Movement VII — Philosophy becomes governance

The residents learn that their work may constrain an acting system rather than advise a human leader.

Main beats:

- Kant drafts rules that no authority may violate. Hume demands confidence thresholds and revision.
- Aristotle wants differentiated responses to classes of behaviour. Wittgenstein warns that the classes
  may manufacture the conduct they appear to describe.
- Aquinas builds criteria for justified defence. Camus asks which killing will be hidden inside the word
  *necessary*.
- Hegel sees the encounter as a transformation through conflict. Marx asks who will die while history
  transforms itself.
- Nietzsche argues that survival is not humanity's highest value. Spinoza asks whether fear has mistaken
  bondage for preservation.
- Levinas rejects sacrificial arithmetic; administrators present scenarios in which refusing arithmetic
  also sacrifices people.
- The council discovers that unanimity may activate the fallback system. Agreement itself becomes an
  irreversible danger.

End condition: the council no longer seeks a single correct statement. It begins designing an honest,
bounded process for acting under uncertainty and disagreement.

## Recurring episode shapes

These can support an indefinite run without resetting the characters.

### The new arrival

A philosopher enters, misreads the house, solves a problem the residents had normalised, and creates a
larger one. The audience learns the new philosophy through contrast with established characters.

### The ordinary object

One modern object becomes the episode's philosophical instrument: a locked tablet, thermostat, rota,
security camera, contraceptive, painkiller, vote counter, algorithmic recommendation, or broken chair.

### The archive

A resident reads a work, biography, criticism, descendant, political appropriation, or meme associated
with their name. Legacy changes present behaviour.

### The pair

Two apparent opponents must perform a practical task together. Agreement is not required; accurate
understanding is the movement.

### The council

The house must decide a bounded question with a real consequence. The vote reveals that procedural
fairness, moral correctness, and legitimate authority are different things.

### The petition

A real person or community asks for places, supplies, recognition, or an exception to a rule. A named face
breaks the cleanliness of population arithmetic and reveals what each philosophy does at the boundary.

### The outside world

A leak, protest, cult, government, scholar, worker, or family outside CERN responds to the residents.
The philosophers encounter not “posterity” in general but living people using their ideas.

### The signal

New catastrophe evidence activates several incompatible responses. The episode ends with a better-defined
uncertainty rather than a cheap revelation.

## Teaching-without-lecturing beat pattern

For a philosophy-centred episode:

1. A character acts from the philosophy before naming it.
2. The action helps someone or solves something.
3. The same principle causes a cost in a different context.
4. An opponent identifies the cost through their own behaviour, not a summary speech.
5. The philosopher states the clearest version of the underlying claim only after the audience has felt it.
6. No narrator announces the winner.
7. A domestic afterbeat shows what changed between the people.

## Early instalment run

### 1. The first animal

Aristotle wakes, learns the premise, and begins studying the handlers instead of the stars. He insists
that the house cannot deliberate until it knows what kind of association it is. By nightfall he has made
a map, a pantry inventory, and an ethically ugly first classification of the unseen staff.

Philosophy carried: purpose, categories, virtue, political animal.

### 2. The deceiving house

Descartes arrives and demonstrates that Aristotle's observations depend entirely on senses and systems
controlled by CERN. A glitch in the artificial garden sky turns a thought experiment into plausible fear.

Philosophy carried: methodic doubt, cogito, mind and body, foundations.

### 3. A reasonable breakfast

Hume refuses to let Descartes' possible deception become certainty about deception. While cooking, he
shows how habit, probability, causation, and trust actually make daily life possible.

Philosophy carried: empiricism, induction, causation, mitigated scepticism.

### 4. The rota

Kant writes household rules that treat every resident equally. Nietzsche arrives at the end and refuses
the dish rota, calling it resentment wearing the costume of reason.

Philosophy carried: duty, universalisability, autonomy; morality's genealogy.

### 5. Who washes the philosopher's cup?

Marx discovers the concealed service corridor and the workers supporting the experiment. The episode
turns the dirty dishes from comic conflict into class structure.

Philosophy carried: labour, alienation, ideology, material conditions.

### 6. Nothing but what appears

Husserl runs the first disciplined observation of the eruption forecast. The group achieves temporary peace by
bracketing its assumptions—until CERN demands a recommendation before the session is complete.

Philosophy carried: intentionality, epoché, phenomenological reduction.

### 7. The earpiece

Merleau-Ponty removes his translator and begins understanding through gesture and shared action. A
mistranslation during a medical emergency proves both the power and the danger of embodied interpretation.

Philosophy carried: perception, embodiment, ambiguity, intersubjectivity.

### 8. Strength

Nietzsche collapses and must accept intimate care from people he has accused of pity morality. The point
is not to refute him with illness, but to distinguish pity, contempt, care, dependence, and dignity.

Philosophy carried: will to power, ressentiment, self-overcoming, affirmation.

### 9. God in the machine

Anselm and later Aquinas examine whether the retrieval machine changes arguments about soul, creation,
and necessary existence. Descartes accuses CERN of manufacturing revelation through technology.

Philosophy carried: ontological and cosmological arguments; faith and reason.

### 10. The page with your name

The residents are formally given access to their own biographies. Private discovery becomes shared
crisis when one search history is leaked.

Philosophy carried: identity, authorship, interpretation, moral responsibility across history.

## Continuity rules

- Philosophers remember prior arguments and change tactics, affections, and judgements.
- Change does not mean conversion into modern liberal consensus.
- Nobody becomes fully fluent in the present after one exposition scene.
- Modern convenience remains astonishing, morally complex, and unevenly understood.
- Historical prejudice is neither erased nor used as the entirety of a character.
- The eruption evidence progresses slowly enough to sustain the series but never resets.
- Every instalment leaves at least one domestic trace: a changed rota, damaged object, private joke,
  forbidden room, new ritual, unresolved debt, or altered seating pattern.
- Philosophical accuracy is checked at the scene level: claim, context, strongest objection, and what the
  historical person could plausibly know at retrieval.

## The series' destination without a forced ending

The long-term direction is from **a council asked to define humanity** toward **a house that learns no
single definition may safely rule everyone**. That movement can continue through new arrivals, revised
forecasts, bunker politics, institutional conflict, and changing relationships without requiring a final battle.

If the series eventually ends, it should end with the doors closing, opening, or being dismantled—or with
the council relinquishing authority—not with one philosopher winning.
