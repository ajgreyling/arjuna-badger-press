# Series Volume Roadmap

The series is expandable, but the original nineteen-person experiment has a four-volume backbone. A
twentieth retrieval can open the next phase without invalidating this structure.

## Volume One — *The House of Reasons*

Arrivals: Aristotle, Descartes, Hume, Kant, Nietzsche, Marx.  
Primary movement: from observation to material power.  
Domestic centre: kitchen, rota, cameras, hidden service corridors.  
Survival question: who is authorised to choose?  
Institutional reveal: the self-sufficient house is sustained by excluded labour.  
Climax: workers gain council standing.  
Bridge: Husserl wakes into a house that has already chosen sides.

Detailed preparation: complete in `VOLUME_ONE_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `CHAPTER_SKELETONS_VOLUME_ONE.md`.

## Volume Two — *The Body of Evidence*

Arrivals: Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger, Levinas, Gadamer.  
Primary movement: from disciplined description to the ethical limits of understanding.  
Domestic centre: medical suite, translation system, garden, bodies under surveillance.  
Survival question: can a population model understand the people it sorts?  
Institutional reveal: retrieval ages and bodily conditions were deliberately selected.

Major sequence:

1. Husserl brackets the council's accumulated assumptions and briefly restores procedural calm.
2. Workers challenge whether phenomenological suspension is possible for people whose allocations are expiring.
3. Merleau-Ponty demonstrates that earpiece translation loses gesture, rhythm, fatigue, and bodily relation.
4. A medical emergency produces contradictory translated records.
5. The council learns each retrieved body was chosen from several possible historical moments.
6. Heidegger arrives and names the machine's treatment of persons, land, and survival as standing reserve.
7. His analysis reorganises the house before his Nazi affiliation is exposed through controlled legacy access.
8. Residents debate removal, censorship, complicity, and whether insight survives its speaker's actions.
9. Levinas arrives after being shown Heidegger's record; ethics becomes a face-to-face accusation.
10. A bunker triage exercise reduces named communities to population units and disability coefficients.
11. Gadamer attempts a fusion of horizons between workers, philosophers, scientists, and the compromised teacher.
12. Elena rejects reconciliation without changed authority; dialogue is tested against power.
13. Public clips of Heidegger and Nietzsche are appropriated by an exclusionary survival movement.
14. The house must answer without letting CERN edit away disagreement.

Climax: the council publishes an anti-triage principle naming bodily dependence as a universal human
condition, then discovers several bunker systems cannot meet it without reducing total capacity.

End state: ethics and embodiment are inside the charter; the cost cannot be hidden.  
Bridge: Plato arrives and argues that the house has mistaken inclusion for good rule.

## Volume Three — *The Ark and the City*

Arrivals: Plato, Aquinas, Anselm, Spinoza.  
Primary movement: from ideal government and providence to immanent survival.  
Domestic centre: constitutional design, education, religion, children, archives, reproductive policy.  
Survival question: what kind of society should the bunkers intentionally create?  
Institutional reveal: several bunkers plan hereditary authority and compulsory population programmes.

Major sequence:

1. Plato finds a political order already built and diagnoses it as appetite managed by procedure.
2. He proposes guardian education, censorship of destructive stories, and rule by demonstrated competence.
3. Marx attacks the material selection of guardians; Kant attacks paternalism; Aristotle recognises the power
   of the constitutional whole.
4. A youth shelter asks whether children should inherit their parents' bunker class.
5. Plato's education plan helps a fractured house learn, then becomes the template for a coercive bunker curriculum.
6. Aquinas arrives and distinguishes survival, common good, law, authority, and moral limits.
7. Religious communities petition to preserve incompatible sacred duties, calendars, foods, and family structures.
8. Aquinas builds a synthesis that works until a bunker commander claims necessity permits killing dissenters.
9. Anselm arrives and asks what reason can establish when empirical life is disappearing.
10. The machine's apparent resurrection success intensifies disputes about soul, copy, continuity, and divine order.
11. Descartes and Anselm form an unstable foundations alliance; Hume presses the evidential wound.
12. Spinoza arrives and refuses both providential catastrophe and a God who chooses survivors.
13. Fear, hope, obedience, and crowd behaviour become the subject when forecast certainty jumps publicly.
14. Spinoza's affective calm helps the house resist panic but threatens the personal language of grief.
15. The council learns that archive capacity forces deletion: not every text, language, genome, or history fits.
16. Plato wants a formative canon; Marx asks who owns it; Gadamer asks what tradition requires; workers demand
    preservation of practical knowledge excluded from libraries.

Climax: the council designs an ark curriculum, then refuses to make it compulsory or closed. The resulting
plural archive needs more space than allocated, forcing philosophers to choose between their own full works
and other human records.

End state: the blueprint has education, law, religious freedom, and archive principles.  
Bridge: Hegel arrives and says their contradictions are not failures but the movement of history.

## Volume Four — *History After the End*

Arrivals: Hegel, Sartre, Camus, Wittgenstein.  
Primary movement: from historical necessity and radical choice to limits and linguistic humility.  
Domestic centre: punishment, emergency power, propaganda, revolt, final charter language.  
Survival question: may the future justify what is done before the doors close?  
Institutional reveal: the charter is already training automated allocation and governance systems.

Major sequence:

1. Hegel interprets the experiment as history becoming conscious of itself under catastrophe.
2. Marx attacks reconciliation that arrives after victims pay the price.
3. A state forcibly relocates a community to build a continuity site and cites the universal future.
4. Hegel's recognition framework illuminates bunker master/worker dependence while his totalising tendency
   makes present suffering appear necessary.
5. Sartre arrives and insists that every claim of role, model, history, or human nature can become bad faith.
6. Residents receive private offers: release, family places, influence, or immunity in exchange for endorsements.
7. Sartre performs refusal publicly while privately wanting the audience to ratify it.
8. Camus arrives knowing enough of his life to recognise Sartre but not their later public rupture, depending
   on the locked retrieval date.
9. Their conflict develops around revolt, murder, solidarity, and whether limits betray liberation.
10. A bunker uprising asks the council whether violence against an exclusionary allocation is justified.
11. Levinas and Camus oppose sacrificial futures for different reasons; Nietzsche challenges survival at any price.
12. Wittgenstein arrives last and questions the grammar of *merit*, *humanity*, *necessary*, *consensus*, and
    even *blueprint*.
13. He exposes several disputes as equivocations but cannot dissolve the bodies waiting outside real doors.
14. The council discovers unanimity will be interpreted as authorisation by an automated system.
15. Agreement becomes dangerous; dissent becomes a design requirement.
16. Each resident must decide whether to retain, transfer, randomise, or abolish the protected nineteen places.
17. The charter is rewritten as a bounded, revisable constitution with explicit uncertainty, minority reports,
    appeal, material rights, and prohibitions on single-authority control.
18. Institutions attempt to compress it into a score. Noah must choose between accessibility and betrayal.

Climax: the council relinquishes unilateral authority and releases the raw disagreements alongside the
charter. The nineteen protected places are placed under the same rule they propose for everyone else.

End state: the original experiment can end here without “solving” catastrophe. The world has a blueprint,
an archive of disagreement, and no guarantee it will obey either.

## Expansion hinge — The twentieth chair

The four-volume backbone deliberately exposes that the council is male and disproportionately European.
The next phase should not simply add a corrective guest. It should reopen selection itself.

Possible mechanisms:

- workers and outside communities nominate candidates;
- the original selection committee's rejected files leak;
- the retrieval energy can produce one person or expand a shelter module, making thought materially costly;
- candidates may include Hannah Arendt, Simone Weil, Simone de Beauvoir, W. E. B. Du Bois, Frantz Fanon,
  Confucius, Nāgārjuna, Ibn Sina, or a thinker outside the institutional category “philosopher.”

The chosen twentieth resident should receive the full archive before entry. They arrive able to read the
house as a historical object, reversing the usual power of established residents over a disoriented newcomer.
