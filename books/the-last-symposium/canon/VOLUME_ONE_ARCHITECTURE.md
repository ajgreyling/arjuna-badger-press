# Volume One Architecture — *The House of Reasons*

Parent series: *The Last Symposium*  
Draft status: pre-prose skeleton v0.1  
Scope: first six retrievals — Aristotle, Descartes, Hume, Kant, Nietzsche, Marx  
Shape: 24 chapters in six four-chapter movements  
Target chapter length: 2,500–4,000 words  
Estimated draft length: 75,000–90,000 words

## Volume promise

Six dead philosophers wake one by one in a filmed house at CERN and are asked to help decide who will
survive a predicted super-eruption. Before they can design a civilisation, they must discover who feeds,
cleans, records, translates, and controls their own supposedly self-sufficient home.

The volume teaches six philosophical orientations through escalating domestic conflicts:

- Aristotle: kinds, purposes, virtue, habituation, political association;
- Descartes: methodic doubt, foundations, mind/body distinction, the deceiver problem;
- Hume: empiricism, induction, causal habit, sentiment, proportional belief;
- Kant: autonomy, duty, universalisability, persons as ends, procedural legitimacy;
- Nietzsche: genealogy, ressentiment, herd morality, self-overcoming, survival as a questionable good;
- Marx: labour, alienation, ideology, ownership, material conditions, class power.

## Volume question

Can people design a just survival system while remaining blind to the people and systems already keeping
them alive?

## Volume answer

Not honestly. Any charter about the future is fraudulent until the council understands the material
conditions of its own authority and admits affected people into the room.

## Reader knowledge at the opening

- A major catastrophe is forecast, but neither its date nor certainty is public.
- CERN has revived at least one historical philosopher.
- The facility is filmed.
- The public does not yet know the residents have reserved bunker places.

## Reader knowledge at the close

- The forecast is probabilistic and politically curated.
- The house is materially sustained by a hidden workforce.
- The philosophers' feeds are being edited and distributed outside.
- Every retrieved philosopher has a provisional bunker place.
- The selection committee excluded many candidates.
- The council has no binding authority, but its language is already influencing policy.
- Marx has forced the first non-philosopher workers into a formal council session.

## Emotional movement

**Wonder → suspicion → conviviality → order → revolt → exposure.**

Aristotle makes the impossible manageable. Descartes makes management suspect. Hume makes uncertainty
livable. Kant makes life governable. Nietzsche makes governance morally suspicious. Marx reveals the
infrastructure that all five mistook for nature.

## Structural rule

Each arrival initially appears to solve the previous movement's central problem, then reveals the cost
or blind spot in that solution.

1. Chaos needs Aristotle's observation.
2. Aristotle's confidence needs Descartes' doubt.
3. Descartes' doubt needs Hume's practical probability.
4. Humean custom needs Kant's explicit principle.
5. Kantian law needs Nietzsche's genealogy.
6. Nietzschean refusal needs Marx's material organisation.

## Catastrophe lock for Volume One

Working event name: **the Asterion Forecast**.

- A fictional subglacial caldera system in West Antarctica shows accelerating deformation, gas release,
  seismic migration, and deep thermal change.
- A multinational model called JANUS estimates a non-trivial probability of a civilisation-disrupting
  eruption within years, with a wide and politically contested uncertainty interval.
- The danger is not lava reaching most people. It is atmospheric loading, cold, crop failure, supply-chain
  collapse, displacement, conflict, and prolonged infrastructure failure.
- Governments cannot wait for certainty because viable shelters, seed systems, energy stores, and food
  production take years to build.
- The forecast may be honest, manipulated, misunderstood, or all three at different institutional layers.
- Volume One does not prove the eruption will occur. It proves decisions are already occurring.

Science note: all quantitative probabilities, eruption mechanisms, atmospheric consequences, and bunker
requirements remain research-gated before prose drafting.

## The modern ensemble in Volume One

- **Dr Mara Voss:** project director; controls access, briefings, and public mandate.
- **Dr Amina Okafor:** volcanologist and JANUS liaison; intellectually honest about uncertainty.
- **Dr Samira El-Khoury:** physician responsible for retrieved bodies; refuses to call them assets.
- **Léa Fournier:** translation and archive engineer; knows where mediation enters the system.
- **Mateo Ruiz:** facilities lead; visible to residents, but institutionally instructed to act like part
  of the house rather than a political participant.
- **Elena Bako:** night cleaner and union representative; the first hidden worker Marx sees clearly.
- **Noah Vale:** ethics secretariat and official recorder; drafts summaries that quietly become policy language.

Modern characters are not straight men whose only function is to ask “What do you mean?” Each has
knowledge, leverage, private stakes, and a philosophy embodied in practice.

## Point-of-view system

Close third person, rotating.

- No interior POV for a philosopher in the chapter of his arrival until after the retrieval threshold.
- Modern POVs prevent the resurrected men from monopolising reality.
- No omniscient explanation of who is philosophically correct.
- The cameras may show behaviour but never grant the narrator access to institutional omniscience.
- Each chapter carries one dominant POV; brief documents or transcript fragments may precede it.

Volume One dominant POV allocation (two threshold chapters split deliberately):

- Aristotle: 6 chapters, plus shared threshold passages in Chapters 1 and 24
- Descartes: 2 chapters
- Hume: 3 chapters
- Kant: 3 chapters
- Nietzsche: 2 chapters
- Marx: 2 chapters
- Mara: 1 chapter, plus shared threshold passages in Chapters 1 and 24
- Amina: 1 chapter
- Samira: 1 chapter
- Elena: 1 chapter

## Document inserts

Use sparingly—maximum one short insert every three chapters.

- retrieval consent form with impossible assumptions;
- JANUS probability bulletin;
- camera transcript with translation confidence flags;
- household rule sheet with handwritten objections;
- edited public-feed description;
- facilities work order;
- leaked bunker-allocation memorandum.

The inserts must create dramatic irony or reveal mediation. They may not replace scenes.

## Volume movements

### Movement A — The political animal (Chapters 1–4)

Aristotle wakes alone, classifies the house, and establishes a workable order. His methods are genuinely
useful. His first moral failure is treating staff roles as natural categories rather than imposed ones.

Movement turn: a second retrieval is announced after Aristotle has begun believing the house is his to order.

### Movement B — The deceiving house (Chapters 5–8)

Descartes demonstrates that every source of evidence is controlled. A garden-sky malfunction gives his
suspicion material force. Aristotle and Descartes must still make a practical recommendation from uncertain
data.

Movement turn: Hume arrives and eats the breakfast they were too philosophically occupied to trust.

### Movement C — Custom and probability (Chapters 9–12)

Hume shows that ordinary life runs on habit and proportion, not foundations. A revised forecast forces the
house to decide when uncertain danger justifies action. A public petition gives the probability a face.

Movement turn: the residents' improvised vote is challenged by the arrival of Kant.

### Movement D — A rule for everyone (Chapters 13–16)

Kant formalises household and council procedure. The rules improve dignity and predictability, then expose
their own legitimacy problem: future residents and outside victims did not consent. The reserved bunker
places are disclosed.

Movement turn: Nietzsche arrives as the first resident who does not want the honour being imposed on him.

### Movement E — The bad conscience of the rota (Chapters 17–20)

Nietzsche attacks equal obligation, the performance of moral goodness, and survival as the unquestioned
highest value. His critique liberates resentment and licenses selfishness in unequal measure. A bodily
episode exposes the difference between pity and care without resolving it.

Movement turn: Marx arrives during a dispute over who failed to clean the kitchen and asks who cleaned it
before any of them woke.

### Movement F — Behind the wall (Chapters 21–24)

Marx follows labour rather than ideas. The service corridor reveals the workers, editorial system, and
material machinery behind the house. He organises rather than merely denounces. The council's first charter
language leaks into a real bunker policy while those affected remain excluded.

Volume close: workers take seats at the philosophers' table. CERN announces that the seventh retrieval
will proceed at dawn regardless of the council's strike.

## Subplot tracks

### Forecast integrity

Ch 1: catastrophe presented as mandate.  
Ch 6: garden glitch establishes mediation.  
Ch 8: confidence intervals are omitted from an executive briefing.  
Ch 11: JANUS probability rises because of a model revision, not a physical event.  
Ch 18: Nietzsche asks who benefits from fear.  
Ch 23: Marx finds different briefings for different audiences.

### Cameras and public audience

Ch 2: residents learn everything is recorded.  
Ch 7: Descartes tests edit discontinuities.  
Ch 10: Hume performs for a camera and admits it.  
Ch 15: Kant requests a private deliberative space.  
Ch 18: Nietzsche weaponises the confession room.  
Ch 23: edited clips are found in the service system.

### Reserved places

Ch 3: oblique reference to resident continuity planning.  
Ch 12: petitioner asks whether the dead have priority over the living.  
Ch 16: nineteen places revealed.  
Ch 19: each resident privately responds.  
Ch 24: workers demand the philosophers declare their own eligibility first.

### Translation

Ch 1: basic semantic friction.  
Ch 5: Descartes questions whether translated thought remains his.  
Ch 9: Hume enjoys and tests idiom.  
Ch 14: Kant's duty vocabulary produces misleading modern equivalences.  
Ch 17: Nietzsche refuses a flattened translation of rank and nobility.  
Ch 22: workers discover the system gives philosopher speech higher confidence priority.

### Aristotle and Mateo

Aristotle mistakes Mateo's institutional role for his social nature. Mateo initially tolerates this to
keep the house functioning. Their practical respect grows, making Marx's later exposure more painful.

### Mara and the mandate

Mara believes a usable imperfect charter may save lives. Every concession to speed makes the process less
legitimate. By the close, she must choose between cancelling the seventh retrieval and defying the states
funding the only functioning programme.

## Volume climax mechanics

The climax is not a speech or vote. It is a change in who can enter the room.

1. Marx proves hidden workers are essential to the experiment.
2. The workers stop non-medical service for a bounded period.
3. The residents experience the house as infrastructure rather than environment.
4. Mara offers consultation without membership.
5. Kant's own rule against treating persons merely as means is used against the council.
6. Aristotle must choose between preserving deliberative order and admitting that his original categories
   were politically produced.
7. The council votes to give workers seats and access to the unedited forecast.
8. The door opens; the victory is real but incomplete because the wider bunker system remains unequal.

## Final image

Elena sits in Aristotle's customary chair because it is the only empty one. Aristotle brings another
chair from the wall rather than asking her to move. Before anyone speaks, the retrieval machinery begins
its dawn cycle for Husserl.

This is an outline image, not locked prose.
