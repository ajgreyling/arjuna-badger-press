## Facility topology

Project Mnemosyne occupies a restricted extension of the CERN campus. The residence is above the deepest
machine infrastructure but below ordinary public access.

### Resident-visible layer

- **Retrieval chamber:** not visible from the house; sterile, circular, equipment partly hidden behind panels.
- **Medical suite:** two treatment rooms, observation bay, medicine lock, private washroom.
- **Arrival corridor:** connects medical suite to bedroom wing; no windows.
- **Bedroom wing:** twenty rooms, identical dimensions, individually adaptable, no cameras inside bedrooms.
- **Central dining room:** one long modular table, twenty-four chairs stored around edges.
- **Kitchen:** induction cooking, visible stores, smart ration inventory, no automatic cleaning advertised.
- **Lounge:** low furniture, wall display, configurable briefing surface.
- **Library:** tables, notebooks, tablets, no open internet and initially no physical books.
- **Garden:** enclosed planted court under artificial daylight ceiling; real soil, controlled air and rain.
- **Workshop:** hand tools selected for safety; broken items accumulate here.
- **Confession room:** camera, seat, prompts; recordings framed as testimony and education.
- **Communications chamber:** screened outside petitions and formal transmissions.
- **Briefing room:** secure displays, model visualisations, voting interface.

### Hidden/support layer

- continuous service corridor behind kitchen, bedrooms, garden, and camera walls;
- food staging and dish sanitation;
- laundry and clothing adjustment;
- waste and environmental sanitation;
- translation racks and manual quality queue;
- camera maintenance and edit-marking stations;
- staff rest room with inferior air and no natural-light simulation;
- control-room threshold; workers cannot enter the primary control room without escort.

### Access classes

- Red: project executive/security.
- Blue: medical and retrieval.
- Green: technical/archive.
- Amber: facilities and environmental.
- White: resident-visible access.

Colour labels are visible on bands; Aristotle initially mistakes them for descriptions of natural role.

## Camera rules

- No cameras in resident bedrooms, showers, toilets, or active clinical treatment zones.
- Communal areas have multiple visible cameras and some disclosed audio-only safety pickups.
- Raw footage is cryptographically archived; access and integrity claims require later technical research.
- Editors mark clips but cannot alter the raw archive under stated rules.
- Public releases are delayed and curated.
- Residents cannot directly see public reactions in Volume One.
- Search histories and translation logs are recorded but classified as personal data—until later leakage.

Continuity check for every scene: which cameras can see it, who can monitor live, who can retrieve it later,
and whether the character changes behaviour because of that knowledge.

## Translation rules

- The earpiece renders meaning into a language modelled as immediately comprehensible to the wearer.
- The resident's speech is captured, interpreted, and rendered for every listener.
- Ordinary referents translate quickly; concepts without clean equivalence create a subtle tone and visible
  transcript flag.
- Proper names remain phonetic until associated with a tablet record.
- The system can preserve a source term when rendering would erase an important distinction.
- Latency rises with novelty, overlapping speech, pain, metaphor, and deliberate wordplay.
- Staff can inspect alternative renderings but cannot establish intention from output alone.
- Philosophers do not sound quaint merely because the translation source is old.

Volume One translation continuity:

- Day 1: Aristotle learns the flag tone.
- Day 2: Descartes requests logs and discovers a nine-second archive discontinuity.
- Day 3: Hume tests idiom and performance.
- Day 4: Kant challenges moral vocabulary equivalence.
- Day 5: Nietzsche forces preservation of key source terms.
- Day 6: Marx learns philosopher queues outrank worker correction queues.

## Tablet rules

- Rugged child-safe tablet with no visible brand.
- Frozen Wikipedia plus curated modern-orientation package.
- Personal legacy pages locked through Volume One; full opening occurs in a later movement.
- Basic maps, timeline, science primers, household manuals, and approved catastrophe documents available.
- No messaging, social media, general search engine, or live web.
- Every search is logged.
- Residents can make private local notes, but CERN can technically recover them; this is not clearly disclosed.
- Tablet camera and microphone are physically disabled.

## Notebook rules

- Paper notebook, stitched binding, numbered pages.
- Cameras may film an open notebook if line of sight permits.
- Staff undertake not to open a closed notebook without medical emergency or security authorisation.
- Each notebook has a differently coloured thread marker, not a printed resident name.
- The notebook is the nearest thing to private interior space outside bedrooms.

## The Asterion Forecast

What characters may know in Volume One:

- Multiple independent physical signals indicate instability beneath a fictional West Antarctic caldera.
- JANUS integrates observations into conditional scenarios.
- “Probability of eruption” is meaningless without time horizon and event definition; briefings often omit both.
- Model revisions can change the headline number without a new physical event.
- Even without eruption, preparation decisions create displacement, markets, secrecy, and coercion.
- Shelter capacity is radically unequal across states and private actors.
- Food production, energy continuity, disease control, and legitimate governance matter more than simply
  placing people underground.

What remains unknown:

- whether the largest scenario is physically plausible as described;
- whether the model is biased high or low;
- whether funders accelerated retrieval because of forecast evidence or political opportunity;
- which states possess private model variants;
- whether bunker capacity numbers include concealed private sites;
- whether “nineteen places” are physical seats or protected allocation tokens transferable between sites.

## Bunker system

There is no single global bunker.

- state shelters vary from serious continuity facilities to political theatre;
- military installations prioritise command, communications, and technical continuity;
- private compounds reserve places through wealth and employment contracts;
- community projects emphasise food, water, and mutual aid but lack hardened infrastructure;
- seed, genome, archive, and industrial-recovery sites may contain few permanent residents;
- allocation status changes as models, money, construction, and alliances change.

The charter's influence therefore occurs through standards, model policy, public legitimacy, and copied
language—not a magic global vote.
