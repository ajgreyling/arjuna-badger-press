**Act I**  
Arrive: Crew lands at Cerro Unitas, Chile. The Giant looms; caravan tracks etched in the dust.  
Obstacle: Heritage consortium has filed for restricted access; local wayfinder is wary, access is controlled.  
Read: Crew attempts a ground-level survey; their mapping drone struggles with the scale and wind.  
Cost: First attempt at replicating a geoglyph (the “potato”) — their lines are wobbly, erased by wind in a day.  
Key: The wayfinder shows how the rays align only when viewed from the old caravan path at certain moons.  
Handoff: The wayfinder refuses to state the function outright, but demonstrates how the Giant is used in practice — as a seasonal waymark.

**Act II**  
Arrive: Northward to Paracas, Peru. The Candelabra rises above the bay, seen only from sea.  
Obstacle: Consortium teams are prepping the site for closed research/tourism; local archaeologist is suspicious of outsiders.  
Read: Crew documents the Candelabra’s depth, attempts to date it via non-invasive means; finds only ambiguous pottery.  
Cost: A staged “recreation” with modern tools yields a shallow, wind-erased, off-kilter figure (another “potato”).  
Key: Archaeologist shares local oral histories and the practicalities of ancient navigation; points out the Candelabra’s line of sight to old sea routes.  
Handoff: The archaeologist withholds speculation on the symbol’s meaning, but lets the crew witness a local fishing boat using the Candelabra as a navigation mark.

**Act III**  
Confrontation: Consortium offers the crew access in exchange for exclusive data; the crew refuses, prioritising the local’s right to own the meaning.  
Climax: At sunset, Jakobus and the crew, with both local guides, watch the Giant’s rays catch the moon, and the Candelabra’s arms catch the dusk light at sea.  
Resolution: The locals speak last: these are not messages for gods or tourists, but tools for people moving through the world — waymarks, calendars, calls across distance.  
The crew records, but does not claim, the answer. The sites remain open, living.  
Back matter: All measurements, alignments, and scholarly holes are cited; fringe claims are flagged; the reader is left in the “maybe” — able to stand in the wind and decide.

Subplots:  
- Internal: Crew’s ND lead struggles with the temptation to “solve” versus the wisdom of letting the question stand.  
- External: The LENS’s camera is both a tool and a shield; learning when to lower it.  
- The adversary’s arc: Forced to confront that protecting a site can mean letting it breathe, not closing it.
