A neurodiverse, found-family crew races the Pacific coast to read two monumental geoglyphs — the Atacama Giant and the Paracas Candelabra — before a well-funded heritage consortium can lock down their meaning. The crew’s edge: their wiring lets them see the world as a map of routes, signals, and patterns, but at every turn, the land’s own readers — a Chilean wayfinder and a Peruvian archaeologist — hold the keys the crew can’t Google or measure. The book never states its theme: it lives in the crew’s refusal to close the question and their respect for the locals’ right to speak last. The Why Files register: play the official story straight, show how hard the “easy” answer is, name the one real unresolved fact, wink, and let the function (not the myth) re-open the wonder. Intended for readers who crave rigour and wonder at once — the ones who want to stand in the draught of maybe. POV is close-third to the ND crew (never the local, never Jakobus); the story’s bounds are the real sites, the checkable facts, and the refusal to preach or solve. Creative driver: the chase to read before the meaning is fixed, and the moment the crew has to let the local own the answer.
