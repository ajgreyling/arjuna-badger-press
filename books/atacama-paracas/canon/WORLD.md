The real Pacific coast: from the stark, rainless expanse of Chile’s Atacama Desert, where the Giant surveys a pampa crossed by old caravan routes, to the wind-scoured Paracas Peninsula, its trident geoglyph cut deep above the sea. The map is a corridor — the crew follows the old trade and migration paths, tracing how the land’s own geometry shaped the signs. The wonder is physical: the Giant’s rays visible only from the pampa, the Candelabra’s arms seen only from a boat, the salt crust that lets a line endure for centuries. The chase runs north by land and sea, through dust, salt, and shifting light, always in sight of the horizon. The living world: desert herders, coastal fishers, local archaeologists and wayfinders, guides who treat the marks as infrastructure, not relics. The crew is always the outsider, the place is never a puzzle-box for them to crack; the people who live here have the last word.
