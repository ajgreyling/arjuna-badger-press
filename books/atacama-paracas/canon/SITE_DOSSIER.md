## Atacama Giant / Cerro Unitas — Chile

- **Site:** Cerro Unitas, Atacama Desert, Chile.
- **Figure:** Anthropomorphic geoglyph, **119 m** tall (largest known prehistoric anthropomorphic geoglyph on Earth).
- **Date:** Attributed to regional Andean cultures, **c. AD 1000–1400**.
- **Construction:** Combination of *extractive* (scraping surface to reveal pale subsoil) and *additive* (piling dark stones on lighter ground); built on a hillside for visibility from below and afar.
- **Context:** One of **~5,000 Atacama geoglyphs**, many of which align with llama caravan routes as signposts/landmarks.
- **Official Interpretation:** Represents a deity, shaman, or authority figure; part of a regional tradition of monumental ground art.
- **Checkable Open Hypothesis:** **Luis Briones, *Antiquity* 2006** — the "rays" or "antennae" from the head may function as a **seasonal/lunar marker**; lunar alignments with the rays could indicate planting/rainy season onset. This is a peer-reviewed, published hypothesis, but remains unproven.
- **Fringe Claims (labelled):** "Alien astronaut" or "antenna being" interpretations (the rays are real features, but the ET gloss is speculative and not supported by evidence).
- **Grounded Angle:** Infrastructure for wayfinding — geoglyphs as caravan route markers; the Giant is placed for maximum visibility across open pampa.
- **Scene-Ready Details:** 119 m human figure on a bare hill in the driest non-polar desert; head with radiating rays/antennae, possibly tied to lunar setting positions.
- **The Wink:** Is the crown of rays a halo, a headdress — or a working lunar gnomon that still points to where the moon sets at the turn of the season?

## Paracas Candelabra — Peru

- **Site:** Northern face of the Paracas Peninsula, overlooking Pisco Bay, Peru.
- **Figure:** A **~170 m tall**, **~60 m wide** geoglyph with three-pronged "candelabra" or "trident" form, cut into the hillside.
- **Visibility:** Positioned to be seen from **~19 km out to sea** — a rare geoglyph oriented toward the ocean.
- **Construction:** Cut **~0.6 m (2 ft) deep** into hardened, saline soil (much deeper than Nazca lines); stones possibly used to line trenches; the salty crust stabilises it and prevents wind erosion.
- **Dating:** **No direct date for the geoglyph itself.** Paracas-style pottery found *nearby* is dated **~200 BC**, but there is no confirmed link between the pottery and the geoglyph's creation.
- **Official Interpretation:** Loosely associated with the Paracas culture; sometimes linked to the Nazca lines, but attribution is unconfirmed.
- **Checkable Open Question:** The monument's date, maker, and purpose remain unknown; the only firm date is from nearby pottery, not the geoglyph itself.
- **Fringe Claims (labelled):** Datura "homing beacon for hallucinogen sailors," Freemasonry/Viracocha symbolism, "giant seismograph" — all speculative and not supported by direct evidence.
- **Grounded Angle:** Sightline engineering — a monumental sign visible from far out at sea, possibly a maritime navigation beacon. The depth of the cut and crust composition explain its survival.
- **Scene-Ready Details:** 170-metre sign carved for reading from the open ocean; cut 2 ft deep; undated and floating in attribution.
- **The Wink:** A 170-metre sign carved to be read only from the open ocean — by whom, for sailors of what, and how would a coastal people aim it at a horizon they couldn't stand on?
