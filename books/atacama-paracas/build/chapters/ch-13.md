# Chapter 13 — The Wink: A Signal Aimed at the Sea

The boat smelled of diesel and fish-guts and sun-cooked rubber, and that was before Reyes cut the engine and let us drift.

"There," she said, and pointed without looking, because she didn't need to look. She'd done this a thousand times. "Now you see it."

We turned. The Paracas Peninsula was a long, low, biscuit-coloured animal lying down in the haze, and out of its northern flank, as if someone had pressed three enormous fingers into wet clay and pulled them slowly toward heaven, rose the Candelabra.

Nobody said anything for a moment. Even the fish — we kept a little resin cichlid suction-cupped to the wheelhouse window, a heckling mascot we'd carried since Chile, who under his breath had opinions about everything — even the fish held his tongue.

Then Mara, our LENS, lowered the camera she'd had glued to her eye since we left the jetty.

I noticed because she never does that.

"From the land it's nothing," she said. "I drove past the headland yesterday. You can't read it. You can't even tell it's a shape."

"You weren't meant to," Reyes said.

Let's do this properly, because the Candelabra deserves it.

A hundred and seventy metres tall. Sixty wide. A trident, a candelabra, a tree, a glyph — pick your noun, they all fit and none of them stick — cut into the hardened, salt-laced slope of the peninsula above Pisco Bay. The official story, the careful one, the one the textbooks tell with their hands folded: it is *loosely* Paracas. The Paracas people worked this coast around two centuries before Christ, buried their dead in fabulous textiles, knew the desert and the sea. Pottery of their style turns up near the figure. Some scholars tie the Candelabra to them. Others reach a little further south and east and link it to the Nazca lines, the famous ones, the hummingbird and the monkey and the spider.

That's the respectable account, and every word of it is hedged, and the hedging is the honest part.

Because here is the thing the brochures skip: **nobody has ever dated it.** Not the figure. Not once. The pottery is dated — pottery's easy, pottery has carbon in its slip and context in its sherds — and the pottery whispers *circa 200 BC* from somewhere on the hillside *nearby.* But a pot beside a glyph is not a glyph. A coin in a field is not a cathedral. The link is assumed, sensible, unproven. The Candelabra floats.
