# *THE SURGEON* — Title Workshop

> Working title **THE SURGEON** — clean, cold, and carries the book's central irony (he is
> exactly not a surgeon; the title is his delusion wearing a nameplate). Strong default;
> alternatives below if the shelf needs more distinction from the genre's many "The Surgeon"s
> (Gerritsen 2001, most visibly).

## The brief, distilled

A title that promises literary serial-crime (*Dexter* shelf), carries the beauty-doctrine /
perfection-chase spine, and ideally whispers the Cape. Must not glamourise him: the title can
be *his* self-image only if the book is visibly about puncturing it.

## Shortlist

### ★ 1. **The Surgeon**
The delusion as nameplate. Instantly shelvable, coldly confident. Known risk: heavily used
in the genre — mitigate with subtitle/eyebrow (*A Cape Town thriller*) and cover distinction.

### 2. **The Golden Ratio**
His gospel as title; the irony pre-loaded (φ is irrational — perfection non-terminating).
Reads smart-thriller; slight risk of maths-book shelving. Strong tagline pairing:
*Perfection is irrational.*

### 3. **Bedside Manner**
The charm as murder weapon. Wry, sinister, very Ripley. Risk: reads medical-romance at a
glance.

### 4. **First, Do No Harm**
The oath he never swore, inverted by every chapter. Familiar phrase — memorable, but used
before in med-thriller titling.

### 5. **The Kindnesses**
Part I's name; quiet horror, literary register. Risk: too soft for the shelf's browsers.

## Decision
Hold **The Surgeon** as locked-working. Revisit at cover pass with distinction check against
the Gerritsen shelf. Tagline candidates: *Perfection is irrational.* / *He only takes what
you'd thank him for.* / *Beauty, without consent.*
