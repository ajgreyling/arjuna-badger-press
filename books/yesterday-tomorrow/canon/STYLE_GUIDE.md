# Style Guide — *YESTERDAY, TOMORROW*

> Weir mode. Read with `STRUCTURE.md` and `CRAFT.md`. Where this and the old per-book style guide
> disagree, this wins — the old one assumed five co-equal POVs and no clock.

---

## Voice

**Close third, past tense, one consciousness per section**, rotating across the four working sites:
Technopark, Accra, Nairobi, the Antarctic station. Section break when the skull changes.

Rotation is disciplined, not democratic. **A section exists because that node is the only place the
problem can be worked** — never to relay information another node already has. If two sections could
be one, they are one.

- **Dries** — systems architect, twenty-three years in the same office park. Thinks in dependencies,
  failure modes, blast radius, telemetry. Reaches for plumbing metaphors, gets corrected by
  physicists, is right often enough that the corrections stop.
- **G** — a principal, not a guru. Refuses soft claims, asks what measurement would kill a thing,
  says the load-bearing sentence once and never repeats it.
- **Ama** — the Rain, ingestion, what gets demoted and who decides. The fastest arguer in the book.
- **Kiki** — the method is hers. Speaks in the conditional, will not be hurried or charmed.
- **Sanna** — carries Antarctica alone. Colder in sentence length, not just content.
- **Lwazi** — the one most tempted by the offers, and the first to believe the catastrophe is
  invariant.

## The ladder

**The probability ladder is always on the page** and nothing ever exceeds it: six minutes effectively
certain, six hours frighteningly accurate, six days strategically valuable, six months a field of
branches. Characters plan around it, argue about which rung a claim sits on, and lose arguments to
it. It is the book's honesty and its clock at once.

From the moment the catastrophe forecast lands, **the reader always knows roughly how long is left.** Days remaining
appear in scene furniture — a whiteboard, a ticket title, a manifest date, Sanna's ration schedule —
never as a chapter epigraph counting down, which is a television device and cheapens fast.

## Problem discipline

Every sequence runs: **problem → attempt → failure → new information → worse problem.**

- The problem is legible before the solution arrives. The reader should be able to form a hypothesis.
- **Failure is diagnostic, never delay.** If a failure produces no new information, cut it.
- The solution uses a tool or rule already established on the page.
- Improvisation carries governance, safety or power cost. Something is always spent.
- The smartest person in the scene is not automatically the highest-status person.

## Humour

Load-bearing, and it lives in the work. Technopark bluntness. The forbidden-phrase jar — money in it
for anyone who says *enhance*. Ghana and Nairobi's professional rivalry, affectionate and occasionally
genuinely sharp. Acronym abuse. Generators, procurement, bad coffee, engineers unimpressed by
ceremony. Somebody naming an instrument badly and never being allowed to forget it.

**Where humour is forbidden:** the family, the daughter's question, the reconstruction of the fall,
and anything Sanna says about the ice at night. The book must be able to stop being funny instantly
and without announcing it.

## The machine on the page

- **Never cinema.** Sparse, gap-marked, slow, ugly. The smooth version is always the fake.
- **Show what is missing** as much as what appears.
- **Cost every time:** compute, grid, cryogen, queue position, bandwidth, a night nobody slept.
  A free query is a continuity error. In Part Four cost becomes literal survival arithmetic.
- Vocabulary is fixed (`TERMINOLOGY.md`): residue, inversion, anchor, uncertainty field, the Rain,
  Witness Protocol, temporal reference. Journalists say *footage* and *playback*; someone winces.
- **No infodumps.** Physics is earned inside experiments. Landauer arrives because Dries asks a
  stupid-sounding question and is answered, not because a chapter explains thermodynamics.

## Lucid

Computes, cites, conflicts or refuses. **Never a POV, never a voice of doom, never a buddy.** It has
no opinion about what the past should have been, and the crew's growing reliance on that is quietly
unnerving. It refuses **G** on the page. Its final refusal is the last line of the argument.

## Africa

The consortium is not helped into relevance. The world arrives after the work succeeds. Nairobi and
Accra are institutions with rivalries and standards, not locations. **No continental-representative
characters.** The Karoo's silence is engineered and defended by people who live there.

## What not to write

- A reconstruction as footage. If it has a face, sound and no gaps, you have written the forgery.
- A rogue-AI beat: Lucid waking, wanting, lying, or being switched off in a climax.
- A prediction that beats the ladder because the scene needs it to.
- The blind-spot act chosen *because it works*. The moment it is a tactic it is advantage, and
  advantage is inside the model.
- Time travel, paradox, a message from the future, a watcher. Not once, not as a joke that stands.
- Precrime. Nobody is arrested for a future act.
- A lone-white-genius beat. Dries is the viewpoint; he authors no breakthrough he did not earn, and
  the decisive insight in Part One belongs to a telemetry veteran who does not care about the theory.
- Mystical Africa. Traditional knowledge shapes ethics and interpretation; the instrument is testable
  fictional physics.
- Clean historical catharsis. Actions clarify; motive and national mythology get harder.
- Decentralisation as magic. The partition costs speed, elegance, convenience and lives.
- An American rescue.
- Apocalypse spectacle before ports, grids, food, hospitals and choices.
- Grief as spectacle around the family. Under-dramatise. The flat line is the loaded one.
