ACT I: ARRIVAL
- The crew arrives in Uffington as preparations for the scouring festival begin. They introduce the official story: Bronze Age origin, OSL dating, the tradition of scouring. The consultancy is already present, pitching a restoration campaign to the local council and historical society.
- OBSTACLE: The consultancy wins early support; the crew are seen as outsiders, their motives questioned. Edie Vane, wary but curious, becomes their hesitant guide.
- READ: The crew attempts to document the scouring process, but struggle to grasp its rhythm and meaning. Frik (Deaf ND lead) notices the unspoken rituals among the scourers—gestures, glances, the way chalk is handed down. The LENS captures moments of care, missed by others.
- COST: The council votes to approve preliminary "restoration" plans; the consultancy stages a trial re-chalking using modern methods, which produces a "potato"—the lines are too crisp, the figure loses its wildness, the community feels alienated.
- KEY: The crew is invited to join the real scouring, not as observers but as hands. Edie, seeing their willingness to listen, lets them in on the lived practice—the unbroken, wordless transmission of care.

ACT II: THE CHASE TURNS
- The consultancy pushes to formalise the restoration, bringing in experts, drones, and digital models; they argue for "historical accuracy" but miss the point of the living act.
- The crew, embedded with the scourers, learns the depth of the tradition: the tools, the songs, the meals, the silent moments. Frik deciphers the ritual in gesture; the others start to sense the act's function is not to recall a meaning, but to keep the act itself alive.
- OBSTACLE: The consultancy uncovers a fragment of ancient chalk beneath the turf, claiming it as "evidence" for their restoration vision. Tensions rise; the community is split.
- COST: A sudden storm threatens the festival and the unfinished scouring—risking the Horse's outline vanishing under grass.
- KEY: The crew, Edie, and the community work through the night to finish the scouring. Jakobus reads the made thing: the act is what holds the Horse, the hill, and the people together.

ACT III: FINALE ON THE CHALK
- The consultancy, seeing the community's resolve, withdraws—realising the act cannot be owned, only joined.
- The final scouring is completed at dawn. Edie, standing with the crew, names what the scouring is for: not remembering the past, but making a future—care that outlasts meaning.
- The crew departs, changed; the Horse remains, not because its meaning is known, but because the act of care continues.
- The wink: The original meaning is still lost. The book closes with the question—what else do we keep, when we cannot remember why?
- BACK MATTER: Detailed notes on the real Uffington White Horse, the OSL dating, the festival, and the tradition. Every cited date, trench, and practice is real; the unbroken chain is inferred, not proven. The meaning is not claimed; the tradition is alive.
