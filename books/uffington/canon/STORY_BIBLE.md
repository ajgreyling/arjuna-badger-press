A crew of neurodiverse, marginal, never-quite-belonging investigators travels to Uffington, England, home of the 3,000-year-old White Horse cut into the chalk. The official story is aired with respect: Bronze Age people carved the figure, and every generation since has maintained it, even as its meaning slipped away. But the crew digs into the real puzzle: how does a tradition of care persist, unbroken, when the reason for it is lost? The thrill is in the chase—a rival consultancy aims to "restore" the Horse to their own vision, while the crew, with the help of returning local Edie Vane, tries to read what the scouring is really for. The Why Files register is straight-faced, affectionate, and wry: the official story, the potato (how hard the "easy" is), the hole (the unresolved fact), the wink (the unanswered), and finally, the function. Never preach, never sneer, never confirm or deny the maybe. The intended reader is the curious skeptic and the wonderer both, those who crave the honest gap between certainty and awe. POV is close-third through the ND crew; they own the telling, but the local out-reads them at the turn. Content bounds: real place, real people, no mythos confirmed or denied, no "outsiders crack the code" triumphalism, dignity always. The creative driver: the puzzle of care outlasting meaning, the thrill of the chase, and the permission to say "I don't know."
