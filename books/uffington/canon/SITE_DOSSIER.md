Uffington White Horse — Oxfordshire, England

**REAL:**
- A stylised chalk hill-figure, ~110 metres long, carved on the upper slopes of White Horse Hill below Uffington Castle, Oxfordshire, England.
- Dated by Optically Stimulated Luminescence (OSL) — Palmer & Miles, Oxford Archaeological Unit, post-1990 dig: **~1380–550 BC** (Late Bronze Age / earliest Iron Age). This overturned older attributions to the Saxons or King Alfred.
- Constructed by cutting **trenches ~1 metre deep**, packed with crushed white chalk. This engineering prevents soil creep and maintains the figure's sharpness.
- The figure is not visible in full from the ground above; its form is legible only from the valley floor or from the air.
- If left untended, the figure becomes overgrown and disappears within approximately 20 years.
- **Continuous maintenance ("scouring")**: The White Horse has been re-chalked and re-cut by the local community every ~7–10 years for approximately **3,000 years**. This tradition is documented back to at least the Middle Ages and, by implication, likely stretches unbroken to the figure's creation.
- The **original meaning and purpose** of the figure have been lost for centuries; by the Middle Ages, local folklore already misidentified it as a dragon.
- During WWII, the figure was deliberately turfed over to prevent German bombers from using it as a navigational landmark. It was uncovered and restored after the war.
- The "Scouring of the White Horse" was a major local festival, documented in Thomas Hughes' *The Scouring of the White Horse* (1859) — a primary source for 19th-century practices.
- The site sits in a landscape rich with other prehistoric monuments, including Uffington Castle (an Iron Age hillfort) and Wayland's Smithy (a Neolithic long barrow).

**CLAIMED / LABELLED FRINGE:**
- Folklore claims: the figure is a dragon, not a horse; it gallops to Wayland's Smithy at night.
- These tales demonstrate that the *meaning* was lost even as the *practice* of scouring continued.
- No credible evidence for supernatural movement or direct continuity of original ritual meaning.

**NOTES:**
- OSL dating of buried quartz provides the hard science basis for the Bronze/Iron Age dating.
- The engineering (1-metre-deep trenches) is observable and measurable in the present day.
- The maintenance tradition is attested in written records from the Middle Ages onward; the true antiquity of the unbroken chain is inferred by the figure's survival and OSL dating.
