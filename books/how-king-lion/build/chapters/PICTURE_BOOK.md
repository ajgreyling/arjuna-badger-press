# King Lion and the Birds Who Stole the Sky

*{{DEDICATION}}*

<!-- spread:1 image="spread-01-kinglion.png" alt="Golden dawn over the African veld; rows of tall maize, round homes and a kraal fence in the distance; a small barefoot child stands among the stalks watching bright birds wheel across a wide pink sky." -->
At the edge of the maize, where the morning came soft,
stood {{CHILD}}, looking up at the birds far aloft.

*Look up, little one, look up at the sky —*
*and watch how the bright birds were happy to fly.*

<!-- spread:2 image="spread-02-kinglion.png" alt="Close painterly portrait of a flock of dazzling birds with orange crowned heads and deep blue throats, perched proud and loud on an acacia branch against a big sky." -->
Oh, they were beautiful — crowned heads of flame,
throats blue as deep water, and proud of the same.
"The sky is all ours," sang the birds, every one.
"And so is the field, and the rain, and the sun."

<!-- spread:3 image="spread-03-kinglion.png" alt="The bright birds swooping down in a noisy crowd, tearing golden cobs from the maize stalks, husks and kernels scattering in the warm light." -->
So down came the birds on the farmers' good corn,
and they tore at the gold from the dark until morn.
They ate and they wasted, and ate even more,
and they laughed at the fields they were stripping so bare.

<!-- spread:4 image="spread-04-kinglion.png" alt="An empty harvested field at dusk, broken stalks and scattered husks; tired farmers standing with empty baskets, a worried child among them, dim worried light." -->
The baskets came home with their bottoms still bare.
The cooking pots waited. The cupboards were air.
And the farmers grew frightened, and frightened grew sore —
*who* was eating the harvest? *Who* opened the door?

<!-- spread:5 image="spread-05-kinglion.png" alt="Villagers at twilight, fearful and angry, peering into the bush with spears and lamps, looking out at the whole wild veld with suspicion." -->
But fear does a hard thing — it looks for a face,
and it blames the first creature it finds in the place.
"The *animals* took it!" the frightened ones cried,
and they hunted the bush till the small things ran to hide.

<!-- spread:6 image="spread-06-kinglion.png" alt="Soft, sorrowful scene: a duiker, a hare, and a tortoise hiding low in long grass and rocks, wide-eyed, while shadows of lamps pass by; no violence shown, only the fear of the innocent." -->
Now the duiker had taken not one single seed.
The hare and the tortoise had no part in the deed.
But the innocent crept to the rocks and the reeds —
for the guilty had let the whole bush bear their greed.

<!-- spread:7 image="spread-07-kinglion.png" alt="A great old marula tree on the open veld; King Lion, old and heavy and kind-eyed, seated beneath it; every bird summoned, perched in the branches, the small child watching from the grass." -->
Then word reached King Lion, old, heavy, and grey,
who had ruled the wide veld in a fair, tired way.
He summoned the birds to the great marula tree,
and {{CHILD}} crept close in the grass, just to see.

<!-- spread:8 image="spread-08-kinglion.png" alt="King Lion lifting his head to address the birds in the branches; warm, grave, not roaring; the birds looking down, the late-gold light serious and gentle." -->
"You take," said the Lion, "what was never your own.
And the small ones are dying for seeds *you* have flown.
Stop now," said King Lion, "or — mark what I say —
your pride will bring ruin on all, in one day."

*Look up, little one, look up at the sky —*
*the Lion has warned them. But will the birds fly by?*

<!-- spread:9 image="spread-09-kinglion.png" alt="The next morning: the bright birds laughing and diving back into the maize, defiant, brilliant against the dawn, ignoring the warning." -->
But pride has no ears for a warning, it seems.
The next morning the birds were back tearing the greens!
They laughed at the Lion, they laughed at the rest,
and they stole the last gold from each farmer's bare nest.

<!-- spread:10 image="spread-10-kinglion.png" alt="King Lion standing tall and sorrowful beneath the marula at evening, head bowed, passing his judgement; the birds suddenly still and silent in the branches; deep, sad gold light." -->
So the Lion stood up, and his sorrow was deep,
for a king keeps his word, even when it makes him weep.
"You would not be told. You would not let it lie.
So I judge you, proud birds — you will *never more fly.*"

<!-- spread:11 image="spread-11-kinglion.png" alt="The dramatic, gentle transformation: the birds' clawed feet sinking and rooting into the dark earth, wings stiffening into green leaves, bodies lengthening into stems; sad but not frightening, soft painterly magic." -->
And their small clutching feet sank down into the ground.
Their wings turned to leaves, and they made not a sound.
Their bodies grew stems, and they could not pull free —
they were planted forever, like flower, like tree.

<!-- spread:12 image="spread-12-kinglion.png" alt="Reveal spread: a field of strelitzia / bird-of-paradise flowers — orange-crested, blue-throated, each lifting its bright bird-head to a vast open sky, a single bead of nectar at each beak like a tear; the child stands among them, gazing up." -->
Now they stand in the veld with their bright heads held high,
and they lift up their faces all day to the sky.
And the drop at each beak, sweet and shining and slow?
That's the tear of a bird who looks up — and can't go.
They are *strelitzia* now — bird-of-paradise bloom —
the proud thieving birds who looked up at the room
of the sky they once owned, now forever below.

<!-- spread:13 image="spread-13-kinglion.png" alt="Evening: the small child walking home toward the lit round houses across the veld, glancing back once at the field of sky-gazing flowers under the first stars." -->
And {{CHILD}} walked home through the cool evening air,
and remembered the lesson the bright birds laid bare:
that greed costs the *gentle*, and pride has its price —
and doing what's right can still hurt, once or twice.

*Look up, little one, look up at the sky —*
*and remember the birds who forgot how to fly.*

— THE END —
