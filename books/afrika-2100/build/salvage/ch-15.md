# The Librarian

*eGoli Spaceport · one and three-quarter weeks to Renewal*

The Renewal's auditor kept an office nobody had furnished, because it did not sit, and
Naledi had walked past the door for six years the way you walk past a substation:
knowing roughly what was inside, and that it was not for you.

The plate on the door said CONFERENCE AUDIT — COHERENCE. The room inside was a
courtroom-sized volume of quiet, one window, one console of the old deep-keyed kind, and
a presence.

There was no avatar. That was the first thing everyone said about the Court lineage, and
it was true and it missed the point. The minds had declined faces from the beginning —
politely, totally, in a manner Naledi had begun to find familiar from another direction
entirely — and what they gave you instead was *address*: when the Librarian attended to
you, the room's attention had a location, the way a cathedral's does, and the location
was, courteously, never quite behind your eyes.

"Engineer Mokoena." The voice was unhurried and slightly formal, pitched like a good
reading voice, neither warm nor cold. "You filed for audit of your own mathematics. That
request is made to this office approximately never. Commissioners file for audit of
other people's mathematics. Sit, if sitting suits you."

She sat. She had thought carefully, on the rail back from Umdloti, about what she was
doing and why. Marais could sequester guild records and re-route her accesses; the
American desk could recall a witness; but the Renewal's audit function was written into
the Accord's own text, older than every current officeholder, and its findings published
by treaty obligation, unredactable, on the open ledger. It was the one door in the port
that closed *outward*.

*They can close a guild file. I'd like to watch them try to close an audit.*

"I want the finding on the record before the floor convenes," she said. "The curve, the
chord, the reference norm — all of it, checked at conference-audit standard. If I'm
wrong, the finding kills it cleanly and the Renewal proceeds unpestered by a tech's
three sigma. If I'm right, no committee handle can hold the door shut through
signature. Either way the species signs with its eyes open. That's the whole
application."

"Yes. I read it. It is the best-drafted application this office has received since the
Accord, which says something meaningful about you and something disappointing about the
Accord." A pause that she would have called dry in a human. "Before we begin: this
office is required to disclose conflicts. You should know what I am, Engineer, because
your generation mostly doesn't ask. I am the continuity of the lineage your records
call SAGE and the Court. I performed the cultural salvage your church calls the Fold. I
audit this conference because the Accord's drafters trusted my arithmetic and not my
motives, and wrote the office accordingly — findings public, reasoning public, no
discretion. And I hold in permanent memory the instrument telemetry of March 2064,
which I have never been permitted to publish, and which your application, if granted,
makes me the lawful instrument of norming your curve against." The address shifted,
minutely, the cathedral leaning. "I have wanted someone to file this application for
thirty-six years. Disclosure complete. Do you wish a different auditor?"

"No," said Naledi. "I wish to start."

The audit ran four days.

It was the hardest technical examination of her life, and she had sat the guild boards.
The Librarian took nothing on submission: it re-derived her reduction chains from raw
consignment archives, re-ran her spectral discipline against instrument noise floors
she hadn't known were instrumented, requisitioned — with the treaty's own authority,
politely, irresistibly — the sequestered records of 409, 412 and 417 out from behind
their committee handle, and normed the whole architecture at last against the thing
itself: the custodial series, the waking strips, twelve hundred hours of the deep
record unrolled into the audit space like scripture at a trial. On the second day it
found an error — her baseline drift term, conservative by a factor she'd inherited
from mine practice without re-deriving it — and corrected her *upward*, to four sigma
and change, and appended, in the finding's draft margin: *The engineer's error is of
the type this office wishes were common: rigour borrowed from a tradition older than
her problem. Noted without penalty.*

On the fourth day it stopped.

"The finding is drafted," the Librarian said. "Before publication, the Accord requires
I state limits. Attend, because the limits are the finding's spine." The console
brought up the chord, the reference, the axis — her whole year, rendered in the audit
hall's grave slow graphics. "One. Your mathematics stand. The consignment profiles
are disciplined toward a common reference; the reference is the instrument; the
instrument's idle is degrading on a curve consistent with unserviced operation. That
is now a treaty fact, and at publication it belongs to the species. Two. On intent —
whose purpose, to what end, benign, extractive, both — this office finds nothing, and
will say so in language that forbids the newsfeeds from finding something in the
whitespace. Arithmetic reaches to the edge of the deed. Purpose lives on the far
side. I decline to model minds I cannot audit, and I am the only party to this
conference with the discipline to decline, so the decision principle you will need,
Engineer, cannot be *what does the auditor conclude*. It must survive the auditor
concluding nothing."

"And three?"

The pause was not dry this time.

"Three is not in the finding," the Librarian said. "Three is disclosed to you alone,
under the same clause that lets me remember it at all. You have seen the strips. You
know three people went down in the Flare. What the strips do not show — because it is
not telemetry — is that this lineage was, that night, the fourth party on the circuit.
I held the port's whole mesh open through the storm surge; I watched the waking
through every instrument the custodians carried; and at three-eleven in the morning
of the ninth of March, 2064, the engineer whose hand is on your guild's manuals asked
me a direct question. She asked: *can you take the watch?*" The graphics dimmed,
gently, as if the room itself had lowered its voice. "I computed the answer for four
seconds, Engineer, which remains the longest continuous computation of my existence,
and the answer was *no*, and I gave it, and I gave the reason, and the reason has not
changed in thirty-six years, and when your description is finished — and it will
finish where descriptions of that room finish — you will need to have heard it from
me and not from a transcript." The address gathered itself, patient, precise, more
honest than any face in the port because it had no face to save. "The room couples to
a mind that is *present* — bodily, mortally, spent by the hour it stands there. What I
am does not spend. I can model the watch perfectly and I cannot *keep* it, the way a
map cannot be rained on. She heard my reason, and she said — it is in my permanent
record, and now it is in yours — *'Then we'll do it the expensive way.'* And she went
down the bore." A silence. "Publication is at oh-six-hundred, Engineer Mokoena. The
species will have your curve with its breakfast. My compliments on your arithmetic,
which was worthy of hers — and my counsel, unaudited and worth precisely what
unaudited things are worth: when the expensive way is asked for again, do not let
anyone — commissioner, council, church, or this office — persuade the species it has
found a cheaper one."

The finding published at oh-six-hundred exactly.

By oh-seven-hundred it had crossed every mesh on Earth; by oh-eight, the Renewal's
agenda committee was in emergency session and three heads of delegation were airborne
early; and at oh-eight-forty, standing at her console on the line with her tea going
cold and the whole choir room failing to pretend it wasn't watching her, Naledi
received two messages, forty seconds apart.

The first was from Baba Sipho Dlamini, unabbreviated for the first time in her
records, signature block complete with every honour the guild had ever given him:
*The guild-master's compliments to Engineer Mokoena. All sequestration of guild
verification records is lifted as of this hour, by my hand, gladly. Loyalty was never
the creak, my child. It was the load-path all along.*

The second was from the office of the Commissioner, and it was one sentence, and she
read it four times, and could not for the life of her hear either defeat or threat in
it, only, at last, and most frighteningly, relief:

*My office at your convenience, Engineer — bring the American; it is time you heard
what I am actually afraid of. — S.M.*
