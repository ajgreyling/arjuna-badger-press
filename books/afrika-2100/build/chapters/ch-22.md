# Chamber One, eGoli · Renewal day

The liaison bench was put in during the 2074 fit-out and nobody has ever sat in it. It is a low steel form with a rail, bolted to the terrazzo below the rostrum, and the ushers use it for water crates. At 07:15 the ushers moved the water crates.

Naledi got in at half six through the goods door because the concourse was already solid, and she sat in the guild block with a brass slide-counter in her jacket pocket for no reason she could defend. The pocket was the wrong shape for it. It knocked her ribs whenever she leaned forward.

Item 1 was ratification of the previous minute. Item 2 was the Chair’s ruling on Annex C as substantive. Item 3 was a question to the counterparty, tabled Wednesday, seconded by four delegations and the host, and the order paper printed it in full because Adv. Sithole had insisted the wording be fixed in advance and not improved from the floor.

The Chair read it out at 09:04.

*The Accord parties ask the counterparty, at the counterparty’s convenience and without prejudice to the schedule: what does the counterparty require of Earth, and what will it cost Earth to provide it well?*

Then she said: “Liaison Sela is present and has indicated the counterparty will respond on the record.”

---

Sela came up the centre aisle from the west doors and not from the berth tunnel, which meant walking the whole length of the floor with six hundred and ten people watching, and did it at the same pace throughout. Two of them. The second one carried nothing and stood behind the bench at the rail and did not sit for the next fifty minutes.

Sela is a little over two metres and stands very straight because the vertebral column does not curve like a human’s, and the hands have four digits with the same nail plate on each, and the skin at the wrist where the sleeve ends is grey-brown and dry-looking, like the outside of a river stone. That is the whole of it. Naledi had seen it a hundred times on the berth line and had never once been able to stop cataloguing it.

Sela sat, and put a folded sheet on the rail, and did not unfold it.

“The question is in two parts,” Sela said. Protocol English, the flat vowels the liaison school teaches, a voice pitched to carry without effort. “The counterparty will answer both. The counterparty asks the Chair to note the time, because the second part has a length.”

“Noted. 09:07.”

“Part one. What is required of Earth.”

Sela put one hand flat on the rail.

“Reference pitch. A node of the network is in service on this planet. It has been in service, without maintenance, for a period this counterparty states as not less than one hundred and eighty thousand of your years. Its calibration is the oldest calibration in the network still held at source. It is now out of tolerance. The requirement is that the calibration be held true at source, which means it must be held by hands on this ground, and that reference be issued outward on a schedule, in the medium, which is worked gold at nine-nine-nine-nine-nine fine, formed to the tolerances in Annex A and barred on the ninth digit.”

The Chinese principal, Hou, had his pencil down. Nobody wrote.

“Quantity for maintenance of this node alone: sixty-one to sixty-eight tonnes per cycle, depending on wear. The remainder of the four hundred and sixty is carried outward. The counterparty has taken it outward for twenty-seven years. The counterparty does not apologise for the arrangement and does not defend it. It was the arrangement Earth would sign.”

“Liaison,” the Chair said, “the floor will want the second part.”

“Part two is the cost,” Sela said. “The counterparty will state it in six items. The counterparty asks that the floor not receive any item as a threat, because none of them is one, and the counterparty has no leverage on any of them.”

Somebody in the third block laughed once, high and surprised, and was quiet.

“One. A watch. Present, conscious, on the ground, continuously, without interruption of longer than the interval in Annex C, and forever. Not a monitoring installation. The node couples to residence. It does not couple to instruments, and it does not couple to us.”

Naledi felt the floor go still in a particular way she had felt exactly once before, in a stope, when a rockfall two levels down had made every man on the crew stop chewing at the same time.

“Two. The end of certain kinds of privacy on this planet. A held calibration is a public number or it is not a calibration. Your ledger is adequate. The counterparty notes that this cost is the one your delegations have been least willing to price.

“Three. Institutional continuity across intervals longer than your institutions have ever held. Your Accord is twenty-seven years old and this is its second renewal and the second renewal has been difficult. The interval that matters is not twenty-seven years.

“Four. The loss of the option to be wrong quietly. A calibration held at source, wrongly, is worse than a calibration lost. If this planet takes the work and does it badly and does not say so, the network will issue from an error for as long as the error stands, and the counterparty will not be able to see it from where the counterparty is. The counterparty asks the floor to hold that sentence for a moment before the next item, because it is the item the counterparty is most concerned that the floor understand.”

The Chair said nothing. Sela waited eight or nine seconds, which in that room was a very long time, and the fence noise came through the chocked upper doors, and then Sela went on.

“Five. Persons. The watch will be stood by named individuals whose lives will be organised around it, and this counterparty’s records indicate that on your planet such persons are made into something else by the populations they serve. This is a cost. The counterparty is not able to advise on it. The counterparty’s own arrangements do not have this failure mode and we are not competent in yours.

“Six. The knowledge that you did not build it and cannot yet repair it. Only hold it. The counterparty has no schedule for when that changes and does not expect it within the lifetime of anyone in this chamber.”

Sela’s hand left the rail.

“That is the cost. The counterparty holds the position that it is a fair cost and that the requirement is legitimate, and the counterparty notes that a fair cost is still a cost and that Earth may decline. If Earth declines, the counterparty will continue to purchase worked gold under the existing schedule for as long as the medium holds usable pitch, which our estimate puts at between forty and ninety years, and then this counterparty will stop coming.”

“And the node?” the Chair said.

“Will drift. Then idle roughly. Then not.”

“And the network?”

“Will hold the second-oldest calibration at source,” Sela said, “which is not this one.”

---

The Chair called the floor at 09:31 and it went the way a farm meeting goes when the price is finally spoken: slowly, and about the wrong things first, and then very fast about the right one.

The Belgian asked about indexation. Someone from the Nigerian mission asked, twice, and correctly, whether the sixty-one tonnes was consumption or circulation, and got *consumption, the medium fatigues,* which cost the room ten minutes of arithmetic. A woman from the Indian delegation stood and asked whether the counterparty had made the same request of any other party on this planet at any time in twenty-nine years, and Sela said no, and she said thank you, and sat, and the Chair had to ask the gallery for quiet.

Then the third block, and the third block had the question everyone outside the fence had come six thousand kilometres to hear.

The delegate was a man from a Gauteng constituency seat named Ndlovu who is not Abokuqala and has not once said he is. He stood with his hands behind his back.

“Liaison. You said the node couples to residence and not to you. There are four thousand people at the eastern gate today who hold that this ground’s hands means this ground’s blood. They hold that your people chose Africa and chose the Firstborn. I am asked to put it to you plainly, and I will: does the counterparty hold that any human ancestry is nearer to this work than another?”

“No.”

“Will the counterparty elaborate.”

“The counterparty will not rank human genetics.” Sela had not moved. “That refusal is stated at protocol level and the counterparty does not vary it. The counterparty will state a mechanical fact, which is within the Accord’s disclosure schedule, and the Chair may record that the counterparty offers it and was not required to. The coupling assessment is a function of residence: mass of the ground-made body, duration of habit on the same ground, spent presence. In 2064 the node took eleven persons. The counterparty holds their assessments. The variable that explains the eleven is haulage years. There is no ancestry term in the function. There is not a term to remove.”

“The movement will say your silence is agreement.”

“The counterparty is aware that its silences are read,” Sela said. “The counterparty declines to become an argument between humans about humans. The counterparty notes that this position has cost the counterparty goodwill in every decade of contact and the counterparty maintains it.”

Ndlovu stood there a second. Then he said, “For the record, I asked and I received an answer,” and sat down, and outside the doors the drums did not stop, because nothing on the floor reaches the fence for an hour and by then it has been cut into three pieces.

---

It was the young Steward who asked the last one, from the observers’ rail, and he had to be recognised twice because the Chair did not see him.

“Tefo Radebe, custodian order of the deep workings. Liaison. Why did you not ask us this in 2073?”

“The counterparty asked in 2073,” Sela said. “The request is in the negotiating record of the eGoli Accord, seventh session, as a proposed volume three. It was withdrawn by the counterparty on the eleventh of March 2073 at the request of the then delegations, who advised that no party could sign a permanent obligation and that the Accord would fail with it in. The counterparty accepted that advice. The counterparty has held the request since.”

“Twenty-seven years.”

“Twenty-seven years.”

“Why not raise it again?”

“Because the counterparty was told, by parties competent to say so, that it must be asked and not offered,” Sela said. “The counterparty does not fully hold your reason for that. The counterparty has kept to it.”

And Sela reached out and unfolded the sheet on the rail, one fold, then the other, and turned it around so it faced the floor, and it was the amendment text of the withdrawn volume three, typed on a 2073 form, with the withdrawal stamp across it and a date in a human hand, and the paper had gone the colour of weak tea along the fold lines from being carried.

That was all. Sela did not read from it and did not explain it and folded it again and put it in a sleeve pocket.

Naledi had her hand flat on her sternum and did not remember putting it there.

## Gallery, north end, Chamber One · Renewal day

They let Ruth out of quarters at eight for the floor, because Hargrove had put in for it himself and had not told her he had.

She was out of uniform. That was the condition and it was a fair one. Brown trousers, grey shirt, no cover, and a warrant officer three seats down who had been told to keep her in view and who at 09:40 had stopped pretending to.

She had the whole of it from a bad angle again: the top of Sela’s head, the second one standing at the rail the entire time without shifting weight, the Chair’s hands.

She had come up expecting to be angry somewhere in it. What she got instead, at about the fourth item, was a thing she recognised so completely that her eyes stung and she had to look at the ceiling girders for a while.

*The loss of the option to be wrong quietly.*

In 2081 she carried an order down to the Susquehanna and read it out and eleven people died in the spring, and the Army wrote it up as weather. It was not weather. Nobody said so for four years because saying so was expensive and because the men who knew better had been polite.

Somebody up here on the ground has to hold a number true and say when they have got it wrong, forever, out loud, and nobody above them can see the error from where they are.

That is the whole job. She has done small versions of it in a granary in Chester County with a moisture meter and a book, and she knows exactly two things about it: it is dull, and it is the only kind of honesty that scales.

She thought: *they waited twenty-seven years to be asked.*

Then she thought, and it was not a generous thought and she let herself have it: *or they waited because being asked is worth more than being agreed with, and they know that, and they are a keeping species and they keep that too.*

Both could be true. She has sat under enough preaching to know that a man can mean every word and still be running a collection.

The old man beside her was a Fold assembly clerk from Antwerp with the coin on a cord and he was crying without any fuss at all, and when Sela said item five, *persons, made into something else by the populations they serve,* he said, in English, to nobody: “Ja. Ja, that one is ours.”

Ruth put her hand in her pocket and turned the coin with her thumb.

Not payment. Not offering. Not surrender. Rent on your own house — and this morning the landlord had come up the middle aisle in front of everybody and read out what the roof would cost to keep, and had not sanded a single figure, and had brought a piece of paper with a fold worn through it.

She would say it that way at home. Miriam would want *be kind* and Miriam could have it in the same breath; there was room for both sentences in this and there had not been room in the Registry. She could tell Bishop Zook the load was honest and now say what it was rent on.

Below her, the Chair adjourned to eleven for the drafting group, and six hundred and ten people stood up at once, and the sound of it was like grain going into a bin.

Ruth stayed sitting. The warrant officer waited.

Down on the floor Sela stood, and the second one stepped back from the rail exactly one pace to let Sela out, and the two of them went up the centre aisle again at the same pace they had come down, through a room full of standing people, and nobody put a hand out and nobody spoke to them, and the water crates went back onto the bench before they had reached the doors.
