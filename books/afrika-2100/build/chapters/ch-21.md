# Berth 7C and the Ledger Registry, eGoli · six days to Renewal

The order came Tuesday at 06:40, hard copy, which is how the Army does anything it means.

Ruth read it standing up at the berth office counter with her tea going cold. Two paragraphs. She was relieved as quartermaster of the consignment effective on receipt, the vault authority passing to the defence attaché’s office; she was to be on the Thursday lift out of eGoli; and she was directed, in the second paragraph, to withdraw her witness signature from ledger post 2100-10-26-0411 on the grounds that the signature had been given without mission authorisation.

Nobody had signed it who had ever loaded a wagon. That was not a fair thought and she had it anyway.

Sergeant Beiler read it after her. He read slowly, the way he reads everything, moving his thumb down the page.

“The third one isn’t logistics,” he said.

“No.”

“They can’t order you to unsay a thing.”

“They can order me to say a different thing and record it.” She folded the sheet in three and put it in her breast pocket. “Get the inventory book. If I’m handing over I’m handing over properly.”

---

They did the handover from half past seven until noon, which is how long two hundred and forty-one crates take when you count them and do not merely believe in them.

Bond Vault Three runs under the west end of the chamber block, cut into the old reef rock, cool as a springhouse. Ruth walked the aisles with the book and Beiler called the numbers and a South African vault officer named Mpho signed each rank as they cleared it. Eleven barred crates in the third aisle, the seals intact, the ninth digit on each callout exactly what the manifest said it was. She had checked that digit every week since Norfolk. She checked it again.

Four hundred and nine kilos. Wagon, rail, ship, rail. Two men hurt at Wilmington and one horse put down at Front Royal on a hill she had chosen herself.

At eleven the attaché came down. Lieutenant Colonel Hargrove, forty-five, clean, decent enough, a man whose whole job this year was to make America look unembarrassing in a country that no longer needed it.

“You’re doing this thoroughly,” he said.

“It’s the job I’ve got until noon.”

He waited while she finished a rank. Then: “The Registry closes at four. Go at two, say it in six words, get on the plane, and in eighteen months you have a majority and this is a paragraph in a file nobody opens. Ruth, I am telling you what I would want somebody to tell me.”

“I know you are.”

“You witnessed a South African engineer’s family document at four in the morning on foreign soil, in uniform, in a week when the President’s people cannot get four hundred and sixty tonnes agreed.”

“The document’s true.”

“That isn’t the objection.”

“I know,” she said. “That’s the part that’s giving me trouble.”

He looked at the crates a while. “The mission’s position is that we don’t take sides in a host-nation quarrel.”

“We’re not the side. We’re the ones paying rent.” She closed the book on her finger. “Sir, I’ll be at the Registry at two. I’ll be in uniform. You should know before I go what I’m going to say, so you can be somewhere else.”

---

She ate at the berth canteen because you eat. Beans, pap, a slice of the sweet bread they do on Tuesdays.

There was a message on her mesh from Lancaster, sent Monday evening their time, from Bishop Zook’s daughter Miriam, who keeps the assembly ledger now that his hands shake.

*Ruth. Your name was read out on Sunday, from the Johannesburg post, twice, and there was gladness and there was argument after. Some are saying the assembly should not be attached to a treaty quarrel. Some are proud. Dat’s wie it is.*

*One thing. If you speak, will you say Be kind and leave it there. The full line in a newspaper does us no good with people who are looking for a reason to think we are coarse. Father says he blessed the load, not the politics. He also says he would like to hear you say it his way, once, so he knows you still know it.*

Ruth read it twice, and then a third time for what it cost Miriam to write it, which was something.

She put her hand in her pocket and turned the coin over with her thumb, which she has done so many times the milling is gone smooth on one edge. Ohio, 2071. A man named Yoder who cut dies for horse brasses before the Flare and did not soften anything he cut. Both sentences all the way round the rim, the ugly word deepest because he pushed hardest on the downstroke.

Sunday softens it. She has known that since she was fifteen, standing in a barn in Intercourse with three hundred people saying two words where there had been six, and it is not a lie, and it is not the whole coin. Her grandmother’s Bible has a psalm with a page corner turned down at *he that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not*, and her grandmother was not a subtle woman.

Her brother stayed. He is buried at Weaverland and she has stood there twice. In 2081 the Army’s plan for the Susquehanna zone put the winter seed on the wrong side of the water and she carried the order down and read it out to men who knew better and were polite about it, and eleven people died that spring who would not have. She has had twenty years to learn what muscle owes knowledge. That is the whole of what she knows about this, and it is not a speech, so she will not make one.

She answered Miriam: *Received. I’ll say it the way it is. Tell your father the load was honest. R.*

---

The Ledger Registry is a room on the second floor of the annexe with eight terminals, a public gallery of nine chairs, and a Registrar named Adv. Nomsa Sithole who has done this since 2082 and looks like she would rather be doing it in a smaller room.

There were more than nine people. Press had heard, and delegates’ clerks, and two Stewards standing at the back in working jackets, and up on the end chair the Chilean who had read Annex C. Naledi Mokoena was not there. Ruth had asked her not to come, and she had understood, and that was that.

“Captain,” the Registrar said. “You are here on post 2100-10-26-0411, witness signature, item two of three.”

“Yes, ma’am.”

“Your mission has filed a request that the witness signature be withdrawn as unauthorised. Procedure is that the witness attends in person and states withdrawal, or refusal, with grounds. Grounds go on the record verbatim. Do you understand the record is public and permanent.”

“I do.”

“Then state your business.”

Ruth stood square, hands at her sides, the way you stand for a reading.

“I refuse to withdraw. The signature stands.”

The Registrar’s stylus did not stop. “Grounds.”

“Two. First, the technical one. I stood at that counter in the sealed length with a brass instrument in my hand and I heard the beats and I counted them with Engineer Mokoena and our counts agreed inside one. I witnessed what I saw. If I take my name off it, the number is unchanged and I am a liar. So the Army would be buying nothing.”

“Recorded. Second.”

“Second is Fold grounds, and I’d ask you to write it out.”

“That’s what verbatim means.”

“Yes, ma’am.” She heard somebody in the gallery shift. She kept her eyes on the Registrar’s shoulder, which is where you look. “I was ordered home. I’m refusing the order and I want it on the ledger that I’m refusing it as a believer, not as a rebel, because I have no quarrel with my Army and it will do what it has to with me.

“The ethic is two sentences. Everybody in this building knows the second one. It’s on the coins and it’s on the arches and we say it at assembly and it’s a good sentence. *Be kind.* The first one is: *Don’t be a cunt.*

“The first one is about this.”

Nobody laughed. She had thought somebody might, and had decided in advance she would wait it out, and did not need to.

“It’s the harder sentence and that’s why it gets left off. Kindness you can do standing still. The first one is a prohibition, and a prohibition costs you something the day it applies. It applies today. Withdrawing a true signature to make my mission’s week easier, and letting a woman stand alone under a thing I put my name to, and going home Thursday and telling people at home I was there when I was there and did that. That is the conduct the first sentence names. There isn’t a softer word for it that stays honest, and I am not going to hunt for one in front of a Registrar.”

The Registrar wrote. It took her a moment, and she wrote it all.

“Anything further?”

“One correction for the file. My own consignment. I have asked three times whether it was payment, offering, or surrender, and I had it wrong three times. It’s rent on a machine that’s ours. I’ll tell them that at home and it will not go down well.” She paused. “That’s not for the ledger. That’s for you, because you asked me on the ship what I thought I was carrying and I gave you a poor answer.”

“It is on the ledger now,” Adv. Sithole said. “Sign here, and here. Refusal to withdraw, witness present, grounds stated.” She turned the pad around. “Captain. In eighteen years nobody has spoken that sentence in this room. I want you to know I have heard it plenty. Just not in here.”

“It doesn’t go well in rooms.”

“No.”

---

Hargrove was waiting on the landing with a warrant officer and a clipboard, which was correct of him. He had not been somewhere else. She gave him that.

“You’re relieved and confined to quarters pending disposition,” he said. “I’m to take your authority card, your vault keys, and your side arm.”

She had them ready on top of the inventory book. Card, two keys on a ring with a lead seal, the pistol cleared and the magazine separate. He signed her receipt and she checked what he wrote, because a handover you don’t read is a handover you didn’t do.

“The book’s current to noon,” she said. “Aisle three seals intact, ninth digit verified this morning, and Mpho has the cold-side log. Beiler knows the crate that’s out of square. Don’t let them re-stack aisle one to make it look tidy for the cameras. It’s on timber that’s older than it looks.”

“Ruth.”

“Sir.”

“For what it’s worth, that was not a mess.”

“It was a bit of one,” she said. “Is there water on the north concourse tonight?”

He blinked. “I’ve no idea.”

“Somebody should see about it,” Ruth said, and went down to find her quarters.
