# Chapter brief — First Contact (2071)

> Feeds **O-01** (chapter map). Brief only — no prose here.
> **This brief deliberately breaks two locked rules.** Author-authorised 2026-08-13: *"rules can
> be broken if needed."* The breaks are named, costed and bounded below — a rule broken on
> purpose and written down is canon; a rule broken quietly is drift.

## Rules broken, and what each buys

| Rule | What it says | Broken how | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer H gate** (`TIMELINE.md`) | No Layer H fragment longer than a page; always shoulder a present beat | One full chapter set in **2071**, no 2100 frame | First contact becomes an *event the reader attends*, not a document they're told about |
| **C-11 · POV** | Naledi primary; Ruth secondary; Priya in scene never POV | One chapter in **Gogo Dineo Mokoena's** POV | The novel's central silence gets a face before it becomes a mystery |

**Bounded:** exactly one chapter, one exception each. Priya stays non-POV even here (C-11 holds
where it matters). The rest of the book keeps both rules. If a second Layer H chapter is ever
proposed, this brief is the precedent to argue against, not for.

## Why Gogo Dineo, and not Naledi-reading-a-record

The safe version was a 2100 scene in which the 2071 transcript is read. It respects every gate
and it is worse. First contact experienced as a document is first contact at one remove — the
reader is told the room failed rather than sitting in it.

**Gogo Dineo Mokoena is the right eyes** because she is the book's unexploded ordnance: the
grandmother who left something off the Accord's map and died in 2098 with the silence intact
(C-08). She was *there*. Putting the reader beside her at the landing means:

- The Act I itch (Naledi worrying at a family silence) has a face and a room behind it from the
  first pages, instead of arriving as backstory.
- The reader sees **what she saw** — and, critically, *not* what she withheld. She makes a
  decision on the page whose content is not shown. That is the honest way to open a mystery:
  the reader knows a choice happened and not what it was.
- Her death in 2098, two years before the story, becomes a loss the reader has actually
  sustained rather than a canon fact.

## Placement — prologue position, before Act I

Set before the 2100 clock starts. This is the one structural cost worth paying: it front-loads
one flashback so the rest of the novel never has to stop for one.

**It must not spoil C-07.** The chapter shows the landing and the omission-in-progress; it does
not show the deep workings, the node, or what the gold is doing.

## What the chapter carries — LOCKED to canon

**2071, Witwatersrand. The open landing.** Grounded, daylight, no invasion (C-10). A craft
visible on approach since 2066 finishes arriving in front of everyone.

1. **They were already coming.** Diri's tapes date the departure to **before the 2064 Flare**
   (C-06) — the waking-note answered a journey already begun. Everyone in that room believes
   the note summoned them. The reader is given the arithmetic and no one on the page does it.
   *The false key, planted at the front of the book.*
2. **What humanity asked.** Hours of it: intentions, weapons, origin, are we alone, is there a
   god, what do you want. Answered at protocol level or declined (Rule 9). Exhaustive, and not.
3. **What nobody asked.** *What do you need from us, and what would it cost us to give it well?*
   The climax line — *asked for the first time in twenty-nine years* — is now a debt the reader
   watched being incurred.
4. **Gogo's omission.** She is survey. She records the ground truthfully and leaves one thing
   off. The reader sees her decide, sees her hand stop, and does not see what it was about.

**The Anunnaki name** is applied by delegates inside the room, repeatedly, reaching for the
oldest word they have. The visitors neither accept nor correct it. Narration never adopts it
(Rule 1, C-17).

## Hard constraints — these do NOT bend

- **No visitor POV, no interiority, no telepathy.** Protocol and consequence only. Breaking the
  Layer H and POV gates does not license breaking Rule 9; that one is load-bearing for C-13.
- No twist reveal, no gotcha. Flat and loaded — Bouwer-discipline. The chapter's biggest fact is
  an absence and must not be italicised.
- The visitors' 2071 answers resolve nothing the climax needs.
- Gogo is competent and unmystical. She is a surveyor at work, not an oracle.

## The record still gets used — later, and cheaper

The transcript-read beat survives as a **short Act II/III scene**, not a chapter: Naledi meets
the founding document as evidence rather than liturgy, and either she or the Librarian does the
arithmetic on Diri's dates. The prologue plants the false key; this pays it. Receipt-faith,
saint-hunger, Stewards and Marais each read the same transcript differently — each gets its best
argument (C-13).
