**The story this ledger is trying to tell:** a tuning engineer who loves her guild cannot un-see
a discrepancy, and an Amish-raised captain carrying her country's gold cannot un-hear what her
own coin says when she is alone. Neither is trying to save the world. Both are asked, by people
they respect, to do the easy wrong. The book is the cost of refusing — and the discovery that
the question everyone has been arguing for twenty-nine years is not the question.

**Write to the beat, not to a length.** Each chapter runs as long as its beat needs and stops.
Some are a few pages; some are the longest in the book. The ▽ marks the ones that should feel
short on the page — not a word budget, a *tempo*. Anything that reads padded to hit a size is
worse than a short chapter.

**POV:** Naledi primary, Ruth secondary, **Gogo in the prologue only** (C-18). Priya in scene,
never POV (C-11). No visitor POV, ever (Rule 9).

## Drafting law

1. **Story first; the scanners come last.** `tools/prose_tics.py` and `tools/semantic_dupes.py`
   are free and local, and they are for *after* a draft exists — a late hygiene pass, never a
   drafting target. Writing to satisfy a counter produces exactly the flat, even prose the
   counters were built to catch. Draft badly and fix, rather than draft to a metric.
2. **No chapter opens with weather or a cold mystery box.** Competence and itch (settled craft).
3. **Every weather gets its best argument** — Give, Keep, Understand-first, saint-hunger,
   receipt-faith, Abokuqala. No cartoons (C-13).
4. **Layer H fragments stay under a page** everywhere except ch-00, which is the one authorised
   exception (C-18).
5. **Bouwer-discipline** on Ruth's brother, Gogo's silence, the hunger-year wrong order: flat,
   loaded, no gotcha.
6. Narration never adopts the mythic names; characters do (Rule 1, C-17).
