# The Visitors — who they are, exactly

> Closes the "who are the aliens" question for AFRIKA 2100. Binding where it says LOCKED.
> Governed by MYTHOS_RULES Rule 1 (mythic naming), Rule 2 (gold), Rule 9 (visitors never
> endorse), and CANON_CHOICES C-06 / C-07 / C-13. Nothing here opens visitor POV.

## The one-line answer

**They are the Builders' heirs: a keeping species, come back to service a machine they
themselves can no longer couple to — and the tradition that called them Anunnaki was pointing
at something real.**

## What they are — LOCKED

1. **Heirs, not ancients.** The Builders are gone. These are their inheritors, continuous with
   the tradition the way a maintenance company is continuous with a founder. *Characters know
   this distinction and argue about it.* (Rule 1, carried from RELIC.)

2. **A keeping species.** Their whole civic grammar is custody: things are kept, watches are
   stood, records are held. This is why the Renewal reads to them as a maintenance contract and
   to Earth as a treaty — the mismatch is not deception, it is species-level idiom.

3. **They tried to stand the watch themselves, and were refused.** The deep finding
   (CHAPTER_LEDGER historical note, C-07-consistent): the room couples to **residence** — the
   ground-made body, the deep habit, the spent presence — not to presence, not to genome. The
   visitors have presence. They do not have residence. The machine would not take them.
   **"They were never here for the gold"** (Gogo Dineo's letter). They are here because the
   only hands that can hold the instrument are hands made by this ground.

4. **What they want is therefore honest and sad:** worked gold as reference pitch, and — under
   the volume amendment — a maintenance handover to the only species that can do the work.
   They are a counterparty asking Earth to take its own machine back.

5. **What they call themselves: nothing the page ever hears.** Protocol designation only.
   Asked directly, they answer at protocol level or decline. (Rule 9 — never endorse, never
   deny beyond protocol, never open.)

## The Anunnaki question — LOCKED as naming, not as fact

**Humanity names them. They do not confirm the name.**

- The oldest name to hand is the Sumerian one, and the fit is uncanny: a people who came for
  gold, to mend a dying world. The **repair manifest echoes the Enki tradition almost
  line-for-line**, and characters who know the texts notice on the page.
- So the traditions get their eerie vindication — *the belief pointed at something* — without
  the novel ever asserting the ancient-astronaut claim as history. Narration never adopts the
  mythic names. Characters do, constantly, and disagree about it. (Rule 1.)
- **This is the same grammar as saint-hunger around G in 2035:** a century that needs a face
  puts a crown on something that refuses one. The Anunnaki name is a crown. The visitors decline
  it the way G declined his — by not answering.

### Why the novel must NOT make them literally the returned Anunnaki

Three reasons, all structural rather than squeamish:

1. **It arms the Firstborn.** Abokuqala's heresy (C-13, Rule 9) holds that the visitors deem
   unbroken African bloodlines sacred. The book refutes this with **physics, not sermon**:
   coupling tracks lived ground, not ancestry — witness Priya Ellis, and witness the room
   refusing the visitors themselves. The Anunnaki frame is *inherently* a creation-and-bloodline
   frame. Make it literal and the movement's central claim acquires in-world support, the
   refutation collapses, and the novel accidentally endorses a racial theology it was built to
   dismantle.
2. **It imports a real-world libel.** The ancient-astronaut literature around this material
   carries the Great Zimbabwe claim — that African monuments were built by someone other than
   Africans. A novel set in Africa, whose central heresy is about African blood and alien
   sanction, cannot name its aliens the Anunnaki as fact without walking into that. The estate's
   own anunnaki compendium flags exactly this hazard and handles fringe authors as
   subjects-under-glass, never sources. Same discipline here.
3. **It would demand backstory the book has refused.** "No visitor POV, no interiority" is a
   locked beat. Naming them as a known mythic people invites origin, motive, and history the
   novel has decided to withhold — and the withholding is what makes them a counterparty rather
   than a cast member.

**Net:** every reader who wants them to be the Anunnaki gets to have that, earned, from inside
the text. No reader is told it by the narrator. That is strictly more powerful than the assertion
would have been, and it is the only version that survives the book's own ethics.

## On the page — how they appear

Protocol, manifests, liaisons, berthing, consequences. Physical description spare and factual.
No POV, no interiority, no telepathy-with-the-reader. They wait like a freight company.

**The one moment they must answer:** at the consent climax they are *asked*, for the first time
in twenty-nine years, a question they must answer or refuse in front of everyone. Whatever they
say there is the only unmediated thing the reader ever gets from them — and it must not resolve
the Anunnaki question either way.
