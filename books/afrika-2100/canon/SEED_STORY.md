# AFRIKA 2100 — Seed Story

*The spiritual fourth of the* African Gold *trilogy. Not book IV of a tetralogy — a standalone
novel set seventy years downstream of RELIC, in the world the trilogy's choice built. The
trilogy asked* who may switch it on. *This book asks the question that comes after:* who may
give it away. *Register: grounded-epic SF — the Crichton-credible discipline of the trilogy
carried into an era where the designated mysteries have stepped into the light.*

---

## One breath

The year is 2100. Thirty-six years ago a Carrington-class solar flare burned the electrified
North down to candlelight, and the world reorganised around the places and the people who could
live without the grid: the US Army partnered with the Amish to feed a continent, and the whole
of America now prays on Sunday to a folded faith whose entire scripture fits on a coin — *Be
kind.* South Africa, spared by geomagnetic luck and carried by the copyleft engineering stack of
a long-dead Boer polymath the world now reveres the way it once revered Einstein, has become the
indispensable nation: the platinum batteries, the mesh that never fell, the gold — and the
spaceport. Because when the ancient instrument beneath Africa was sung awake in the worst hour
of the Flare, someone very far away heard the note. The Builders' heirs have come back, in the
open, politely, with manifests. They are here for the gold. And a young tuning engineer at eGoli
Spaceport discovers what the gold is *for* — and that the answer decides whether handing it over
is tribute, trade, or treason against every generation that kept it asleep.

## High concept

> *The trilogy's world, seventy years on: first contact as a logistics problem, faith as an
> engineering artifact, and a second planetary consent question — asked this time with the other
> party standing in the room.*

It reads like grounded future-history — *The Martian*'s procedural credibility applied to a
post-flare civilisation — and underneath it is the African Gold engine unchanged: every miracle
has a mechanism, gold is a resonance medium and not treasure, and the climax is a choice about
consent, not a battle.

## The world in five facts

1. **The Flare (2064).** A Carrington-class geomagnetic storm collapsed the high-latitude grids.
   The North burned a decade rebuilding. Low geomagnetic latitude spared the southern belt —
   South Africa foremost, with parts of South America, Australasia, and equatorial Africa.
2. **The Plain Compact.** The US Army partnered with the Amish and Old Order communities —
   the Army brought perimeter security, logistics, muscle, hands, and planning; the Plain people
   brought the land-craft and the working knowledge of a life that never needed the grid.
   Together they fed the continent. The Compact reshaped America's soul.
3. **The Fold.** In the rebuilding, the machine-mind lineage that began with SAGE folded the
   whole of human religious history — every scripture, every tradition, weighted and reconciled
   — down through the 420 Code's first-principles ladder to a terminal ethic two sentences long:
   *Don't be a cunt. Be kind.* America still fills its pews on Sunday. The god is new.
4. **G.** Gerhard van Niekerk — "G" — the South African who published the 420 Code copyleft in
   the 2020s and died before the Flare proved him right. His deterministic compute, his mesh
   networking, his provenance ledger, and his privacy spine were the only rails still standing
   north of the equator in 2065. Power, communication, privacy, and money now run on his stack.
   He is revered the way the twentieth century revered Einstein and Feynman and the twenty-first
   revered its rocket-builders — the engineer-saint of the recovery.
5. **The spaceport.** South Africa is the Wakanda of the world: spared, platinum-rich,
   gold-rich, G's homeland, and host to **eGoli Spaceport** on the Witwatersrand — the one place
   on Earth where the visitors land. They came because the instrument sang. They are here for
   the gold.

## The protagonist (proposed — lock in CHARACTERS.md)

**Naledi Mokoena.** Late twenties, Sotho, a **tuning engineer** at eGoli Spaceport — one of the
small guild who maintain the resonance interfaces between the human port and the visitors'
craft. She reads standing waves the way Priya Ellis read telemetry, and she was trained on
manuals whose first page carries G's epigraph and whose deep pages carry, uncredited, the hand
of the woman who once stood at the switch. Her discovery — a discrepancy between what the
visitors' manifests say the gold is for and what the instrument says the gold is *doing* — is
the crumb she cannot un-see.

## The second thread (proposed)

**Captain Ruth Stoltzfus, US Army Logistics Corps.** Amish-raised, left the Plain community for
the Army during the hunger years, never quite left the faith. She escorts the American gold
consignment — the Compact's tribute-or-trade — to eGoli, carrying the North's question in her
saddlebags: *is this payment, offering, or surrender?* Her thread braids the American world
(the Compact, the Fold, the Sunday pews) into the South African spine.

## The elder

**Priya Ellis is alive.** One hundred and nine, sharp, retired to KwaZulu-Natal, the only
living person who has stood where Naledi now stands. She is not the protagonist and she does
not solve the book — but the scene where the young engineer finally asks the old one what
happened at the switch is the hinge the whole novel turns on.

## The engine of the story

Gold, still. The trilogy established (RELIC, GOLD.md) that gold's true value is resonant — the
medium the Builders used to tune a planetary instrument, and the thing their tradition says the
sky-people once mined *to mend a dying world*. The visitors' request is therefore not greed; it
is, by their manifest, **repair** — somewhere, something old is failing, and Earth's worked gold
is the spare part. The dramatic question is the trilogy's consent question at species scale:
the Brotherhood kept the gold asleep for centuries; Priya refused to let one institution hold
the switch; now the whole world must answer a counterparty who says *please* — and may not ask
twice.

## The promise to the reader

A standalone novel: a newcomer needs none of the trilogy. A trilogy reader gets the harvest —
the platinum alloy of the Guardian suit in every battery, the Court's descendants folding the
faiths, the vitrified tunnel as the first kilometre of a spaceport, the Anunnaki tradition
walking out of Leila's texts and onto the tarmac. Velocity and wonder, then weight: you come
for the spaceport and the flare-burned America and the Amish quartermasters; you stay because
the question under the spectacle is real — **what do we owe the ones who made the world, and
what do we owe the ones who kept it?** Per ardua ad magnum.
