## Zulu Language & Code-Switching

### When isiZulu appears:

- **Home/family scenes:** Arin and Nomsa code-switch; intimate moments privilege isiZulu
- **Labor solidarity:** Union meetings, mine workers speak isiZulu as group language
- **Ancestor references:** Imbeleko ceremony, spiritual grounding
- **Emotional truth:** Characters often revert to first language under stress or emotion
- **Technical precision:** Engineering terms may be English even in isiZulu conversation (mining slang, robotics terminology)

### Key isiZulu phrases (where appropriate):

- **"Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu"** — A person is a person through other people (ubuntu philosophy; central to Arin's journey from isolation)
- **"Ukuthembisa"** — To promise/commit (Themba's name root; foreshadowing Arin's commitment to Guardian)
- **"Amandla"** — Power/strength (badger-inspired SAGE philosophy)

### Do NOT translate every isiZulu phrase.

Context carries meaning. Readers understand "Mama" without footnote. Emotional truth comes through even when words are foreign. This is how South African multilingualism works in lived experience.

## Naming Principles for Pipeline

When generating new scenes or minor characters:

1. **Zulu protagonists get Zulu names** — Ndlela, Dlamini, Nkosi, Khumalo, Mthembu, Zuma, Xaba
2. **South African English/Afrikaans speakers keep English surnames** — Price, Venter, Walsh, Okonkwo
3. **Name meanings matter** — Never random; every name should carry thematic weight
4. **Code-switching is authentic** — Characters naturally move between languages; this is NOT exotic, it is normal SA life
5. **Honor apartheid legacy in casting** — Who has access to universities, corporate roles, danger? History matters.

## Historical / Cultural Notes for Writers

- **Zulu surnames often begin with consonant clusters** (Dlamini, Nkosi, Ndlela, Nkomo, Mkhize)
- **Zulu female names often carry prefix No-, U-, or E-** (Nomsa, Umuntu, Esnene)
- **Male names often carry -and- or -and- stems** (Mandla, Bongani, Thabo)
- **Apartheid created naming gaps**: Some Zulu families use English names due to colonial pressure; reflect this tension in diversity of naming within families
- **Mining culture created its own language**: Zulu, Sesotho, Setswana, mining slang all merge in mine crews; capture this authentically
