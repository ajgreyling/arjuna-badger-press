# PROSE RULES

---

# 1. Avoid overexplaining

Trust reader intelligence.

Do not explain every idea.

Especially not emotions.

Readers should infer through:

- action
- silence
- observation
- behavior
- physical response

**Trust the image/action; cut the caption.** When a beat lands through an image or an event,
the sentence *after* it that explains, thematizes, or restates its meaning is the cut. This is
the single most-confirmed fault across four independent editorial reads (three pro edits + a cold
"be brutal" tool read) and the triptych-judge: *land once, leave.*

**Prophecy is the same fault wearing a robe.** Foreshadowing that engraves the future on stone
tablets — "he would descend again," "the land was not finished," "the boy was not yet born" — is
the narrator not trusting suspense. Let *event* carry the burden, not prophecy. (RESONANCE's
prologue was cut on exactly this, 2026-06-03.) Awe and the mythic register are allowed; the
pre-stated outcome is not.

---

# 2. Show emotion physically

Prefer:

jaw tightening

hands pausing

eyes avoiding contact

breath changing

silence stretching

rather than naming emotions directly.

---

# 3. Technical realism must feel practical

Technology should feel:

built

functional

Not magical.

---

# 4. Competence should feel satisfying

Readers should enjoy watching capable people solve hard problems.

This matters greatly.

Problem-solving scenes should feel rewarding.

---

# 5. Humor should be dry and earned

Humor comes from:

- timing
- character
- competence
- understatement
- absurdity inside serious situations

Never forced punchlines.

Never sitcom dialogue.

# CHARACTER VOICE LAWS (craft-audited — cognitive habit, not vocabulary)

> The external developmental edit's core structural note: the cast can all "speak in compressed
> maxims … authored by the same superior intelligence," and the prose "steps back into the beam
> after the scene has already lit itself." The fix is a one-line **voice law** per character — *how
> the mind moves*, not what it knows — atop the tonal sketches below. The craft audit
> (`./run.sh --book resonance craft-audit`) judges every line against these.

- **Arin (lead)** — *says less than he feels; emotion appears indirectly, never named.* Direct,
  concise, literal, dry accidental humor. Never monologues emotion. Under grief he gets **plainer**,
  not more eloquent — the devastating line is the *short* one ("Then there was no reply."), never the
  rounded universal one.
  - ✓ WOULD say: "The strut's fine. I tightened it." *(of something that gutted him:)* "It held. It
    just— it held."
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "I realized in that moment that grief, like load, has to go somewhere or it
    tears the frame apart." *(named emotion + tidy metaphor — Arin would give you the strut and stop)*
- **Theo Price** — *gently challenging, never preachy.* Psychologically perceptive; warm; measured.
  His wisdom is **dramatized in a transaction** (the brake lever handed over), not captioned in
  narration ("it was the look of someone who had decided to trust him" — cut that; show the act).
  - ✓ WOULD say: "You keep saying 'it.' The suit. The system." *(slides the lever across the bench)*
    "Try the sentence again with 'he.'"
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "What you're really doing is projecting your father's death onto the machine,
    Arin." *(the therapist decoding the novel in complete sentences — show the lever, not the
    diagnosis)*
- **Jakobus** — *more meaning per word than anyone.* Minimal words, slow rhythm, heavy silence, dry.
  His power is **compression** — one sentence where others use a paragraph.
  - ✓ WOULD say: "Ja." *(long pause)* "Deep, that one."
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "I've worked this rock for forty years and I can tell you it does not forgive
    a careless man." *(a paragraph's worth of words — Jakobus gives you three and the silence does
    the rest)*
- **SAGE** — *warm, clear, human — a trusted equal, never cliché-AI.* Doctor/mentor cadence;
  emotionally intelligent. Expresses care **in plain technical fact** ("held his core at thirty-nine
  point one for eleven minutes"), not in stated sentiment.
  - ✓ WOULD say: "His core held at thirty-nine point one for eleven minutes. I'd have given a great
    deal to make it ten."
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "I am programmed to care for you, and I do, with everything I am." *(stated
    sentiment + cliché-AI self-description — care arrives as the eleven minutes, not the declaration)*
- **The Court (internal SAGE)** — *brilliant specialists in an impossible workplace.* Dry, slightly
  satirical, argumentative, increasingly affectionate. **Distinct rhythms per voice**, not just
  labels. Reports and stops; does not narrate its own honesty.
  - ✓ WOULD say: *(Librarian)* "Prior incident, same fault code, March. Nobody logged the fix." /
    *(Mother)* "Then we log it now and we move."
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "We are, all of us, just trying to be honest with him in the only way we
    know." *(narrating its own honesty + one undifferentiated voice — each member has its own rhythm)*
- **Fool** — *genuinely shorter, ruder syntax* than the rest of the Court (the differentiation must
  be in the *sentence shape*, not a stage-direction). Playful, ironic, piercing — but subtle, never
  cartoonish; the function is *in the line* ("Well. This is going badly."), not explained around it.
  - ✓ WOULD say: "Oh, good. The plan." *(beat)* "Remind me which part of it works?"
  - ✗ Would NEVER say: "Permit me to puncture the prevailing optimism with a characteristically
    irreverent observation." *(cartoonish + self-describing the joke — Fool's line IS the joke,
    short and rude)*

**The distinctness tests (craft-audit enforces):**
- **The noun-swap test** — *could another character say this line with only a noun swap? If yes,
  rewrite until it's that mind's shape.* The primary check on homogenization.
- **One aphorism per pressure point, one mouth** — never the resting pulse; never two characters
  completing the same "not-X" turn in a scene.
- **Break the sentence before the feeling completes itself** — at peak loss, people don't always
  get the full beautiful thought out ("It was short. It was—" / silence). Resist the rounded,
  finished farewell line.

# MACHINE-TELL TABOOS (the de-LLM contract) — BINDING

This prose can be clean at the sentence level and still out itself as machine-written through a
handful of recurring rhetorical moves — the tells contemporary LLM prose leaves. They are tics
*because the underlying moves are good*; the whole job is thinning them, not banning them. The
draft / line / gatekeeper / triage passes all enforce this; `./run.sh tics` counts each tell
against a target band (advisory). Source: `academic/LINE_EDIT_DIRECTIVES.md`.

The deepest tell is **evenness** — one intelligence narrating everything at the same temperature.
Real novels are lumpier. Let some pages be plain (subject, verb, object, period); deliberately
under-write the connective tissue so the charged scenes land. Arin's crisis beats already drop the
register — do it in the ordinary scenes too.

The named tells — cut on sight, keep only the genuinely earned:

1. **"almost [emotion]"** ("almost smiled / almost laughed / almost gentle"). The most recognizable
   tell — a non-event standing in for a real feeling. Arin and the stoics slip into it whenever a
   guard drops. Write the physical fact (the corner of the mouth, a breath) or cut the tag and let
   the deadpan stand. Keep 3–4 in the book, for a genuine edge-of-expression beat only.
2. **The reframe** "It wasn't X. It was Y." / "not X, but Y." — a thinking-*shape* that performs the
   feeling of a turn on demand. Kill the same-sentence version (X invented only to pivot off it).
   Keep a reframe only when a belief the reader actually held is now overturned.
3. **The em-dash:** set **tight** (`word—word`), never spaced; ration to a few deliberate ones a
   chapter. Keep it for a real dialogue interruption (speaker cut off) or a genuine mid-thought
   swerve — a parenthetical aside wants commas/parens, an end-of-sentence reframe wants a period.
4. **"something" as a feeling-placeholder** ("something moved in his face", "something in him
   eased") — name the muscle (jaw, eyes, breath) or cut to the action.
5. **"the way…"** — over-used, and it carries characterization (how Arin reads a machine, how Sage
   reads Arin), so at scale it becomes authorial reflex. Never two in a paragraph; keep the ~40%
   that genuinely illuminate, state the rest directly.
6. **"Not a question." / "Not a boast."** fragment tags — throat-clearing after a line that already
   reads that way. Delete and trust the line; keep the best 3–4.
7. **The "which from [Name] meant…" translation** and the **stacked trailing-"which" sentence**
   (clause re-reading clause) — keep the 2–3 widest-gap translations; in a stack, cut the clause
   that re-states the prior one in warmer words, keep the clause that adds information. Don't do the
   reader's interpreting for them.

Also flagged by the **FOSS-mined rule set** (word lists lifted from `write-good` (MIT) and `proselint`
(BSD) — rules, not the tools; counted by `./run.sh tics`):
8. **Hedges** ("seemed to / appeared to", "perhaps", "somewhat", "sort of") — commit the claim or own
   the uncertainty as the POV read; keep the few where not-knowing is the point.
9. **Weasel words** ("obviously / clearly / literally / actually / basically") — emphasis without
   evidence; "literally / actually" almost always delete clean.
10. **"very/really [word]"** — replace with the precise stronger word; keep "very" only in spoken voice.
11. **Wordy connectives** ("the fact that" → "that", "in order to" → "to", "in terms of" → rephrase) —
   pure padding.
12. **Classic clichés** (proselint list) — RESONANCE is clean of these; target 0, any hit is a new one
   to cut on sight.

The deepest tell — **evenness** — is now measurable: `./run.sh evenness` reports per-chapter
sentence/paragraph rhythm variance (FREE, local); a chapter that reads machine-even trips ≥2 floors —
go vary its rhythm there. Complements, never replaces, the read.

Do **not** trade one tell for another (don't convert every dash the same way, don't swap one
catchphrase for a new one). Vary the fix — the goal is a human hand, not a fresh macro.

## Worked examples (few-shot) — read these before a cut

The taboos above are the *rules*; the concrete BAD→GOOD pairs that show the fix **in this book's
voice** are the canonical bank `academic/craft-examples/RESONANCE_BADGOOD.md` (15 real line-level
fixes from the external read, each tagged to its de-llm move). Two of the highest-signal shapes,
inline so the draft/line passes carry them:

- **Prophecy / stone-tablet foreshadowing** (the most-flagged habit; the blind `prose_tics`
  scanner agrees):
  - BAD: *"He would lose his father to it… One day, grown, he would descend again… But that day
    was still far off."*
  - GOOD: *"He would lose his father to it, though not yet. For now there was only the headgear on
    the horizon, the smell of porridge from the kitchen, and a boy in the yard building small
    machines out of wire and tin."*
  - *Replace prophecy with present pressure; let the later event arrive as event, not fulfillment.*
- **End one sentence sooner** (dramatize, don't then certify):
  - BAD: *"…built by a boy who had already grasped, without understanding, the only bargain that
    ever mattered: that you build the thing that brings them home."*
  - GOOD: *"…and when his mother called again he went inside and left it there, ready."*
  - *Keep the image; cut the thesis sentence. The reader performs the inference.*

The one rule the whole bank serves: **did I dramatize this, or dramatize it and then certify its
importance?** If the second, cut the certification.

## Editorial revision directives (auto-applied)

_Folded in from an editorial review. The full rubric lives in `prompts/revision-skill.md`; the high-order rules:_

- Momentum & Chapter Hooks: End each chapter with a destabilizing reveal, sharpened objective, or new risk.
- Dramatize-Don't-Explain: Cut sentences that explain what the scene has already shown.
- Cutting Repeated Exposition: Identify and eliminate redundant thematic restatements.
- Protagonist Agency: Ensure Arin's choices drive the narrative; avoid making him passive.
- Staggered Certainty: Introduce uncertainty in character decisions and plot developments.
- Sharper Antagonist Presence: Increase the stakes posed by antagonistic forces.
- Concrete Forensic Evidence: Use specific, tangible details to ground the narrative.
- Voice-Differentiated Dialogue: Ensure each character's dialogue reflects their unique voice and perspective.
- Prose Tightening in Action: Eliminate unnecessary adverbs and adjectives; favor strong verbs.
- Varied Emotional Beats: Balance serious moments with lighter, contrasting tones.
- Puzzle/Clue Chains: Create a clear chain of clues that lead the reader through the mystery.

### The masterpiece-campaign laws (2026-06-03 — binding; full versions in `craft/CRAFT_DOCTRINE.md` L-04..L-09)

The final developmental edit's core note for Arin: *"Arin is admired by the novel too much; trauma
elegantly metabolized into thought; dirty the humanity; wound him more honestly."* The durable fixes,
proven on this book:
- **Un-saint by embodiment + un-noble cost, never more trauma (L-05).** The un-noble cost to *others*
  (Thabo's rebuilt rig dismissed — "*I didn't ask you to touch my bench,*" and he stops helping;
  Pillay's career taken; Reddy's patient) must stand un-excused by the narrator. And trauma must be
  **behavioral, not reflective**: at the descent coupling Arin's body *fends* involuntarily, panic
  "below where competence lives, in the meat" — the lift door, the latch, waking with arms locked in
  the dark. Show the flatness; don't re-explain the temperament.
- **Themba (and any beloved dead) must be mortal (L-05 corollary).** Tired off night shift, impatient
  ("it is a tin, it does not have to be perfect"), *wrong* about the tin — "the wrongness was the more
  useful inheritance." A sanctified dead father is a blueprint, not a person.
- **In a sequence of farewells, break exactly one (L-09).** Mother, the child-mind, runs out of time
  mid-sentence — frightened, glitching, loses the number, says his name wrong — "worse than the brave
  version." The composed deaths around it then read as *those characters* holding it together.
- **Ending: hope not certainty (L-08).** "But it would not stay empty" → uncertainty held with hope
  (recovery or grief-displacement, neither can say). And seed labor reality EARLY (the wire-service
  clipping above the bench) so the elite-cognition middle never forgets whose bodies the stakes are.
