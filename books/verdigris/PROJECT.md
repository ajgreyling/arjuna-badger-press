# Project — *Verdigris*

> **Type:** original IP · **Genre:** sci-fi horror / eco-weird / climate-gothic · **Status:**
> scaffolding · **Length:** one novel (~110k) · **Substrate:** near-future warming Earth, ground-level
> and fungal

## The one-line

The world went warm enough that the fungi caught up with us — and the only thing that reliably kills
them is copper, so we mist ourselves green and call it survival; but the mycologist holding the
sprayer thinks the bloom is the planet's immune system, and the mushrooms are trying to hand our
humanity back before the copper burns away the cure with the disease.

## Provenance (binding — original work)

- **Original IP. No borrowed source.** No characters, world, institutions, scenes or sentences are
  drawn from any existing work. This is not a homage and carries **no** estate/attribution notice.
  (Different posture from the Faithful Modern shelf, e.g. [`../verdigris/`] stands alone the way
  [`../the-firmament/`](../the-firmament/) does.)
- **Kinship of engine, declared not hidden:** the fungal-apocalypse tradition, ambiguous-invader
  ecohorror, and the entheogen-as-message strand are acknowledged influences on the *shape*. Nothing
  is transcribed. If a scene reads as a reference to a specific existing work, it has failed and is
  cut. (See [`canon/CANON_CHOICES.md`](canon/CANON_CHOICES.md#cc-06-anti-derivative.)
- **Science posture:** the biology is *extrapolated*, not invented from nothing — real mycology (heat
  ceilings, oligodynamic metals, aflatoxin neurotoxicity, entheogen pharmacology) pushed one turn past
  the present. Plausibility is a craft obligation; see [`canon/PREMISE_AND_BIOLOGY.md`](canon/PREMISE_AND_BIOLOGY.md).

## The three load-bearing ideas (why this book exists)

1. **The weapon is a poison to us too.** Copper is oligodynamic — it kills microbial life cheap and
   fast, which is *why* it works on the bloom. But colloidal copper at the doses that scour fungus is
   also cumulatively toxic to the people breathing it: Wilson's-disease-by-choice, a nation slowly
   copper-poisoning itself to stay unpuppeted. Survival is a heavy-metal habit. The green on our skin
   is verdigris — the same corrosion that eats a statue.
2. **The bloom may be the answer, not the enemy.** Ezra's heresy: the fungal surge tracks not
   temperature alone but *something like grief* — it blooms hardest where human cruelty concentrated
   (the camps, the drowned coastal slums, the abandoned). The aflatoxin strain zombifies; but the
   *psilocybin* strain, and the widening pharmacopeia of entheogens erupting alongside it, do
   something else — they appear to be trying to reinstall empathy in a species that traded it away.
   The horror is not knowing which reading is true, and acting anyway.
3. **Lost humanity as literal biology.** The book's spine: humanity didn't figuratively lose its
   humanity — the warming and the cruelty that drove it are, in the bloom's logic, a measurable
   deficiency the mycelium is *correcting*. Zombification and enlightenment are two dialects of the
   same organism reaching for the same broken thing. The reader is never told which the author
   endorses.

## The central, un-resolved question

Is copper salvation or lobotomy? Every act of spraying saves a body and may kill the one thing —
the bloom's offered rewiring — that could save the species. The book holds both jaws shut and never
arbitrates. (House all-sides law, applied to an ecosystem: indict the machine of cruelty that caused
the warming; do not flatten either the terror of being puppeted or the seduction of the offered
grace.)
