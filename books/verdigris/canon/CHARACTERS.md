# CHARACTERS — *Verdigris*

## Dr. Ezra Mokwena — the mycologist (POV)
Late forties, once an academic mycologist studying fungal communication (the "wood-wide web"), now a
science officer attached to a municipal spraying corps because that is where a mycologist eats. He
mixes copper and he loves fungi, and the contradiction is eating him before the book starts. His
turn — from exterminator to heretic — is the spine. He is **not a prophet and not a madman**; the
book keeps him precisely on the seam. His liver is failing from two directions (copper and, later,
aflatoxin) and his revelations arrive on the same rising curve as the damage, so the reader can never
bank them. *Voice: clinical, precise, self-aware; the more transported he gets, the more carefully he
reaches for the exact word, which is its own kind of unreliable.*

## Nomsa Dlamini — the corps captain
Runs Ezra's spraying crew. Has hollowed more of her own people with mercy-copper than she can count,
and would do it again. **Reading A embodied — by a hero.** She is not wrong and she is not cruel; she
is the book's argument that the invader-reading is held by the best of us, with the receipts. Her
arc: watching the man she relies on for the science start preaching the thing she sprays. *Voice:
short, load-bearing, gallows-dry; counts saves the way Ezra counts species.*

## Thabo — the un-puppeted (PP-05)
A crew member hollowed by aflatoxin in Part I — a person the reader likes, dispossessed on the page —
whom the psilocybin strain then, impossibly, *partly returns*. The single most important ambiguous
datum in the book: proof of Reading B, or a parasite's crueller trick. What "comes back" as Thabo is
and isn't Thabo, and the book never rules. Ezra's love for him is what tips him into the heresy.

## Dr. Priya Sun — the copper order's scientist
Head of the refinery's research arm; brilliant, funded, and institutionally committed to Reading A
because the entire economy depends on it. Not a villain — a good scientist inside a machine that
cannot afford Ezra to be right. Her clashes with Ezra are the book's real debate, both of them
citing the same data. *Voice: rigorous, funded, tired of romantics.*

## The mycelium — the second voice (see STYLE_GUIDE)
Not a character with intent; a **register**. A chorus that surfaces in Ezra's psilocybin chapters and
between parts — first-person plural, patient, geological. Everything it "says" is deniable as Ezra's
chemistry. It is the book's most dangerous instrument and its use is rationed. It never states a
checkable fact.

## The Green Seer (PP-08)
A figure in the largest bloom — a person greened past any survivor Ezra has seen, apparently lucid,
apparently the bloom's fruiting intelligence, possibly just the most advanced hollowing wearing
serenity. The Part III destination. What they offer, and whether an offer is even being made, is the
final refusal to arbitrate.

## Voice law (binding)
No character is a mouthpiece who is simply right. Nomsa/Priya (Reading A) and Ezra/the chorus
(Reading B) must each land their best case on their best day. The moment one side gets a strawman,
the book has failed.
