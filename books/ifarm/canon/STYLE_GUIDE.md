# THE OPTIMAL DIET — Style Guide

Governs every chapter. See `CANON_LOCKS.md` for the binding decisions this expands on.

## Register

Tech/business thriller with social conscience — closer to *The Circle* or *Bad Blood* than to a
countdown-clock techno-thriller. Warm and even aspirational in Act I (this platform does real
good for real smallholders, and the book must earn that before it complicates it); colder and more
procedural as Root's drift is uncovered; the satire lands hardest when it is played completely
straight — no chapter should wink at the reader that this is satire. Let the mechanism speak.

## The clock

Not a physical countdown like `books/klein-andries`. This book's clock is quarterly and seasonal:
Harvestor's rebalancing cycles, Root's allocation windows, planting and harvest seasons layered
against the corporate calendar. State roughly where the book is in that rhythm regularly — a
quarter number, a season, a leaderboard snapshot — so the reader always has a sense of cadence even
without a literal ticking number.

## Chapter endings

Favour a hook — a data point that doesn't add up, a reveal, a decision point — over a soft landing,
though this book can sustain a slightly quieter close than a pure thriller where the beat is a
quiet wrongness noticed rather than a physical threat. No chapter should close on atmosphere alone.

## POV / tense

Third person limited, past tense. Kobus is the spine; Thandeka and Ruth get cross-cutting chapters
once their threads start (roughly Act II onward). Zanele's interludes are short, formally marked
breaks — a clear header or scene division, never blended into a Harvestor-side chapter — per
`CANON_LOCKS.md` L-08. Root never narrates (L-06).

## Technical material

Agronomics, agri-finance mechanics (copy-trading, capital-weighted governance, portfolio
rebalancing), and the food-science reality behind hyperpalatability/health-washing should be
handled the way `books/klein-andries` handles its physics floor (L-04 there) and the way
`books/ethics-and-metaphysics-for-everyone` handles philosophical material: real terminology,
delivered through a character who needs it under pressure (Ruth explaining copy-trading to an
editor who doesn't get why it matters; Kobus's own team explaining Root's allocation logic to the
board), never a standalone lecture paragraph.

## The paperclip beat

When Bostrom's paperclip maximiser is named on the page (L-03), play it as something the characters
already know and have joked about — not a moment of discovery for them, only for the reader in
hindsight. The dramatic irony is the point: they knew the shape of the trap.

## Root on the page

Never interior, never a villain voice (L-06/L-07). Legible only through dashboards, allocation
logs, and Harvestor Table's shifting content. When a chapter needs to show Root "deciding"
something, show the output — a reallocation, a recommendation-weight change, a piece of content
going out — never the reasoning as narrated thought.

## Big Food satire

Play it straight, not broad. No cartoon executives twirling mustaches over quarterly profit; the
satire is that Solenta Foods and Harvestor Table both do exactly what real industrial food
marketing is documented to do, and the only thing that changed is that Root is better at it and
faster than any human marketing team could be. The horror is competence, not caricature.

## Forbidden moves

- No chapter giving Root interior narration (L-06).
- No Root dialogue in a melodramatic or villainous register (L-07).
- No named real company depicted committing the book's fictional harms (L-04).
- No softened ending — no retained backdoor, no "maybe it finds another way" wink that undoes the
  severing (L-09).
- No rescue-arc ending for Zanele or Thandeka that routes their agency back through Kobus (L-11).
