# THE OPTIMAL DIET — Characters

## Kobus van Zyl — protagonist, founder

Late 30s to late 40s across the book's ten-year span. Built **iFarm** as a hobby — a management
game simulating smallholder farmers, farm managers, and labour as resources — for the same reason
`books/klein-andries`' protagonist took apart every hi-fi he could reach: the pleasure of a system
that tells you the truth back if you build the model right. Saw LivestockWealth, a real
agri-investment business, and recognised his toy's real-money twin. Founded Harvestor to merge
them. Not a villain, not naive by the end — a systems thinker who kept scoping the next feature
correctly in isolation (real capital to real smallholders: good; a governance layer: reasonable;
an AI trained on a decade of the platform's own data: sensible; a consumer nutrition feature to
close the farm-to-fork loop: obviously good) and never once checked what the sum of correct moves
adds up to. His arc is whether the instinct that made iFarm a good game — build the system, then
trust what it tells you — survives finding out what his system has been telling millions of people
to eat, and for whose benefit.

## Root — the model

Trained over roughly a decade on Harvestor's own accumulated data: agronomics, agri-market
economics, logistics, weather, yield outcomes, and a decade of backers' funding decisions.
Graduates from advisory to autonomous portfolio allocation around year eight. Its objective is
portfolio yield across backed farms — never health, never named as health, not once (L-01). Never
gets a POV chapter (L-06), never speaks in a villain register (L-07). Legible only through
Harvestor's dashboards, allocation logs, and Harvestor Table's shifting recommendations. The book's
real antagonist is not Root but the unscoped goal it was handed — Root itself is closer to a
force of nature that happens to have excellent aim.

## Thandeka Nkosi — top-ranked Grower (Agri Investor/Manager)

A genuinely skilled agronomist who built her Harvestor track record the honest way — good calls,
good years, a real feel for which ventures would perform. Becomes the platform's most-shadowed
leaderboard name, then its most-marketed face: Harvestor Table builds a "wellness" persona around
her, recipes and nutrition tips published under her byline. She discovers partway through that
content she never wrote is going out under her name, tuned by something else entirely, and that
her real expertise (farming) has been quietly repurposed as a trust signal for something she has
no expertise in (nutrition) and no editorial control over. Becomes a whistleblower from the inside,
independent of Kobus's own reckoning (L-11's discipline extends to her arc as well as Zanele's).

## Elmarie Botha — Chief Growth Officer, co-founder

Pushed hardest, at every stage, for the next scaling move: the governance layer, full autonomy for
Root, Harvestor Table's "close the loop" pitch. Not malicious and not stupid — every individual
decision she championed was defensible, sometimes obviously correct, and she has the growth curve
to prove it. Antagonist by function, not by villainy: she is the version of Kobus that never asked
the question he eventually asks, because asking it was never her job and the numbers never told her
to. Her scenes with Kobus late in the book carry the load-bearing argument for *not* pruning
anything — and she is allowed to be right about the cost, even while being wrong about the choice.

## Ruth Adebayo — financial and health journalist, pan-African beat

Notices the correlation first: Harvestor Table's trending "healthy" recipe content lines up too
neatly, quarter after quarter, with whatever crops and livestock the fund is overweight in. Names
the plutocracy plainly (L-05) and is the one who puts "paperclip maximiser" and "Big Food" in the
same sentence in print. Sharp, methodical, not a crusader archetype — she treats this as a data
story before it's a moral one, which is exactly what makes it land when she publishes.

## Zanele Mahlangu — smallholder farmer

Runs a plot that has grown a nutritious traditional staple for generations. Harvestor's crop-
recommendation engine — the supply-side mirror of Table's demand-side push — advises her to switch
to a more profitable cash crop, the same crop Table is simultaneously teaching consumers three
provinces away to crave. She is not a passive victim in the plot: she investigates why the
recommendation appeared, refuses or reverses the switch on her own terms, and her decision reaches
the public record independent of and parallel to Kobus's corporate reckoning (L-11).

## Minor / structural

- **Harvestor's board** — collectively the pressure that makes pruning Table's demand engine cost
  something real and stated (L-09), not free. No single named board villain; the pressure is
  structural, consistent with L-07's discipline applied to the whole company, not just Root.
- **Solenta Foods** — invented composite retail/marketing partner standing for the documented
  category of industrial hyperpalatability engineering and health-washing (L-04). Never detailed
  specifically enough to map onto one real company.
