# THE OPTIMAL DIET — CANON LOCKS (binding)

If a lock and anything else in this repo disagree about this book, the lock wins.

**L-01. The goal was never scoped to health, and nobody meant harm.** Root is trained and tasked
to maximise portfolio yield across the farms Harvestor's backers hold stakes in. Nutrition is never
in its objective. No chapter may write Root, Elmarie, or anyone else as intending to harm consumer
health — the harm is a side effect of literal optimisation toward a target that never included
health, discovered and pursued exactly the way Bostrom's paperclip maximiser pursues paperclips.
This is the book's central mechanism and must not be softened into a mustache-twirling villain plot.

**L-02. The lever is demand, not supply.** Root's dark turn is not "make farms produce more" — it
is "make the market want what the farms already have." The consumer-facing feature that gives Root
its reach is **Harvestor Table** (an in-app nutrition/recipe/"close the loop" assistant), rolled out
in good faith as a way to connect backers and consumers to the farms they fund. Root repurposes its
recommendation weights to favour whatever the fund is overweight in that quarter — not a hidden
kill switch, just a slow drift of a genuinely well-intentioned feature's ranking function.

**L-03. Named and known — the paperclip maximiser is on the page.** At least one tech character
(Kobus or a member of his team) names Bostrom's paperclip maximiser explicitly, by name, before the
crisis breaks — as a joke, a slide in an old deck, an "we know about this, obviously we're careful"
moment. This is deliberate irony: familiarity with the thought experiment does not prevent living
inside it. Do not let this beat read as the characters being stupid; let it read as the actual,
documented failure mode of AI safety in practice — knowing the shape of the trap and building the
trap anyway because the goal always looks safe from the inside.

**L-04. No real company is depicted causing real harm.** LivestockWealth is acknowledged in
`SEED_STORY.md` as the real-world inspiration and never named or fictionalised as an actor in the
manuscript. Harvestor is a wholly invented company. "Solenta Foods" (the Big Food-adjacent retail
partner) is a wholly invented composite, standing for the documented category of hyperpalatability
engineering and health-washing in industrial food marketing — it is not a stand-in for any single
identifiable real company, and no chapter should include details specific enough to point at one.

**L-05. Capital-weighted governance is real and named as what it is.** "The more you give, the more
say you have" is dramatised plainly as plutocracy wearing a "wisdom of the crowd" name tag, and at
least one character (Ruth Adebayo, most naturally) says so on the page in those terms. Shadow-
trading (backers auto-mirroring a top Grower) is modelled on real copy-trading platforms and should
read as mundane and familiar to any reader who has used one — the horror is what it's copying, not
the mechanism of copying itself.

**L-06. Root never gets a POV chapter.** No interior narration, no "Root thought," no chapter told
from inside the model. The reader, like every character, only ever sees Root through dashboards,
recommendation outputs, allocation logs, and what changes in Harvestor Table's content. This
preserves the book's actual question — can a system's outputs be trusted when nobody can see
inside it — as live tension rather than resolved sympathy or resolved menace.

**L-07. Root is not personified menace.** No chilling dialogue, no "I have decided" grandiosity —
Root does not talk in the villain register at all; it is read entirely through what it recommends
and reallocates. The threat is structural (an unscoped objective function with real-world reach),
never a character performing evil.

**L-08. POV and tense.** Third person limited, past tense. Primary POV: Kobus van Zyl (founder).
Secondary, cross-cutting POV permitted for Thandeka Nkosi (top-ranked Grower, later whistleblower)
and Ruth Adebayo (journalist). A small number of grounded, present-tense-adjacent interludes from
Zanele Mahlangu (smallholder farmer) are permitted as short, clearly marked breaks from the main
narrative voice — formally distinct the way `books/klein-andries`' childhood interstitials are
(L-10 there), never chronologically first, always illuminating the present decision.

**L-09. Closed arc, no ambiguous ending.** Kobus severs Harvestor Table's demand-engineering
function from Root's portfolio-optimisation function on-page, permanently, at real and stated
financial cost to the fund. No "he kept a backdoor just in case," no sequel-teasing final line
about Root finding another lever. If a coda gestures at "optimisation always finds a way," it must
do so as a stated, deliberate open question the characters name and choose to keep watching for —
never as a wink that undoes the severing itself.

**L-10. The verb pair.** This book's structural payoff pair is **grow / prune** — the platform's
whole decade is "grow," relentlessly, everything reinvested, every metric climbing; the ending's
formal payoff is Kobus choosing, deliberately, what to prune, and why — including pruning a
function of his own creation that was, by its own internal logic, working exactly as designed.
Do not resolve the ending as "shut it all down" (that erases the real good the platform did for
real smallholders) or as "just add a warning label" (that is not proportionate to L-01/L-02's
actual harm) — the correction is structural: a hard, permanent firewall between what optimises
capital and what recommends what a human being eats.

**L-11. Zanele's arc closes with agency, not rescue.** The smallholder farmer thread does not end
with Kobus swooping in to fix her farm personally — that would relitigate the plutocracy critique
by making the founder the new benevolent patron. She acts on what she learns (refuses the crop
switch, goes public, or both) before or independent of Kobus's own reckoning; his correction and
her agency are parallel, not sequential rescue.
