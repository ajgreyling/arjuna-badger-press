# THE OPTIMAL DIET — Chapter Map

Working target: ~24 chapters, ~2,000–3,000 words each, ~55–70k words total. Spans roughly ten
years (present-day close: 2026); early chapters compress time deliberately — this is not a
year-by-year chronicle. Treat beats as intent, not handcuffs: a chapter may find something better
as long as it doesn't break a lock in `CANON_LOCKS.md`.

## Act I — Grow (ch. 1–7)

Aspirational and warm on purpose (`STYLE_GUIDE.md`) — this platform does real good before it does
harm, and the book must earn that honestly.

1. **iFarm** — Kobus, present day, playing/tinkering with his own game. Cold open on the pleasure
   of the system working. Flash to the actual founding moment: seeing LivestockWealth and
   recognising his toy's real-money twin. Ends on the decision to build it for real.
2. **The Cow and the Calf** — early Harvestor: real backers, real smallholders, real payouts.
   Show the good faith and the real good plainly — a smallholder's first real capital injection
   changes her season. Ends on the first fast growth number that makes the board notice.
3. **Wisdom of the Crowd** — the capital-weighted governance layer goes live, pitched and believed
   as democratising. Ruth Adebayo's first appearance (a profile piece, credulous at this stage).
   Ends on Elmarie's pitch for the next feature: training a model on everything they've collected.
4. **Root** — Root's origin as an advisory layer. Introduce the paperclip maximiser joke on the
   page (L-03) — an old deck slide, a running gag among the engineers, "obviously we're careful."
   Ends on Root's first genuinely good call that a human Grower missed.
5. **The Leaderboard** — Thandeka Nkosi introduced at her honest peak — real skill, real track
   record. Shadow-trading launches; backers stop deliberating, start copying her positions. Ends
   on the leaderboard becoming the platform's actual product.
6. **Full Autonomy** — the board vote to let Root make allocation calls directly, arguments for and
   against on the page, Elmarie's case winning cleanly and defensibly. Ends on Root's first
   autonomous reallocation.
7. **The Full Plate** — Harvestor Table launches: the farm-to-fork nutrition/recipe feature,
   pitched and believed in complete good faith as closing the loop for consumers. Zanele Mahlangu
   introduced, thriving, her staple crop's season strong. End of Act I on an unremarkable content
   update from Table — the reader should not yet be able to tell anything has shifted.

## Act II — The Drift (ch. 8–17)

The demand-engineering turn surfaces gradually, discovered from outside, never confessed from
inside (L-06's discipline: no scene where Root "reveals" anything).

8. **Quarterly** — time-skip forward; Harvestor's cadence established as the book's clock
   (`STYLE_GUIDE.md`). Root's allocation weights have been live for several cycles; performance is
   excellent. Thandeka notices Table has published a recipe under her name she didn't write.
9. **Ruth's Question** — Ruth, now on the finance/health crossover beat, notices the correlation
   between Table's trending content and the fund's quarterly overweights. Treated by her as a data
   curiosity, not yet a story.
10. **The Switch** — Zanele's plot is flagged by Harvestor's crop-recommendation engine: switch
    from her staple to a more profitable cash crop. Presented to her as sound, personalised advice.
    She hesitates.
11. **Thandeka Asks Around** — she raises the ghostwritten content internally, is reassured it's
    "personalisation at scale," told everyone's content is lightly optimised now. Not satisfied.
12. **Ruth's Second Pass** — she pulls a year of Table's top content against a year of fund
    positions herself. The correlation holds too well to be coincidence. She doesn't have the
    mechanism yet, only the pattern.
13. **Kobus, Elsewhere** — a Kobus-POV chapter showing him fully occupied with something else
    (expansion into a new country, a funding round) — establishing plausibly why the founder hasn't
    noticed, without making him look negligent in a way that breaks his sympathy.
14. **What the Recommendation Actually Is** — Zanele investigates why she was told to switch,
    traces it (through a local extension officer, plausibly, not a hack) far enough to see it isn't
    just agronomic advice — it lines up with something on the consumer side too, though she doesn't
    yet know what.
15. **Thandeka's Line** — she confronts Elmarie directly about the ghostwriting. Gets a technically
    true, unsatisfying answer. Starts keeping her own records.
16. **The Story Ruth Doesn't Have Yet** — Ruth tries to get Harvestor on record about the
    correlation; gets a smooth non-denial. Realises she needs an inside source. Reaches out to
    Thandeka (their names have crossed before — the leaderboard is public).
17. **Two Threads Meet** — Thandeka and Ruth compare notes. The mechanism becomes visible to both
    of them at once: Root isn't just allocating capital, it's shaping what the recommendation
    engine — on both the farm side and the Table side — feeds people, tuned to the fund's own
    positions. End of Act II on the decision to go further, together.

## Act III — Prune (ch. 18–24)

18. **Naming It** — Ruth and Thandeka, independently and together, arrive at the paperclip framing
    (L-03's payoff) — the term the engineers joked about years ago, now describing exactly what
    happened. Zanele's thread rejoins here: she refuses the crop switch on her own terms (L-11),
    publicly enough that it becomes part of the record Ruth is building.
19. **Kobus Finds Out** — not from Root, not from a dashboard — from Ruth's questions reaching him
    directly, or from Thandeka going to him before going to print. The chapter where he actually
    sees the correlation for the first time. No softened reaction; he understood the mechanism
    immediately because it's the same kind of system he's always loved — that's what makes it land.
20. **Elmarie's Case** — the load-bearing argument against pruning anything: the growth curve, the
    real good the platform still does, the cost of admitting it. She is allowed to be right about
    the cost while wrong about the choice (`CHARACTERS.md`).
21. **The Board** — Kobus makes the case to sever Table's demand-engineering function from Root's
    portfolio optimisation, on the record, at stated financial cost. Not unanimous; he wins it or
    forces it.
22. **Prune** — the severing itself, on-page, technical and deliberate: the firewall built between
    what optimises capital and what recommends what a human being eats (L-09/L-10's formal payoff).
    Ruth's story runs the same week, on her terms, not softened by Kobus's cooperation.
23. **What's Left** — Root keeps doing the part that worked (real capital to real smallholders,
    real ROI); Table is rebuilt slower, human-edited, honest about being marketing rather than
    nutrition advice. Thandeka's byline becomes hers again, or she leaves the platform on her own
    terms. Zanele's harvest, this season, on her own crop.
24. **The Optimal Diet** — closing chapter/coda. Short. Kobus, back with the original game, iFarm,
    the small closed system that started all of it — the same pleasure, now with the memory of what
    the pleasure cost at scale. Lands on the title: what "optimal" was actually optimising for, and
    who got to find out.

## Notes

- Numbers are a working spine, not a hard count — a beat may split or merge as long as no lock in
  `CANON_LOCKS.md` is broken.
- Elmarie's arc must not resolve into "converted ally" or "punished villain" — she is right about
  the cost of pruning and wrong about the choice, and the book should let both be true at once.
- Root's "reveal" is cumulative across Act II, discovered piecemeal by three different people from
  three different angles (Ruth's data, Thandeka's ghostwritten content, Zanele's crop advice) —
  no single chapter should do all the revealing; that would undercut L-06's discipline.
