## LOCKED — direction set (2026-06-23, drafting pass)

All formerly-OPEN creative gates are now decided. These are binding on drafting.

### The world & texture
- **Setting: Earth, near-future, real.** No invented planet, no relocated-China, no US-default suburbia.
  The *institutions* are built and specific so the prose reads native (mandate #1) without floating into
  generic "world English." Internationalist by default — humanity acts as a species under the countdown,
  not as one nation.
- **The array: the Deep Listening Array (the DLA),** a continental radio-astronomy installation on a high
  desert plateau — our original facility. Its culture (the night shifts, the correlation room, the
  superstitions of people who listen for a living) is the native-texture anchor of Movement I.

### Cast (LOCKED names — native-English, varied, zero Liu echoes)
- **Ada Reyes — the Analyst** (Movement I). Reads humanity's loudness correctly; writes the unread
  first-true-line. The book's first eyes. *Not* Ye Wenjie — no wronged-astronomer revenge arc.
- **Marcus Hale — the Sender** (Movement I). The articulate, sympathetic public scientist who argues the
  world into sending the Hail. His reason (LOCKED below) is hope, not vengeance.
- **Cole Ridgeway — the Strategist** (Movement II). Cold, lateral game-theorist who deduces the law and
  forges the mutual-exposure deterrent. *Not* Luo Ji — no slacker-savant chosen-one arc.
- **Sergeant Nadia Brandt — the Cop / ballast** (Movement II). Plain, dry, profane-warm; the native-English
  tuning fork; keeps the human story earthed while the cosmos goes abstract.
- **Esme Calderón — the Inheritor** (Movement III). Tender, morally exposed; inherits the deterrent's pin
  at deep-time scale; her mercy is the lever the law uses against everyone. *Not* Cheng Xin — the choice,
  cost, and person are invented from zero.

### The Sender's reason for the Hail (LOCKED — original, sympathetic, NOT revenge)
Marcus Hale sends the Hail out of **hope, reasoned into hubris**: he believes a silent universe is a
*lonely* one, not a *dangerous* one — that silence means absence, and that ending the loneliness is the
noblest thing a thinking species can do. He is not damaged or vengeful; he is *persuasive and wrong*, and
the reader should half-agree with him before the countdown starts. The folly must be **ours, collectively**
— a civilization's optimism, not one broken man's grudge. (This is the deliberate inversion of Liu's
Ye-Wenjie motive; see CHARACTERS.)

### The observation/sabotage net — mechanism (LOCKED — grounded, NOT folded-proton-computers)
**The Net** (working name): the enemy's forward agent is not omniscient computing folded into a particle.
It is a **swarm of self-replicating microprobes**, dispatched ahead of the main threat at relativistic
speed, that arrive decades before anything else and do two jobs: (1) **watch** Earth at fine grain by
seeding the inner system with sensors too small and too many to find or kill; (2) **sabotage** a *specific*
class of human progress — they degrade high-energy physics experiments (subtly corrupting results at the
detector level) so humanity cannot make the leap that would let it fight back or run. Grounded rules: the
Net obeys lightspeed, can be *partially* mapped and fought, and its menace is **patience and reach**, not
magic. (NOT the sophon: different physics, different vulnerability, no FTL.)

### The single-object weapon — physics (LOCKED — this is the cable-cut scene, kept "as is")
**The Filament.** The Net's first overtly *physical* act, and Movement I's detonation. A single strand of
monomolecular carbon nanofilament — invisibly thin, immensely strong — strung taut across a known transit
corridor by Net microprobes anchoring its two ends. The weapon is the **geometry**: anything that crosses
the line at speed is sliced cleanly into sheets, the cut so fine the victim does not even register it as a
cut until the pieces separate. One pass, unanswerable, indifferent. (See the dedicated set-piece spec
below. This is the famous "ship cut by the wire" image kept at full brutal calibre — staged as **our** cable,
**our** ship, **our** people, so it is homage, not transcription: ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.)
> **NOTE:** the *true* droplet-grade detonation of Movement II (PP-007, the single-object weapon's one
> pass against a fleet) is a SEPARATE, larger event. The Filament in Movement I is its terrible *first
> demonstration* on one vessel — the foreshadow that makes the later horror land. Both are "perfect"-bar
> set-pieces; they are paced one per movement (PLOT velocity gate still holds — I.5 and II.5 are distinct).

### The defensive doctrine — institution (LOCKED — NOT wallfacers)
**The Reckoners.** Not four secret strategists with unquestionable authority. The Reckoners are a small,
public, *despised* bureau whose entire job is to maintain humanity's one deterrent: a dead-man system that,
if Earth is struck, automatically broadcasts the **enemy's** location to the whole dark forest — exposing
the hunter to every other hunter. The deterrent only works while the species is willing to be a suicide
bomber holding everyone else's coordinates. Its tragedy is that the keeper of the pin must be someone the
universe believes will actually pull it. (Original structure; mutual-exposure logic kept.)

### The final cosmic stroke — device & cost (LOCKED — NOT a dimensional foil)
**The Quietus.** Deep-time, Movement III. When the deterrent finally fails (Esme's mercy, PP-006), the
forest answers not with a filament or a swarm but with the cheapest, most total tool a hunter has: a
**lightspeed beam of engineered dust** — a relativistic sand-blast that scours the inner system to bare
rock over the course of a single afternoon, no malice in it, just the law completing. The cost: it is
*cheap*, which is the horror — Earth was never worth more effort than a swept floor. (Original device; the
"law applied at full scale" function kept, the dimensional-collapse form discarded.)

## THE FILAMENT SET-PIECE — spec (the "as is" scene, binding)

This is the scene AJ asked to keep "as is" — the ship cut by the carbon cable. Kept at full calibre;
re-grounded as ours.

- **What:** the research/relief vessel **Meridian Reach** (our ship, our crew of ~forty) transits a known
  corridor near the array's deep-space relay. The Net has, weeks earlier, anchored a single monomolecular
  filament across that corridor — invisible, taut, waiting.
- **The cut:** the ship crosses the line at transit speed. The filament passes through hull, bulkhead,
  bone, and fuel line without resistance, slicing the entire vessel into parallel sheets a few metres
  apart. For one or two seconds **nothing appears wrong** — the cut is so clean the ship still looks whole.
  Then it begins, gently, to come apart along the planes, the sections sliding, the lights still on inside
  each slab as it separates, people still at their stations in cross-section, before air and physics catch
  up.
- **The horror is the cleanness and the delay,** not gore. Trust the image; do not caption the awe
  (STYLE_GUIDE idea-as-detonation). The reader should understand what happened a half-beat before the
  characters watching the feed do.
- **Whose eyes:** witnessed from the array (Ada's successor / the relay watch), not from inside the ship —
  we see it from outside, on a feed, helpless, which is worse. (Deep-time relay: Ada has died by now; her
  warning was filed and unread — PP-010.)
- **Function:** this is the moment the abstract countdown becomes *physical and personal*; the proof that
  being found means death delivered by something that does not even hate you. It pays off PP-001 (bright =
  found) and seeds the dread that justifies the whole long century (Movement II).
- **DO NOT:** copy the source's specific ship, crew, dialogue, or framing; reproduce its sentences; or
  name the device after the source's. Keep the *physics and the dread*; invent everything else.
