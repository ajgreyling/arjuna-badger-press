# Adaptation Doctrine — *The Long Silence*

> How this book is faithful to Liu Cixin's *Remembrance of Earth's Past* without being a copy of it. The
> governing rule for every canon and manuscript decision in this project.

## The line we hold

Liu's trilogy is under copyright; the English text is also his translators' (Ken Liu, Joel Martinsen)
copyrighted work. We are not licensed. Therefore fidelity is to **theory and engine**, and the text is
**ours**. Two failures to avoid, equally:

1. **The legal/ethical failure** — reproducing his plot, scenes, characters, named devices (sophon,
   droplet, wallfacer, the dark-forest strike), or sentences; or doing the same thing with names swapped
   and details nudged. A "find-and-replace adaptation" is a derivative work we have no right to make, and
   the house has never made one.
2. **The craft failure** — the *same* failure wearing a different coat. Beat-for-beat copying with
   substitutions produces airless, second-hand prose (the "taxidermy test"). And here the craft failure
   is doubly specific, because we are explicitly trying to **out-craft** the source in two named places:
   the translation stiffness and the sag (see below). A copy can't fix what we copied it to fix.

## What we keep (the engine — these are *ideas*, not expressions, and they are Liu's gift to the form)

- **The Dark Forest as moral physics.** Two axioms — survival is the first need of any civilization, and
  civilizations grow while the universe's matter does not — plus two mechanisms — the **chain of
  suspicion** (you cannot know if a distant other is benign, nor can they know it of you, and the
  uncertainty compounds across light-years and centuries) and the **technological explosion** (any
  civilization, however backward now, may leap ahead of you in the time your message takes to arrive).
  Conclusion: the rational move toward any newly-revealed civilization is the **pre-emptive, anonymous
  kill**. The cosmos is therefore a forest of silent hunters. This is the book's physics. It is Liu's
  idea, kept whole, expressed entirely in our own words and worked examples.
- **The broadcast-folly** (the reader's own hook, fused to Liu's). A species advanced enough to split the
  atom and broadcast on radio is, by the forest's logic, **suicidally loud** — hanging a lit address on a
  dark and armed street. The contradiction (atom-splitting power + shouting-on-radio naivety) is the
  thesis the whole book dramatises.
- **Idea-as-detonation.** Liu's signature is making a *concept* land like a set-piece. We keep that
  calibre — each movement turns on one clean, devastating reveal staged as event.
- **The deep-time scale-shift.** A story that begins inside one human lifetime and ends at the horizon of
  cosmic time, dwarfing its own characters on purpose. The bleakness is structural, not editorialised.

## What we invent (everything that is *expression*)

- **Every name, character, relationship, and backstory.** No analyst is Ye Wenjie; no strategist is Luo
  Ji; no inheritor is Cheng Xin; no cop is Da Shi. They occupy *functions* in the engine — the one who
  makes us loud, the one who learns the law, the one who must choose whether to keep the silence — and
  they share none of Liu's lives, scenes, or lines.
- **Every device.** Our alien observation/sabotage mechanism, our single-object weapon, our defensive
  doctrine, and our final cosmic stroke are **original mechanisms with their own grounded rules** (see
  MYTHOS). They are **not** the sophon, the droplet, the wallfacer program, or the two-vector foil with
  a coat of paint. They rhyme with the *function*; they do not copy the *form*.
- **Every scene and beat.** The PLOT rhymes with the trilogy's spine (loud first contact → the long
  defended century → the law kept or broken at deep-time scale) the way any tragedy rhymes with any
  other. The actual events are ours, set in places we built.
- **The substrate.** Near-now realist Earth — a continental array, real radio astronomy, real geopolitics
  of a planet that has to decide collectively whether to be quiet — sliding into hard-SF deep time.
  Liu-faithful in *rigor and scale*, not in furniture.

## The two craft mandates that are unique to THIS book

This homage is not neutral about its source. It is built to **fix two things AJ named**:

1. **Native English, not translated culture.** The source sometimes read as "Chinese dressed in English."
   *The Long Silence* must read as prose **born in English** — idiom, rhythm, dialogue, and cultural
   texture native to the language, not transposed. (The setting is its own world, not a relocated China
   and not an Americanised one; see WORLD. The *prose* is the thing that must be native.)
2. **No sag.** The source had boring stretches. This book **protects velocity**: no chapter that is a
   lecture in disguise, no info-dump standing in for a scene, no dead middle. Theory is delivered as
   pressure and consequence — discovered, paid for, never narrated at the reader. (See STYLE_GUIDE.)

## The test, applied to every page

Before any scene ships, two questions:
1. *If Liu's trilogy did not exist, would this scene still be a real scene — its own people, its own
   logic, its own surprise?* If it only makes sense as "the part where the sophon thing happens," it is
   taxidermy. Cut it and build the real one.
2. *Does this page read as native English, and does it move?* If a paragraph is stiff with transposed
   syntax, or if the page is explaining instead of happening, it has failed the two mandates this whole
   project exists to satisfy.

## Provenance, disclosed

Per the house thesis (*immersion is the craft obligation; provenance is disclosed, never hidden*): the
foreword names the debt to Liu Cixin and his translators plainly. We do not pretend the theory is ours.
We pretend nothing.
