# WORLD — *The Long Silence*

> The substrate is **near-now realist Earth** sliding into **hard-SF deep time**. Liu-faithful in *rigor
> and scale*, not in furniture: no relocated China, no Americanised default. The setting is its own world,
> built so the prose can be **native English** (STYLE_GUIDE mandate #1) without being culturally generic.

## The loud Earth (Movement I substrate)

- **The array: the Deep Listening Array (the DLA).** A continental radio-astronomy installation on a high
  desert plateau — our original facility — where the book opens. It is where humanity's *loudness* first
  becomes legible as a datum: not the deliberate hail yet, but the sum of eighty years of leakage and
  weapons-test signatures, plotted as a brightening dot on a dark map. **Ada Reyes**, who reads it
  correctly, is the first runner. The DLA's night-shift culture — the correlation room, the people who
  listen for a living and their superstitions — is Movement I's native-texture anchor (mandate #1).
- **The condition of being loud.** Bomb-test electromagnetic signatures; planetary radio leakage; and
  finally **the Hail** — a deliberate, high-power, triumphant broadcast sent by people who believed a
  silent sky meant an empty one, or who wanted contact badly enough to gamble the planet on it. The book's
  central irony: we had the *power* (fission/fusion) eighty years before we had the *wisdom* (silence), and
  used the gap to advertise.
- **The geopolitics of going quiet.** A planet cannot un-broadcast, but it can argue about whether to keep
  broadcasting — and that argument (who gets to speak *for* Earth; who profits from contact; who refuses
  to believe the forest is real) is the human engine of Movement I. Original factions, original politics,
  native-idiom argument.

## The reply (the countdown)

The reply to the Hail is **not a message**. It is the observation/sabotage net's arrival vector and a
duration — a countdown measured in centuries, the travel time of something already dispatched. First
contact is not a handshake; it is a fuse, already lit, with the spark visible from the start.

## The long century (Movement II substrate)

- A defended Earth across roughly a century — long enough that **no one alive at the Hail survives to the
  crisis**, which is the structural point (the deep-time relay). Society reorganises around a slow-motion
  siege: a generation born into the certainty of an arriving end, the economics and despair and strange
  flourishing of that, original throughout.
- **The defensive doctrine** is founded here (MYTHOS): the institution built on mutual-exposure deterrence
  — humanity's one card under the Dark Forest is the threat to broadcast the *enemy's* address too, making
  Earth a place not worth the risk of a loud kill. Original institution, original internal logic and cost.
- **The single-object weapon** makes its one pass here — the movement's set-piece, at the "perfect"
  calibre AJ named.

## The hunters' cosmos (the frame of all three movements)

- The galaxy as a **dark forest**: many civilizations, almost all silent, all having reached the same
  conclusion. We never get a tour of them; they are felt as *absence and consequence*, never
  anthropomorphised (MYTHOS hard line). The few signs we get are terrifying precisely because they are
  partial.
- **Deep time** (Movement III): the horizon stretches past every human institution to the heat-death
  scale. The cosmos's indifference is the final character. The law is proven not by a villain but by
  physics running to completion.

## Texture rules (so the world reads native, not transposed)

- Place-names, institutions, idioms, food, weather, bureaucracy: **invented or native-English**, never
  transliterated from a real culture and never defaulting to American suburbia. The world should feel
  *specific and built*, the way good secondary-world SF does — that specificity is what kills the "dressed
  in English" stiffness.
- Science is rendered **concretely and correctly** (real radio astronomy, real orbital mechanics, real
  game theory) — rigor is the source's gift and our floor. But always **in scene**, never as lecture.
