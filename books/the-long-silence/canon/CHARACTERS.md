# CHARACTERS — *The Long Silence*

> Original cast. Each occupies a **function** in the Dark Forest engine; **none** shares a life, scene, or
> line with Liu's people (ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE). Voice law: **rhyme the function, never copy the person.**
> The deep-time relay means we bond with each runner and then bury them — interiority is the fix for the
> source's thin-character sag (SOURCE_NOTES §"where the source sagged" #3).

## The relay (the spine of the cast)

No single protagonist survives the arc. The baton passes across three movements. Each runner learns one
clause of the law and dies before the sentence is finished; the reader assembles the whole.

| Runner | Movement | Function in the engine (rhymes Liu's role — does NOT copy the person) | Name (LOCKED) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Analyst** | I — THE HAIL | the one who first reads humanity's loudness correctly and hears the countdown for what it is | **Ada Reyes** |
| **The Sender** | I — THE HAIL | the one who makes us loud on purpose — the Hail's author, for reasons that are *not* villainy | **Marcus Hale** |
| **The Strategist** | II — THE LONG CENTURY | the one who deduces the Dark Forest law clause by clause and forges the mutual-exposure deterrent | **Cole Ridgeway** |

## Voice laws (the noun-swap test is binding — Craft Doctrine §7)

Each principal must survive the noun-swap test: swap two characters' names and the lines must no longer
fit. No two voices interchangeable.

- **Ada Reyes (the Analyst)** — precise, observational, a reader-of-signals temperament; the dread enters
  through what she *notices*, not what she says. Quiet competence; the book's first eyes. Dies in Movement I
  (the relay); her unread first-true-line is the line the species needed and ignored (PP-010).
- **Marcus Hale (the Sender)** — articulate, persuasive, *sympathetic* — the folly must be human and
  arguable, never cartoon hubris. His reason is **hope reasoned into hubris** (silence = loneliness, not
  danger), never revenge (CANON_CHOICES). The reader should half-agree before the countdown starts. The
  broadcast-folly only indicts us if we recognise ourselves in him.
- **Cole Ridgeway (the Strategist)** — cold, lateral, a game-theorist's clarity; carries the book's hardest
  ideas, so the prose around him must *move* (anti-sag). Forges the Reckoners' mutual-exposure deterrent.
  Menace through finished thought, not bombast.
- **Sgt. Nadia Brandt (the Cop / ballast)** — plain, dry, profane-warm; the native-English anchor; the one
  who says the human thing in a room full of cosmic abstraction. Crucial to mandate #1: her idiom proves
  the book is born in English. (She, not he — keep the ballast voice ungendered-by-default in drafting.)
- **Esme Calderón (the Inheritor)** — tender, morally exposed; the book's heart and its final tragedy — the
  one whose goodness is the lever the Dark Forest uses against everyone. Inherits the Reckoners' pin; her
  mercy fails the deterrent (PP-006). Hope and ruin in the same mouth.

## The aliens (anti-character by design)

The hunters are **not** a character. Per MYTHOS, they are withheld — a consequence, not a motive we can
argue with. No alien POV chapter; no alien speech that explains. They obey the law. That is all we are
allowed to know, and the withholding is the horror.

## Casting cautions (do NOT do)

- No Ye Wenjie, Luo Ji, Cheng Xin, Da Shi, Wang Miao **as people** — no shared backstory (the wronged
  astronomer; the slacker-turned-savant strategist; the merciful inheritor; the loyal cop; the
  nanomaterials scientist). The *functions* recur in any Dark Forest story; the *lives* are ours.
- The Sender is **not** a trauma-driven misanthrope pressing a button in revenge (that is Liu's specific
  scene and motive). Our Sender's reason is original — and must be sympathetic enough that the reader
  feels the folly is *ours*, collectively, not one damaged person's.
- The Inheritor's mercy-that-dooms is the *function* we keep; the person, the choice, and the cost are
  invented from zero.
