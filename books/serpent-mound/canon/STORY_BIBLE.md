A neurodiverse crew of “impossible” outsiders races to read the Great Serpent Mound before a corporate heritage foundation can buy, brand, and “settle” its story. The premise is a chase against time and institutional certainty: two credentialed science camps war over the mound’s age (Adena? Fort Ancient?)—and the “easy” answer keeps collapsing under real, checkable facts. The official story is played straight, the potato revealed (nobody can close the case), and the genuine hole left open: why is the mound here, and what is it *for*? The crew’s lens is their wiring: they see what others miss, not as wound but as gift. The camera belongs to the crew, close-third, never omniscient; the local Native archaeologist/cultural authority owns the final reading and the “what is it for.” The Why Files register: rabbit-hole curiosity, affectionate toward skeptics and builders, never sneering, always with a wink; the reader is invited to say “I don’t know”—and that is the wonder. Intended reader: adults/teens who want rigor and awe, who’ve been burned by both credulity and cynicism, who crave the ‘maybe’ and the dignity of not knowing. Content bounds: no “aliens did it,” no caricature, no omnipotent cabals; the adversary is clean-handed and human. The creative driver: the unresolved, checkable fact (the real dating war and the solstice line), dramatized through the crew’s neurodiverse lens and the local’s authority.
