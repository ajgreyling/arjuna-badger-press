ACT I: ARRIVAL
- The crew arrives at Serpent Mound, called by a tip that the foundation is about to close a deal for a “World Heritage Experience.” Dr. Mara Tallchief (local archaeologist/custodian) is running a last, contested survey before the land changes hands.
- The official story is laid out: the dating war (Adena vs. Fort Ancient), the solstice alignment, the mound’s unsolved function.
- Eric Sandoval and the foundation team are on site, prepping for acquisition and planning the “definitive” visitor narrative.

OBSTACLE
- The crew joins Mara’s survey, running new ground-penetrating radar, reviewing core samples, and checking the solstice sightline. Every method yields a “potato”: core samples are inconclusive, carbon dates contradict, the “easy” story won’t lock.
- Frik blurts the inconvenient truths: the radiocarbon can’t close it, the alignments are real but not exclusive, both camps cherry-pick data.
- The foundation pushes for a single, marketable story, pressuring Mara to sign off on a date and a culture.

READ
- The solstice sunset is observed: the head aligns precisely. The crew and Mara hike the ridge, mapping water flows, noting how the coils correspond to hidden springs and underground streams.
- Jakobus reads the made thing: not who, not when, but *what is it for*—the coils as water map, the placement as horizon marker, the function as living tool.
- Mara, holding her authority, weighs what to share; the crew learns to listen rather than interpret.

COST
- The foundation threatens to sideline Mara if she won’t “settle” the story. The crew faces the risk of being barred from the site.
- Frik’s bluntness alienates both camps, but also cracks open the debate, forcing the real uncertainties into the open.
- The local community divides: some want the world to know, others want the mound left unbranded, unclaimed.

KEY
- Mara presents her reading—grounded, physical, rooted in land and water, not in myth or market. She offers it *on her terms*, declining to close the age debate but showing the mound’s function as a living map/tool.
- The crew documents, but does not claim, the answer; Frik, for once, withholds a fact, letting Mara’s voice stand.

HANDOFF / CLIMAX
- The foundation, faced with the unresolved truth, agrees to a shared stewardship: the story stays open, the site’s function is foregrounded, and the “mystery” is preserved as part of the visitor experience—but Mara retains interpretive authority.
- The crew departs, changed; the reader is left in the draught of the maybe.
