SITE: Great Serpent Mound (Ohio, USA)

REAL:
- The Great Serpent Mound is a prehistoric effigy mound located in Adams County, Ohio.
- The mound is over 1,300 feet (approximately 400 meters) long, representing an uncoiling serpent with a spiral-shaped tail and an oval-to-egg-shaped feature at the mouth.
- The precise date of construction is genuinely unsettled and the subject of an ongoing scientific debate.
    - Radiocarbon dating of charcoal from beneath the mound (1996, University of Cincinnati; Lepper et al.) suggested a construction date around 1070 CE (±70 years), associating the mound with the Fort Ancient culture.
    - Earlier archaeological consensus, based on artifacts and mound morphology, attributed the construction to the Adena culture, dating it to approximately 320 BCE (with a typical range of 800 BCE–100 CE for Adena sites).
    - Some radiocarbon samples from earlier excavations (e.g., Putnam, 1880s; later re-analyses) yielded older dates consistent with Adena activity, but may represent earlier occupation or use of the site rather than mound construction itself.
    - As of 2024, the dating controversy is unresolved in peer-reviewed literature; both camps (Adena and Fort Ancient) have credentialed, published advocates.
- The mound is precisely aligned to the summer-solstice sunset: the serpent’s head points to the horizon point where the sun sets on the longest day of the year (confirmed by archaeoastronomers, e.g., Romain 1991; Lepper 1998; Ray Hively & Robert Horn 1982).
- Additional alignments to lunar standstills and other solstitial events have been proposed but are more debated and less universally accepted.
- The mound sits atop a geologically unusual feature: a meteorite impact crater (the Serpent Mound Cryptoexplosion Structure).
- The site is not a burial mound; no burials have been found within the effigy itself, though nearby conical mounds contain Adena burials.

CLAIMED (FRINGE/UNCONFIRMED/LOCALLY HELD/CONTESTED):
- Some fringe theorists claim the mound encodes astronomical or mathematical knowledge beyond what is currently accepted for either Adena or Fort Ancient cultures (e.g., precise lunar calendars, or cosmic “messages”).
- Various unsourced claims of “giant skeletons” or “lost civilizations” linked to the site are widely circulated but are not supported by credible archaeological evidence.
- Some local Indigenous oral traditions reference the mound as a place of power, but direct, continuous tradition linking current Nations to the mound’s original builders is debated among scholars and Tribal historians.
