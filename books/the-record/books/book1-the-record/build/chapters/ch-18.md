# Chapter 18

`INSUFFICIENT GROUNDED RECORD`

Not a sentence naming a perpetrator. Not a confession. A person addressing another person, or calling
for them, or saying their name inside words the residue could no longer distinguish.

did not bold it.

Only then did the consortium search the name.

The first records were ordinary: electoral rolls, employment lists, newspaper indexes, digitised

One remained.

A man missing since the late apartheid period.

His official file recorded no connection to the site. No employment, detention, interview or visit.
The surviving administrative history placed him elsewhere until it stopped placing him anywhere.

Dries asked Lucid whether the residue established that the missing man had spoken.

`NO.`

`NO. NAME-BEARER IDENTITY`  
`NOT PHYSICALLY RESOLVED.`

`YES, AMONG OTHER COMPATIBLE NAME-BEARERS.`  
`SEE CONFLICT SET.`

The machine gave them no permission to collapse possibility into biography.

Ama read the missing-person file anyway.

Near the end was a sentence copied through three inquiries: there was no evidence the man had ever
entered the temporary facility believed to have occupied the district.

The sentence had survived longer than the building.

Under it, Ama placed the result the machine could honestly support:

At that location, during the admissible historical interval, somebody had said the name.
