# Chapter 25

The machine found four people and was allowed to say none.

The result consisted of load paths through the old floor.

Three crossed the admissible interval continuously. The fourth entered under weight, weakened near
the centre of the room and returned intermittently before ending at a different coordinate. Every
path described force entering surfaces over time. None contained a body.

Ama labelled them A through D.

Technopark labelled D `SUPPORTED_PERSON`.

She rejected the product.

“It is a person,” Dries said.

“It is an intermittent load path.”

“With two legs.”

“Sometimes.”

“The Anchor constraint follows it.”

“Within the current model.”

Lwazi joined the review from Karoo. He rotated the force volumes, hid the Anchor products and restored
them. Path D broadened without disappearing. Near one wall, its weight divided between the floor and
another moving path.

“Someone was holding him up,” Dries said.

“Or restraining him,” Lwazi replied.

“Or helping him walk.”

“Or D knelt and touched another person.”

“The load is too high for a hand.”

“Shoulder.”

“Furniture.”

Ama opened the uncertainty policy fixed before the run. A count required independently sustained
load paths consistent with human mass and movement. The present field supported four. Posture,
assistance, injury and restraint required geometry the query had not earned.

She changed the product name to `LOAD_PATH_D` and removed every interpretation from the finding.

Dries restored `person_count: 4` in the summary.

“That is supported,” he said.

“At what threshold?”

“The registered one.”

“For what duration?”

“Three continuous, one intermittent.”

“Then say that.”

The certified result became:

`FOUR DISTINCT HUMAN-COMPATIBLE LOAD PATHS.`  
`THREE CONTINUOUS. ONE INTERMITTENT.`

It was the first stable physical statement about the historical event.

Kiki printed it without the paths first. The page looked like an inventory result. Four lines of
type, a custody root and six signatures occupied less space than the warnings explaining what the
count did not establish.

Lwazi asked to see the visual field again.

Ama left the four paths as unfilled volumes. Dries had coloured them during debugging so he could
follow crossings, but colour made them look like four persistent bodies moving through a room. The
physical result was less obliging. A path widened when the floor lost resolution, narrowed at the
surviving wall and vanished where the present structure no longer constrained it.

“People will call these four suspects,” Lwazi said.

“Then the release says human-compatible load paths every time,” Kiki replied.

“They will shorten it.”

“We do not shorten it for them first.”

It answered none of the family's questions.

Presence remained unresolved because the Anchor constrained Path D without establishing the missing
man's identity. Departure remained unresolved because all four paths ended inside uncertainty before
the bounded interval closed. Accountability remained unresolved because a count was not a name.

The government briefing group requested access to the omitted interpretations. Kiki supplied the
public uncertainty map instead.

Jacobs asked whether `person_count: 4` could support an operational list of possible identities.

“It can support the number four,” Ama said.

“A number limits the list.”

“To everyone who might have been there.”

“That is not everyone.”

“It is not evidence of which four.”

He accepted the answer in the manner of someone preserving the question for a different office.

The daughter received the finding through her attorney before the briefing group met.

Her response came back as a handwritten note scanned into the custody file.

`FOUR INCLUDING HIM, OR FOUR AND HIM?`

Ama read the question aloud.

Nobody could answer.

The attorney had included a photograph of the paper on the daughter's kitchen table. The empty
tobacco tin held down one corner. Ama closed the image before the system could retain it with the
technical product.

For the first time since the history converged, the four paths became a number that could be too
large or too small for one family. If D was the missing man, three other people had shared the room.
If D was not, then the Anchor had followed a material route beside an event containing four unknown
people and the first result had found everybody except its subject.

Dries wanted to answer with probability. The identity threshold had been fixed; Path D sat below it
by less than three per cent. A sentence beginning *most likely* could cross the distance in ordinary
language.

Kiki opened the signed questions and put the threshold beside them.

The sentence remained unwritten.

They spent another day trying to distinguish whether the Anchor participated directly in Path D or
only shared the same material environment. The distinction did not converge. More compute narrowed
position without narrowing identity.

At the review, Dries proposed retaining the “held up” interpretation as an internal hypothesis.

Ama allowed it into the uncertainty ledger with three competing explanations beside it and no
preferred rank.

“You think I am wrong,” he said.

“I think you may be right before the evidence is.”

On the wall, four paths crossed the room without faces, names or motives. Path D weakened, returned
and weakened again.

The machine had counted the living weight in the room.

It had not yet found the man the family had lost.
