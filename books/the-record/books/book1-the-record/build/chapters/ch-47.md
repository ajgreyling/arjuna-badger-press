# Chapter 47

The instruction to Ghana arrived as a request between ministries.

It asked the university to suspend processing of all South African historical material connected to
the closed consortium, preserve existing records and cooperate with the criminal review. The language
acknowledged Ghanaian sovereignty in the first paragraph and described the consequences of using it
in the second.

Ama read the request in the ingestion hall while the racks waited.

South Africa could close its instruments, seize its Anchor and disable its cloud accounts. It could
not require Ghana to delete what Ghana already held. Ama had refused that clause when the consortium
agreement was drafted, at the time for reasons she called resilience and everyone else called
difficult.

The clean lane would use no seized material. One path began with demoted fragments preserved from the
first Rain, each fixed before the historical case was named. The second began with fresh scans of the
engineering-yard cores, transported under the owner's custody root. Their instruments, calibration
histories and contamination fields did not overlap.

If both converged on the same event geometry, neither the family's token nor the closed consortium
would be the sole source of the finding.

Ama wrote the job notice before she opened the queue.

She named herself as authorising officer. She attached the South African request, the family's
execution schedule, Kiki's substitute quorum and the expected grid draw. She sent copies to her
university, Ghana's ministry, Naledi's office and the Nairobi public mirror.

Defiance without provenance would become another priority exception.

The university director called.

“Delay until the ministry responds.”

“The response is already in the packet.”

“Our ministry.”

“The lane is scheduled for tonight's power window.”

“You are not personally authorised to create a diplomatic dispute.”

Ama looked at the signature field. “Then the dispute can identify me accurately.”

She signed.

At 22:00, the night operator moved two ordinary research jobs behind the historical run and brought
the racks to load. Cooling demand rose. A transfer switch shifted part of the hall onto the
university's stored-power allocation. The cost display attached electricity, deferred jobs and
carbon intensity to the root.

Before the first result arrived, the ministry requested Ama's access badge. The university froze her
travel. South Africa's diplomatic note described the run as interference with an active court
process.

The racks stayed live.

An administrator telephoned the night operator and instructed him to halt them. He asked for the
signed execution order. She said it would follow. He said the current signed order came from Ama and
continued the run while waiting.

By morning, the demoted path recovered the floor transition, vehicle load and post-impact cleaning
sequence at reduced resolution.

The stone path recovered them independently.

Their uncertainty fields overlapped around the fall, diverged at the demolished threshold and
converged again on the second vehicle arrival. Neither contained a face. Neither supplied the missing
hand.

Ama published the convergence receipt to Nairobi before opening the detailed products. Four quorum
keys released the checkpoint. The daughter watched from the rented office as Dries's local solver
accepted both paths.

The university director arrived in person with campus security and a written suspension of Ama's
badge. She signed the receipt for it and remained beside the operator. The badge controlled doors,
not authorship of a job already running under the public root.

“You are putting yourself outside the institution,” the director said.

“I am not outside the pipeline,” Ama replied. “There is no outside.”

At noon, the convergence held through a second calibration.

Ghana and South Africa recalled senior diplomats. The university opened disciplinary proceedings.
The Rain kept falling into a record no single node could close.
