# Chapter 14

Kiki refused certification at 17:26.

The refusal occupied one page.

She wrote it in the room after everyone left, using the same table whose displaced edge had become
the most important seventeen millimetres in Stellenbosch. The grit remained bagged beside her. The
post-run measurements were signed. The reconstruction root had been published before inspection.
Every person who entered after the seal was cut appeared in the custody log.

None of that repaired what happened before the experiment began.

At 17:31, Lwazi returned.

He did not knock because the room had no rule about doors now.

“You refused it.”

“I did.”

“On what basis?”

Kiki turned the page toward him.

He did not read beyond the heading. “The edge was anchored before we opened the room.”

“The reconstruction was.”

“The room state matches it.”

“The inspected room state matches a stated portion of the reconstruction.”

“Seventeen millimetres.”

“Within six.”

“A movement nobody recorded.”

“A movement absent from the approved conventional records.”

“What more do you want?”

“The experiment root anchored publicly before the action window.”

He picked up the refusal and read it standing. The isolated oscillator's initial state, the sealed
action-key commitment and the sensor-registration package had been signed across the consortium
before the run. Their common root lived in Nairobi's Witness Protocol ledger and in mirrors at the
five nodes. It had not been published to an archive outside consortium control until after the room
was opened.

“All five nodes would have to collude,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Ghana, Nairobi, Karoo and Technopark would have to rewrite their histories in agreement.”

“And Antarctica, though its participation was null.”

“That is absurd.”

“It is unlikely.”

“No. It is absurd.”

“Absurd is not excluded.”

Lwazi placed the paper on the table carefully, as if anger might contaminate the evidence.

“We have an independently measured physical displacement that appears in sensor families with no
common hardware, after a blind action, in a sealed room.”

“Yes.”

“And you believe we substituted the experiment root.”

“No.”

“Then what are we doing?”

“Separating what I believe from what the protocol can establish.”

“The protocol exists to protect the science.”

“The protocol exists to expose the grounds on which the science may be trusted.”

“This is why people hate auditors.”

“People hate auditors because they encounter us after deciding what happened.”

He walked to the wall where the table plan had been marked. The expected edge remained in blue tape.
The measured edge, seventeen millimetres away, was marked in red.

“If we announce,” he said, “other laboratories can attempt replication.”

“Announce what?”

“That a blind persistence test recovered an unrecorded displacement.”

“Without certification, you can say the consortium observed a reconstruction consistent with the
post-run room state.”

“That sentence is designed to die before the second paragraph.”

“It is designed to survive the first hostile question.”

“You are protecting procedure from discovery.”

Kiki looked at the two lines on the wall.

The discovery was probably real. She knew this with the same private conviction that made refusal
necessary. If she felt nothing, the signature would be easy. The danger began when belief became a
reason to lower the bar for the evidence that produced it.

“Discovery without procedure is a story powerful people can choose to believe,” she said.

“This story embarrasses nobody.”

“Not yet.”

The word altered the room.

Lwazi looked at her then and saw past the delayed announcement to the instrument pointed somewhere
outside a training room. A custody dispute. A grave. A sacred object. A classified site. In each
case, somebody would prefer the result and somebody would prefer the flaw.

“You think they will use this refusal against us later,” he said.

“They should.”

“Even if the result is true.”

“Especially then. False results are easier.”

He read the final paragraph.

Kiki had not rejected the data. She had not alleged misconduct. She had certified the integrity of
each submitted record and refused the claim that their agreement excluded after-the-fact consortium
substitution.

“What would the repeat require?” he asked.

She gave him the second page.

Public pre-registration before sealing. External anchoring of the action commitment and clock root.
Two physically separated ingestion paths. Fewer objects. Mechanical selection for most actions. No
researcher with access to both the action key and reconstruction output. A published uncertainty
policy fixed before the solver ran.

“This will take weeks.”

“Yes.”

“Karoo will lose its instrument window.”

“Then the next window.”

“The university wants an announcement tomorrow.”

“The university is not part of the quorum.”

“They own the room.”

Kiki looked around. The smart switch had been removed. The access point was gone. The table now had
an evidence label. Ownership had not made the room authoritative.

“They can announce the room,” she said.

One corner of Lwazi's mouth lifted. He took the two pages and went to the door.

“For what it is worth,” Kiki said, “I think you found it.”

He stopped.

“That makes this worse,” he said.

“I know.”

After he left, Kiki signed the refusal and published it beside the reconstruction root.
