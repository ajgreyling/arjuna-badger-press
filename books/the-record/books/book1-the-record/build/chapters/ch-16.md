# Chapter 16

The second object was below the floor.

Ama noticed it after the certification package had closed.

The public result contained only the supported 2.7-second interval: table edge, cylinder, incomplete
motion volume and the fixed uncertainty field. Ghana retained the rest of the inversion products,
including every fragment too weak or contradictory to enter the claim.

One of those fragments persisted nine centimetres beneath the registered floor plane.

It had no stable geometry. In some transforms it appeared as a narrow dark mass. In others it became
two separated volumes or vanished entirely. Its confidence was too low to call it an object and too
structured to dismiss as ordinary haze.

Ama demoted it.

The queue raised it again on the next run.

She changed the time bound. It remained.

Changed the room coordinates by half a metre. It weakened but did not move with the sealed-room
geometry. Removed the operator interval. The table, cylinder and motion path vanished. The lower
fragment did not.

At 02:10, Accra's night operator found her still at the control desk and placed a cup beside her
without asking what she wanted. The racks drew steady power. Rain from an actual storm moved over the
roof in waves, overwhelming the office microphones and changing nothing in the registered products
from Stellenbosch.

Ama called Technopark.

Dries answered on the fourth attempt, camera off.

“The certified result is wrong,” she said.

He came fully awake. “How?”

“Not the claim. The merge.”

“That sentence is designed to kill sleep.”

She shared the lower fragment.

He was silent long enough to load it locally. “Floor reflection?”

“No.”

“Registered material beneath the slab?”

“Not in the building plan.”

“Construction debris.”

“Maybe.”

“Then why is the result wrong?”

“Because it does not belong to the same decay model as the table. Ghana delivered it in the same
query package because the coordinates overlap. Technopark rendered the layers together because your
solver separates space better than time.”

Dries rotated the combined field. The lower shape remained beneath the table, inviting the eye to
interpret a relationship the evidence did not support.

“If we publish this view,” Ama said, “people will say the operator moved it.”

“The operator could not reach through the floor.”

“People will say the machine saw through the floor. That is worse.”

“It does see through—”

“No. It constrains prior states from present residue. Do not let the renderer teach you its own
metaphor.”

She split the products. The table interval occupied a narrow recent layer. The fragment spread across
a much broader temporal range whose upper bound included the experiment and whose lower bound did
not converge.

“How old?” Dries asked.

“The ingestion layer cannot answer.”

“Karoo?”

“Can tell us the transition is physically structured. Not when.”

“The isolated oscillator?”

“It dates the run. The fragment is not the run.”

The answer left one node.

Antarctica maintained long-baseline temporal references whose noise environment was older and quieter
than any instrument available to the consortium in ordinary operation. Sanna had refused inclusion
in the cheap experiment because there was nothing yet that justified the calibration cost.

Now there was.

Dries drafted a request while Ama assembled the evidence package. She removed every label that could
suggest table, room or buried object. Sanna would receive correlation structure, coordinates, the
admissible temporal range and the models already killed. She would not receive the interpretation
Technopark wanted.

At 02:47, the Antarctic link showed a scheduled outage in thirty-three minutes. The station's final
flight window was under weather review. After it closed, no replacement equipment would reach them
for months.

Ama signed the request and sent it through Nairobi.

Kiki returned it four minutes later.

`DEFINE THE CLAIM ANTARCTICA`  
`IS BEING ASKED TO TEST.`

Ama changed `date the anomalous object` to:

`Determine whether one admissible temporal model can contain`  
`both registered correlation families.`

Kiki signed.

The package reached the station with twelve minutes left in the radio window.

Sanna acknowledged receipt in one line.

`YOU HAVE NOT FOUND AN OBJECT.`  
`YOU HAVE FAILED TO SEPARATE TWO DATES.`

Ama read it twice.

Then the Antarctic link opened an audio channel with nine minutes left.

“Why did you retain it?” Sanna asked.

“Retain what?”

“The lower family. It failed your certified claim.”

“Failure is a storage class.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Ghana demotes unsupported structure. We do not delete it merely because the current query cannot
use it.”

Sanna requested the first ingestion receipt. Ama supplied it. The unresolved layer had entered the
Rain before the table reconstruction succeeded, before anybody had a reason to want a buried object
and before Technopark rendered the two families together.

“You kept a result you did not believe,” Sanna said.

“I kept a fragment that had not earned belief.”

The channel held only station noise for several seconds.

“Send every demotion,” Sanna said. “Not only the fragments your model raises again.”

The request would triple the Antarctic package.

Ama sent it.

Then she changed the fragment's label from `subfloor_anomaly` to `unresolved_layer`.
