# Chapter 30

Ama prepared the answers in the order the family had asked them.

The first rested on three independent constraints. The stable phonetic segment reduced the possible
name-bearers to one person connected to the place and period. The brass token carried his employment
number and material participation in the bounded history. Within that history, the Anchor travelled
with Path D before and during the fall.

No constraint alone established identity. Together they crossed the confidence threshold fixed
before the run.

`PRESENCE: SUPPORTED.`

The second answer required a biological boundary. Lucid found no respiratory pressure sequence,
self-supported load or other sign of autonomous movement associated with Path D after the impact.
The mass left the site only during transfer into the vehicle on its return.

The medical reviewers would certify no more than the question required.

`DEPARTURE ALIVE: NOT SUPPORTED.`

The third answer remained open. Four load paths had occupied the room, but none of the other three
had yet crossed the registered identity threshold.

Ama printed the findings and their uncertainty maps. She did not bring a reconstruction.

The family met them at the attorney's office after closing time. The pharmacy downstairs pulled its
security gate across the entrance while Ama arranged the pages. The daughter came with her mother
and an uncle. Kiki sat beside the attorney. No one from the state had been told the meeting time.

Ama began standing.

“Please sit,” the daughter said.

Ama sat.

She explained how the first finding joined the name, the token and the physical path. The mother
asked whether the result would survive without the token. Ama said the location and name would
remain, but identity would fall below the agreed threshold.

“So the object mattered.”

“Yes.”

“And it was with him.”

“The supported history places it with him.”

The daughter touched the tobacco tin through her bag. “Answer the question.”

Ama put aside the derivation.

“Yes. Your father was physically present at the site.”

The mother closed her eyes. Nobody spoke for long enough that Ama began counting the seconds and
hated herself for noticing.

She gave the second answer after the attorney nodded.

“He did not leave the site alive.”

The uncle stood, walked once to the office door and returned to his chair. The daughter remained
still.

“Do you know where they took him?” the mother asked.

“No. The authorised coordinates end at the site boundary.”

“Can you find out?”

“A new query may be able to follow the vehicle. We did not ask it.”

The daughter looked at her. “Because we didn't ask.”

“Yes.”

It was the first answer that seemed to help and Ama did not trust that impression.

She told them the third question was pending. Other people had been present. Their identities were
not yet established, and no name would be supplied on inference alone.

The daughter asked, “Was he afraid?”

Ama had expected the question because the consent instrument expected it. Preparation did nothing
to make the room easier.

“We did not reconstruct his experience.”

“Could you?”

“The system may find physical signals compatible with distress. It could not know every part of what
he experienced.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

Ama felt relief that clause 4 had held before anyone tested it in this room. Shame followed close
enough to feel like the same response.

The attorney turned the query terminal towards the daughter. It displayed only the three authorised
questions and a blank submission field.

“Ask it,” she said.

The daughter typed: `WAS HE AFRAID?`

Lucid returned before compute began.

`REFUSED: QUERY OUTSIDE CONSENTED BOUNDARY.`

Below the refusal, the interface cited the exclusion of perception, sensation, distress and final
awareness. It offered no answer and no estimate.

The daughter read the receipt twice.

“Keep that,” she said.

Kiki asked whether she meant the refusal.

“With the other answers. If they ever say we wanted to know everything, keep that.”

The attorney requested a signed copy under the same custody root as the findings.

Ama remained after the pages were collected and the terminal closed. There was nothing useful left
for her to explain. The mother asked who would tell the government. The attorney said she would,
after the family had gone home.

At the door, the daughter stopped beside Ama.

“Pending means you will ask again?”

“Only the third question. Under the same boundary.”

“Then ask it.”

Ama watched the family descend the narrow stairs above the pharmacy. She had arrived carrying three
answers. She left with permission for one.
