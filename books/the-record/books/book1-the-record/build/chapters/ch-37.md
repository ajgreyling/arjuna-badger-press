# Chapter 37

Ama recognised the signing authority because she had approved it.

The key belonged to a regional records-continuity firm, accredited after a coastal flood destroyed
museum collections, clinic samples and two municipal archives while their emergency requests waited
behind commercial jobs. The firm was not a shell. It had thirty-two employees, audited offices in
three countries and a decade of legitimate recoveries.

Its authority came through Ghana's emergency queue exception.

Ama had written the exception herself.

An accredited broker could move one small job ahead of the ordinary queue when delay threatened
irreversible loss of an Anchor. The broker authenticated its own assessment, not the identity of its
client. That separation had been deliberate: a clinic should not have to name a patient, and an
archive should not expose a vulnerable depositor merely to preserve material before rain or heat
destroyed it.

The external query had used every field correctly.

Live continuity risk: attested.

Compute allotment: within limit.

Broker signature: valid.

Client: sealed from the operators.

No credential had been stolen. No threshold had been bypassed. Somebody had purchased privacy from
a safeguard built for people who could be harmed by disclosure.

Ama called the firm's duty officer. He remembered the job because the historical coordinate had
appeared in the news, but his records contained only a sealed client token and three payment
receipts. The request had entered through an authenticated institutional channel.

“Was there an Anchor at risk?” Ama asked.

“The client supplied an attestation from a licensed custodian.”

“Did you inspect it?”

“We are not permitted to learn the protected material unless the risk cannot be assessed otherwise.”

“So you verified the custodian, not the Anchor.”

“That is the privacy design.”

He was neither evasive nor pleased. His firm had followed a rule whose legitimacy depended on not
opening the client box whenever an operator became curious.

Ama requested the payment path instead. Three ordinary institutional transfers had funded the
allotment, each below enhanced review. One originated from a commodity desk already listed among the
consortium's sponsors. It served dozens of clients and disclosed none.

The desk's sponsor identifier also supplied the delayed market feed to the Ethical Casino, the
consortium's fake-chip teaching tool. That agreement was public, no-edge and restricted to market
candles. It explained why the desk was in their graph. It did not explain the query.

The trail ended in normality.

Technopark wanted the broker's authority revoked. Naledi's office wanted its client list under seal.
Kiki warned that either action would punish every clinic and archive using the same blind. The
broker's lawyers reminded them that client confidentiality was not evidence of conspiracy.

Ama opened the queue's full decision history.

She had described the system as neutral because its scheduler did not know race, wealth, party,
nationality or institutional prestige. It knew urgency classes, compute bids, statutory priorities
and continuity risk. Those categories had arrived from a world where some institutions could afford
lawyers to define urgency and others submitted a paragraph through a broken portal.

The rule did not know who people were. It knew which paperwork powerful people could buy.

She published the history under her own name: every priority change, every delayed request and every
reason. A doctoral soil study had waited behind a sponsor calibration because the sponsor's contract
contained a service guarantee. A public archive had waited behind a private medical claim because
its loss estimate arrived without an accredited signature. The family query had moved ahead of both
after Ama made an ethical determination no scheduler could have reached.

Some decisions remained defensible. Others improved only when their subjects stayed abstract.

The university asked her to delay publication pending reputational review. Ama published before the
meeting ended.

Then she rewrote the emergency exception.

Client identity would remain sealed. Emergency priority would require two independent attestations:
one from the continuity broker and one from a domain custodian with no financial link to the client.
Neither would reveal protected material to Ghana. The second signature would slow the queue and make
collusion harder. It would not make collusion impossible.

At the node review, Dries called it compromise.

“It is plumbing,” Ama said.

“Plumbing nobody can exploit?”

“There is no such plumbing.”

He asked whether the original exception had been neutral.

“Neutral means the rule does not know who you are,” she said. “It does not mean nobody taught the
rule who matters.”

The revised exception went live that night.

Six days later, a small archive requested emergency priority for water-damaged recordings. Its
independent custodian was travelling and could not sign before the material degraded. Under the old
rule, the job would have entered immediately. Under Ama's repair, it waited fourteen hours and lost
three reels.

She added the loss to the public history.

The purchased tunnel had narrowed.

It had not become free.
