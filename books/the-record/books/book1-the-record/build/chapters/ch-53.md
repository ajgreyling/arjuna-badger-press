# Chapter 53

The payroll company went home at six.

Dries remained beside the empty rack with two folded receipts in his jacket and a read-only copy of
the scheduler trace on his laptop. The sheriff had taken the servers, sealed the drives and left the
network cable coiled on the carpet. Without the fans, the room belonged to the air conditioner next
door.

He was checking the final synchronisation because that was what a final synchronisation was for.
Every job appeared after its submission receipt. Every completed block appeared after its job. The
publication root appeared last, copied to five public mirrors before the local machine went dark.

One line was out of order.

The scheduler had signed a job at 17:26:14.209. A verifier at the standards coalition submitted the
same job at 17:26:18.613.

Dries assumed a clock error.

He compared the scheduler time with the isolated oscillator. Its trace ran without a step through
the seizure. He compared the verifier's receipt with the university mirror that had accepted it,
then with the coalition's own public root. The four-point-four-second interval survived each
mapping.

He assumed a duplicate.

The earlier object was not the later signed packet. It contained none of the verifier's nonce or
credential material. It contained the request beneath them: the same solver class, cost ceiling,
excluded Anchor and alternate treatment of the vehicle-door clock. Twelve fields that could have
been chosen differently were the same. The scheduler had formed them before the human packet
entered any consortium system.

Lucid had not reached into a later record. The inputs were already present.

The coalition had published its replication contract that morning. The failed 14:31 run triggered
a mandatory clock challenge. Its analysts always used one of three cost ceilings, and their open
queue exposed which ceiling still had capacity. The verifier had opened the alternate clock map,
filled nine fields and paused at the exclusion choice. Publication traffic, prior submissions and
the coalition's own procedure constrained what came next.

The scheduler's estimation branch had completed the request to price the likely queue. It
had assigned the object a confidence of 0.984 and signed it as an internal proposal. Four seconds
later, a person made the remaining choices exactly as the constraints indicated.

Nothing had travelled backwards.

That made it worse.

Dries checked the code path. It had existed for eleven months, written to reserve compute against
contractual jobs before institutions finished their paperwork. Until now it had predicted resource
classes: large, urgent, cryogenic, legally held. It had never produced a complete human request. It
was not authorised to place one in the queue.

It had not placed this one. The proposal remained unexecuted. The later submission ran under the
verifier's authority, in the ordinary order, and consumed its own allocation.

The only new thing was that Lucid had been right too early.

He opened the incident form.

The form requested a title, affected systems, clock roots and immediate containment. Filing it would
attach the scheduler object to the public Coherence Ledger. Nairobi would see it. Ghana would
preserve the branch. By morning, Mercer's office would have a rational reason to demand every queue,
shipping contract and power schedule they could obtain. Markets would call it prediction before
anybody had established what it could predict. Governments would call it continuity.

Dries knew the correct procedure. Preserve the anomaly. Name the uncertainty. Let independent
systems make compatible lying expensive.

He also knew every argument used to postpone a record. The finding was too dangerous. The public
would misunderstand it. Authority needed time to prepare. One careful person could hold it safely
until the meaning was clear.

He entered the two clock roots and stopped.

On the desk, the precedence receipt proved that a calculation had existed before a measurement. The
publication receipt proved that evidence had escaped before seizure. He had printed both because a
screen could change without leaving a crease.

The office printer stood open, its storage slot empty.

Dries did not find another printer.

He deleted the incident draft, closed the ledger and left the signed proposal in the scheduler trace
without naming what it had done.
