# Chapter 40

For six weeks, Sanna eliminated explanations.

Outside the Antarctic station, winter reduced direction to instrument readings. The sun remained
below the horizon. Wind buried the marked route between the main building and ARIADNE twice a day,
and each trip began with a radio check, a clipped line and another person watching through glass
until she reached the next door.

Inside, the Anchor carried two dates that refused to become one.

The disappearance interval survived every independent correlation. The stopped watch indicated a
later time. The consortium director had treated the disagreement as residual alignment error and
asked Sanna to widen the event window until both fitted. She refused. A wider window would hide the
conflict without resolving it.

Her first model blamed the watch.

It failed because the trapped hands, damaged gear train and magnetic remanence agreed on the stop
state.

Her second model blamed Antarctica.

It failed against three calibration histories and one result she wished had failed with them.

The third treated the fused token and watch fragment as a single object throughout their history.
That model produced a clean date, the wrong room and impossible corrosion.

Sanna printed its failure map and pinned it above her desk.

The court order prevented new work on the seized Anchor, but the certified scans and temporal
products remained available for review. She divided them by material interface. Brass carried the
older contact history most strongly. Steel carried a later magnetic environment. Degraded lubricant
retained a thermal sequence shared by neither. The glass edge held both, separated by a fracture.

One object did not imply one event.

The fourth model allowed the token, watch and glass to meet after the disappearance.

It was wrong too. Microscopic wear showed that token and watch had travelled together before their
damaging fusion, while the glass joined later. Sanna revised the order and lost four days to a clock
drift in her own local cache. The backup proved the drift. She started again from the signed source
products.

She reported the local failure before she knew whether it changed the result. Her director advised
her to correct the cache quietly because the signed source remained clean. Sanna entered the drift
in the station log and sent the affected product list to Ghana.

Ama answered during the next narrow data window.

`ACCRA WILL REPLAY EVERY CONSUMED TRANSFORM.`  
`DO NOT SEND A CLEANED LIST.`

The replay found two products whose values were correct but whose local order had been inherited
from Sanna's bad cache. Neither altered the model. Ama preserved the mismatch anyway.

`YOU MAY CITE GHANA AS INDEPENDENT CHECK.`

Sanna changed the draft from `local error did not propagate` to `independent replay found no value
propagation and two ordering conflicts.` It was longer and true in more places.

By week five, the later layer constrained an interior space with fluorescent lighting, a persistent
magnetic field from shelving or electrical plant, and long periods of stillness interrupted by two
movements years apart. It resembled storage, not interrogation.

She asked Kiki for public inventories of evidence facilities used after the temporary installation
closed. Kiki supplied building plans and retention schedules without the restricted case files.
Sanna did not search for the missing man's name.

The station mechanic helped her reproduce the compact-shelving field using an obsolete motor stored
for parts. He knew nothing about the Anchor. Sanna gave him the frequency, duty cycle and distance;
he mounted the motor beside a steel cabinet and complained that her requested load would strip a
gear. The gear stripped after nineteen minutes.

Its magnetic transient did not match.

He replaced the damaged gear from a coffee tin of salvaged parts and altered the rail pressure.
The second run entered the same family as the later layer. Sanna registered his name as experimental
operator. He asked whether that meant he would be in the paper.

“If the model survives,” she said.

“Then I hope it survives. That motor has been useless for six years.”

One former store matched the lighting frequency, wall orientation and magnetic field created by an
obsolete compact shelving motor. Its maintenance archive recorded an evening failure at 21:14,
followed by manual movement of several shelves and heat damage in a jammed motor housing. The store
was decommissioned years later. Its contents had been dispersed under incomplete transfer lists.

Sanna added the building only after fixing the physical result.

The model converged.

The Anchor had participated in the disappearance, travelled elsewhere, entered an evidence store and
been damaged during the later storage event. The watch hands stopped when the mechanism was damaged.
They did not record the missing man's death. Decades of stillness in the store formed the second
temporal layer that had confused the first inversion.

No information had arrived before its cause. No event had travelled backwards. Matter had simply
participated in more than one history.

Sanna wrote the finding without those reassurances. The measurements did not need to deny a time
loop nobody had established.

`STOPPED WATCH STATE DATES LATER MATERIAL DAMAGE,`  
`NOT THE DISAPPEARANCE INTERVAL.`

The consortium director declined to sign. He argued that identifying the evidence store from public
building records exceeded temporal analysis and risked contaminating the result with archive
expectation.

Sanna removed the store identity from the primary finding. The material layers remained.

He declined again.

She signed alone and attached every failed model, including her local clock drift. Antarctica's
governance board accepted the result for external review without consortium certification.

Ama signed no temporal claim. Ghana signed that Sanna's cited inputs, rejected transform and replay
history were the products she said they were. The narrower signature mattered more to Sanna than an
endorsement would have.

At dinner, two colleagues discussed the weather until she left. A scheduled promotion interview
disappeared from her calendar. The next ship was four months away.

The mechanic had left the stripped gear beside her plate with its teeth marked in red. On the back
of the maintenance tag he had written the run number and `NOT USELESS`. Sanna carried it to ARIADNE
because throwing it away would alter the custody joke and because she did not know where else to put
it.

Sanna walked back to ARIADNE under a rope stiff with ice. Nothing in the station had changed because
she was right. The same doors required the same gloves; the same wind pushed at the same shoulder.

On her screen, the two dates remained separate.

That was the result.
