# Chapter 21

The photograph arrived face down.

The missing man's daughter placed it on the table between Ama and Kiki and kept her fingers on one
corner.

The meeting took place in the attorney's office above the pharmacy. No screen. No projector. No
government representative. Ama had brought a paper diagram of the five consortium nodes and left it
inside her folder because even that looked too much like a presentation once the family entered.

There were three of them: the daughter, her older cousin and the attorney. None introduced themselves
through their relationship to the missing man. They gave their names, asked how long the meeting
would take and declined the coffee offered by reception.

Ama explained the result again.

The daughter stopped her twice.

“When you say the name was present, you mean somebody said it?”

“The physical residue constrains a phonetic segment compatible with it.”

“That means yes.”

“It means the segment is consistent with the name. We cannot identify the speaker or the person
addressed.”

“Could it be another man with the same name?”

“Yes.”

“Could your machine be wrong?”

“Yes.”

The daughter looked at Kiki. “And you?”

“I can establish which records the consortium used and what operations were performed,” Kiki said.
“I cannot certify that their interpretation is true.”

“Then why are you here?”

“To prevent them from telling you those are the same thing.”

The cousin laughed once. It was not a friendly sound, but it made the room less formal.

The attorney asked what additional evidence would narrow identity.

Ama described anchors without using the word as if it were magic. Matter known to have participated
in an event could constrain the inversion coordinates and reduce admissible histories. An object did
not store a video. It joined a distributed record already present in physical consequence.

“You want something of his,” the daughter said.

“Not today.”

“But eventually.”

“Only if you authorise a bounded query.”

The daughter turned the photograph over.

It showed a man beside a car, one hand resting on the open door. He was younger than Ama had imagined
and less solemn than the inquiry photographs. Sun narrowed his eyes. A child had been cropped from
the lower edge badly enough that one shoe remained.

The daughter pushed it toward them.

“There,” she said. “Now you know his face.”

Ama looked.

The ordinary intimacy of the image was more dangerous than any classified file. It supplied a body,
a posture, an age, the relationship of a hand to a car door. Feed it to the solver and every
ambiguous volume would begin to resemble him. The machine might produce a more recognisable answer
and become less truthful in exactly the same operation.

Kiki did not touch the photograph.

“We cannot scan this,” Ama said.

The cousin leaned forward. “You just said you need evidence.”

“We need constraint from the event. This is memory of the person.”

“It is a physical object.”

“Yes, but not from the place we are querying. If we give the reconstruction his face before it has
earned one, we will not know whether the residue identified him or the software obeyed us.”

The daughter's hand remained on the table.

“So you came here to ask who he was,” she said, “and now you do not want to know what he looked
like.”

“I want to know. The instrument must not.”

That was the first sentence Ama said in the meeting that did not require correction.

The daughter drew the photograph back. She studied it herself for a moment before returning it to an
envelope.

“What can it answer without the picture?”

Ama gave the smallest honest list. Whether a person occupied the site in the bounded interval.
Whether that person left. Whether other physically distinguishable people were present. Not what he
thought. Not what he feared. Not why another person acted.

“You can see fear?” the attorney asked.

“Potentially, through physical and experiential consequence at much greater cost and uncertainty.”

“But you will not.”

“Not without explicit authority. We can make the exclusion executable.”

The attorney wrote that down.

At the end of the meeting, the family took the photograph with them.

Nothing entered the machine.

Ama watched the envelope disappear into the daughter's bag and understood that refusing data could
also be a form of measurement: it revealed which answer they were willing to corrupt to obtain.
