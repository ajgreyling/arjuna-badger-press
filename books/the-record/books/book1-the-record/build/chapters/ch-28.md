# Chapter 28

The room remembered the cleaning better than the fall.

Dries found the asymmetry in the job costs. Every attempt to sharpen the seven-tenths of a second
around impact consumed compute and returned the same broken path. When he moved the query forward,
into the hour after Path D reached the floor, the admissible histories collapsed quickly.

There had been water.

There had been a solvent compatible with institutional cleaning stock of the period.

Something absorbent crossed the concrete in repeated arcs. Furniture moved. The human-compatible
mass associated with Path D moved without returning to a self-supported gait.

The vehicle left and came back.

Its second arrival compressed the rear suspension differently from the first. Lucid could not name
the added mass. It could constrain it.

Dries put the two vehicle intervals beside each other. “The door.”

Sanna aligned the panel resonance. The second opening belonged to the return.

This time the door stayed open.

A separate weight appeared at its lower edge and remained there while three load paths crossed
between the room and the yard. The weight did not move like a foot. It was dragged into place and
removed afterwards.

“Held open with something heavy,” Lwazi said.

Ama entered `DOOR OBSTRUCTION: SUPPORTED` and left the object unnamed.

The reconstruction did not show people carrying a body. It showed load transferring from concrete
to moving supports, disappearing at the broken threshold and entering the vehicle suspension. No
single step was complete. Together they left fewer alternatives than the fall itself.

Dries checked the cost panel because the speed bothered him.

“We are spending less.”

“More signal,” Sanna said.

“More time,” Ama added. “They worked in the room for ninety-four minutes.”

Duration explained part of it. It did not explain why the cleaning products resolved before some
ordinary movement. Dries isolated the wipe paths, the water flow, the solvent evaporation and the
repeated shifting of weight. Each operation had changed the room to remove a previous
change.

Landauer had described the minimum cost of erasing a bit. The floor itself was too small to account
for anything they measured here; practical erasure operated many orders of magnitude above it. But
the direction held. Forgetting required physical work. Wiping, carrying, washing and returning with
a vehicle had exported heat and scattered matter into every surface the cleaners touched.

The cover-up had tried to reduce the available record and, by doing so, created more record.

He called the others back.

“We have been treating the Reader as a witness to events,” he said. “It is better at witnessing
work.”

Kiki read the derivation before she answered. “Put the qualification about the thermodynamic floor
in the finding.”

“It will kill the sentence.”

“Then the sentence deserves to die.”

He added it.

The certified products showed a fall that occupied less than a second and an organised alteration
of the site that continued for more than an hour. Someone moved the fallen mass. Someone applied
cleaning agents. The vehicle made a second trip. None of those acts could restore the room to an
earlier state. They could only replace one physical history with a more complicated one.

“Killing him was cheap,” Dries said. “Making him disappear cost them.”

Ama looked up from the finding. “We have not certified killing.”

He heard how quickly he had crossed from result to story.

“The fall was cheap,” he corrected. “The disappearance cost them.”

She accepted that wording for discussion, not for the record.

The post-impact branch finished before dawn. It found no self-supported departure by Path D. It
found the human-compatible mass entering the vehicle during the second opening and leaving the
bounded site without recovering a gait.

The machine could not yet say whose body had been moved.

It could say nobody carried an accident away by accident.
