# Chapter 39

G arrived at Karoo with no luggage and a printed question.

The court order had closed historical compute, not the public Lucid corpus. Ordinary citation and
derivation queries remained available. G carried the page into Lwazi's control room, placed it beside
the instrument logs and asked for a terminal without privileged access.

“You have your own,” Lwazi said.

“Then you would say I prepared the result.”

“If you use mine, you will say I prepared it.”

“Good. We understand custody.”

The question concerned Record: the part of the 420 Code that made state change directional because a
physical distinction, once made and propagated, could not be erased for free. The Anchor now carried
two temporal layers. One aligned with the disappearance. The stopped watch belonged to another
material event whose date and cause remained unresolved.

G asked whether the case data supported the claim that Record established the physical arrow used by
the derivation.

Lucid cited the published Code, Landauer, the consortium's certified temporal conflicts and the
limits on the seized case products.

Then it refused.

`INSUFFICIENT GROUNDED RECORD.`

G narrowed the question. Did the existence of independently ordered material layers support Record
as a necessary explanation?

`NO. MULTIPLE COMPATIBLE EXPLANATIONS REMAIN.`  
`SEE CONFLICT SET.`

The alternatives included later damage to the watch, transfer through a distinct environment,
incorrect temporal alignment and an unmodelled interaction among materials fused in the Anchor.
None required a reversal of time. None established G's account either.

He supplied his own derivation as an explicit premise.

Lucid accepted the document, cited it and marked the inference gap unchanged.

“Ask it conditionally,” Lwazi said. “If the derivation holds, what follows?”

“That would answer a different question.”

“It would give you the extension.”

“It would give me my premise back wearing a result.”

G asked whether any grounded source in the corpus closed the gap between the formal derivation and
the Anchor's layered timestamps.

`NO.`

Whether the 420 Code author's confidence altered the evidence weight.

`NO AUTHOR-WEIGHT RULE IS PRESENT`  
`IN THE REGISTERED INFERENCE POLICY.`

The control room became quiet. Lwazi had spent years arguing with G as if the machine were the
institutional version of the same argument: G's corpus, G's terms, G's kill switches, made executable
and harder to interrupt. Half the consortium treated Lucid's restraint as G's temperament embedded
in software.

The terminal had just refused its author without drama.

G read the receipts from top to bottom. He was not embarrassed. He looked satisfied in the abrasive
way he did when an instrument broke the result everyone had gathered to celebrate.

“Good,” he said. “If it agreed with me for my sake it would be worthless to me.”

Lwazi expected him to leave the matter there. Instead G signed the failed query root and asked for it
to be attached to the public Code discussion.

“People will say the machine rejected the theory,” Lwazi said.

“It rejected the extension.”

“That qualification will not survive either.”

“Then perhaps you should stop offering qualifications only when they help.”

They printed the receipt on the same office printer that had produced the original 420 derivation
check years before. The paper curled as it cooled.

The unknown timestamp remained unknown.

Sanna would have to resolve it from measurement. The corpus had declined to turn authorship into
evidence.
