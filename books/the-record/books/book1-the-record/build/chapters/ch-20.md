# Chapter 20

Ama was given a telephone number and instructed not to explain how they had obtained it.

The number belonged to the missing man's daughter.

She did not call it.

Instead she called the attorney named in the most recent inquiry file, a woman whose practice occupied
two rooms above a pharmacy and whose public biography contained no work on scientific evidence. The
receptionist asked the nature of the matter. Ama said historical inquiry. The receptionist asked
which institution. Ama named the African Record Consortium.

The line went quiet for thirty seconds.

The attorney answered herself.

“Is this call recorded?”

“For consortium audit, yes.”

“Stop it.”

Ama opened the call controls. The recording was mandated for external contacts concerning restricted
research. She could suspend it only by entering a reason that would itself be logged.

She selected `REQUEST OF CONTACTED PARTY`.

The red indicator disappeared.

“It is stopped.”

“Who has seen the result?”

“The technical leads at five consortium nodes, the responsible minister and a limited government
briefing group.”

“Who owns the machines?”

Ama had prepared for questions about the missing man. “No one institution owns the complete
capability.”

“That was not my question.”

“Karoo operates the instruments. Ghana holds ingestion. Technopark operates the inversion engine.
Nairobi controls the witness protocol. Antarctica supplied temporal reference.”

“So five institutions own parts.”

“They hold authority over parts.”

“Has the name entered a government file?”

Ama looked at the briefing notice on her desk. “Yes.”

“Has the family been named in it?”

“I have not seen the distribution copy.”

“Then you do not know.”

“No.”

“What do you want from them?”

The consortium's contact brief said verification of identity, historical context and possible
material anchors. Each phrase turned the family into an instrument component.

“We want to tell them what we found before the state chooses how they hear it,” Ama said.

“Going first does not make the approach ethical.”

“No.”

“It means your institution has chosen the order in which it applies pressure.”

Ama let the correction stand. “If we wait, a government office may contact them first and call it
consultation.”

“And if you call first?”

“We can tell them what the machine does not know.”

The attorney asked Ama to state the result.

She did so without the machine's mythology. At the coordinates of a former security installation,
physical residue constrained a late-apartheid interval. A stable phonetic segment matched the missing
man's name among other admissible bearers. The evidence did not establish his presence, identity,
fate or the identity of the speaker.

“You have a name in a room,” the attorney said.

“A name at a location.”

“And you want the family to make it a man.”

“We want permission to ask whether the evidence can distinguish that.”

“Permission from whom?”

“The surviving family.”

“Does your machine require their permission?”

Ama looked at the Rain moving across the secondary display. Matter did not consult ownership before
retaining consequence. The solver required a query, compute and constraint—not consent.

“No,” she said.

The answer altered the call more than any technical claim had.

“Then why ask?”

“Because capability is not custody.”

The attorney did not reply immediately.

When she did, she set conditions for a first meeting. No reconstruction. No screens. No request for
an object, document, photograph or sample. No government representative. No press office. Two
consortium members only, one of whom must be able to explain what the method could expose beyond the
question being asked.

“They may decide not to proceed,” she said.

“Yes.”

“And if they do?”

Ama could not promise that the state, a court or some later operator would never point another
instrument at the coordinates. She could promise only what this consortium would do now.

“We stop the historical query.”

“Not the research.”

“No.”

“Not other queries at the same place.”

“We can bind those under consortium authority.”

“But not the world.”

“No.”

The attorney wrote something down.

“You are not the first people to arrive early and call that consent,” she said.

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“I know now.”

The meeting was scheduled for the following week.

Ama returned to the consortium call with less access than she had been sent to obtain. No object. No
photograph. No identity confirmation. No promise that the family would authorise anything after
hearing them.

Lwazi called it a delay.

Ama called it the first result the family had produced without entering the machine.
