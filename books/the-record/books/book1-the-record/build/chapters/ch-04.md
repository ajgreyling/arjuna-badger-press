# Chapter 4

By nine o'clock, everybody who had always supported the project had found Dries's email address.

The first message came from the office of the rector. Its subject was `COORDINATION`, capitalised as
if coordination were an emergency service.

The second came from a laboratory in Europe whose previous contribution to the 420 work had been a
reviewer's comment describing it as numerically imaginative. They congratulated the African Record
Consortium on *our shared progress* and offered to host an independent verification environment.
The environment included eighteen thousand GPU-hours, a communications team and a governance board
on which the African nodes would hold two of eleven seats.

The third came from a private science fund. It offered enough compute to remove the consortium's
queue for a year.

In exchange, the fund wanted first access to “non-public derived insights.”

On the side monitor, the consortium's Ethical Casino traded fake chips against delayed real market
candles. Its header carried the disclaimer `NO CLAIMED EDGE`. The teaching tool accepted prices and
public news, never consortium sensor data. It lost money before breakfast and displayed the loss
without apology.

Dries read that phrase twice. Then he created a folder called `LONG-STANDING INTEREST` and moved all
three messages into it.

His office looked over a car park and the blank side of a building renamed so often that nobody
under forty knew what its letters had originally meant. Beyond it, traffic had thickened along the
R44. The mountain had emerged into full daylight, indecently clear, while his inbox reproduced
itself faster than he could classify it.

Stellenbosch wanted a controlled announcement.

The Department of Science wanted a briefing before the controlled announcement.

Another department wanted to know whether the measurement had national-security implications.

The university's insurer wanted confirmation that the work involved no radioactive material,
biological agent or prototype propulsion system.

A documentary producer wanted an exclusive interview with G and had attached a proposed title:
`THE MAN WHO FOUND THE EQUATION OF EVERYTHING`.

Dries forwarded that one to G without comment.

The reply arrived seven seconds later.

`No.`

He filed it under answered correspondence.

At 09:14, Ama called.

She did not greet him. “Why is the public mirror still pointing at yesterday's calculation
package?”

Dries checked. “Because the new package is in the release candidate bucket.”

“The receipts are public. The inputs are public. The calculation that joins them is not.”

“It passed fifteen minutes ago.”

“Then why is it not mirrored?”

He opened the deployment queue. A hold had been placed on the release by university communications,
pending alignment of the technical package with the announcement.

“Coordination,” he said.

“What?”

“Nothing. I found the hold.”

“Whose?”

“Stellenbosch.”

“The number is already public.”

“They are worried the explanation will escape before the statement.”

“The explanation is the part that should escape.”

Dries removed the hold. He had the authority because the release pipeline still treated
communications as advisory. That would change by lunch.

The package propagated to Accra's mirror, then Nairobi's. Ama watched the same status page from her
side.

“Good,” she said. “Now the result belongs to whoever can kill it.”

“That sounds like G.”

“G does not own all the useful sentences.”

She ended the call.

At 09:22, Kiki sent a question in the consortium channel.

`Who is authorised to call this a prediction?`

The responses arrived immediately.

`LWAZI: nobody yet`

`COMMS: consortium spokespeople`  
`once wording agreed`

`DEAN: principal investigators`

`G: the date is authorised`

Kiki ignored all of them and attached a one-page notice. It distinguished the frozen expression,
the later measurement and the present claim of compatibility. Under `AUTHORITY`, she had written:

`The Consortium may attest that the expression was fixed before the measurement and that the stated
comparison was executed. It has no authority to bind external interpretation of the result.`

Dries downloaded it before somebody improved the sentence.

The phone on his desk rang. Not his mobile—the fixed phone supplied with the office, used mostly by
reception when a delivery driver could not find Meson Street.

“Venter.”

“Doctor Venter?”

“No.”

There was a pause. “Mister Venter, then. I am calling on behalf of the Office for Scientific and
Cultural Collections.”

Dries had never heard of it. The caller's accent gave him nowhere useful to place her, and her
formal English sounded prepared rather than foreign.

“Which office?”

She repeated the name without adding a country or institution.

“How can I help?”

“We are trying to understand the scope of the persistence claim discussed in this morning's
release.”

“There is no persistence claim in this morning's release. A constants expression survived a revised
measurement.”

“Of course. We are asking about the broader research programme.”

“That programme is public.”

“The corpus is extensive.”

“Yes.”

“Does the proposition *what has happened cannot unhappen* imply that an object of uncertain
provenance could preserve information about events preceding its documented custody?”

Dries stopped moving.

On his monitor, messages continued entering the long-standing-interest folder. The European lab had
sent a revised governance proposal. The private fund had clarified that first access did not mean
ownership. A second government office wanted a classified briefing to determine whether a
classified briefing was necessary.

“Who are you?” he asked.

The caller gave him a name he did not recognise and repeated the office.

“What collection?”

“The question is general.”

“No general question has ever arrived by fixed line.”

Silence. Not offence. Calculation.

“Several custodial institutions hold material whose physical history is older than, and may differ
from, its catalogue history,” she said. “Some of that material is culturally sensitive. Some is
sacred. Some is disputed. We would welcome assurance that no inference could be made without the
custodian's consent.”

Dries looked at the frozen expression on the dashboard and understood that the woman was not calling
about protons.

“We have no instrument capable of doing what you are describing.”

“Is it impossible?”

The correct answer was yes. The project had no detector, no inversion method and no evidence that
ordinary historical events left anything recoverable. Lwazi would have said the premise was a
formal implication being mistaken for an apparatus. G would have asked for the proposed kill
switch. Kiki would have distinguished impossibility from absence of demonstrated capability.

Dries said, “It has not been demonstrated.”

The pause lasted half a second too long.

“Thank you,” the caller said. “That is helpful.”

“It wasn't intended to be.”

The line went dead.

He searched the office name. It existed in three jurisdictions, attached to unrelated departments.
One administered museum loans. One advised on repatriation claims. One appeared only in a budget
appendix beside funding for archival preservation and interfaith liaison.

He sent the call details to Kiki.

`DRIES: Somebody with sacred objects wants a consent guarantee`  
`for a machine we have not built.`

`KIKI: Can we give one?`

`DRIES: We have not built it.`

`KIKI: That was not my question.`

He left the answer blank.

At 09:47, reception brought him a visitor card for a man waiting downstairs from the national
security liaison office. The card described the purpose of the meeting as `INFORMAL ORIENTATION`.

At 09:49, the European laboratory's director wrote personally to say he had followed G's work for
many years.

Dries searched their correspondence. Three years earlier the same director had declined a data
sharing request with a note stating that the framework lacked sufficient institutional maturity.

He moved the new message into the folder.

At 09:52, the private fund increased its offer.

At 09:54, the university replaced the communications hold Dries had removed with a legal hold he
could not.

At 09:55, Ama's mirror made the technical package available from Ghana anyway.

The national-security liaison was still waiting downstairs.

Dries printed Kiki's notice, folded it once and put it in his jacket pocket beside the precedence
receipt.

Before leaving, he changed the long-standing-interest folder from private to visible across the five
nodes.

There were forty-one messages in it.

By the time he reached reception, there were fifty-three.
