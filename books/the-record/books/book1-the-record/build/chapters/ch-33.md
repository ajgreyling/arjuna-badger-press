# Chapter 33

The unauthorised run had left a receipt.

Kiki found it while checking that Naledi's suspension had propagated through every consortium node.
Four queues reported stopped. Antarctica reported an abandoned alignment. Technopark reported no
active historical jobs and one completed job whose root did not belong to the Witness Protocol.

She assumed a bookkeeping error.

The receipt survived a second pull.

Its query window overlapped the disappearance. Its coordinates described the old security
installation within eleven metres. No Anchor had been supplied. The operator had paid the higher
uncertainty cost and asked the physical field directly.

Kiki checked the authorised ledger. The job was absent.

She checked the government observer keys. Absent.

The execution signature was valid and unknown to the consortium.

The result itself sat behind an external custody key. The receipt exposed only what every Lucid read
had to expose: input class, constraint root, compute consumed, conflicts, output hash and proof that
the read had altered system state. A query could conceal its answer. It could not conceal that it had
asked.

She called the five nodes back into session.

Dries read the receipt without speaking. Ama asked whether the Anchor identifier appeared under a
different field. Sanna verified the timestamp against her own clock. Lwazi asked for the output.

“We don't hold the key,” Kiki said.

“Who does?”

“The receipt doesn't identify a person.”

“Then it isn't much of a receipt.”

“It proves the job existed.”

Ama opened the consent instrument. “Clause 2 prohibits queries at the site outside the three family
questions.”

Kiki read the clause once.

Then again.

The subject was the African Record Consortium. The obligations attached to its nodes, employees,
agents and authorised contractors. The external signer occupied none of those classes.

“It doesn't prohibit this,” she said.

Ama moved closer to her camera. “Of course it does.”

“It prohibits us.”

“The site is the same.”

“The site isn't a party to the instrument.”

The definition appeared on the signature page. They had treated it as an ordinary identification of
the parties, not as an operational path through the solver. An outside laboratory could ignore their
paper. Kiki had assumed it could not acquire a valid authority inside their architecture.

The receipt proved that assumption had never become a rule.

The Anchor belonged to the family. The paper records belonged to archives under their own law. The
residue under an office floor belonged, if it belonged to anyone, to the present landowner. But the
past configuration inferred from those materials had no recognised owner and no mechanism for
consent.

The family had lent them an object. Nobody had granted the family custody of history.

Kiki opened clause 5, looking for a record of what the consortium had withheld. The clause had
worked exactly as drafted. Intermediate products capable of supporting the excluded experiential
layer had been destroyed within the required period. The deletion receipts were intact.

The products were not.

If somebody later accused the authorised team of examining fear or pain before deletion, Kiki could
prove that prohibited products no longer existed. She could not prove that no human had viewed them
first. The mechanism that enforced restraint had burned the evidence of restraint.

Eleven days of drafting had produced a fence around the people who agreed to stand inside it.

“I wrote a warning where we needed a mechanism,” she said.

Dries shook his head. “You cannot bind an unknown operator by contract.”

“Then the query path should have required the family root regardless of operator.”

“It protected the Anchor.”

“The job didn't need the Anchor.”

Nobody answered.

Lwazi requested the solver class from the receipt. It had not asked the family's three questions.
Its output specification requested a human-readable spatial reconstruction with generated
completion permitted where the record failed.

Kiki read the words twice.

“A picture,” Ama said.

“A plausible one,” Dries replied.

The unknown job had consumed less compute than their certified run. It had purchased fluency by
removing the requirement to show uncertainty.

Naledi joined from her office. “Can it be stopped?”

“It completed before your order,” Kiki said.

“Can the result be revoked?”

“We cannot revoke a key we do not control.”

The minister asked who else knew.

Kiki looked at the five node windows, the external signature and the output hash of a reconstruction
none of them could open.

“We built a machine that can't be used secretly,” she said. “That's not the same as one that can't
be used against you.”

Somebody outside the quorum already knew which room to ask about.
