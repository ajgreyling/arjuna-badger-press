# Chapter 24

The first request asked for everything.

It came from the government briefing group twelve hours after the consent instrument was signed:
full spatial resolution, experiential reconstruction where physically supported, identity search
against restricted archives and retention of all intermediate products pending national-security
review.

Dries loaded the request into the scheduler to price it.

Lucid refused before producing an estimate.

`QUERY OUTSIDE CONSENTED BOUNDARY.`

The refusal listed the conflicts. Experiential reconstruction violated clause 4. Restricted-archive
identity search exceeded the authorised evidence sources. Indefinite intermediate retention violated
clause 5. “Everything” did not constitute a bounded question.

Jacobs called three minutes later.

“We need to understand the capability before deciding what protections apply.”

“The capability just explained the protections.”

“Can the restrictions be overridden?”

“Not under this query root.”

“By an administrator?”

“No.”

“By you?”

“No.”

“By G?”

“Especially not by G.”

Dries closed the call before Jacobs could turn the answer into a request for architecture diagrams.

The authorised query contained three questions and no adjectives.

Presence. Departure alive. Other identifiable people.

Ghana opened a narrow ingestion lane and booked nine days of the queue. Three projects moved behind
it. One studied crop-resilient soil transitions. Two belonged to doctoral candidates whose
supervisors had already written to ask why a bereaved family outranked submitted research.

Ama published the queue decision in her own name.

Karoo narrowed its instruments around the registered site coordinates. Antarctica supplied the
historical interval and nothing more. Nairobi attached the executable consent boundary to every
solver request. Technopark received the Anchor scan through a custody interface that allowed material
constraints without transferring ownership.

The token did not contain a scene.

Its brass, steel, corrosion, glass and microscopic mineral inclusions constrained where matter could
have interacted and which admissible histories survived. Remove the Anchor products and the interval
spread. Insert them and millions of past configurations became incompatible with the present
residue. The object was an address into a record distributed across floor, structure, field and
everything touched afterward.

On day one, the scheduler showed 11,408,221 admissible configurations.

There was no image window.

The display showed the questions, active constraints, uncertainty budget, compute consumed and one
integer falling as the solver excluded pasts that could not have produced the surviving evidence.

By evening, 7.2 million remained.

On day two, Ghana found a lineage conflict in one geological product. Restoring the unsupported
fragment increased the count to 8.9 million and erased fourteen hours of apparent progress.

Nobody suggested deleting it again.

The restoration consumed the power window promised to a doctoral soil experiment. Ama called the
candidate before the supervisor could turn the delay into an administrative email. The samples
would remain stable. The field conditions they were meant to model would not; by the time the queue
opened again, the first rains might have changed the site.

“So the family goes ahead of food security,” the candidate said.

“This run goes ahead of your run under an exception I signed,” Ama replied.

“Is that different?”

“Only if the signature remains attached when people decide it was wrong.”

She added the delayed work, estimated seasonal loss and her reason to the public queue history.
Dries saw the new cost appear beside the configuration count.

“That makes the experiment look indefensible,” he said.

“Then it should survive looking indefensible.”

On day three, Antarctica rejected a temporal model that treated the stopped watch as the event
clock. The count fell below four million.

On day four, Dries arrived before sunrise and found six people watching the integer from the
reconstruction room. They had brought chairs from other offices without noticing the irony.

`1,106,442`

The number paused.

Dropped to 604,118.

Paused again.

“When does the picture start developing?” a university observer asked.

“It isn't developing,” Dries said. “It's excluding.”

The observer waited for the interface to contradict him.

It did not.

At 09:17, the scheduler attempted an intermediate search against stress-linked movement because it
would reduce the configuration count more efficiently than geometry alone.

Lucid refused.

`EXPERIENTIAL INFERENCE`  
`OUTSIDE CONSENTED BOUNDARY.`

The solver took the slower route.

At 10:06, one of the commodity processors exceeded its registered thermal limit and dropped from the
pool. The job stopped at the last signed checkpoint. Its unfinished block had consumed ninety-one
minutes and produced nothing the ledger could accept.

Dries wanted to restart on the remaining machines.

Kiki refused until the failed node's local clock, power trace and partial cache were compared with
the checkpoint root. The comparison took two hours. The university observer asked why a failed
computer should delay a result it had not contributed to.

“Because we know it failed,” Kiki said. “That fact is now part of the run.”

The facilities manager brought a portable fan from the server store. Dries registered its serial
number, power draw and position before switching it on. Air moved across four cheap cases in a room
built for meetings. The configuration count resumed where the last proved block had ended.

At noon, 90,004 histories remained.

At seventeen hundred, 311.

Each survived the known material interactions, temporal references, independent sensor families and
three-question limit. They differed in position, number of people, sequence of movement and whether
the man represented by the Anchor had ever occupied the site.

The integer fell to twelve.

Then three.

The last elimination consumed seven hours and enough power for the facilities manager to send an
angry graph of the building load.

At 01:58, the family attorney requested the current refusal history. Kiki sent it without waking the
daughter. The file contained the government's maximal query, the solver's stress-linked shortcut and
two malformed diagnostics Dries had submitted while tracing the failed processor. The machine had
said no to the government, to itself and to him under the same root.

Dries read the three receipts while the final pair of configurations remained. One contained four
load paths. The other contained five, with the fifth surviving only if a mineral inclusion on the
Anchor was treated as contamination.

“Can we rerun the inclusion at a lower weight?” he asked.

Ama said, “Not without changing the rule after seeing what it removes.”

The fifth path stayed admissible until the registered contamination model killed it.

At 02:43 on day five, the display changed.

`ADMISSIBLE CONFIGURATIONS: 1`

Nobody cheered.

The single history was not yet an answer. It was the least past configuration compatible with the
record they had allowed themselves to read.

Dries opened the uncertainty field.

Four load paths crossed the old floor.
