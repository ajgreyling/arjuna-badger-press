# Chapter 44

The second request contained less authority than the first.

Ama told the family what remained: six stone cores from an engineering yard, four commodity servers,
copies of certified products the closure order had not erased and a method that could execute but no
longer certify itself through the consortium.

She also told them what had failed. An outsider had queried the site without their Anchor. The smooth
video had used the old photograph. Government had sealed the authentic findings while denying the
video. The consent instrument had bound only the people who signed it.

The daughter listened with the tobacco tin on the table.

“You found another object,” she said.

“Material from the old building.”

“It isn't ours.”

“No.”

“The machine can run without us.”

“Yes.”

“Then why are you here?”

Ama had used capability and custody as separate words before. The distinction sounded thinner after
the leak.

“Because the questions are still yours,” she said. “And because we have already shown that a rule on
paper does not protect you once the people holding the machine decide it is inconvenient.”

The attorney asked whether the proposed run was lawful.

“The stone sits outside the seized site and is held under its owner's consent. The compute order
prohibits consortium historical work. The consortium has been closed. A court may still find that the
offline run breaches the purpose of the order.”

“That was several sentences for no.”

“It may be unlawful.”

“And you want the family to authorise it.”

“No family consent can make it lawful. We are asking whether we may proceed under the same human
boundary.”

The mother asked what the new material could change.

It could reproduce presence and departure without relying on the token's broken custody. It could
test the other load paths against an independent part of the old site. It might recover enough
sequence to answer accountability. It could also contradict every answer they had given.

“If it does?” the mother asked.

“We bring you the contradiction.”

Nobody tried to call that reassurance.

The attorney placed the original three questions on the table.

Was he present?

Did he leave alive?

Was there supported physical evidence that another person was accountable for his death or
concealment?

“Unchanged,” the daughter said.

Ama agreed.

“No fear. No pain. No last thoughts.”

“Unchanged.”

“No face made from his photograph.”

“No generated faces, voices or surfaces.”

“And I am in the room.”

The attorney turned towards her. “During execution?”

“They keep telling us what the machine refused, what the machine cost, what the machine did while we
waited somewhere else. I am in the room this time.”

Kiki explained that no one person could witness every node. The daughter could sit beside the local
solver, see the requests before submission and hold a stop control whose use would enter the ledger.

“Will it actually stop?”

Dries answered from the call. “At the next checkpoint. The current block may finish, but its output
will not open.”

“Show me before we begin.”

“Yes.”

The family did not sign a new blanket instrument. The attorney attached an execution schedule to the
old one, naming the engineering-yard samples, offline hardware, three questions and the daughter as
an active witness. Every previous failure remained appended. Nothing was superseded.

The daughter signed last.

“This is not trust,” she said.

Ama looked at the document, with its old clauses, new exceptions and recorded damage.

“No,” she replied.

It was permission already partly spent.
