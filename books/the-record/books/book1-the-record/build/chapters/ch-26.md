# Chapter 26

The first sound recovered from the room was a chair being dragged across concrete.

It arrived in Antarctica as a pressure band with most of the frequencies missing. Sanna listened
once through the control-room monitor, then switched the sonification off. The scrape had lasted
less than two seconds. Hearing it again would add nothing to the measurement.

On the adjacent screen, a second trace rose and fell in a slower register: weight entering the old
yard through four tyres, the front pair compressing first, then the rear. The surface no longer
existed. Its response did, weakly, in the material beneath the business park.

Technopark called it a vehicle.

Sanna called it a load distribution consistent with one.

Nobody objected. They were learning her verbs.

The engine was easier. Ignition had disturbed the local electromagnetic field in a repeating pattern;
after ignition came a noisier alternator signature, and after that a cooling curve. Lucid held three
candidate engine classes and refused to choose among them. All three were common in government
fleets of the period. They were also common in taxis, delivery vans and stolen cars.

`VEHICLE PRESENT` survived review.

`STATE VEHICLE` did not.

The cost display ticked while they argued: generator reserve, compute allocation, Antarctic
correlation time. Sanna had already surrendered the next calibration block. If the weather closed
before the replacement window, the date uncertainty would widen again and three days of work would
become a more expensive version of perhaps.

Dries asked for the pressure products around the vehicle body.

“The family did not authorise speech reconstruction,” Kiki said.

“I asked for pressure.”

“Pressure becomes speech if you chase it hard enough.”

Ama narrowed the request before it reached the queue. No lexical recovery. No speaker separation.
Only transient events that could bear on presence or departure.

The reduced query returned a blunt impact, a soft irregular pulse and a panel resonance.

The impact matched the chair.

The pulse repeated inside a range compatible with breathing, though not with one identifiable
person. It might have been close to a wall, or close to the Anchor, or simply the least damaged part
of the field. Sanna marked it human-compatible and went no further.

The panel resonance took an hour.

At first the solver offered six ordinary sources: a cupboard, an internal door, an external door, a
vehicle door, a sheet of metal lifted and dropped, or two unrelated impacts that happened to share
frequencies. Ghana removed the wall candidates by testing their expected response against the
surviving floor paths. Karoo removed the loose sheet. The external door remained possible until
Sanna aligned the transient with a small change in the rear suspension load.

Someone had opened a door on the vehicle.

They certified the event without deciding who.

Then Lucid returned a conflict receipt.

The same resonant panel appeared in a later interval.

Sanna assumed the time alignment had folded. She rebuilt it against the stopped watch layer, then
against the damage layer, then without the Anchor at all. The distance between the transients
changed by fractions of a second. The second event remained later.

Dries leaned towards the display. “Same door?”

“Compatible panel geometry,” she said.

“Same vehicle?”

“The axle spacing persists.”

“Then it opened twice.”

Kiki asked what the second opening established.

They tested occupancy change and obtained only overlapping ranges. They tested direction of travel
and lost the rear surface. They tested the fourth load path and found that it approached the yard in
one interval, but the uncertainty widened before contact with the vehicle.

The machine would certify two openings. It would not certify what passed through either one.

“We have a door,” Dries said. “It opened twice. The second time is the one that matters and we cannot
yet say why.”

On the control-room window, ice grains travelled sideways through the floodlights. Sanna watched
them until the next batch completed.

The first opening sat near the chair scrape and the breathing. The second stood apart, surrounded by
low activity that had not yet been queried.

Nothing in the recovered sound accused anyone.

It was a chair, a breath and a door used twice.
