# Chapter 36

Every hash verified.

Kiki began with the leaked video's manifest and worked backwards. The rendered frames matched the
generator output. The generator output matched the spatial product. The spatial product matched the
external receipt. Its input roots matched public sensor products and a valid query package.

No file had been substituted after signature. No timestamp had been altered. No custody link was
missing.

The signing authority was valid.

She repeated the audit on a clean machine in Nairobi, then asked two colleagues who knew nothing of
the case to reproduce it. They reached the same root.

One of them said, “So the leak is authentic.”

“The chain is authentic.”

“That is what I mean.”

“It is not what I mean.”

Kiki opened the query package. Its instructions were visible now that the file had been published:
preserve measured geometry; select a single admissible position for each figure; complete missing
surfaces from period references; produce faces from publicly available identity media; render
speech compatible with the phonetic bands; omit no completed field from the final scene.

The operator had asked for certainty. The system had supplied generated certainty and recorded the
request correctly.

Nothing in the Witness Protocol prohibited a valid authority from signing a bad interpretation. It
established who signed, when they signed, which inputs they used and whether the output changed
afterward. The protocol had no opinion about whether ninety-one centimetres made a lie.

Kiki searched the sealed-room design minutes.

Her own voice returned from three years earlier, flattened by transcription. The minute preserved
the distinction as two propositions: integrity carried evidence into the room; it could not decide
what that evidence meant.

The limitation had been minuted, approved and cited in the protocol specification. She had taught it
to students. She had corrected journalists who described hashes as truth. Yet somewhere between the
sealed room and the historical run, she had begun to feel safer when the chain went green.

Dries joined the audit call. “Can you revoke the signer?”

“After identifying the authority class.”

“Can you mark the output fraudulent?”

“We can mark its certainty unsupported.”

“People need a word shorter than that.”

“Then they will use the wrong word.”

He asked her to certify whether the leaked result had been tampered with.

“It has not.”

“Whether it is true.”

“The geometry is mostly measured. The faces and speech are generated. One position selects an
admissible edge and conceals alternatives.”

“That's not yes or no.”

“Correct.”

Kiki published the audit under her own key. Within minutes, accounts promoting the leak quoted its
first finding: `ALL HASHES VERIFIED`. The paragraph explaining that sequence and integrity did not
establish interpretive truth appeared below the crop line.

The government press office asked her to change the first finding to `AUTHENTICITY NOT ESTABLISHED`.
She refused. That would make the audit politically useful and technically false.

The family's attorney asked a different question.

“Does a valid chain make the use of the photograph authorised?”

“No.”

“Put that first in the family notice.”

Kiki did.

By evening, the consortium's green verification mark appeared beside the smooth video on three
networks, detached from her report. Her own protocol supplied the symbol.

She called the five nodes and showed them the signer path she had begun to trace. Before she shared
it, she replayed the old design minute once more.

“I told you what it couldn't do,” she said. “I didn't believe me either.”
