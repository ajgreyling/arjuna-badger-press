# Chapter 17

Sanna had twelve minutes to decide whether the oscillator came south.

The replacement unit sat on a scale in the cargo room beside a box with her name on it. Combined,
they exceeded the aircraft's revised allowance by 6.4 kilograms. Weather had closed one approach and
the crew wanted enough reserve fuel to turn north without asking the storm for permission.

The logistics officer stood in the doorway holding the manifest.

“One or the other.”

Sanna cut the tape on her box.

Inside were winter clothes she did not need, two books she had already read, coffee better than the
station supply and a parcel wrapped by somebody whose handwriting she had spent four months avoiding.

She removed the parcel first.

The scale dropped by 1.1 kilograms.

She took out the coffee, one book and a pair of boots. The oscillator still needed nine hundred
grams.

“The whole box can stay,” she said.

The logistics officer changed the manifest. He did not ask what was inside. That was one reason
Antarctica suited her.

The replacement oscillator went onto the aircraft.

The plane landed in weather that turned before unloading finished. Its crew kept one engine running.
Cargo moved hand to hand across hard snow. The oscillator reached the station. The personal box
remained at the coastal staging point and would either travel north with the aircraft or wait through
winter without caring which.

At 18:22, the plane lifted into a white horizon.

At 18:31, Technopark asked for a date.

Sanna read the request twice.

The package contained no image and no object, despite the filenames in its transformation history.
It contained two families of correlation: one narrow and recent, fixed by the isolated experiment
clock; one broad, weak and entangled with the same coordinates. Ghana had already shown that a single
decay model distorted both. Karoo had shown the lower family was structured across independent
sensors. Nairobi had listed the claim Antarctica was authorised to test.

Sanna rejected the word *date* in her reply.

`I CAN TEST WHETHER THE LOWER FAMILY IS COMPATIBLE`  
`WITH REGISTERED TEMPORAL REFERENCES.`

Dries answered:

`what does that give us`

`AN INTERVAL.`

`how narrow`

`THE ICE TOOK FORTY THOUSAND YEARS.`  
`I WILL NEED UNTIL THURSDAY.`

It took six days.

The station's reference did not remember Stellenbosch. Ancient ice was not a bottle containing the
world's past. Its ordered lattice, trapped atmospheric chemistry and relation to long-baseline
particle observations gave Sanna a low-noise calibration against which temporal correlation models
could be killed. The work eliminated histories. It did not play one back.

The first model placed both layers inside the sealed-room experiment and failed against the
oscillator root.

The second treated the lower family as construction-era residue and required an environmental state
incompatible with the geological integration.

The third spread it across a century and explained everything by explaining nothing.

Sanna killed each one in the station log.

On the third day she rejected a Ghanaian transformation that made the lower boundary cleaner by
smoothing three demoted fragments into one sequence. Ama replied without defending the node.

`REJECT TRANSFORM 8C. SOURCE FRAGMENTS FOLLOW.`

The unsmoothed products widened the interval by eleven years. Sanna kept them. Accra rebuilt its
lineage table and attached her objection to the transformation history instead of replacing the
failed version.

After that, Ama sent no messages asking when the result would be ready. She sent power transitions,
demotion roots and one correction to a null value whose undocumented meaning had survived since the
first architecture diagram. Sanna used all of them.

Outside, the last workable twilight withdrew. Wind erased the aircraft tracks. The external route
closed first in the operations schedule, then in fact. For the next eight months people could argue
with her but not arrive to improve the argument.

On the fourth night, a cosmic-background reference narrowed the lower boundary. On the fifth, a
neutrino calibration shifted the upper one. She rebuilt both from raw station products before
allowing herself to compare them.

The unresolved layer predated the current building.

Not by years.

By decades.

She checked the municipal construction date included in the blind metadata only after fixing the
interval. Technopark's office could not be the source. Neither could the development that preceded
its present lease.

The remaining admissible window lay in the late apartheid period.

Sanna wrote the finding as four sentences. The first stated the interval. The second listed the
references. The third listed the models excluded. The fourth said what the result did not establish:
no object, no event identity, no human action, no historical interpretation.

She signed it and waited for the next radio window.

The station telephone displayed two missed personal calls accumulated during the work. One number
belonged to the parcel's sender. The other belonged to Ama, who had called after the institutional
link closed and left no message.

Sanna cleared the notification without returning either.

At 06:40, Stellenbosch entered summer light. Antarctica had been dark for hours.

She transmitted the date.
