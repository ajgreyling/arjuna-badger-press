# Chapter 13

The first thing the machine found was the edge of a table.

Not the table. An edge.

It appeared in the revised inversion as a plane of increased constraint, one metre above the floor,
running for sixty-three centimetres before dissolving into unsupported space. The field did not know
the colour, material or purpose of the surface. It knew that across four independent sensor families,
past states containing matter on one side of that plane were less compatible with the present residue
than states containing matter on the other.

Lwazi rotated the reconstruction volume.

The edge remained.

Everything else moved.

Probability occupied the room in torn layers. A broad volume might have been the operator or the
chair or several incompatible solutions sharing coordinates. Near the floor, a weak transition
appeared and vanished with each change in the decay model. The table edge alone survived every
admissible transform.

“Wrong position,” Dries said.

Lwazi checked the registered room plan. The measured edge should have been eighteen millimetres
farther north.

“Uncertainty?” he asked.

Ama's answer came from Accra. “Six millimetres at the supported section. Wider at both ends.”

“Then it is wrong.”

Nobody defended the result.

That was the discipline G had made socially expensive and technically ordinary: when a live limb
missed, the limb died. They did not move the room plan. They did not widen a confidence field after
seeing the answer. They did not call eighteen millimetres close because the first twelve days had
produced nothing at all.

Lwazi reran the wrong-coordinate controls. The edge vanished outside the room and weakened when the
time bound moved. It belonged to the experiment. It was also in the wrong place.

“Action leak?” Kiki asked.

“The action key does not contain table coordinates,” Lwazi said.

“Room inventory does.”

“Registered before sealing.”

“Then a substitution after registration could create the discrepancy.”

Dries opened the custody trail. The folding table had been weighed, photographed and marked outside
the room. Two technicians carried it in. Kiki sealed the door. No record existed of its position
after entry because recording that position would have supplied the answer.

“Open the room,” Lwazi said.

Kiki did not move. “The reconstruction package has not been sealed.”

“Seal it.”

“The root is internal.”

“We have all signed.”

“Internally.”

Lwazi looked through the glass wall at the corridor leading to the test room. The door had remained
closed since the action witness left. Behind it stood an ordinary folding table whose position had
become an argument among five nodes.

“What would satisfy you?” he asked.

“Publish the result root and the stated edge coordinates to the external archive before inspection.”

“That delays opening.”

“Yes.”

“The field may be a software artefact.”

“Then the archive will preserve an artefact.”

“We need the room for another booking at four.”

Kiki looked at him.

Dries sent the package.

The heritage archive accepted the root eight minutes later. Accra mirrored it. Nairobi issued an
inspection notice binding the published coordinates to the post-run examination. Only then did Kiki
release the door seal.

The consortium had assembled itself in the corridor. Technicians, administrators, two physicists
from Karoo still present after the rehearsal, and the facilities manager who had come because the
booking calendar said the room was due to become induction training at sixteen hundred.

Kiki photographed the intact seal and cut it.

Lwazi opened the door.

The table stood where the room plan said it should.

For a moment, the failure reassured him.

Then Dries crouched.

One leg did not sit flat. A grain of construction grit had lodged beneath its rubber foot. Under the
weight of the table, the foot had slipped sideways until it found the floor, rotating the table by a
fraction of a degree.

Lwazi fetched the steel measure.

They did not touch the table. Kiki registered the room state, then directed measurements from the
wall control points to the supported edge. The facilities manager read the tape. Dries wrote the
figures. Kiki made them repeat each distance in reverse order with another person holding the end.

The measured displacement at the stable section was seventeen millimetres.

Uncertainty, six.

Lwazi looked from the table to the printed confidence slice in his hand.

No camera had recorded the movement. The instruction capsules did not mention it. The witness had
registered the operator's actions after they were complete and had not measured the furniture. The
room plan contained the position everyone expected, not the one the table had acquired.

The machine had found a detail nobody had entered.

Dries reached toward the grit.

“Don't,” Kiki said.

He stopped.

She placed an evidence marker beside it, photographed the leg and added the accidental displacement
to the inspection record. Her face revealed no wonder. Her hands did.

The facilities manager looked around the room. “So it works?”

Lwazi answered before anyone else could.

“It found an edge.”

“The table.”

“An edge compatible with the table.”

“That moved.”

“Apparently.”

“By itself.”

Dries said, “With help from Stellenbosch construction.”

Somebody laughed. It spread through the corridor too quickly, relief finding the first available
shape.

Lwazi did not join them yet.

He returned to the wall display and hid every layer except the supported plane. It floated alone in
black space: incomplete, colourless and seventeen millimetres away from the history they had written
down beforehand.

For the first time, the empty room had disagreed with its record and been right.
