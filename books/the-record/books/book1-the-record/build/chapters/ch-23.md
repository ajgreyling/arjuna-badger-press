# Chapter 23

The first draft allowed reasonable scientific use.

The family's attorney drew a line through the phrase.

“It is standard language,” the university lawyer said.

“That is why it is dangerous.”

They met in Nairobi because Kiki refused to draft an ethical boundary inside the institution whose
scientists wanted access. The room belonged to a legal clinic. Its table carried no consortium logo.
The Anchor remained in sealed custody elsewhere and was represented only by its schedule number.

Kiki had written the procedural spine over a weekend: subject, Anchor, retention, revocation,
execution. Ama supplied the exclusion clause. The family attorney supplied distrust in complete
sentences.

The university lawyer restored his composure. “Reasonable scientific use permits validation and
method development without requiring a new instrument for every transformation.”

“It permits whatever a scientist later persuades another scientist is reasonable.”

“Subject to ethics review.”

“Whose?”

He named the university committee.

“Not the family's.”

The phrase stayed deleted.

The attorney struck a second clause granting the consortium the right to retain derivative training
material after the query closed. The lawyer explained that a model could learn from uncertainty
structure without retaining identifiable content.

“Then train it on somebody who consented to become structure,” she said.

The third deletion removed a warranty that the family would not seek an injunction if an authorised
answer caused political or reputational harm.

“You want them to promise not to object to a result before knowing the result,” the attorney said.

“We need publication certainty.”

“You need a different family.”

The lawyer asked for a break.

Kiki remained at the table with Ama and the attorney. Between them lay nine pages of clauses written
for a capability no legal system had named.

“What exactly are they allowed to ask?” the attorney said.

Ama gave the three questions the family had settled.

Was the missing man physically present at the site within the bounded period?

Did he leave the site alive?

Was there supported physical evidence that another person was accountable for his death or
concealment?

“The third expands the subject,” Kiki said.

“It answers accountability,” the attorney replied.

“It may expose people who never consented to a query.”

“The dead man did not consent either.”

Nobody at the table possessed a sentence that repaired that.

Ama opened her exclusion clause. No reconstruction of the subject's perception, sensation, distress
or final awareness could be attempted, retained or published, even if it arose incidentally while
answering the permitted questions.

“Can the system obey that?” the attorney asked.

“Lucid can reject a query outside the boundary,” Ama said.

“That is a policy.”

“Not if the query package cannot execute without the boundary attached,” Kiki said.

She rewrote the consent schedule as an executable limit. Every solver request would carry the three
permitted questions, excluded layers and authorised resolution. A query seeking experiential detail
would fail before consuming the Anchor.

“I have written you a fence,” she said. “I want you to understand that a fence is a thing people
walk around.”

The attorney looked at the pages. “Then we decide what happens when they do.”

They required all outputs supporting the permitted questions to enter the Witness Protocol. Any
intermediate product capable of supporting the excluded experiential layer would be destroyed within
seventy-two hours of certification.

Kiki regarded that as the strongest sentence in the instrument. Ethics would not depend on a future
operator choosing not to look. The prohibited material would cease to exist.

The attorney required publication consent in writing.

The consortium required enough retention to reproduce the permitted answers.

Ama required every deletion to be logged.

“Logged by whom?” the attorney asked.

“Nairobi records the execution and Ghana records removal from the ingestion path,” Kiki said.

“Can either record prove nobody looked before deletion?”

Kiki turned the retention diagram towards herself. A prohibited intermediate would exist long
enough for the solver to recognise its class, isolate it and prove that the permitted answer did not
depend on it. During that interval, access controls could prevent an ordinary operator from opening
it. An administrator with the right keys could still alter the controls. A court could order them
altered. A person could look and later remove the access event if enough systems agreed to lie.

“No,” she said. “The deletion receipt proves deletion. It does not prove innocence before deletion.”

“Then retain the access record.”

“The access record may identify the prohibited product strongly enough to become a map back to it,”
Ama said.

The attorney sat with the choice. Keep evidence capable of proving misuse and preserve a route to
the thing the family had forbidden, or destroy the route and make later innocence harder to prove.

They kept the deletion clause. Kiki added that no human-readable rendering of an excluded product
could be generated before destruction. It reduced the ways to violate the boundary. It did not
remove them.

On the eighth day, the attorney asked a question the document had avoided.

“Does this boundary travel with every copy?”

“Every consortium product,” the university lawyer said. “And every product transferred to an
authorised contractor.”

“Write the people, not the product.”

He amended the first clause. The obligations bound the African Record Consortium, its nodes,
employees, agents and authorised contractors. The attorney checked the list twice and moved on to
revocation.

After eleven days, the document read like a lease.

It bound the African Record Consortium. It did not bind a court, a state, a foreign laboratory or an
instrument built later. Nobody said this aloud during execution.

It authorised living relatives to consent on behalf of a query concerning the dead. Nobody wrote
what happened when the last relative died.

It destroyed the intermediate evidence that could later prove the consortium had honoured the
excluded boundary.

Nobody at the table could repair all three failures at once.

The daughter read every page before signing. She initialled the three questions individually and
crossed out the blank lines beneath them so nothing could be added later.

“If it finds more?” she asked.

“Lucid must refuse to return it,” Kiki said.

“And if somebody asks differently?”

“Under this instrument, they cannot.”

The daughter rested her pen on the final page.

“Under this instrument,” she repeated.

Then she signed.
