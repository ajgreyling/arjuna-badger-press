# Chapter 34

Dries first saw the past on a television above the Technopark coffee counter.

He had gone downstairs because the office machines had stopped taking jobs and there was nothing
left to pretend he was supervising. Around him, people ordered breakfast, watched market prices
slide beneath a football replay and complained about the lifts. Then the channel changed.

The old security room filled the screen.

It had walls, electric light and four men with faces.

The image moved smoothly through the angle the certified reconstruction had never occupied. A chair
scraped across concrete. One man breathed hard. Another spoke with the round authority of a trained
actor performing an old regional accent. The revered figure stood beside the missing man while a
security officer entered from the yard.

No uncertainty crossed the picture. No geometry broke at the demolished threshold. When the
vehicle door opened, the camera followed it.

Someone at the counter stopped mid-order.

Dries moved closer until he stood beneath the screen. He recognised the measured axle spacing, the
chair position and the exterior door angle. The narrow office dimensions were right. Even the
vehicle's suspension drop appeared at the correct moment.

The room had been built from their result.

The faces had not.

The missing man's face came from the family photograph used in anniversary coverage of the case. It
had been aged, turned and fitted to a body the machine had never recovered. The revered figure had
been assembled from public speeches and news footage. The security officer's face looked specific
enough to ruin a living family and generic enough to evade identification.

The presenter called the video an exclusive reconstruction produced by the African Record
Consortium.

Dries said, “No,” to the television.

Nobody heard him.

The segment cut to a studio analyst who explained that new imaging techniques had enhanced the raw
historical record. A graphic showed noisy sensor data entering Lucid and a complete scene emerging
from the other side.

“Enhance,” the barista repeated, not joking. “So it was there all along.”

That was what the smooth version promised: not an interpretation, but obstruction removed. The
public had been told a machine could recover history. Now it was being shown history recovered.

The real products looked, by comparison, evasive. Load paths. Confidence bands. A pressure event
that might be breathing. A fall represented by a field reaching concrete. Every honest gap resembled
something the consortium had chosen to hide.

Dries returned upstairs without his coffee.

The reconstruction had already crossed the networks. Clips carried subtitles in six languages.
One political account called it proof of heroic intervention. Another isolated the same twelve
seconds and called it proof that the liberation movement had murdered its own. A former minister
demanded immediate arrests. A historian with two million followers asked why universities had
suppressed the footage.

The family's attorney sent one message to the quorum.

`THE FACE WAS NOT AUTHORISED.`

Dries opened the leaked file beside the certified geometry. The attack had not invented the room
from nothing. It had taken the output hash from the external receipt, preserved most of the measured
structure and generated detail wherever the result admitted alternatives. The complete scene was
more stable than any of the histories that supported it because the generator did not have to keep
conflicts alive.

The technical metadata claimed no uncertainty products had been omitted.

It treated generated completion as a method of displaying them.

Jacobs called from the government briefing group. “Can you prove it is false?”

“The faces are unsupported. The voice is synthetic. There is no camera path.”

“Can you prove the event shown did not happen?”

Dries looked at the moving figures. Much of it could have happened. That was why it worked.

“Not all of it.”

“Which part?”

He did not know yet.

The video reached the fall. The editor cut away at contact, replaced the uncertainty with the
daughter's old photograph and returned to the vehicle as if grief were an establishing shot.

In the office below the television, phones began ringing.

The fake had given the country the thing the machine had refused to make: a past that could be
watched once and understood.

The room was not the forgery.

Neither was the vehicle, the chair, the open door or the order in which the four load paths entered
the surviving field. Dries stripped the leak down until faces became featureless meshes, speech
became timing marks and the beautiful camera disappeared. What remained fitted the certified
geometry so closely that the two rooms occupied one screen.

He found the difference by measuring empty space.

The leaked reconstruction placed the revered figure inside the room during the fall. The certified
path held him at the threshold, beside the open vehicle door, until after impact.

The displacement was ninety-one centimetres.

Dries checked the coordinate roots. Same survey. Same historical building plan. Same uncertainty
budget around the demolished threshold. The leaked query had not fabricated a new path. It had
selected one edge of the admissible field, translated the figure across the doorway and fixed him
there as if no competing position survived.

Inside, he appeared close enough to touch the missing man before the fall.

Outside, he could have been watching, arriving or trying to enter.

The generated video chose inside and supplied the hand the record could not prove.

Lwazi joined him at the reconstruction wall, where Dries had marked both positions with tape on the
current office floor. The distance between them was shorter than one stride.

“That's it?” Lwazi asked.

“That's it.”

They walked the leaked path. From the inner mark, the revered figure occupied the contact field and
the generator could animate intervention. From the outer mark, the old vehicle door blocked the
eastern witness and the timing of entry remained unresolved.

One position made him part of the fall. The other made him a witness to its aftermath.

Neither said what he later chose to do.

Dries sent the comparison to the five nodes. Ama verified the coordinate transform. Sanna verified
that no timing correction justified it. Kiki asked whether the translated point remained inside the
original uncertainty volume.

It did, narrowly.

“Then they will say it is an interpretation,” Jacobs said on the government call.

“It is,” Dries replied.

“You called the video false.”

“It presents one admissible edge as the only measured position.”

“That sentence will lose to the video.”

Nobody disputed him.

They prepared a public technical notice with two diagrams. The first showed the certified field as a
blurred volume crossing the threshold. The second showed the leak's single figure inside it. Press
staff repeatedly asked them to colour the correct position green and the false position red.

There was no correct point to colour green.

The consortium could show that the certainty was fabricated. It could not prove the selected
position impossible.

At the briefing, a reporter held the diagrams side by side and asked why less than a metre mattered.

Dries stepped onto the taped marks.

“Here, he is outside when the man falls. Here, he is close enough for the video to give him an
action. They didn't fake the past. They moved one man ninety centimetres.”

The question travelled across the room before he finished speaking: which version did he personally
believe?

He looked down at the tape.

Belief would fit inside the gap easily. So would a nation's preferred history.

“The record supports both positions,” he said. “The video hides that.”

By evening, clips of his answer circulated under captions claiming the consortium had admitted the
leak was physically accurate. The diagrams lost their uncertainty shading when people cropped them
for phones. Green and red versions appeared anyway.

In the empty office, Dries lifted the tape from the floor. Ninety-one centimetres came away in his
hand.
