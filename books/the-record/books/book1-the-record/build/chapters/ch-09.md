# Chapter 9

The generator failed by less than half a second.

It was not supposed to start at all.

The rehearsal used no sealed action and produced no scientific data. Its purpose was to push a
synthetic pulse through every approved sensor path, sign the streams at origin, ingest them in Ghana
and confirm that Technopark could place the resulting products on one timeline without anybody
learning more than their envelope allowed.

At 14:03:11, municipal power sagged.

The building transfer switch waited, decided the sag was a loss and called the generator. The
generator turned. Coughed. Turned again. Technopark went dark for the length of one surprised breath
before the batteries caught the load.

Every status light returned green.

The rehearsal failed.

Dries saw it in the clock map before the room lights had reached full brightness. Karoo's synthetic
pulse arrived at Ghana in the correct sequence and appeared at Technopark twice: once three hundred
and eighty milliseconds before the local trigger, and once seventy-one milliseconds after it.

“Nobody touch anything,” he said.

The technician nearest the transfer panel withdrew his hand.

Lwazi came out of the candidate room carrying a sealed instruction capsule that had never been used.
“What happened?”

“We travelled.”

“What?”

“Locally. Less than half a second. Funding will be difficult.”

Dries expanded the timeline across the wall monitor. Each node had signed its own events correctly.
Nairobi's sequence root was intact. Ghana had preserved both arrivals. No packet had duplicated on
the network. The contradiction existed only after Technopark projected the streams onto its local
clock.

Lwazi read the offsets. “Clock step.”

“Which clock?”

“The building reference.”

“That is not a clock. That is a label in a diagram.”

The facilities manager arrived with the resigned expression of a person summoned whenever physics
discovered a maintenance contract.

“Generator is running,” he said.

“It started late.”

“It started inside specification.”

“What specification?”

“Eight seconds to stable supply.”

“We lost four hundred milliseconds.”

“Then you had seven point six seconds left.”

Dries took him to the transfer cabinet. The controller log showed the sag, the decision threshold,
the start command and stable generator voltage. The building monitoring service showed the same
events with different times. Its network switch had remained on battery; the transfer controller
had rebooted and recovered time from a local gateway whose own reference had stepped when the
generator came online.

“Service interval was last month,” the manager said.

“For the engine.”

“And the switch.”

“For switching electricity. Not for telling the universe when it happened.”

The manager crossed his arms. “That was not in the service request.”

Lwazi made a sound behind them. Dries turned.

“You're enjoying this,” Lwazi said.

“Yes.”

“We lost the rehearsal.”

“We found a clock dependency that does not exist in the architecture.”

“That is not better.”

“It is considerably better today than after a successful run.”

On the consortium call, the other nodes were less receptive to his happiness.

Kiki suspended the experiment root. Karoo wanted its instrument window restored. Ama wanted to know
why Technopark had transformed signed source times through an unregistered building clock after
ingestion.

“For display alignment,” Dries said.

“Display is not evidence,” Kiki replied.

“I know that now.”

“You knew it yesterday.”

“Yesterday it was a principle. Today it has a generator.”

Ama put the two Ghana products side by side. They were identical except for the time transformation
requested by Technopark. Her pipeline had retained the original source clocks, the transformed
projection and the mapping between them.

“If we had normalised on arrival,” she said, “you would have one clean wrong answer.”

“Instead we have two ugly ones.”

“You are welcome.”

The repair cost less than the replacement toner order waiting on Dries's desk. They bought an
isolated timing oscillator with holdover sufficient for the experiment window, powered it from its
own battery and registered its signal as a separate evidence stream. The building clock could fail,
step or become philosophical without changing the experiment root.

Kiki required a rehearsal of the oscillator failure.

Lwazi required a rehearsal of the backup to the oscillator failure.

Ama required both raw clock streams to remain attached through ingestion rather than being collapsed
into one canonical time.

The facilities manager required them to stop describing a generator that had worked as failed.

They rewrote the incident report.

`BUILDING POWER TRANSITION WITHIN SPECIFICATION.`  
`EXPERIMENTAL TIME MODEL INCOMPLETE.`

Dries printed a copy and taped it inside the timing cabinet.

The next morning they killed municipal power deliberately.

The generator coughed at almost the same point. The lights went out. The building clock stepped.

The isolated oscillator did not care.

On the wall, one timeline remained still while everything around it changed.
