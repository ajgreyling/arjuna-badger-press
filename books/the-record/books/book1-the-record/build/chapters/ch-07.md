# Chapter 7

Ama knew the experiment had been designed in Stellenbosch because the first data diagram contained
one box labelled `AFRICA`.

It sat beneath the sensors and above the reconstruction engine, a blue rectangle with three arrows
entering and one leaving. Inside the box, in smaller type, somebody had written `INGEST / VERIFY /
CALIBRATE`.

She shared the diagram on the consortium call and asked who owned it.

Nobody spoke.

“It has a version history,” she said.

Dries lifted one hand on camera. “It was a conversation starter.”

“It has started one.”

Ama worked from a narrow office beside the Accra racks when she needed people to leave her alone.
The room had no exterior window and contained a transfer-switch panel that clicked every time the
building changed its opinion about power. Through the wall came the steady pressured air of cooling
systems and, beneath it, the less steady argument of two engineers replacing a drive without taking
the array down.

On her second screen, raw samples from Karoo arrived in six formats with four clock conventions and
one undocumented null value.

“The box means the distributed ingestion layer,” Dries said.

“Then write distributed.”

“There wasn't room.”

“There was room for Africa.”

Lwazi joined from Karoo wearing headphones large enough to look protective. Behind him, a warning
light pulsed above a door. “Can we decide what the experiment needs before we decide who is insulted
by the drawing?”

“The drawing already decided what the experiment needs,” Ama said. “It needs the sensors to send
everything south, one place to decide what matters, and the rest of us to decorate the path.”

“That is not what I intended,” Dries said.

“Architecture does not keep your intentions.”

Kiki's microphone opened in Nairobi. “Before this becomes productive, may I add another problem?”

“You always do,” Ama said.

“The diagram shows verification after ingestion. If the raw stream is transformed before its origin
and sequence are attested, I can prove only that Ghana gave Technopark a file.”

“Ghana would give Technopark an excellent file.”

“I would still prove only that you gave it to them.”

The rivalry was old enough to survive affection and useful enough that neither woman softened it.

Dries deleted the blue box.

“Fine. Empty page. What can nobody else do?”

They began with Karoo.

Lwazi's instruments could preserve weak physical correlations across electromagnetic, geological
and vacuum-noise streams because the site had been engineered to notice things other places called
silence. It could not decide which correlations belonged to one event. It could not afford to keep
everything forever. It could not authenticate its own interpretation.

Dries drew Karoo as an ear.

Lwazi objected to the icon and accepted the function.

Ghana received the torrent. Ama's systems separated events without smoothing unsupported fragments
into false continuity. They preserved lineage through transformation and kept uncertainty attached
to the thing it qualified. Her node could remember structure no single sensor knew it contained. It
could not manufacture evidence absent from the stream.

Dries drew a stack of disks.

Ama replaced it with the word `MEMORY`.

“Modest,” Kiki said.

“Accurate.”

Technopark integrated the sensor models and built the inversion engine. It could ask what past state
was least inconsistent with the present residue. It could also produce a convincing answer to
a badly framed question at enormous speed.

“Brain,” Dries said.

“Workshop,” Lwazi said.

“Brain is shorter.”

“That is not evidence.”

Nairobi designed the experiment chain: registered objects, sealed choices, independent clocks,
quorum approval, reproducible calculation and a receipt for every transformation. Kiki could prove
that a recorded operation occurred in the stated sequence. She could not prove the operation's
interpretation was physically true.

“Conscience,” Dries said.

Kiki looked displeased. “Protocol.”

“Conscience is not an operational role,” Ama said.

“Thank you.”

“It is, however, what you will call yourself when refusing our deadlines.”

They left it as `WITNESS`.

Antarctica was not on the call. Sanna's response to the new experiment had arrived as a signed note
with twelve numbered objections and an offer buried beneath the eleventh. The station maintained
temporal references derived from ancient low-noise ice, neutrino observations and cosmic-background
correlations. None could tell them what happened in a room in Stellenbosch. Together they might help
distinguish when one layer of residue had participated in an event rather than another.

Ama added `CLOCK` before anybody could draw a snowflake.

The page now contained five nodes and no centre.

Dries studied it. “The solver still runs at Technopark.”

“A solver runs at Technopark,” Ama said.

“There is one implementation.”

“Then make it portable before we give it data.”

“That is outside scope.”

“So was a blue box called Africa.”

The transfer panel clicked behind her. The racks changed from municipal supply to battery without
losing a packet. On the other side of the wall, an engineer cheered once and was told by somebody
else to wait for verification.

Dries drew arrows between the nodes. Every arrow acquired a condition.

Karoo could transmit only registered streams. Ghana could transform them only while preserving
lineage and uncertainty. Technopark could run inversion only against an approved query package.
Nairobi could certify process but not scientific truth. Antarctica could date a correlation but not
name the event that produced it.

“This is going to be slow,” Lwazi said.

“Yes,” Kiki said.

“Expensive,” Dries said.

“Less expensive than being wrong quickly,” Ama said.

Nobody on the call could complete the experiment. That fact had initially looked like a problem of
coordination. On the page it began to look like the first honest property of the design.

Dries saved the diagram as `architecture-v1`.

Ama watched the filename appear.

“No,” she said.

“What now?”

“The first diagram was also architecture.”

He waited.

“Call this version two.”

Dries changed the number.

Only then did she approve it.
