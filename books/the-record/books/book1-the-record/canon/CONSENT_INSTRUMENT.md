# The Instrument of Consent — working draft

> **Status: wording NOT locked.** What is locked (L-10) is the *shape*: three permitted questions,
> an explicit exclusion of the subject's inner experience, and Lucid enforcing the boundary as a
> query limit rather than a policy. The clause text below is a draft for drafting against.
>
> This document is load-bearing in Beats 23a, 24, 32, 34a and 44. It is also **wrong in five ways on
> purpose** — see *Designed flaws*. Do not fix them in the draft; they are the plot.

---

## The text (draft)

> **INSTRUMENT OF CONSENT TO HISTORICAL QUERY**
>
> Between the consenting parties named in Schedule A, and the African Record Consortium.
>
> **1 · Subject.** One historical event, bounded in place and in time as set out in Schedule A.
>
> **2 · Anchor.** The query may be constrained only by the object described in Schedule B, which
> remains at all times the property of the consenting parties.
>
> **3 · Permitted questions.** Three, and only three:
> **3.1** Was the subject physically present at that place, within that period?
> **3.2** Did the subject leave that place alive?
> **3.3** Was any other person present whose identity can still be established?
>
> **4 · Excluded.** No reconstruction of the subject's perception, sensation, distress or final
> awareness may be attempted, retained or published — whether or not it arises incidentally to
> question 3.
>
> **5 · Retention.** Outputs supporting question 3 are retained under the Witness Protocol. All
> intermediate products capable of supporting anything in clause 4 are destroyed within 72 hours of
> certification.
>
> **6 · Publication.** Nothing is published without the written agreement of the consenting parties.
>
> **7 · Revocation.** The consenting parties may revoke this instrument at any time, in writing,
> with immediate effect.
>
> **8 · Execution.** Signed by the consenting parties and by the Consortium.

## Designed flaws — each one pays off later

1. **It binds the asking, not the answer.** The instrument constrains **the Consortium**. It does not
   constrain a state, a court, a foreign operator, or any future instrument pointed at the same
   coordinates. The residue is not the family's property; only the Anchor is. *(Payoff: Beat 34a —
   the unauthorised run breaks no clause, because whoever ran it was not a party.)*
2. **No successor clause.** Consent is held by living relatives. Nobody drafted what happens when the
   last of them dies, and the machine's whole problem is that the dead consent to nothing.
   *(Payoff: the series' deep question — does privacy survive death — arrives as a missing paragraph
   rather than as a debate.)*
3. **Clause 5 is an erasure obligation.** Destroying the intermediate products is a physical
   operation with a price (`PHYSICS_ANCHORS.md`), it is logged, and it removes the only evidence that
   the boundary was ever honoured. **The ethical clause makes the ethics unauditable.** *(Payoff:
   when the consortium is accused of having looked, it cannot prove it did not — it destroyed the
   proof, as required, on schedule.)*
4. **It is permission-shaped, not prohibition-shaped.** Clause 3 is a list of what may be asked, and
   lists of permissions get amended upward under pressure. A list of what may never be asked would
   not have. *(Payoff: the pressure to add a fourth question is the whole of Act II's ethics.)*
5. **There is no referee.** An agreement between a scientific institution and a bereaved family,
   enforced by the institution. *(Payoff: Nairobi's substitute quorum in Beat 46 is the fix, arrived
   at far too late and for a different reason.)*

## Rule for drafting

Characters may notice flaws 1 and 3 late and painfully. **Nobody notices flaw 2** until it is
irrelevant to fix. Flaw 4 is noticed by the person applying the pressure, not by its authors.
