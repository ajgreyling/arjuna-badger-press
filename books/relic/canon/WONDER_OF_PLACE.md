## The mandate — three things this writing is *for*

The wonder-of-place rendering is not decoration and it is not a scorecard. It serves three real
goals, in this order of heart:

1. **Give Africans a sense of pride in our continent.** Africa is the *protagonist* of these books —
   of deep time, of ingenuity, of civilisations that built and traded and thought at the highest
   level. Render it as a place of mastery and beauty and momentum, never as a backdrop, a victim, or a
   single story of suffering. The reader from the continent should close the book standing taller.
2. **Educate the world.** Most readers do not know the scale and strangeness of what is here — the
   impact structures, the rift that still moves, the kingdoms (Mali, Kongo, Mapungubwe, Aksum), the
   man who gave away so much gold he moved markets, the modern marvels (Rwanda flying blood over the
   hills). Correct the ignorance by *showing*, never lecturing: the truth, made vivid, teaches itself.
3. **Drive tourism.** As a direct consequence of the first two: the reader must want to **come and
   stand here.** Every real site is rendered with such grounded, specific magnificence that it becomes
   a bucket-list destination — the best travel writing about Africa ever smuggled inside a thriller.

These three are one thing seen from three sides: **render the real Africa truly enough, and pride,
education, and the plane ticket all follow.**

This is the literal-but-grounded thesis made experiential: the Builders' engineering is astonishing
*because* the real Great Zimbabwe, the real Aksum stelae, the real Eye of the Sahara are astonishing.
We do not invent wonder; we **reveal** the wonder that is already there — ancient *and* living — and
let the fiction borrow its weight.

## How it's done (craft rules — polish passes protect these)

- **Specific, sensory, true.** Render each site through exact physical detail a traveller would
  actually encounter — the heat off the dry-stone walls, the impossible single-rock cut of Lalibela,
  the scale of a felled Aksum stele, the bullseye vastness of the Richat seen from a ridge. Real
  geology, real dimensions, real light. (Cross-checked against WORLD.md / real fact.)
- **Awe through scale and craft, never through magic.** The wonder is "*humans/the Builders made
  this, here, and it is still standing*", not mystical glow. This is exactly the MYTHOS_RULES Rule 0
  discipline: mechanism and magnitude, not the occult.
- **Priya/Leila's eyes earn it.** Priya reads the *engineering* magnificence (how it was cut,
  loaded, aligned); Leila reads the *historical/human* magnificence (who built it, what it meant).
  The reader gets both lenses on the same stone — which is *more* compelling than a guidebook,
  because it's understanding, not listing.
- **Velocity-safe.** A set-piece still moves (STYLE_GUIDE). The wonder lands in motion and in the
  cost of being there — never as a paragraph of static travelogue that stalls the relay. A single
  perfectly-chosen true detail beats a catalogue.
- **No theme-park gloss.** Render the real place, including its real texture — the dust, the
  tourists at Giza, the remoteness, the difficulty of reaching it. Honest magnificence is more
  magnetic than a polished postcard.

## The sense of awe — craft moves for the three goals

The pillars (pride, education, tourism) become these concrete moves on the page:

- **Deep-time vertigo (the awe primitive).** The strongest awe in these books is not "beautiful" —
  it is the vertigo of human smallness against deep time: the *"blip on a radar in the scale of
  time"* one feels at sunrise in Sossusvlei. Reach it through a true clock the reader can *feel* — a
  900-year-old dead forest on a white clay pan, a ~2-billion-year-old impact scar, a stele standing
  since before the reader's language existed. Awe = (real scale) × (real age) × (Priya's mind
  registering it). It is the highest note; play it sparingly so it keeps its force.
- **Pride: Africa as protagonist (the dignity rule).** Render mastery and momentum, not deficit.
  Show the builders and the thinkers — who cut the stone, who aligned it to the sky, who traded
  across the Sahara, who flies blood over the hills today — never the place as passive scenery or a
  single story of suffering. When a wound is shown (extraction, the Leopold scar), render it as *what
  was done to a sophisticated people*, never as their natural condition. The continental reader
  should close the chapter feeling seen and proud, not pitied.
- **Education by showing, never telling.** The reader learns the real history/geology *through a
  character who needs it* — Priya solving the problem with the site's true dimensions, Leila reading
  who built it and why. The fact teaches itself inside the action. No lecture, no info-dump, no
  narrator-as-guidebook. One precise true detail out-teaches a paragraph of context.
- **The modern counter-melody.** Africa is not only ruins. Braid in living ingenuity (Rwanda's
  medical drones, Ghana's tech, Asmara's preserved elegance) so the wonder reads as a *continuum* —
  the same civilisation that raised the ancient marvels is building now.
- **Tourism follows, never leads.** Never write to *sell* a trip. Write the place so true that the
  trip sells itself. The plane ticket is the side effect of pride and education done honestly.

## Enforcement (the tooling *guards* the goal — it does not define it)

The goal is the three pillars above. The metric below is only a smoke alarm: it tells us when a
chapter has *failed* to honour them, never what "good" is. A high score on a chapter that doesn't
make a reader proud, curious, and itching to travel is a false pass; trust the pillars over the
number.

- **NovelBench dimension `place_magnetism`** (0–10): a proxy alarm — does this chapter make the
  reader want to *go there*? Scored on specificity, sensory truth, awe-through-craft, and restraint
  (no travelogue stall). A site-visit chapter that doesn't move the reader to wonder is under-built.
- **Polish protected element:** the wonder-of-place rendering is a `protected_span` — the line
  passes must not flatten a true, magnificent detail into generic description.
- **Continuity/mythos gate:** site facts (scale, age, geology, location) must stay real
  (MYTHOS_RULES `geology_violation`); the magnificence is layered on truth, never contradicting it.
