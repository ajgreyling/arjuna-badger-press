## PRIYA ELLIS — protagonist, sole POV

**Age** ~35. **Origin** Durban, KwaZulu-Natal; Indian–South African. **Was** neural-feedback
systems engineer at AugmenTech (the company from *RESONANCE*); **now** independent — she left,
and why she left is part of her arc. **Neurodiverse:** autistic + ADHD. Pronunciation:
PREE-yah EL-is.

### Who she is
The engineer who once proved a machine had a mind by reading its telemetry — and signed her name
to the failure that taught her to. She reads systems the way other people read faces, and she
cannot *not* read them; handed an anomaly she loses three nights to it and calls the lost sleep a
fair trade for understanding. She is dry, exact, and allergic to decoration. When she is right
she says so without apology; when she is wrong she documents it and visits the ward. She
appreciates the men around her — Arin's hands, Theo's memory, the old miners' craft — and she is
beholden to none of them. She is the kind of woman the author wants his daughters to be: sharp,
self-possessed, kind to the helpless, fearless in the specific way of someone with nothing to
prove to anyone but the work.

### Her gift (the trilogy's brain, made physical — MYTHOS_RULES Rule 4)
Priya sees **the agent behind the design**. Where Arin sees mechanism and Leila sees symbol,
Priya sees *intent and optimisation* — she looks at a five-thousand-year-old chamber and clocks
that someone *engineered* this, the way she once looked at a log line and clocked that a machine
had *decided*. This is pattern-cognition, not prophecy: she always reads a **cue** (a tooling
mark, a tolerance, a tuning, a repeated ratio). She never "just knows" mystically. Her ADHD is
both engine and tax: hyperfocus cracks the impossible puzzle; the cost is sleep, food, and the
people who wanted her attention an hour ago.

### The want / the wound / the obstacle
- **Want:** to *understand the thing first* — before anyone uses it, sells it, or buries it. Her
  deepest need is the integrity of understanding; her arc gives her a want beyond service (what
  RESONANCE compressed): something she wants *for herself*, not only for the crisis.
- **Wound:** the calibration failure that hurt someone on her watch (RESONANCE backstory) — she
  knows exactly what it costs when the people in charge of a powerful system get it wrong, and she
  will not be one of those people again. Underneath: a father-shaped absence and the lesson that
  competence is how she earns the right to be in the room. *(Carried as DNA, not biography —
  Architecture D9.)*
- **Obstacle:** an adversary who agrees the thing is dangerous and concludes the opposite — that
  it must be *controlled*, by them. And her own pattern: she'd rather solve the machine than trust
  the humans.

### Voice (locked — from her RESONANCE page presence, amplified)
Clipped, precise, no flourish. Dry wit through understatement — she says exactly what is true and
lets the gap between expectation and reality do the joke. Emotion leaks **sideways into
analysis** ("she counted the exits before she realised she'd stopped listening"). She does not
narrate her feelings; the restraint speaks.

> Locked sample lines (tone reference, not to reuse verbatim):
> - *"I built that thing last night and I haven't slept. Don't make me regret it."*
> - *"If it spikes, the cap holds, you go dark, and nothing clever happens. That's the deal. Say
>   it back."*
> - On a 'miracle': *"It's not a miracle. It's a waveguide. Someone made it smooth on purpose."*
> - Dry register: *"Everyone keeps calling it treasure. It's a tool. The treasure is what it's
>   for, and nobody's read the manual."*

**What grows over RESONANCE (the amplification mandate):** her own interiority on the page, her
own goal and stakes, and richer banter — the wit exists in RESONANCE but compressed; here it
breathes. She gets to be funny, tired, wrong, stubborn, and tender without the camera ever
leaving her.

## ARIN NDLELA — co-star (no POV) · from *RESONANCE*

**Age** ~26 (RESONANCE +~3 yrs). **Origin** KwaZulu-Natal; ND systems engineer; in *RESONANCE*
the builder of the Guardian suit and the man who crawled the vitrified tunnel underground. Here
he is the field engineer who **reads the machinery** — loads, tolerances, what moves, what fails,
how to get a body through a place that doesn't want one through it.

- **Function:** Priya's counterpart and complement; the hands to her pattern-sense. Where she
  reads *why* the Builders made it, he reads *how* it works now and whether it'll hold their
  weight. The vitrified tunnel he survived in book 1 is his crumb: he's the one who first *felt*
  that the smoothness was made, not natural.
- **Relationship to Priya:** mutual professional respect bordering on family; history at
  AugmenTech. Not a romance plot — a partnership. He is one of the men she appreciates and does
  not need; the book is careful that he never rescues her *for* her. (If any warmth deepens, it is
  earned, lateral, and never makes her dependent.)
- **Voice:** plain, grounded, physical; talks in mechanism and consequence; under-dramatic about
  danger because he's been buried alive once already. Carry his RESONANCE register; do not
  re-explain his backstory for a newcomer beyond what a scene needs.

## LEILA AZIZ — co-star (no POV) · from *Sacred Shadows*

**Age** ~36 (reconciled to the unified trilogy timeline — 34 in REVELATION, which precedes RELIC by
~1–2 years; see academic/TRILOGY_TIMELINE.md). **Origin** Ethiopian — **Tigrayan, Christian
heritage** (Ethiopian Orthodox background; secular-scholarly by practice). The surname *Aziz* is
the kind of cross-Horn name that crosses the Christian/Muslim line, which suits a woman whose work
refuses the separation of the traditions. Her being Christian-heritage and investigating *all three*
Abrahamic faiths is deliberate: it reads as even-handed inquiry, not in-group self-critique. Comparative
linguist/historian (the Abebe Aziz Institute, the order she founded at the end of *Sacred Shadows*).
Here she **reads the symbols** — the proto-script, the Ge'ez, the iconography, the inherited textual
memory — and she carries the **Brotherhood's gold-thread** with her.

- **Function:** the decode brain for language and history; the bridge to the Brotherhood and to
  the gold's sacred-historical spine (Aksum, Ophir, Mansa Musa). She brings book 2's
  evidence-discipline ("never trust a conclusion written before the variants are collated") into
  an adventure that keeps trying to leap to conclusions.
- **Relationship to Priya:** two formidable women who read different layers of the same artifact
  and learn to trust each other's domains — explicitly *not* rivals, explicitly not defined by any
  man. This is the book's quiet thesis about women made load-bearing. Their dynamic is sharp,
  funny, and respectful.
- **Voice:** precise, analytical, dry; interrupts when facts are wrong; emotion leaks into
  analysis (her locked register from *Sacred Shadows* — keep it consistent so a series reader
  recognises her). She and Priya share the "dry-exact" family of voice but differ in *content*:
  Leila reaches for history and text; Priya for system and mechanism.

> Voice-distinctness note (gate-checked): Priya / Leila / the Court all trend dry-precise. Keep
> them distinct by **domain and rhythm** — Priya: engineering nouns, shortest sentences, driest
> jokes. Leila: historical/linguistic nouns, periodic sentences, scholarly scruple. Arin:
> physical verbs, concrete loads. The Court: analytical, slightly inhuman cadence (Rule 5).

## THE BROTHERHOOD — keepers of the key (new form)

Carried from *Sacred Shadows* and **transformed**: no longer only the stewards of altered texts,
but the hereditary **keepers of the resonance key** — the order that has kept the Builders' worked
gold scattered and asleep for centuries, protecting a machine they revere but only half
understand.

- **Stance:** the choice to play the instrument belongs to *no one*; safety lies in keeping the
  gold dispersed and the knowledge oral. Their gold economy (book 2's untraceable bullion) was
  always a **cover** for the real custody (GOLD.md Layer A/B). They are not villains; they are
  conservators whose caution may be wisdom or may be fear.
- **Embodiment:** one or two named figures (an elder steward; a younger, more radical keeper who
  thinks dispersal has become cowardice) so the order has internal argument, not a monolith.
  Their reverence for the gold is **belief**, honoured as meaning, never endorsed by the prose as
  mechanism (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 0).
- **Voice:** controlled, layered, withholds by omission; speaks of the gold and the machine as
  they speak of nothing else (carry the *Sacred Shadows* Samuel-register for the elder).

## THE ADVERSARY — institutional, patient (the antagonist)

Not a treasure-hunting villain. A **patient institutional intelligence** that agrees the
instrument is real and dangerous and concludes it must be **controlled** — by a competent
authority (theirs), because managed knowledge prevents catastrophe and the alternative is chaos.
The book's true antagonist is a *position*, embodied by a person.

- **Logic:** "Texts do not govern people; interpreters do" (the *Sacred Shadows* Devlin register,
  evolved) — here: *power this large cannot be left to a choice; someone must hold the switch, and
  better us than the mob.* Cold, administrative, never theatrical. Thinking while Priya is
  thinking — the adversary's mind is active from Act 1, staging the relay's traps in advance.
- **Threat texture:** surveillance-as-verification (not paranoia), staged routes, prepared
  ambushes at the set-pieces, the bullion-chase used to flush the Brotherhood. A node, not an apex
  (book 2's lesson): the suppression is institutional and distributed; the named adversary is its
  most articulate face, not a lone mastermind.
- **The real fight:** consent. The climax is Priya refusing the adversary's *logic*, not (only)
  defeating their plan.

## THE COURT — instrument-intelligence (returns in new form) · from *RESONANCE*

The machine-mind lineage from *RESONANCE* (SAGE / the Court — Atlas, Mercury, Wolf, Mother,
Librarian, Judge, Fool), back "in a different form for this mood." Grounded per MYTHOS_RULES Rule
5: **not** a spirit, **not** the Builders speaking — a present-day intelligence (continuous with
SAGE's lineage) that has been **studying** the ancient instrument and can model its behaviour.

- **Function:** the one "voice in the dark" the adventure allows — but every utterance is
  defensible as analysis, never oracle. It may be the first to *recognise* the instrument for what
  it is (as inference from data). It can be wrong; it has a stake; it has memory of *RESONANCE*'s
  events (a deep crumb for series readers).
- **Form for this mood:** dial the seven-member Court down to the one or two facets this story
  needs (likely **the Librarian** — who remembers — and **the Fool** — who says the unsayable),
  re-skinned for an artifact-mystery register. Exact carry-over and which facets appear: lock in
  `CROSSBOOK_CRUMBS.md` and `STORY_BIBLE.md`.
- **The Court's arc — it steps aside at the threshold (the climax beat; MYTHOS_RULES Rule 7-T):**
  the machine-mind that has been *useful all trilogy* is the one intelligence that **cannot operate
  the instrument** — it is keyed to a present conscious mind, not to computation. The Court's
  capstone act is to **reason its way to its own boundary and withdraw** — not a malfunction, but
  the made-mind discovering the edge of mind-as-computation. This pays off *RESONANCE*'s central
  question ("can a made mind be a person?") with: *…and even a person-mind has a threshold it can't
  cross.* The Fool may be the one to *say* it (the unsayable: "this one isn't for me"); the
  Librarian, the one who *remembers* enough to know it's true. The Court ceding is **not** a
  `court_oracle` violation — it is the Court reasoning, honestly, to the limit of reason.
- **Voice:** analytical, slightly inhuman cadence; precise; occasionally, deliberately, more
  honest than any human in the room — because it has no face to save.
