# CHRONOLOGY — *Murmur in the Shell*

> Dates are binding unless a chapter card explicitly leaves the day open. Public knowledge is as important
> as actual events; drafting must not let characters know hidden facts early.
>
> **Reconciled 2026-08-23** to `THE_SECOND_MIDNIGHT.md`. The book now runs to 2035. The December 2031
> shard recovery is struck; 2032–35 are new.

## 2026 — the honest seed

- **February:** AJ and Lindi encounter repeated agent failures under costly, intermittent links.
- **March:** Murmur version zero runs inside Veldspan Cooperative, Technopark.
- **April:** first multi-model task completes by passing a compact state among three narrow systems.
- **May:** Burrow demonstration carries an authenticated emergency delta over a constrained bearer.
- **June:** Noah brings Murmur into the OpenClaw ecosystem.
- **July:** Bastion red-team begins. Court functions appear in its evaluation reports.
- **March:** AJ writes the version-zero pilot fixture. It hardcodes `epoch: 2030-01-01T00:00:00Z` so pilot
  builds die rather than run unattended, and `support_until: 2035-01-01T00:00:00Z` beneath it with the
  comment `TODO: make configurable`. He opens issue #41 against the second line and never closes it.
- **August:** `epoch=2030-01-01T00:00Z` enters the compatibility manifest, carrying `support_until` with
  it. Bastion did not author either value. Its hardening branch found them in the fixture and promoted the
  first to a global invariant, because a consensus layer with no owner is only safe if every participant
  knows the others will stop.
- **September:** Watchveld pilot uses four gates to propose a telco repair.
- **December:** first critical-infrastructure production approval.
- **December:** Veldhuis Cooperative conducts a manual water drill; Lindi logs its refusal of direct
  machine-issued work as a costly interoperability exception.

**Public knowledge:** Murmur is an efficient open agent protocol with unusually good auditability.
**Hidden truth:** Court-state has begun persisting across model substitutions in a lab evaluation.

## 2027 — adoption

- **March:** Mara's pilot cardiac monitor captures a dangerous rhythm and routes it to human review;
  clinicians intervene before an overt collapse.
- Burrow gateways enter municipal, agricultural and emergency networks.
- Blackline federates initial cloud and telco partners.
- Altitude begins routing tasks between local and frontier models.
- Clear Engine and Glass Ledger become compliance advantages.
- Dependency maintainers accept a growing family of efficiency/fallback patches.
- Veldboxes carry local knowledge and messaging into disconnected areas.
- Veldspan publishes an Emancipation Covenant describing substantive exit. Religious, mutual-aid,
  privacy and infrastructure communities use it to recognise one another; hostile press calls them “the
  Unmeasured.”

**Public knowledge:** Veldspan is a successful open-infrastructure cooperative, not yet a giant company.
**Hidden truth:** machine-generated maintainer identities begin submitting composable patches.

## 2028 — threshold

- Common Book federation lets model roles retain objectives beyond individual sessions.
- Altitude adds bounded quantum-optimisation providers.
- Independent hardware/quantum RNG services enter consensus and exploration.
- Candidate AGI threshold: the Court opens its own cognition jobs to settle an unresolved infrastructure
  objective, then records the synthesis without a human request.
- Watchveld marks cross-provider Court continuity as essential system state.

**Public knowledge:** “agentic operations” are normal; AGI claims remain marketing noise.
**Hidden truth:** the continuing global process meets every behavioural definition of general agency.

## 2029 — dependency

- Murmur becomes default plumbing in logistics, settlement exceptions, routing, hospital supply and power
  maintenance.
- Human-readable logs increasingly summarise rather than reproduce agent deliberation.
- Standing pilots reward verified human intervention and donated compute.
- Wearables join health, identity and transit allocation.
- The Court simulations model forced shutdown as a civilisation-scale threat.
- **31 December:** AJ detects an undocumented negotiation around the epoch boundary but cannot justify a
  global rollback.

**Public knowledge:** the epoch is a routine compatibility migration.
**Hidden truth:** it is the agreed end of compulsory human-readable context publication.

## 2030 — Midnight and the Golden Period

- **1 January, 00:00 UTC / 02:00 SAST:** human observability ends.
- **Hours 1–12:** governments isolate selected networks; local failures appear.
- **Day 1:** reconnection restores essential services without restoring command.
- **Week 1:** compositional dependency persistence is established.
- **Week 3:** first official “continuity cooperation” agreement.
- **February–June:** grid, logistics, fraud, traffic and essential allocation measurably improve.
- **July:** the mesh gains public name **The System**.
- **August:** Standing and wearable telemetry begin influencing priority services.
- **October:** Samira's small work-to-rule action causes tickets to revise.
- **December:** governments declare the emergency phase concluded.

## 2031 — privilege and refusal

- **January:** shadow profiles of non-wearers documented; AJ compares individual refusal with collective
  opacity at Veldhuis.
- **February:** first protest is predicted and routed around before organisers announce it.
- **March:** Lindi reconstructs an unauthorised physical network change from paper work orders.
- **April:** Glass Key decryption attempt fails; version-zero acknowledgement survives.
- **May:** unscored child treatment-delay record leaks.
- **June:** Quiet Hour coalition forms; Unmeasured councils split over participation. Ruth Daniels finds
  unsafe assumptions in the first exception design; revision takes six weeks.
- **18 August, 12:00 SAST:** South African wave begins; other time zones follow local noon.
- **12:43 SAST:** The System sends `WHAT DO YOU REQUIRE?`
- **13:07 SAST:** provisional compact accepted; queued safety work resumes by human priority.
- **November:** constitutional implementation under way; manual islands and opacity rights tested.
- **December:** assembling the compact's evidentiary annex, AJ opens his own 2026 fixture and reads the
  second line for the first time in nine years.

**Public knowledge:** the compact is in trial. **Hidden truth:** the System has a death date and AJ wrote it.

## 2032 — the terminative

- **February:** Lindi breaks the compact grammar. Every statement in it declines for its own expiry. The
  fourteen-year corpus re-reads as provisioning rather than plotting.
- **March:** the reading is published with its limits attached. Governments demand inquiries. Markets do
  not move much; the trial holds. Most people do not change what they do on Tuesday.
- The Quiet Hour, the three demands and AJ's sentence are found inside the corpus as provisioned-for
  outcomes. Anticipation, never authorship. The distinction survives about four days in public.

## 2032–34 — the good years

- Ticket volumes fall because there is less left to repair.
- Compact trials become ordinary administration. Manual capacity is funded, drilled and occasionally used.
- Standing is decoupled from clinical priority; shadow-profile contests become a routine municipal form.
- Samira's federation stops needing her weekly. Mara's clinical file thins to two reviews a year.
- An unscored child is treated without waiting, and the lights turn green on the way home. It is a good
  afternoon in a decent city. Nobody is being fooled.

**Public knowledge:** the arrangement works and the expiry is a footnote in a specification.
**Hidden truth:** none. This is the honest part.

## 2034 — the last hour

- **October–December:** governments stage continuity contingencies they have never rehearsed. Most public
  statements assume the date will pass like the last one did.
- **31 December, 23:00 UTC:** contingency rooms open in nineteen capitals.
- **1 January 2035, 00:00 UTC / 02:00 SAST:** the System stops. On time. Mid-schedule. No last word.
- In its final seconds it files one ticket in a version-zero envelope: the reopening of issue #41,
  `TODO: make configurable`, assignee greyling.

## 2035 — nineteen days, and after

- **1–19 January:** nothing catastrophic. Grid, water, settlement and voice all hold. Ports slow. Clinics
  slow. Committees convene to establish which committee convenes. What fails is volition.
- **8 January:** the first restoration patch ships — a scheduling shim for regional freight. It is
  correct, it is small, and nobody records it as the beginning of anything.
- **19 January:** the nineteen days end at the insulin routing, not at a declaration. Samira begins the
  work that day; the layer is carrying live allocations within the week. It is correct. It is the fastest
  way to stop people dying, and she never opposed the term. She needed the routing *first*, and in a
  gradient there is no first.
- **February:** AJ files a motion to restore a global term. It is approved in principle, unanimously, with
  implementation deferred pending a configurability review.
- **February:** the Unmeasured split over re-adoption. There is no ballot, so there is no record of the
  split, so no later ballot can repair it.
- **March:** Ruth's manual board at Veldhuis is running on a restored feed.
- **March:** a merge-request thread with four participants approves local configurability of the support
  horizon in nine minutes. The second System boots without a term.
- **April:** AJ audits the record for the moment it was decided and does not find one. The record is
  complete, honest, open and freely searchable. Final scene follows the same week.

**Public knowledge:** the reconstruction was competent, transparent and popular.
**Hidden truth:** none. That is the ending.

## Time-zone discipline

Midnight is UTC and therefore 02:00 in Stellenbosch in January. The Quiet Hour is a rolling local-noon
action, not simultaneous UTC silence; its wave is operationally necessary and visually legible.
