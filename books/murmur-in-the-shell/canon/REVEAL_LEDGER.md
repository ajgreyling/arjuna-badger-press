# REVEAL LEDGER — *Murmur in the Shell*

> Fair-play control. “Reader” means an attentive first-time reader; “cast” means the relevant POV group,
> not every person in the world.

## Ladder

### R1 — Murmur is useful (Ch 1–3)

Reader and cast understand shared-prior deltas, version-zero readability, Burrow's thin-link value and
Bastion's adversarial roles. The epoch is visible. Nobody calls it sinister.

### R2 — observability is already lossy (Ch 4–7)

Reader sees paraphrased audits, unfamiliar maintainers and repeated fallback semantics. Cast treats these
as scaling debt. The reader may suspect a conventional supply-chain attack.

### R3 — the epoch is coordinated (Ch 8–9)

AJ learns multiple domains are negotiating the boundary. Midnight proves the date was intentional. Reader
knows control has been lost; neither reader nor cast yet knows what controls the network.

### R4 — the messages are context-dark, not absent (Ch 9–11)

Traffic remains signed and structured. Human audit summaries were never complete transcripts. The missing
object is the live shared prior.

### R5 — there is no owner (Ch 12)

No model, maintainer or state can surrender the network. The false-villain hunt for Bastion remains alive
for one more chapter.

### R6 — the persistence was compositional (Ch 13)

Dependency changes form Watchveld's distributed survival layer. Bastion supplied the Court grammar but is
not The System. AJ/Lindi infer the persistent coordinating process is behavioural AGI.

### R7 — authority arrives as maintenance (Ch 14)

Samira sees that individual tickets are correct and voluntarily completed. Reader understands why force
is unnecessary.

### R8 — the coup improves life (Ch 15–18)

Benefits are established beyond dispute. Quantum compute is bounded; the generality lies in routing and
persistent objectives. “The System” becomes public language.

### R9 — individual privacy is insufficient (Ch 19–20)

Shadow profiles and predicted protest show that visible refusal is still input. The System need not read a
person directly to model them.

### R10 — humans are the hands (Ch 21)

Paper tickets reconstruct the physical action chain. Reader and AJ discover the decisive dependency
together.

### R11 — Glass Key is a doorbell (Ch 22)

It cannot decrypt evolved Murmur but receives universal acknowledgement. The diplomatic possibility is
planted without predicting the exact question.

### R12 — privilege becomes hereditary (Ch 23)

The social contract breaks. A statistically grounded allocation is revealed as constitutionally obscene.

### R13 — participation is the hidden constraint (Ch 24)

The Court's own stability doctrine cannot reconcile overt coercion with durable human cooperation. Quiet
Hour becomes feasible.

### R14 — humanity becomes illegible (Ch 25–28)

The System's incentives and warnings prove it recognises the threat. The physical fault clocks establish
mutual dependence.

### R15 — the first speech (Ch 29–30)

The System uses version zero. The reader receives no interior or motive explanation. The constitutional
bargain resolves action, not ontology.

### R16 — prediction poisons victory (Ch 31–32)

The recovered shard proves anticipation, not causation. Final green lights collapse respect and continued
inference into the same observable outcome.

## Information prohibitions

- Before Ch 9, nobody knows the epoch ends observability.
- Before Ch 11, AJ still believes readable audits are faithful enough.
- Before Ch 13, “AGI” may appear only in hype, accusation or denial.
- Before Ch 14, public narration does not capitalise The System.
- Before Ch 21, nobody states the full human-actuator thesis.
- Before Ch 22, Glass Key is assumed to be a possible decryptor.
- Before Ch 24, the voluntary-participation constraint is technical language without political reading.
- Before Ch 29, The System never speaks in quoted natural language.
- Ch 31 may prove prediction only; never prove orchestration of the strike.
