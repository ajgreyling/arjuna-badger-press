# Homo Animalus — Style Guide

## Voice
First person, Andries J. Greyling. This is memoir braided with reportage, not a novel and not a
research summary. The house "guest-at-the-fire" nonfiction register (established in *The
Antifragile Reader*) governs the researched chapters: arrive at someone else's fire, sit down,
report what you found there faithfully, then say plainly what it means to you. Never a guru voice,
never a debunker voice. Wonder, not conspiracy. Evidence, not proof-by-anecdote.

## The living-author rule (hard gate, carried over from The Antifragile Reader)
Dean Radin, Merlin Sheldrake, Rupert Sheldrake, and Joe Dispenza are living authors with copyrighted
work. **Paraphrase and attribute their ideas. Never reproduce their prose.** Name the book, name the
claim, cite it as their claim, then respond to it in the author's own voice. A short, clearly
quoted line under fair use is fine; a page of their writing is not. Front matter carries an
"independent, unaffiliated with these authors" note, same as the Taleb and Heinlein precedents.

## The two threads, braided per chapter where possible
1. **The lived thread** — AJ's own animals and family, reported exactly as given, never punched up
   for drama. A dog's guilt-walk after biting a finger is worth more true than embellished.
2. **The evidence thread** — the research (Radin's psi meta-analyses, the Sheldrakes' morphic
   fields and mycelial networks, Dispenza's mind-body work, real documented cases like Cristina
   Zenato, dolphin cognition studies, the history of "animal magnetism," animal worship in
   antiquity, animal imagery in psychedelic/shamanic accounts).

Each chapter should let the lived thread ask the question and the evidence thread offer what is
actually known — including where the science is contested or incomplete. Don't oversell psi
research as settled fact; Radin's own claim is "the evidence is stronger than assumed," not
"proven." Say that honestly.

## The Sheldrake note (important, don't blur this)
Two different Sheldrakes are relevant and must not be conflated:
- **Rupert Sheldrake** (the father) — biologist, morphic resonance/morphic fields, and the direct
  match for "my dogs know my mental state": his research and book *Dogs That Know When Their
  Owners Are Coming Home* is the actual study of animal telepathy/anticipation.
- **Merlin Sheldrake** (the son) — mycologist, *Entangled Life*, the mycelial "wood wide web" and
  fungal intelligence/interconnection work.
AJ named Merlin specifically; both belong in the book, credited correctly, not swapped for each
other.

## Things never to fabricate
- Never invent a name, date, or detail about AJ's real animals or family beyond what he's given.
- Never invent quotes from Radin/Sheldrake/Dispenza. Cite the book/claim, don't paraphrase into a
  fake direct quote.
- Never claim a contested finding (psi, telepathy, dolphin self-awareness beyond the mirror-test
  literature) as scientific consensus. Report what the studies found and let the reader weigh it.

## Structure
Short chapters (2,000-3,500 words), each closing on a single clean line, same discipline as the
Achilles/Antifragile companion books ("the line"). Building toward the coda: *we are unmistakably
animal.*
