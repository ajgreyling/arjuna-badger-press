# *THE SHELTERING DESERT* (working title) — Story Bible

> **A standalone historical novel — its own world and canon.** An **original retelling of a true story:**
> two German geologists in South-West Africa who, rather than be interned as enemy aliens when the Second
> World War reached the colony, **drove into the Namib in May 1940 and survived in the Kuiseb canyon for
> two and a half years** by their wits and their bushcraft. It is **not** part of *History Before Time*,
> *The Unheard*, or the African Gold trilogy — **no Order, no Jakobus** (it is 1940; he is not born for
> nearly three decades). It shares only the **project's house voice** (grounded, sensory, *Sawubona*
> attention to land and people; the author's reverence for real bushcraft) and the **Namib** itself.
>
> **Tone (binding, the author's charge):** *great real bushcraft; not always a fun read; real story; real
> respect.* Unsentimental. The romance of "desert adventure" is **stripped out** — what's left is
> endurance, hunger, the water problem that never ends, the slow psychological erosion, and two trained
> scientists who kept *observing* the desert even as it nearly unmade them. We honour the men by telling
> it **straight.**

## 0. The true story (DOCUMENTED — the bedrock; see `PLACE_RESEARCH.md` for full sourcing)

Verified, real, and revered. The headline facts (all confirmed, sources in the research note):

- **The two men — both geologists** (this is binding to the POV and the texture). **Henno Martin**
  (1910–1998) and **Hermann Korn** (1907–1946), doctoral students of Hans Cloos, closest friends from
  Bonn. Troubled by the Nazis, they emigrated to South-West Africa in **1935**; before the war they
  mapped the escarpment, worked the Naukluft, and from 1937 located **water wells** for farmers (so:
  geologists *and* working surveyors — they read rock and water for a living, which is the engine of how
  they survive).
- **The dog — Otto** (Korn's dog), the third heart of the party and their alarm system; central, named,
  beloved. (Real beat: Otto gored by an oryx, saved by a sympathetic farming family.)
- **The decision (25 May 1940).** Britain and South Africa at war with Germany; the SA administration of
  SWA began interning German-descended men as enemy aliens. **Rather than surrender to the camps, they
  disappeared into the desert** — two vehicles, basic supplies, a rifle and a pistol, and Otto.
- **The place — the Kuiseb River canyon**, the schist gorges of its upper reaches. First shelter: a rock
  overhang they named **Carp Cliff / Karpfenkliff** (for the carp-filled pool below). Three hideouts over
  the period; the vehicles hidden in side gorges.
- **The survival.** They lived "like the old Bushmen" — hunting mountain zebra, gemsbok/oryx, ostrich,
  springbok, klipspringer, steenbok (biggest kill: a feral cattle bull) — and **fought the water problem
  every single day** (the Kuiseb's pools and waterholes). **The famous crisis is real: lean desert game
  gave too little fat → "rabbit starvation" / protein poisoning + vitamin-C lack;** it degraded them
  mentally ("the distinction between human beings and animals became blurred"); they ate organ meat
  (liver first, for the vitamins), sometimes raw, to cope. **This is the un-fun, un-romantic core.**
- **How it ended (September 1942).** **Korn's beriberi** (thiamine deficiency) grew severe enough that
  they abandoned the desert for medical help in Windhoek. On return they were — remarkably — **not
  interned**, and were taken on as **surveyors.**
- **After.** **Hermann Korn died 9 August 1946** — a night car crash off a railway bridge into the dry
  Gammams riverbed near Windhoek, circumstances never fully settled (accident / recklessness / possibly
  suicide). **Henno Martin** went on to a major geological career (SWA Geological Survey director; UCT
  Precambrian Research Unit; Chair of Geology, Göttingen) and **died in 1998.**

> **Both men are long deceased and may be named and depicted** (fairly, with respect). **The book
> *The Sheltering Desert* (Martin's memoir, 1956 German / 1957 English) is still in copyright** (EU life+70
> → 2068; actively published in English). **Therefore this is an ORIGINAL RETELLING of the true EVENTS,
> not a derivative of Martin's text** — facts and historical events aren't copyrightable; Martin's
> particular prose/structure/expression are. **Stay on the documented facts; build our own narrative and
> our own sentences.** (Binding legal guardrail — §6.)

## 1. The one-line

Two German geologists who read rock and water for a living walk away from a war that would cage them and
into the one place that might kill them faster — and learn that the desert they thought they understood as
scientists will only let them live if they let it teach them how, one hunger and one waterhole at a time.

## 2. Premise & shape

**1940. South-West Africa.** **Henno Martin** and **Hermann Korn** — émigré geologists who fled Hitler's
Germany five years earlier and have made a life of mapping this hard country and finding its hidden water —
face the war's long arm: internment as enemy aliens, the camps, years behind wire for the crime of being
German in a British mandate. They choose the third thing. They load two cars, take Korn's dog **Otto**, and
**vanish into the Namib**, into the Kuiseb canyon, meaning to wait the war out.

The novel is the **two and a half years that follow** — close-third on **Martin** (§3): the building of the
shelters; the daily, grinding hunt; the water that is never enough; the long boredom punctured by violence
(a charging oryx, a flash flood, a near-miss with a patrol or a farmer); and the slow, frightening erosion
of the body and the mind as the lean meat starves them of what flesh alone can't give. It is, structurally,
**a survival story that refuses to be an adventure** — the high is staying alive another month; the cost is
watching your best friend, and yourself, come apart by degrees. It ends not in triumph but in **surrender to
the inevitable** — Korn too ill to go on — and the strange anticlimax of walking back into the world that
was supposed to imprison them, and being, instead, *put to work.*

## 3. POV & voice (LOCKED)

- **Close-third on Henno Martin** — the survivor, the memoir's real narrator, the "quietly resolute,
  self-disciplined" one. The reader lives the desert through the disciplined scientist's eye: precise,
  observing, recording — which makes the moments his discipline *fails* (the hunger-madness, the fear for
  Korn) land all the harder. (Matches the project's house POV — single close-third on a lead.)
- **Hermann Korn — the volatile, brilliant co-lead, seen from outside.** Three years older, gifted, more
  temperamental; the friendship is the spine, and Korn's decline (the beriberi, the darkening) is the
  novel's emotional engine, read off him by Martin who cannot fix it.
- **Otto — the third heart**, never anthropomorphised into a cartoon; a real working dog whose fate the
  reader comes to dread.
- **The author's voice (binding).** The project house style: **grounded, sensory, unshowy; reverence for
  the land and for real competence; the *Sawubona* attention turned on the desert itself.** Where the rest
  of the project reads people, this reads **rock, water, game, weather** — the geologist's literacy as the
  survival skill. **Strip the romance; keep the awe.** The desert is neither enemy nor friend; it is
  *indifferent and exact,* and the book's respect is for the two men who learned to live inside that
  indifference. *(Drafting: let the science be the lens — the way Martin actually saw springbok aggregate
  after rain and kept recording even while starving — that double act, scientist-and-survivor, is the
  book's signature.)*

## 4. Themes (carry as theme, never preach)

- **The land is the teacher; the textbook is not enough.** Two men who *know* the desert as scientists
  discover that knowing-about is not knowing-how — the same humility engine the whole project runs on,
  here in its purest, most literal form (the lab-trained mind made a student of the ground).
- **Endurance without glory.** Survival as attrition, not heroics; the un-fun truth of it; dignity in
  simply *lasting.*
- **Friendship under erosion** — what two people become to each other when the world shrinks to a canyon,
  a rifle, a waterhole, and each other; and what it costs when one of them starts to fail.
- **The absurdity of the cage they fled** — men who would rather risk death in the desert than years
  behind wire for an accident of birth; the war reaching even the emptiest place on earth.
- **German South-West Africa / German South Africa as part of the fabric (§5)** — these are *German*
  protagonists, sympathetically drawn, anti-Nazi émigrés — a thread of the same many-peoples-one-place
  substrate the project honours everywhere; the German presence is *living history*, not a villain's
  backstory.

## 5. The German thread — part of the rainbow-nation fabric (author's note, 2026-06-11)

The protagonists are German, and that is not incidental texture — it is part of the project's standing
conviction that **the German strand is woven into the living fabric of South(ern) Africa, not just its
colonial past.** (The author lives in **Somerset West — "Little Germany"** — a real present-day German
enclave in the Cape: a German supermarket carrying German toilet paper, toothpaste, last week's German
newspapers and magazines; German-speaking Lutheran services; retirees wintering away from Germany's cold.
German South Africans and German Namibians are a continuous, living community.) In *this* book it shows as:
**émigré Germans who FLED Hitler** (the sympathetic, anti-Nazi German — the corrective to the genre's
lazy "German = enemy"); the German-Namibian farming community whose edges they brush; and the quiet irony
that the British mandate would cage the very Germans who hated the regime it was fighting. **Render the
Germanness with the same dignity the project gives every people** — neither romanticised nor villainised;
just *real, and part of this place.* *(This note also grounds the German thread that runs through the
Namibia / Rehoboth material — the Schutztruppe-era horse history, the settler farms; the two bodies of
work are cousins in setting and in honouring the German-Namibian presence.)*

## 6. Guardrails (binding)

1. **Original retelling, NOT a derivative of Martin's text** (§0 legal note). Build from the documented
   facts (`PLACE_RESEARCH.md`); write our own narrative and prose. Do not translate, abridge, or track the
   memoir's specific expression/structure. Facts are free; Martin's sentences are his.
2. **Depict the real people fairly and with respect** — both long deceased. Martin's discipline, Korn's
   brilliance-and-volatility, the real friendship, Korn's ambiguous death (handle with restraint; do not
   sensationalise the possible-suicide; render the ambiguity, not a verdict).
3. **Tell the hardship straight — no romance, no gore-for-thrill.** The diet crisis, the water, the
   psychological erosion are the *truth* of it; render them unflinchingly but never as spectacle. "Not
   always a fun read" is a feature; honour it.
4. **The desert and its peoples with accuracy and respect.** The Topnaar (ǂAonin) Nama of the Kuiseb are a
   real people of that exact country — if they appear, render them accurately and with dignity (they
   *belong* to the Kuiseb as the two Germans never will); the "lived like Bushmen" framing is Martin's
   period phrasing — handle the San/Bushman reference with the care the project's guardrails require
   (avoid the caricature; the survival skill is real and *theirs* first).
5. **The natural history must be real** (it's half the book's soul). Gemsbok, zebra, the Kuiseb's
   hydrology, the !nara melon, the desert's rhythms — get them right; the two men were *scientists*, and
   the book's authority is the accuracy of its desert.
6. **Sensitivity / accuracy read** before publication — Namibian / German-Namibian and natural-history
   checks; the Topnaar representation especially.

## 7. Voice & form

- **Register:** spare, exact, sensory; the patience of the desert in the prose; long stretches of grind
  punctuated by sharp violence and beauty. A survival-and-friendship literary novel, not a thriller.
- **Comparable shelf:** the unsentimental survival classics — *The Sheltering Desert* itself (honoured, not
  copied), Laurens van der Post's desert writing, *Endurance* (Lansing), *The Worst Journey in the World* —
  crossed with the project's grounded-Africa eye.
- **Length:** a focused standalone (the real span is 2.5 years — select and compress; don't transcribe the
  whole memoir's episode-list). The shape is **descent into the desert → the building of a life → the diet
  crisis (the midpoint that nearly ends them) → the long attrition → Korn's beriberi → the surrender → the
  strange anticlimax of being put to work,** with a coda on the after (Korn's 1946 death; Martin's long
  life) handled with restraint.
