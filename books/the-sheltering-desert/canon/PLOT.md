## ACT I — THE DECISION & THE DESCENT (ch 1–4)

**1. The Notice.** *(Windhoek / a farm, May 1940.)* Martin and Korn, five years émigré, hear the war has
reached them: German-descended men to be interned as enemy aliens. The bitter irony lands — they *fled*
Hitler, and the British mandate would cage them for being German. The friendship established: Martin the
disciplined one, Korn the brilliant volatile one. The decision forms, half a joke that hardens to a plan:
*if there's a war, we'll go into the desert.* `[REAL: the internment trigger; their characters]`

**2. Loading Out.** The practical choosing — what two scientists who know this country pack to disappear
into it: two vehicles (a bakkie, a Chevrolet coupé), a rifle and a pistol, tools, instruments, books, what
food they can. **Otto.** The geologist's calculation under it: they are choosing the Namib *because* they
know its water and its rock better than any camp guard knows a fence. The last look at the world that
would jail them. `[REAL: the kit, the two cars, Otto]`

**3. Into the Kuiseb.** *(25 May 1940.)* The drive in; hiding the vehicles in a side gorge; the first
night in the open canyon. The desert as the bible frames it — *indifferent and exact,* neither enemy nor
friend. Martin reads the country as a scientist and feels, for the first time, the gap between *knowing
about* and *knowing how to live here.* `[REAL: the date, the Kuiseb]`

**4. Carp Cliff.** They find and name the rock overhang — **Karpfenkliff** — for the carp-filled pool
below. The first making of a home: shade, wind, a defensible line against hyenas. The first small
competence wins (water from the pool, fish). A false, fragile sense that this might be *manageable.*
`[REAL: Carp Cliff, the carp pool]`

## ACT II — THE BUILDING OF A LIFE (ch 5–8)

**5. The First Kill.** The hunt begins in earnest — gemsbok/oryx, the rifle, the butchering, the
discipline of meat. Martin the scientist still recording even as he learns to be a hunter. The grim
satisfaction of food on the rock. The desert's first real gift, and its price (the stalk, the waste, the
weight of a life taken to keep two). `[REAL: oryx/gemsbok hunting]`

**6. Water.** The chapter that establishes the **never-ending water problem** as the book's true antagonist
— not a villain, a *condition.* Reading the Kuiseb's pools, the waiting waterholes, the fog. The
geologist's eye is the survival skill here; and even it is not always enough. The first time the water
nearly runs out. `[REAL: the Kuiseb hydrology; the daily water fight]`

**7. The Rhythm.** The shape of a desert day and week — the long stretches of boredom that survival
actually is, punctured by sharp need. The two men's friendship under the shrinking world: the talk, the
silences, the small frictions of two people who are now each other's entire society. Korn's brilliance and
his moods. Otto the third of them. `[invented texture, true to the frame]`

**8. The Oryx and the Dog.** **Otto is gored by an oryx** — the violence sudden after the boredom, the
fear for the dog, the emergency care, the brush with the outside world (a sympathetic farming family — the
Siedentopfs — render the German-Namibian farming community with dignity, §5). A reminder that the desert
kills small and fast, and that they are not as alone or as safe as the rhythm let them believe.
`[REAL: Otto gored by an oryx; the farming family]`

## ACT III — THE DIET CRISIS (the midpoint that nearly ends them) (ch 9–11)

**9. The Lean Months.** The dry season; the game goes thin; the fat disappears from the meat. The first
wrongness in the body — the *longing for fat* the bible names. Martin, the recorder, notices the symptoms
before he can name the cause: the weakness, the wrongness, the slow degradation. `[REAL: lean game, the
fat-hunger]`

**10. Rabbit Starvation.** The crisis at its worst — **protein poisoning / "rabbit starvation"** + the
vitamin-C lack. The grimmest chapter; the un-fun core the author insists on. The mind going: Martin's own
words as the frame — *"the distinction between human beings and animals became blurred."* Eating organ
meat, liver first, sometimes raw, to survive. Two scientists watching themselves come apart and *studying
it* even as it happens — the book's signature double act. `[REAL: rabbit starvation; the mental
degradation; liver/raw organ meat]`

**11. The Turn.** Pulling back from the edge — the dietary correction (organ meat, fat where they can get
it, what the desert's own pharmacy offers — the !nara melon, anything with the vitamins). Not a triumph; a
*reprieve.* The understanding, earned in the body now and not the textbook, that the desert will only let
them live on *its* terms, and that they nearly didn't learn in time. `[REAL: the recovery via organ
meat/diet]`

## ACT IV — THE LONG ATTRITION & THE SURRENDER (ch 12–15)

**12. The Watchers.** The war intrudes — a patrol, a search, the sound of an aircraft, the constant low
fear of being found (the 1915 Garub bombing is a generation back, but aircraft over this desert carry
that ghost). The discipline of staying hidden; the second/third shelters; moving when they must. The
strangeness of hiding from a war in the emptiest place on earth. `[REAL: the hiding, the moves between
three shelters; the evasion]`

**13. The Long Grind.** Time itself as the antagonist now — the months, the sameness, the slow wearing-
down of two men past the point where adventure has any meaning. The friendship tested by the attrition;
Martin holding the discipline; Korn beginning, almost imperceptibly, to fail. The desert's beauty
returning in flashes (the game aggregations after rain — *"three to four thousand springbok"* — the
scientist's wonder surviving even here). `[REAL: the duration; the post-rain game aggregations]`

**14. Korn.** **Korn's beriberi** declares itself — the thiamine deficiency, the swelling, the weakness,
the unmistakable decline. Martin, who can read rock and water and game, cannot read a way out of this. The
dread that has been building since the diet crisis arrives in his best friend's body. The thing the desert
finally cannot be out-thought on. `[REAL: Korn's beriberi]`

**15. The Surrender.** *(September 1942.)* The decision to give up the desert — not beaten by capture but
by the body's hard limit. The drive out, Korn failing, toward the city and the camp they fled two and a
half years ago. The strange grief of leaving the canyon that nearly killed them and was, somehow, *theirs.*
`[REAL: the Sept 1942 exit for Korn's medical help]`

## CODA — THE ANTICLIMAX & THE AFTER (ch 16)

**16. Put to Work.** The anticlimax the truth insists on: they are **not interned.** They walk back into
the world braced for wire and are, instead, *handed jobs as surveyors* — the war machine wanting their eyes
for the country they now know better than any man alive. The deflation and the absurdity of it. And the
restrained coda on the after: **Korn's death in 1946** (the night car crash off the railway bridge,
circumstances never settled — render the *ambiguity*, not a verdict; one quiet, devastating beat); and
**Martin's long life** carrying the desert and the friend in him. The last image: the indifferent country,
still there, outlasting them both. `[REAL: not interned, taken on as surveyors; Korn d. 1946; Martin's
long career/death 1998]`
