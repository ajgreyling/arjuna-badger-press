# Cover & Jacket Copy — *The Sheltering Desert* (standalone)

> The binding source of truth for the cover/jacket TEXT. A standalone historical novel — **not**
> part of The African Gold Trilogy, so it carries **no trilogy series line** and no triptych glyph.
> The one non-negotiable, per the author's charge: **the cover must state plainly that this is a true
> story of two real men who survived by real bushcraft.** Tone everywhere: *great real bushcraft; not
> always a fun read; real story; real respect.*
>
> Working title caution: *The Sheltering Desert* is also the title of Henno Martin's own (in-copyright)
> memoir — a distinct title is recommended (e.g. *The Kuiseb Years*, *Carp Cliff*, *The Indifferent
> Country*). Whatever title is chosen, the true-story credit and the bushcraft line below stay.

## Back cover

**Pull line (top):**
> *It really happened. Two men walked into the Namib to keep out of a war — and the desert almost
> kept them.*

**Blurb:**

In May 1940, with the Second World War reaching South-West Africa and every German-descended man
facing internment as an enemy alien, two geologists chose a third way. **Henno Martin** and
**Hermann Korn** — anti-Nazi émigrés who had fled Hitler five years before — loaded two cars, took
Korn's dog **Otto**, and drove into the Namib Desert to wait the war out. They were not adventurers.
They were scientists who read rock and water for a living, and that literacy was the only thing
standing between them and death.

They lasted **two and a half years** in the Kuiseb River canyon. This is the true account of how:
the daily hunt for game, the search for water that never ended, the shelters built by hand, and the
slow, frightening crisis when lean desert meat began to starve them of what flesh alone cannot give.
No romance, no invention — **real bushcraft, told straight**, and a friendship the desert nearly
took apart.

**Credit line (small, near the foot):**
`This novel is an original retelling of documented events. Henno Martin told his own story in `*`The
Sheltering Desert`*` (1956); his words are his. The facts — and these two men — belong to history.`

**House motto (tiny):** `Per Ardua ad Magnum.`  ·  ISBN / barcode block.

## Art direction (brief, for whoever renders it)

Photoreal, unsentimental: the Kuiseb canyon at dusk or hard noon — schist gorge, dry riverbed, a
lone figure (or two) small against the rock for scale; oryx/gemsbok or a windmill on a far rise
optional. **No "desert adventure" glamour** — heat, distance, and endurance, not romance. Earth and
bone palette (ochre, schist-grey, bleached sky); title in the lower third over the darker
foreground. The mood is awe and attrition, not thrill.
