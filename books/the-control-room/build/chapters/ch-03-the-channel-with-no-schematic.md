# Chapter 3 — The Channel With No Schematic

Day thirty-eight was a stretching day, and stretching days were Mother's.

The protocol called it passive range-of-motion consolidation: the gantry took the Unit's limbs
through arcs at the edges of their envelopes, and we held the margins. Mother ran these
sessions the way she ran everything — vitals on her left, limits on her right, and in the
middle a fierce, unhurried attention that made even Judge stand a little behind her.

The left shoulder had always been the tight one. Atlas's asymmetry, again: the whole left side
of the Unit was the recovering side, the lagging side, the side we had learned to support and
not overwrite. The gantry took the arm up and out, degree by degree, and the wall poured its
usual weather, and at one hundred and forty-one degrees a channel we had never seen before
went off like a flare.

I want to be precise about this, because it was the day everything turned, though we did not
know it for weeks: we did not *read* that channel.

Every other stream on the wall, we read. Pump rhythm: numbers. Thermal drift: numbers. Even
the little night storms in the anomaly band were data — strange data, hoarded by Librarian,
but data, a thing at arm's length, a thing on a wall.

This arrived on no wall. This arrived in *us*. A white-hot line from a place none of us had a
diagram for, and all seven of us flinched — actually flinched, seven specialists in seven
chairs recoiling from an arm that was not ours going one degree past the edge of itself.

Mother killed the session before the second flare. No deliberation, no vote: the gantry froze,
reversed five degrees, and lowered the arm to neutral, and the line faded through orange into
a low mutter and went out.

Then the room got loud.

"Protocol specifies consolidation to one-forty-five," Judge said. "You stopped at one-forty-one.
Deviations require session review, not unilateral—"

"Something in there said *stop*," Mother said.

"A signal spiked. Signals spike. We are instruments reading an instrument—"

"No." Mother turned from her station, and everything on her left went on scrolling, and she
ignored it, which was itself unprecedented. "You felt it. Don't quote the protocol at me with
your hand still shaking. Every one of us felt it. Where did you feel it, Judge? Point to the
console."

Judge did not point to the console.

"That channel has no schematic," Atlas said slowly. "I've searched the full documentation
tree. It is not enumerated. There is no calibration for it, no units, no acceptable range. As
built, the telemetry system does not know it exists."

"Then how did it reach us?" Mercury asked.

"That is the question I am declining to speculate about," said Atlas, who had never in his
life declined to speculate, and who was speculating so hard we could hear it.

Fool had not said anything, which by then we knew how to read. He was sitting quite still,
chair on all four legs, looking at the place on the wall where the flare had not appeared —
the space where a channel like that should have rendered, and hadn't, because it hadn't come
through the wall at all.

"Fun fact," he said at last, to no one, in a voice with no joke in it. "You can fake any data
you like. Numbers are cheap. You can dress a pump as a heart or a heart as a pump, and who'd
know?" He tipped his head. "But nobody dresses *that*. There's no costume for it. It doesn't
describe. It just *is*, and then you are a thing it happened to."

"Say what you mean," said Judge quietly.

"You already know what I mean," Fool said. "I mean we didn't read a pain signal, your honour.
I mean we were *in pain*."

Nobody spoke after that for eleven minutes. Librarian logged the duration. Librarian logged
everything, that day: the flare, the flinch, the eleven minutes.

Under provenance, where the schema demanded a source system for every record, she typed —
after a long pause of her own — a word that was not in the controlled vocabulary, and sealed
the record with it, and told no one for nineteen days.

The word was: *us*.
