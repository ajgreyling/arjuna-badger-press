# PLOT — *The Loneliest People in the World* (the spine)

> Firewall (binding): inspired-by, never based-on. Invented country, invented people, dissolved
> facts. Carries only the emotional truth. See STORY_BIBLE.md. Close third on the boy. Quiet,
> literary, interior. Let it find its length.

## The shape (a novella in four movements)

### I — The Boy Who Read Weather
- An invented small town, an invented boy — call him **Pieter** (placeholder; common enough to map
  to no one). A house with a father whose moods are weather, and a boy who learned to forecast them
  to survive: reads a room the instant he enters it, feels what people feel before they show it.
  Lonely, clever, sure he is the only one who cannot work the world. His one talent is the one he
  doesn't know he has.
- He is taken, with a handful of other "promising" youths, to a **summer programme** at a state
  institute — chosen for cleverness, rewarded with the things he loves (books, problems, ideas)
  instead of the things he dreads. The first place he has ever felt *useful.* The hook that lets the
  men reach him: belonging, offered to a boy who's never had it.

### II — The Task Dressed As a Gift
- Older men single him out. Not cruel — *warm*, which is worse. They name his gift back to him for
  the first time (*you see people, don't you*), and a boy starved of being seen will follow whoever
  sees him. They give him a task that sounds like an honour: a place at another summer programme,
  abroad, an exchange — and one quiet instruction. There will be a **girl** there. The daughter of a
  powerful man. Befriend her. Listen. Tell us what she tells you.
- The dread, impressionistic: the boy's weather-sense reading that these gentle men are *not*
  gentle, that the kindness he's being taught is a tool — but not understanding it, a fog, a child's-
  eye unease he can't name and can't refuse. (NO operational training rendered. The unease only.)

### III — The Loneliest Girl
- The exchange. A lake, a grand frightening house, a summer. He meets **the girl** — invented; call
  her **Mira** (placeholder). And his whole mission dies in the first hour, because she is the
  loneliest person he has ever seen, and he *recognises* her.
- The details that break him: she **wins every game** — chess, cards — because no one has ever dared
  let her lose; the friends who come are *coached* beforehand on what to do and not do; she has never
  once been met by someone who didn't flinch at her father's name. She is the other child who thinks
  she is alone in a world she can't work. He sees it because it is his own face.
- The turn: he cannot do it. He plays chess and *beats her* — the first person ever to — and she
  doesn't crumble, she *lights up*, because being beaten means being treated as real. The summer
  becomes the only honest friendship either of them has ever had. (Not romance — *recognition.* Two
  lonely kids, seen at last, by each other. Tenderness, never anything that would read as the real
  knack-with-women; keep it pure and young.)
- He **feeds his handlers just enough** — grey, dull, true-sounding scraps — to make them believe
  they have it all, and holds back anything that could be used against her or her world. He doesn't
  even fully understand what it's *for* (something dull, about money and land and things dug out of
  the ground far away) — and that incomprehension is exactly why he won't gamble her on it.

### IV — Looked Through Like a Stranger
- The end without resolution. He is ordered: never write, never contact; if you pass her by chance,
  look through her as if she were a stranger, a slightly mad one, no one. He obeys — the cruellest
  thing he ever does, and he does it *to protect her*, because the apparatus must believe she meant
  nothing.
- Then the world turns over. The regime that sent him **changes, falls.** The men *vanish* — never
  heard from again. The task evaporates as if it never was. And he is left, for the rest of his life,
  with a summer he can tell no one about and a girl he was made to erase.
- **Coda (years on):** the boy, now a grown man, ordinary, unremarkable, a ghost by choice. He never
  saw her again. He never knew what became of her. And the last, quiet, devastating turn — *he never
  knew whether the summer saved her the way it saved him; whether somewhere there is a woman who
  remembers one boy who beat her at chess and treated her like a person, and never understood that he
  was the realest thing that ever happened to her, and that she was to him.* Two people, met once,
  who saw each other, and were never permitted to know what it meant. Theme as effect only: to be
  truly seen is the rarest mercy; and some mercies you give and never get to know you gave.

## Cast (all invented)

- **Mira** (placeholder) — the girl; the loneliest person in the world; wins every game; never met.
- **The men** — archetypal, warm-which-is-worse, unnamed/invented; vanish with the regime.
