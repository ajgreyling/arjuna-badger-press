# Those Who Came Down

> *The Anunnaki, as the dreamers tell it* · fiction · committed mythos · close third (Enki) · twelve tablets

The third leg of the estate's Mesopotamia shelf. *The Walls of Uruk* tells the poem plainly and
forbids the drift; *The Princely Offspring* walks in through the alien door and kills the reading in
the cuneiform; **this book is the reading itself, played dead straight from inside** — Sitchin's
furniture (Nibiru, the sar, the gold, the made worker), von Däniken's vertigo, told once, whole,
with the house's full craft, and honestly labelled as the dreamers' telling. Cousin to the
`lucid.rodeo/patrick/` warren ("first we tell it like it's true"); this is Act I sustained for a
whole book, with the blink living in the front matter and in the shelf itself.

The house pillar holds from inside the myth: the walkers of the Abzu are clever before any god
touches them, the flawed are given fates and not corners, and the ending hands everything back —
the walls, the city, and writing are human inventions the makers never planned and cannot match.
