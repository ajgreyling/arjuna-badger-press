# STYLE — *Those Who Came Down*

## Register

- **Close third, Enki only, past tense.** Mythic-grounded: the sentences carry weight but stay
  physical. Think the Walls-of-Uruk companion's reverence wearing the HBT line's sensory boots.
- The artifact leads, always: gold dust before the reason for gold; the reef before the mine; the
  clay before the theology; the wedge before the word "writing."
- Hardware impressionistic. Descent craft are fire, thunder, a hull like weather, a door that sighs.
  Never chrome, never a control panel, never technobabble. Von Däniken vertigo comes from what the
  ground-dweller would see, even when the POV is the one riding.
- Time is told in sars and in generations of the short-lived. Let the asymmetry ache: Enki loses
  human friends the way we lose dogs.

## Names

Enki · Enlil · An (stays above) · Ninmah (the midwife; later called Ninhursag) · the Igigi (the
junior labour) · Geshtu-e ("the ear," the slain) · Nibiru (home; the crossing-world) · the Abzu
(the deep gold country in the south of the great land) · the Edin (the plain between rivers) ·
Eridu · Shuruppak · Ziusudra · Uruk · the black-headed people.

## Taboos (house de-LLM doctrine applies in full)

- No "Not X. Y." reframe as a crutch; no em-dash addiction; no triple-beat default rhythm.
- Theme never stated aloud by anyone, god or man.
- No over-explaining a marvel after it lands. Show the wedge; do not lecture on literacy.
- The flood chapter earns its quiet: short sentences, no adjectival grief.
- Never sneer at the humans and never pity them. They are the book's secret protagonists.

## Rhythm

Twelve tablets, 600–1,000 words each. Each tablet ends on a turn or a held breath. Tablet XII ends
on hope, not certainty.
