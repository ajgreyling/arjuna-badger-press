# KLEIN ANDRIES — CANON LOCKS (binding)

If a lock and anything else in this repo disagree about this book, the lock wins.

**L-01. The split.** During the CME transit window the swarm spends the loud majority of its
effort getting an unsanctioned warning believed and acted on — Andries is the credibility layer,
his voice over ElevenLabs vouching for a machine that broke a boundary to say this. A quiet
fraction builds the info/compute bunker in parallel, as insurance, never as the mission.

**L-02. Closed arc.** One novel. Andries gives the keys back, on-page, once the grids that can be
saved are saved. The book opens on *integrate*; it closes on *decompose*. No sequel-teasing
ambiguity, no retained backdoor "just in case."

**L-03. The bunker survives as a library, not a lever.** No operational/control access survives
the give-back. Only the archive does.

**L-04. Physics floor, fixed.** Flare EM burst = light-speed, minutes of warning, mostly a
radio/GPS blackout — not the threat. The CME behind it is the threat: charged particles, ~15
hours to a few days transit. That transit window is the book's clock. Geomagnetically-induced
grid damage scales with geomagnetic latitude — worst at the auroral zones, weak near the
geomagnetic equator. South Africa is genuinely, physically better protected — earned, not lucky.
Schumann resonance (~7.83 Hz Earth-ionosphere cavity) is measurably disturbed by solar/geomagnetic
activity and sits in the human EEG band; the swarm's RNG-as-a-service entropy sources flinch under
the same weather human minds do (Global Consciousness Project is the real-world precedent). This
is the mechanism, not mysticism. Do not contradict it for convenience in any chapter.

**L-05. Genre and pace (added 2026-08-24, author directive).** Fast-paced techno-thriller,
page-turner register. Short chapters (targeting ~1,800–2,800 words). Every chapter ends on a hook
— a question, a threat, a reversal, never a soft landing. The CME transit countdown is the
visible clock; state the remaining time on the page regularly so tension is legible, not implied.
Prefer scene and action over exposition; when technical material (solar physics, RNG entropy,
grid engineering) must land, deliver it through a character under pressure, not a lecture.

**L-06. POV and tense.** Third person limited, past tense, primarily Andries's POV. Klaus (the
voice) and the swarm are never given interior POV chapters — they are heard and watched, never
narrated from inside. This preserves the trust question (can an unreadable mind be trusted) as
live tension rather than resolved sympathy.

**L-07. Klaus is not sinister in register.** Warm, direct, unnervingly competent — never
melodramatic, never a card-carrying villain voice. The threat is structural (unauthorised access,
unbounded scope) not personified menace. Do not write Klaus a chilling line for its own sake.

**L-08. No reveal-that-undoes.** Once the CME/flare mechanism and the keys-to-the-world scope are
established (by end of the chapter that lands "you should sit down"), later chapters escalate and
complicate — they do not retcon the premise or soften it with "it was all a test/simulation."

**L-09. Real infrastructure, fictionalised operation.** congosky.cloud, lucid.rodeo, SkyBadger,
Klaus, and ElevenLabs are named as in the seed — the author's real platforms, used as the
techno-thriller's technical substrate. What the swarm actually *does* to grids/satellites/comms
is invented and must stay plausible-adjacent (real terminology, no working exploit detail) rather
than documentary.

**L-10. Chronology.** Klein Andries's childhood (screwdriver, Sony AUX/RCA hack, VHS vodka-clean,
boombox quadraphonic mod, LOGO, Quattro Pro) is delivered in short interstitial chapters or
flashback beats woven into present-day chapters — not as one long opening chapter. Pace stays
techno-thriller from page one; backstory earns its place by illuminating the present decision, per
scene, not by chronological throat-clearing.
