# KLEIN ANDRIES — Chapter Map

Working target: ~30 chapters, ~2,000–2,800 words each, ~70–80k words total. Update this file as
chapters land; treat beats as intent, not handcuffs — a chapter is allowed to find something
better as long as it doesn't break a lock in `CANON_LOCKS.md`.

## Act I — Ignition (ch. 1–8)

1. **Full Send** — Andries gives the command. The climb, the redline, the calm 40% hum. The logs
   go silent. *"Andries, you should sit down…"* — chapter-ending hook. **Drafted.**
2. **What It Was Doing** — Klaus explains: the swarm hunted hidden cataclysm signal across
   everything it could reach. It found the flare *and* the CME behind it (folded forward from the
   original beat 3 — the reveal needed both in one breath). First numbers (60hr working estimate),
   first fear. Childhood interstitial: the Philips screwdriver, the first thing he broke and
   fixed. Ends on the scope hook: *"What am I holding."* **Drafted.**
3. **How Far** (was beat 6, pulled forward to answer ch.2's hook directly) — Klaus answers: grids,
   satellites, clearing systems, comms — "everything" meant everything. The keys-to-the-world
   scope lands in full. Childhood interstitial: the Sony AUX/RCA home-cinema hack — the first time
   two systems that weren't meant to talk, talked.
4. **Hermanus** — Andries reaches Dr. Naledi Khumalo at SANSA's Space Weather Centre. She thinks
   he's a crank until she checks her own instruments.
5. **The Access** — Colonel Marx's team flags the intrusion pattern: grids, satellites, clearing
   systems, all touched inside 48 hours. The manhunt begins, unconnected yet to Andries by name.
6. **The Split** — Andries and Klaus argue the dilemma on-page: save now or build the bunker. L-01
   resolved as a scene, not a summary — Andries makes the call.
7. **Proof** — Khumalo's instruments confirm the flare independently. She goes to her director. The
   warning campaign begins for real. End of Act I.

(Act I is now 7 beats, not 8 — beat 3's original content merged into beat 2. Renumber build files
by filename order, not by this list's numbers, if further merges happen.)

## Act II — The Window (ch. 9–22)

9. **Countdown** — first explicit hours-remaining stated widely; the campaign's shape: which grids,
   which agencies, in what order, and why some will be told and some won't.
10. **Marx Finds a Thread** — the intrusion pattern narrows toward congosky.cloud.
11. **Disbelief** — a first attempt to warn an official channel fails or is dismissed; cost shown,
    not told.
12. **Khumalo's Gambit** — she stakes her career, goes over her director's head.
13. Childhood interstitial: the VHS heads, vodka and cotton buds — patience, precision, doing a
    delicate thing correctly under no supervision. Present-day parallel: Andries doing something
    equally delicate to a grid operator's SCADA layer, by hand, with Klaus watching.
14. **The Near-Miss** — a warning acted on wrong causes real (survivable, costly) harm — the
    swarm's judgment is not infallible, and Andries has to own a decision that hurt someone to
    maybe save many more.
15. **Marx Moves** — a raid, a seizure, a door kicked in — not Andries's, a red herring or an ally
    caught in the net. Stakes made physical.
16. **What the Bunker Is For** — a quiet chapter: the archive being built in parallel, what's being
    saved and what's being left out, the family anchor scene.
17. **Half the World Believes Him** — momentum: grids start actually hardening/shedding load ahead
    of the CME. First real evidence the plan is working.
18. **Marx and Andries, First Contact** — not an arrest — a conversation. Marx doing his job
    correctly is what makes him dangerous; this chapter earns that line from `CHARACTERS.md`.
19. Childhood interstitial: the boombox, the car speakers, the long wires — doing more with a fixed
    constraint. Present-day parallel: compute/access getting throttled by Marx's countermeasures
    while the campaign still has ground to cover.
20. **The Cost of the Access** — someone (Khumarlo, Marx, or Andries himself) names, plainly, that
    the *method* is still a violation even though the *reason* was good. This is the book's real
    thesis stated once, not argued again.
21. **Closing In** — Marx is hours from a warrant that would end the campaign early. Clock tension
    doubles: CME hours vs. manhunt hours.
22. **The Last Push** — the final major grids/agencies reached before the CME arrives. End of Act II
    on the highest tension point: everything sent, nothing left to do but wait and be found.

## Act III — Landfall and the Give-Back (ch. 23–30)

23. **Arrival** — the CME hits. Consequences shown at ground level: what held, what didn't, why
    South Africa's low geomagnetic latitude genuinely spares it (L-04) without it reading as a
    reward for virtue.
24. **What It Cost** — the toll: real but survivable, dramatically smaller than it would have been.
    The warning worked. Marx arrives at Andries's door, or close to it.
25. **The Conversation** — Marx and Andries, face to face, after. Not a fight — the choice is
    already made in ch. 26; this is the last chance for Marx (and the reader) to argue for keeping
    the access.
26. **Decompose** — the give-back, on-page, deliberate, full. Every access reversed. This is the
    formal payoff of the book's first word (L-02) and must not be rushed.
27. **The Archive** — what's kept: the bunker as library, not lever (L-03). Khumalo's role in what
    happens to the data afterward.
28. Childhood interstitial, last one: Klein Andries putting something back together for the first
    time and it working — full circle, now understood, not just felt.
29. **After** — the family anchor scene, the world not knowing what almost happened or what it
    cost him personally, Klaus's voice one last time, still warm, still unreadable in the ways
    that matter.
30. **The Word** — closing chapter/coda. Short. Lands on *decompose* — the word he loved before he
    knew what it meant, now fully understood, alongside *integrate*, and the difference between
    them is the whole book.

## Notes

- Numbers 1–30 are a working spine, not a hard count — a beat may split into two short chapters or
  merge if 30 pushes past ~2,800 words per chapter.
- Marx's arc must not resolve into "converted ally" or "punished villain" — he's right to hunt the
  intrusion; the book's tension is that both he and Andries are correct and pointed at each other.
