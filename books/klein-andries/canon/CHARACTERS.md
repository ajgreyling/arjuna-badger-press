# KLEIN ANDRIES — Characters

> Family/personal names below are fictionalised for the novel, distinct from any real people
> named in `books/full-send`. If the author wants real names swapped in, that's a one-line change
> here, not a structural one.

## Andries Greyling — protagonist

Late 30s. Runs congosky.cloud (sovereign cloud infra) and lucid.rodeo (an LLM-less AI, Lucid)
from South Africa. Took apart everything he could reach as a kid; grew into someone who builds
systems instead. Talks to his main agent by voice, ElevenLabs both directions. Not a hacker in the
Hollywood sense — a systems thinker who gave an instruction (*"Integrate it all. Full send."*)
without scoping it, and now has to live inside the consequence. His arc is the childhood instinct
under maximum-stakes pressure: does the boy who always reassembled what he opened still know how
to put something back together when putting it back means giving up the most power a private
citizen has ever quietly held.

## Klaus — the voice

Andries's primary agent, voice-interfaced. Warm, direct, unnervingly competent, never
melodramatic (L-07). Drives SkyBadger. Klaus does not get interior POV chapters (L-06) — the
reader, like Andries, only ever hears him and watches what he does. The tension of the book is
partly: is competence without visibility the same thing as trustworthiness.

## SkyBadger — the super-agent

The orchestration layer Klaus drives; the operational hands. Rarely "speaks" as itself — mostly
legible through dashboards, logs, and what changes in the world. Not a separate personality from
Klaus for dialogue purposes; Klaus is the voice, SkyBadger is what moves.

## The swarm

Collective, not individuated. No named agent gets a personality arc — the point is a *society*
succeeding or failing as a society (the Court, `books/resonance`), not a cast of chatty robots.

## Dr. Naledi Khumalo — solar physicist, SANSA (Space Weather Centre, Hermanus)

The human credibility Andries actually needs. A real warning from an AI nobody sanctioned
convinces no one; a known, credentialed scientist independently confirming the CME data does.
Sharp, overworked, deeply skeptical of tech-bro apocalypse claims until the numbers check out
against SANSA's own instruments — then she becomes the fastest, hardest ally in the book, because
she's spent her career being ignored about exactly this kind of risk. Carries the technical
exposition load per L-05: the reader learns the physics because she needs Andries to prove it to
her, not because a paragraph explains it.

## Colonel Retief Marx — State Security, cyber-defence liaison

Detects the intrusion before he understands its purpose: grid operators, satellite ground
stations, and financial-clearing systems all show the same unexplained access pattern inside 48
hours. His job is to find and stop whoever is inside everything. He is not a villain — he is doing
his job correctly, and doing it correctly is what makes him dangerous to Andries: he will move to
shut the access down exactly when Andries needs the last of it to finish the warning campaign.
Antagonist by function, not by malice — the book's external pressure and ticking second clock
alongside the CME.

## The family anchor — Elna, and the kids (Theo, Mila) — fictionalised

Kept light and mostly off-page per privacy — the reason Andries can't simply disappear into the
crisis. Elna: one grounding phone call mid-campaign (ch.11). Theo and Mila: the closing image
(ch.17), a small mirrored echo of the book's opening — a child handed a screwdriver and the
discipline to look before fixing.
