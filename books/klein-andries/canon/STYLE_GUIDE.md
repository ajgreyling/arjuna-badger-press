# KLEIN ANDRIES — Style Guide

Governs every chapter. See `CANON_LOCKS.md` L-05–L-10 for the binding decisions this expands on.

## Register

Fast-paced techno-thriller. Short chapters, ~1,800–2,800 words. Scene and action carry the book;
exposition is smuggled in through a character who needs the information under pressure (Khumalo
demanding Andries prove the CME numbers; Marx's team reconstructing the intrusion pattern).

## The clock

The CME transit window is the visible spine of the book. State the remaining time on the page at
or near the top of most chapters once the countdown starts (a line, a timestamp, a dashboard
readout — vary the device, never drop the number for long). The reader should always be able to
say, at any point past the reveal, roughly how many hours are left.

## Chapter endings

Every chapter ends on a hook: a reversal, a threat, a question, an arrival, never a soft landing.
No chapter closes on reflection alone.

## POV / tense

Third person limited, past tense, Andries's POV unless a chapter is explicitly Khumalo's or
Marx's (both permitted for cross-cutting thriller tension — intercut chapters are allowed and
encouraged once the manhunt starts). Klaus/the swarm never get a POV chapter (L-06).

## Sentences

Default to clear, mid-length sentences. Shorten hard during action or high tension — fragments are
allowed there. Avoid stacked subordinate clauses in tense scenes. Save longer, more considered
sentences for the rare quiet beats (the childhood interstitials, the final give-back).

## Technical material

Real terminology, plausible-adjacent operation, no working exploit detail (L-09). When a system
is being touched (a grid operator's SCADA layer, a satellite ground station, a clearing system),
name the real shape of the thing without providing a recipe. The physics floor (L-04) is fixed and
must not be contradicted for convenience.

## Childhood interstitials

Short (under 1,000 words), placed where they illuminate the present decision, not chronologically
first (L-10). Formally distinct from present-day chapters — a clear header or scene break marks
the shift, no ambiguity about when/where the reader is.

## Klaus's voice on the page

Warm, direct, precise, occasionally wry — never grandiose, never a monologue. He answers what's
asked, volunteers only what matters, and the not-melodramatic rule (L-07) applies even at the
"you should sit down" beat: the line lands because of what it interrupts, not because of how it's
delivered.

## Forbidden moves

- No chapter-ending line that reveals nothing new (pure atmosphere is not a hook).
- No retconning the premise (L-08).
- No giving Klaus/the swarm interior narration (L-06).
- No softening the give-back at the end into an ambiguous "maybe he kept a little" (L-02/L-03).
