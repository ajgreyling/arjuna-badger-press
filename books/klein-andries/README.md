## The one-line

A kid who fell in love with words like *decompose* and *integrate* before he knew what they
meant grows up to run the thing they describe — and then it goes quiet, and speaks.

## Why this is a novel and not a memoir

`books/full-send` is the direct-address memoir — Klaus, named, addressed in the second person,
receipts included. This is the same life, refracted: **Klein Andries**, not Andries Greyling.
Fiction gives the ending room the memoir doesn't have yet, because the memoir can only report
what already happened and this book gets to decide what happens next.

## Decided 2026-08-24 — the ending shape

Both calls made (`canon/SEED_STORY.md` → "Decided 2026-08-24"):

- **L-01.** The swarm spends the CME's transit window mostly on getting an unsanctioned warning
  believed and acted on — Andries as the credibility layer — with a quiet fraction building the
  bunker in parallel, as insurance, never the mission.
- **L-02. Closed arc.** Andries gives the keys back, on-page, once the grids that can be saved
  are saved. The book opens on *integrate*; it closes on *decompose* — the other word he loved
  before he knew what it meant. No sequel-teasing ambiguity.
- **L-03.** The bunker survives, but as a library, not a lever — no control access carries past
  the give-back, only the archive.
