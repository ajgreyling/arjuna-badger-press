## 0. The one thing to get right (the lethal hole is MAINSTREAM, not fringe) [FACT]
The book does NOT stand on the aliens (the decoy — killed in the clay). It stands on the **mainstream,
bulletproof** fact that the Hebrew primeval history (Flood, creation, Eden, Babel, the Law) is in
**deliberate dialogue with older Mesopotamian literature**, transmitted via the **Babylonian Exile** —
*Genesis has a genealogy.* Lean here hardest. The aliens are the thing the book reveres-then-dismantles, **in
the original language**, the only honest place. (Two dignity contracts bind every scene — SEED §5: never
sneer at scripture/the faithful; hold Sitchin's dreamers with love.)

## 1. London / the British Museum (the cold-open ground) [FACT]
- **Room 55 (Mesopotamia)** — the **Flood Tablet (K.3375)** under glass; the **Cyrus Cylinder**; the cuneiform
  from **Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh.** Bloomsbury: the great court's white light, the worn stone
  stairs, the school groups, the gift-shop replicas of the very tablet that disproves the alien doc being
  filmed beside it. *(Texture: the most-read story on Earth, in its oldest hand, behind 8mm glass, while a
  streaming crew lights it to look like a star chart.)*
- **The George Smith beat** [FACT]: the self-taught banknote-engraver who first read the flood here in 1872
  and (per Budge) "began to undress himself." Use it for the wonder-before-the-woo cold open. He died at
  Aleppo, 1876, age 36, chasing more tablets.
- **The reading room / the basement** — where the real work is: drawers of clay, the conservation bench, the
  cold fluorescent light, a scholar with a magnifier and a brush. The *un*-glossy opposite of the doc crew.

## 2. Iraqi Kurdistan (the on-the-ground heart — the safer, visitable north) [FACT]

> of Iraq. Narin gates their access hard (the firewall, lived).
- **Erbil (Hewlêr)** — one of the longest continuously-inhabited places on Earth; the **Citadel (Qelat)** on
  its tell rising over the bazaar, a UNESCO site; the **Erbil Civilization Museum**; tea houses under the
  citadel wall. Safe, modern, oil-money cranes next to six-thousand-year mudbrick. [FACT]
- **The texture of the Region** [FACT]: checkpoints with Peshmerga, the KRG vs Baghdad permit tangle, the

  seam). Heat, dust, diesel, the green of the irrigated belt against the ochre. Kurdish hospitality as a real,
  load-bearing thing (tea you cannot refuse).
- **A real dig / a regional museum** [INVENTION on a FACT base] — a working excavation or a provincial museum
  storeroom as the procedural ground: trays of sherds, a generator, a site guard, the gap between the

- ⚠ Keep southern-Iraq sites (Ur, Uruk, Eridu, Babylon, the Baghdad museum) as **referenced / longed-for /

## 3. The clay (the made things the crew actually read) [FACT]
- A **cuneiform tablet** — wedge-marks pressed in damp clay with a cut reed, then dried or baked; the heft,
  the colour, the way light rakes the wedges. Jakobus reads it as a *made thing* ("what is it for").
- A **cylinder seal** — esp. **VA 243** (Berlin), the "solar system" seal that's actually a **dedication to a
  man, Dubsiga** (DOSSIER §11): a tiny carved stone rolled across clay to leave a worshipper-before-a-god
  scene. The object that *refuses* the alien reading when you actually look.
- A **ziggurat's baked brick** — stamped, bitumen-mortared (the **Babel** tell, DOSSIER §4): the Babylonian
  building technique Genesis 11 describes precisely.

## 4. Language ground (Bashir's territory — the book's thesis is TRANSLATION) [FACT]
- **Sumerian** — a **language isolate** (related to nothing), agglutinative; the tongue the oldest texts are
  in; the **Ninti pun** ("Lady of the Rib" = "Lady who makes live") lives here and dies in translation —
  Bashir's smoking-gun scene (DOSSIER §3).
- **Akkadian** (Babylonian/Assyrian) — Semitic, cousin to Hebrew/Aramaic/Arabic; the flood-epic language; the
  *tehom*/*tiāmtu* cognate sits here (DOSSIER §2).
- **Aramaic / Syriac** — Narin's living heritage tongue (Assyrian Christian); Bashir reaching her in it
  half-opens a closed door (the *Sawubona*-with-the-mouth). A *living* descendant of the Semitic world that
  produced both the clay and the scripture.
- **Kurdish (Sorani)** and **Arabic** — the ground tongues of the modern setting; courtesy handfuls (Jakobus's
  *Sawubona*; Bashir's fluency).

## 5. Food & warmth (where the found-family lives) [FACT]
- Kurdish/Iraqi table: **tea** (istikan glasses, endless), **dolma**, **kebab**, **biryani**, flatbread off a
  tandoor, **masgouf** (split grilled river fish — the Tigris dish) where the river's near; dates, the
  shared cup (rhymes Jakobus's Nubian shared-cup beat). The warmth is real and never at anyone's expense
  (Reservation Dogs / We Are Lady Parts register, never BBC-distance).

## 6. The decipherment (the "real evidence, not woo" jewel — give it to Narin) [FACT]
- **Rawlinson** at **Behistun** (the trilingual cliff, "the Rosetta Stone of cuneiform"); then the **1857
  sealed-translation test** — four scholars (Talbot, Rawlinson, Hincks, Oppert) translate Tiglath-Pileser I's
  prism **independently, under seal**, and **agree** (RAS, 29 May 1857). A blind, reproducible test — the
  exact antithesis of Sitchin's "pick the answer, bend the glyphs." The methodological heart of the book.

## 7. FRINGE — clearly labelled (a character may over-reach; the narrator NEVER confirms) [FRINGE]
- **Sitchin's Nibiru / 12th planet / gold-mining Anunnaki / bred-from-apes** — the decoy. Killed in the clay
  (DOSSIER §8): *nibiru* = "crossing-point" = Jupiter, seen yearly, never attached to the Anunnaki; the 3,600
  "sar" is a numeral; VA 243 is a dedication. **The man held with dignity; the claim dead** (SEED §5B).
- **von Däniken** — the ancient-astronaut thesis; rejected as **colonial pseudoscience** — *and that rejection
  is mainstream* (the pillar). The ONE idea the book may be cold about (it wrote a civilisation out of its own
  story).
- **The "Baghdad Battery"** — a **real Parthian jar, false function** (scroll-vessel, no wiring) — DOSSIER §11.
- **The Nibiru doomsday cult** (Lieder/2003→2012, NASA had to debunk) — the present-day tail of the same
  hunger (DOSSIER §9).

## 10. The wink (the per-book verdict — official story intact, hole open, the fish heckles)
*The alien story is the easy one, and it's wrong, and it falls apart the instant you read the clay. The hard
story is the one nobody wants because it cuts closer: the oldest people we can read made us out of mud and a
dead god's blood to carry their work and to die — and a conquered tribe found that older draft in the enemy's
library and built it into the book half the planet swears by. So: not "were the gods aliens" (they weren't),
but **what were the Anunnaki FOR, and who taught Genesis to bake its bricks the Babylonian way?** Roll the
tape.*
