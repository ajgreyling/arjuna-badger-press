## 1. THE PLACE (real geography, honestly)

**Nevado Sajama and the Sajama Lines, Oruro Department, western Bolivia — the Altiplano, the high plain, near the Chilean border.**

- **Nevado Sajama** — Bolivia's highest peak, a dormant snow-capped stratovolcano standing alone above the plain, the visual and ceremonial anchor of the whole district. The mountain is the centre everything radiates from; you orient by it from anywhere on the plain.
- **The plain** — high, cold, dry, vast. Thin air at roughly 4,000+ metres: every exertion costs, every slope is a negotiation with your own lungs. The light is hard and clean and *long* — distances read wrong, a ridge that looks an hour off is half a day. The colour is pale ochre and bone and the silver-grey of *paja brava* (the tough Altiplano bunchgrass), with the impossible white of the cone above it and a sky that goes a blue so deep at altitude it's nearly violet at the zenith.
- **Queñua (Polylepis) woodland** — the highest-altitude forest on Earth grows on Sajama's flanks: gnarled, red-barked, low trees, surreal at this height. A real and specific texture; use it once, land it, don't caption it.
- **The settlements** — small Aymara communities, adobe and tin, woodsmoke, llama and alpaca herds, the white colonial-era chapels that the lines also link (the practice absorbed the church into itself without surrendering the older centres). Sajama village; the scatter of *estancias* across the plain.
- **The weather** — brutal diurnal swing: searing thin sun by day, hard frost by night; the *surazo* wind; dust. Nothing about this plain is comfortable, and the comfort the crew don't get is part of the honesty.

**The arithmetic the prose does on-page (fact-floor, scene-ready):**
- **~16,000 km of line**, drawn by foot — on the order of **~3× the width of the continental United States**, laid down with no wheel, no instrument, no plan anyone has ever found.
- **~22,525 km²** of patterned ground — **~15× the extent of Nazca** — **very likely the largest single archaeological artwork on Earth**, and one of the least studied.
- Lines run **~1–3 m wide**, **up to ~20 km** each, held **dead-straight over hills and ravines**.
- **Built and re-walked continuously for 3,000+ years** — **ancient when the Inca arrived**, **still walked in Aymara ritual today.** (Contrast Nazca, which fell silent; **Sajama never did.**)

## 2. THE SITE MAP (how the lines actually sit on the ground)

Not a single monument but a **living sacred landscape** — a radial web, not a picture:

- **The nodes.** Lines radiate from and between sacred points: ***wak'as*** (shrines, sacred places — a rock, a spring, a place where something happened), ***chullpas*** (pre-Columbian funerary towers), villages, and the post-contact **chapels**. The mountain presides over all of it.
- **The lines themselves.** Made **subtractively, *as trails*** — walkers scrape away the darker, oxidised surface stones and soil to expose the lighter subsoil beneath, and *keep walking*. **The line is maintained by the walking.** The maintenance mechanism *is the use*: stop walking and it greens over. Its 3,000-year survival is itself an unbroken record of practice.
- **The geometry that matters (the hole, on the ground).** A line **~20 km long and ~1 m wide that will not bend for a ridge — it climbs the hill rather than skirt it.** A line that refuses to curve is not taking the easy path; it is holding a *bearing*, for a reason. The straightness encodes **ritual intent over topographic convenience** — and it was held with **no central authority, no instrument, no elevated vantage** to sight or correct from. **This is the checkable residue.**
- **The map that lies.** From a satellite tile or a drone the web is legible as pattern — and *that legibility is the trap.* The makers never had the overhead view. The thing the air shows you is the thing the feet don't need and never used. **The map looks like progress and explains nothing.**

## 3. THE TEXTURE OF THE WONDER (where it lives, and how to render it)

The wonder is **not** "how could primitive people" (the colonial poison the house refuses) and **not** "aliens" (the woo Sajama's obscurity spared it). The wonder is the *gap*, and three concrete textures carry it:

1. **The cold scale.** Stand on one line and grasp that it is one of *thousands*, that together they out-measure Nazca fifteen times over and the continental US three times across, drawn by foot, and that almost no one has heard of it. The biggest thing humans ever drew is the thing the world hasn't looked at. Render the vertigo of that arithmetic, once.
2. **The held bearing.** Walk to where a line meets a ridge and **does not bend.** Feel in the body what it costs to keep a bearing arrow-straight up and over broken ground with nothing to sight by — and that this was done for *up to twenty kilometres*, and held for *three thousand years*. **Naila proves it cold; the body feels it on the climb.**
3. **The saying.** The hinge the TONGUE hears, that no map could show: **the lines are sentences you walk, not maps you read.** The naming, the order, the saying-while-going — *that* is what holds the bearing. The straightness is the **fossil of the saying.** The largest drawing on Earth is legible only to the body that makes it — and that is a *larger* wonder than any aerial mystery, not a smaller one.

**Render discipline:** land each image once; vary cadence; never caption the meaning. The wonder *grows* across the walk — air → ground → the line itself — and it ends **larger and hers.**

## 4. THE CHASE GEOGRAPHY (the propulsion, on this terrain)

The chase runs *along the line* and *up the country*, paced by altitude and bureaucracy, not car chases:

- **The overhead front (Movement I).** Maps, satellite tiles, drone passes, a field office, a survey camp — the outsider's god's-eye war. The adversary works *here first*, because the *telling* is what's worth money, and the telling is authored from above. This is where the crew nearly lose by *winning* — the map looks like progress.
- **The trailhead and the village (Movement II).** The plain, the settlements, the chapel, the *cancha* (the community ground). Here the TONGUE half-opens a closed door, and the chase becomes a question of *whose authorship*: the adversary's dossier and concession vs. the community's own claim on its own ground. Bureaucracy is the terrain — permits, a UNESCO-style nomination someone else is drafting, a "managed viewing platform" proposal, a consent form.
- **The line itself, up the ridge (Movement III).** On foot, on the line, climbing the hill it refuses to skirt, at altitude, in the cold long light. The thin air *is* an antagonist; the weather can close. The climax happens here — not at a gunpoint but at a **decision about authorship and consent**, and the local's hand on the answer.
- **The constraints (real, binding):** altitude (everything costs breath; the Beast weeps water at altitude — Jakobus's recurring beat); cold and the diurnal swing; distance that reads wrong in the clean light; the closed border nearby; permits and the slow grind of who is allowed to do what on whose ground; the practice's own rhythm (you walk when it is walked, not when the schedule wants). **No omnipotent adversary** — a well-funded, fallible outfit moving faster than a community's paperwork, which is exactly how heritage gets lifted in the real world.

## 5. THE LIVING PEOPLE NEAR THE PLACE (Bible §3.3 — the whole point)

**This is not scenery for a Westerner's epiphany.** It is a real place with real living people who **own the practice**, and the wonder must serve *them*.

- **The Aymara communities of the Sajama district** — herders of llama and alpaca, growers of what the altitude allows, keepers of the chapels and the older centres alike. They are **not** a vanished people whose riddle is left for outsiders; they are **here, now, walking the lines.** They have watched the world ignore the biggest thing on Earth that sits in their backyard, and they have their own, entirely sufficient relationship to it that owes the outside nothing.
- **The *yatiri*** — the ritual specialist, the line-walker, the **local who out-reads the whole crew** on the thing that matters (CHARACTERS §3). She is **not a guide hired to explain her sacred ground to tourists.** She decides what the crew are ready to be told and what they are not. The "what is it for" is **hers to give or withhold.** The win is hers.
- **The community's stake in the chase.** What the adversary threatens is not stone — it is the **practice** and the **telling**: the right to author what the lines *are*, to keep walking them on their own terms, to refuse a platform that would turn a pilgrimage into a spectacle and end the very walking that keeps the lines alive. The crew's role is to help the community **hold the telling on their own terms** — and then get out of the frame.
- **Dignity contract (the FLOOR — Bible §9):** Aymara consultants have a real veto on the people, the practice, the *yatiri*, the language, and what is sacred. The back matter sends curiosity and attention (and the HBT-tourism echo, money) **toward the communities and the practice, on their terms** — pointing the reader at the *real* Tony Morrison record, the *real* Tierra Sajama survey, the *real* scale: go and check; the record is open — **but the meaning stays with the people who walk it.**

## 6. ALTIPLANO RHYME (a deliberate echo, not a repeat — slate)

This book shares the **Bolivian high plain** with the Puma Punku / Tiwanaku book (Book 8): Jakobus far from home on the Altiplano, the **weeping water at altitude**, the *Sawubona* (his courtesy handful of Aymara) in the thin air. Play it as a *rhyme* a binge-reader feels, never a repeated scene. And lay this book *against Nazca* (Book 9) for the reader who reads both: at Nazca the overhead survey **answered** it; here the overhead view is precisely the thing that **keeps you from understanding.** The inversion is the gift.
