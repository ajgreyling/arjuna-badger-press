Chase Skeleton (Act I–III, Why Files Move):

**ACT I: ARRIVAL—The Air**
- ARRIVE: The crew lands in La Paz, travels to Sajama. The adversary’s mapping team is already at work—drones, surveyors, press releases about “opening” the lines.
- OBSTACLE: The crew’s drone surveys and satellite maps show the scale and straightness—but from above, the meaning is invisible. The “god’s-eye” view is a dead end.
- READ: Naila measures the lines on the ground; the team attempts to walk a straight line (the “potato” moment)—they cannot match the ancient precision.
- COST: The mapping team’s presence is beginning to unsettle the local walkers; rumors of “official” explanations and new tourist trails threaten the practice.
- KEY: The TONGUE, through language, gains a cautious invitation from the local walker/yatiri to observe, not interpret.

**ACT II: ON THE GROUND—The Walkers**
- ARRIVE: The crew is brought to a trailhead on the word of the local. The mapping team is denied access—language and trust matter more than credentials.
- OBSTACLE: The crew is permitted only to walk and listen; they cannot record or measure everything. The TONGUE’s discipline is tested—some things cannot be translated.
- READ: The crew walks a segment with the local and other pilgrims. The straightness is felt, not seen; the line is “held” by the words spoken as they walk.
- COST: The LENS is asked not to photograph a ritual moment. Naila’s measuring is gently rebuffed—the straightness is not a technical achievement but a side-effect of the practice.
- KEY: The local explains (in her own time) that the lines are sentences, not roads; the walking and the words are inseparable.

**ACT III: WALKING THE LINE—The Handoff**
- ARRIVE: The crew is invited to participate in a dawn walk—one line, over a ridge, refusing to bend.
- OBSTACLE: The adversary’s team tries to “map” the ritual, but their instruments are useless without the words and the walking.
- READ: The crew experiences the full ritual, but the local withholds the final words; the meaning is felt, not translated.
- COST: The mapping team’s project is paused—the locals assert their ownership, and the crew supports them, declining to “solve” the lines for outsiders.
- KEY: Jakobus, deferring, asks “what is it for?” The local answers only: “It is for walking.” The practice continues.

Climax (Crew Arc + Chase Resolve + Local’s ‘What is it for’):
- The crew accepts that the answer is not theirs to have. The mapping team is out-read and must step back. The local’s agency is affirmed; the lines remain alive, the practice unbroken, the wonder open.

Subplots:
- The TONGUE’s evolving discipline—learning the limits of translation, the ethics of what is not hers to carry.
- Naila’s attempt to model the straightness, and her coming to peace with an un-solvable pattern.
- The LENS’s struggle with when to record and when to witness.
