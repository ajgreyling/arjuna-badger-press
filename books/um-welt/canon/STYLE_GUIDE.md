# STYLE_GUIDE — *Um Welt*

> A modern English-language retelling of Heinz Stücke's fifty-year bicycle circumnavigation.
> Literary non-fiction with novelistic scene-craft: the facts in SOURCE_NOTES.md are load-bearing
> and immovable; everything else — weather, breath, the exact words a border guard used — is built
> to carry them.

## Register

**Close third, present-tense-adjacent past** — the read of someone remembering a fifty-year
journey in long unbroken strides, not a Wikipedia entry with adjectives added. Sentences have the
patience of a man who has nowhere he has to be and fifty years to get there. Let scenes breathe;
this is not a thriller pace, it's a walking pace on two wheels.

- **Embody, don't state** (house law). Never write "he felt free." Write the specific mechanical
  fact that free feels like: the freewheel's tick on a downhill with no one behind you for eleven
  days.
- **The bicycle is a character**, not a prop — the same steel frame for fifty years is the book's
  central fidelity, and its wear (repainted, rewelded, re-spoked, stolen, recovered) should track
  Stücke's own aging in parallel, unstated.
- **Money is a running texture, not an exposition dump.** Selling postcards from the bike, the
  weight of the photo satchel, the arithmetic of a licensing fee — thread it through scenes, don't
  explain the funding model in a paragraph.
- **No romanticism about poverty or danger.** The beatings, the toe, the mob, the frozen river are
  rendered physically and plainly — consequence, not colour. Do not let the prose enjoy his
  suffering for the reader.
- **Comedy is dry and rare** — a man who has crossed 195 countries alone develops a specific,
  undersold humor about bureaucracy, border stamps, and the universal question "why." Let it show
  in three or four scenes, not as constant narration.

## Structural spine

Not chronological-exhaustive (no source supports a full country log) — instead **braided decades**:
chapters organized around documented anchor incidents (Indonesia 1974, Zambia 1980, the Seychelles
1996, Siberia 1997, Portsmouth 2006, the return, the final year), each chapter free to range
backward and forward in memory the way a long-haul traveller's mind actually works, but never
inventing a place or date SOURCE_NOTES doesn't support.

## The seam law (binding)

Where a scene needs a name, a face, a line of dialogue no source records, invent it — but the
**shape of the event** (what happened, where, roughly when) must match SOURCE_NOTES exactly. Never
let an invented scene contradict a documented fact to make a better line.

## Dedication (binding — front matter, before Chapter 1)

**"For Heinz Stücke."** A single unsentimental line, no subtitle, no elaboration — the plainest
possible dedication, in keeping with a book that refuses to romanticize its subject. This is a
dedication to the man himself, written into a still-open wound five days after his death; let its
plainness carry the weight rather than any additional phrase. Do not add dates or a eulogy line to
the dedication page itself — that belongs in the Author's Note.

## Ending

The book ends at his death — 22 July 2026, Hövelhof, peacefully, among friends, neighbours, and
carers — rendered in the same physical, unsentimental register as the rest of the book, not
elevated into eulogy. The Author's Note follows immediately after and is where the book is allowed,
once, to speak in the writer's own voice about writing this five days after the man died.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- No "he never truly stopped moving, even in death" reframe-tells (LLM_TELLS house rule).
- No invented dialogue that states the book's theme aloud.
- No treating a 195-country, 600,000 km life as a checklist — resist the urge to name-drop
  countries not in SOURCE_NOTES for scope-effect.
