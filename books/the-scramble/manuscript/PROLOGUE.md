# Prologue

The dog two doors down had a theory it was being robbed, and it had held this theory for forty minutes. Vance lay on the couch with his shoes on and let the barking come and go like weather. Some nights you took your shoes off. Some nights you knew, the way you knew a storm was coming or a phone was about to ring, that the second you got comfortable somebody would need driving somewhere, and you kept the shoes on as insurance against your own optimism.

In the kitchen Hutch was making eggs. You could hear it — the soft authoritative knock of shell on the lip of the bowl, again, again, a fourth time, a fifth.

"How many eggs," Vance said, not loudly.

"Enough."

"That's a lot of enough."

"Big day tomorrow." A pause, the fork going. "I don't know what it is yet. But I'm building toward it."

The smell reached the couch a minute later, butter and pepper and the particular brown edge Hutch got that nobody else in the house could, and Vance closed his eyes. The dog gave up its case. Somewhere a refrigerator clicked into its second thoughts. The lamp threw a warm coin of light on the ceiling, and the house ticked and settled around the four of them like something cooling that had run hot all day.

This, Vance thought, with no particular alarm. This was the part. If they ever let you keep one hour, file it away, take it out later when the rest had gone bad — this was the one he'd put in. A guy at a stove. The dog wrong about everything. Nobody needing anything yet. He wasn't examining it. That was the whole trick of it, that you couldn't examine it and keep it; you could only lie there in your shoes and let it be true.

"You want eggs," Hutch said. It was not a question. A plate was already coming.
