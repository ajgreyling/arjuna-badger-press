# CHARACTERS — *The Scramble*

> Original cast. Each rhymes with a *function* in Scanner's engine; none copies a person, a scene, or a
> line. Voice laws follow ANTI_PATTERNS §7/§25: differentiate by **cognitive habit and failure mode**,
> not vocabulary. No two equally quotable.

## Vance Roan — protagonist (LIVED) / Subject M (WATCHED)

- **Function:** the man who surveils his own undercover identity. The split self.
- **Who he is:** mid-thirties, ex-paramedic before the unit, which is why he's good with bodies and bad
  with his own. Joined narcotics because he wanted to be useful and the pay was steadier than the
  ambulance. Lives in the house as **"V"** to the others. On the record he is **Subject M**.
- **Mind:** competent, dry, kind in small mechanical ways (he fixes things, he drives people places).
  *Failure mode (his one costly mistake, Craft Doctrine L-06):* he confidently misreads a person he
  loves as safe, and the misreading is the leash that closes on him — and the narrator does **not**
  excuse it.
- **Voice (LIVED):** warm, present, sensory, funny under his breath; gets quieter as he degrades.
- **Voice (WATCHED):** he does not have one — Subject M is rendered in the flat report register, which
  is the point. As Tilt splits him, the LIVED voice starts borrowing the WATCHED register's coldness
  mid-scene, and the WATCHED record starts catching things only the LIVED man could know. The seam
  between them is the book's whole horror, and the prose never names it.
- **Arc:** useful man → watched man → erased man → the one the coda witnesses.

## Dora Vale — the dealer he loves / his unknowing handler

- **Function:** Donna's slot in the engine. **Not Donna.** Her own life, her own reasons.
- **Who she is:** sells Tilt mid-tier; refuses to use it; allergic to being touched without say-so;
  funny in a flat, deflecting way. Vance loves her the way you love a locked door you keep walking up
  to. She is also — this is hers, revealed late, grounded — closer to the institutions than he knows,
  and the love is real *and* the use is real, and the book refuses to resolve which mattered more
  (Craft Doctrine L-08: hope/uncertainty, not certainty).
- **Failure mode:** she reads danger fast and intimacy never; warns him correctly and obliquely, and he
  mishears it (ANTI_PATTERNS §14, partial-warning).
- **Voice:** clipped, deflecting, lands one true thing per scene and immediately undercuts it.

## Renko — housemate

- **Function:** Barris's slot — the charismatic, paranoid, dangerously competent one. **Not Barris.**
- **Who he is:** rigs things — security, cars, theories. Brilliant and wrong in a confident architecture
  no one can follow fast enough to stop. The comedy engine of the house; later, the knife.
- **Failure mode:** builds an elaborate correct-sounding case out of one true fact and four invented
  ones, and acts on it.
- **Voice:** torrential, technical, self-certain, syntactically *clean* (which makes him scary) — the
  one character who speaks in finished prose, and that is characterization, not a tell.

## Hutch — housemate

- **Function:** Luckman's slot — the big, easy, sweet one. **Not Luckman.**
- **Who he is:** the heart of the house; the one everyone's nicer around; the one whose collapse is the
  hinge where the comedy stops being funny.
- **Voice:** plain, generous, short sentences, the room's baseline — so when his syntax breaks, the
  reader feels the floor go.

## "Dent" (Marcus Denton) — the small casualty

- **Function:** Freck's slot — the minor user whose private apocalypse is the book's first real wound.
- **Who he is:** peripheral, funny, doomed; the one the others stop mentioning. His exit is the book's
  L-09 (the one imperfect goodbye in a sequence of farewells — frightened, undignified, no one left to
  make it mean something).

## "Stark" — Vance's handler

- **Function:** the supervisor who hands down the order to surveil Subject M.
- **Who he is:** not cruel, not stupid — administratively surgical, a man who triages because the unit
  is broke. *Embarrassingly-mortal want (L-07):* he is terrified of being no one without the badge; he
  over-manages because the alternative is silence. Says one thing slightly wrong, the cadence of a man
  who didn't mean to.
- **Voice:** flat, procedural; a bureaucrat's menace, which is scarier than a shouter's.

## Northway intake counselor — late-book

- **Function:** competent custody made human. The institution's face.
- **Voice:** clean, warm, scripted-then-not; the kindness is real, which is the obscene part.
