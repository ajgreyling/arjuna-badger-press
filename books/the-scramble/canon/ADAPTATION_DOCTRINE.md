# Adaptation Doctrine — *The Scramble*

> How this book is faithful to *A Scanner Darkly* without being a copy of it. The governing rule for
> every canon and manuscript decision in this project.

## The line we hold

PKD's novel is under copyright. We are not licensed. Therefore the fidelity is to **craft and
engine**, and the text is **ours**. Two failures to avoid, equally:

1. **The legal/ethical failure** — reproducing his plot, scenes, characters, or sentences; or doing the
   same thing with names swapped and details nudged. A "find-and-replace adaptation" is a derivative
   work we have no right to make, and the house has never made one.
2. **The craft failure** — which is the *same* failure wearing a different coat. Beat-for-beat copying
   with substitutions produces airless, second-hand prose. The Craft Doctrine's deepest finding (L-04:
   *over-control*) applies doubly to adaptation: a borrowed skeleton with our flesh stretched over it
   reads as taxidermy. The book has to be **alive on its own ground**.

## What we keep (the engine — these are ideas, not expressions, and they are PKD's gift to the form)

- **The split self made structural.** A man surveils his own undercover identity and does not know it.
  The watcher and the watched are one degrading mind.
- **Dissociation as both plot and prose.** The drug's cost is not death-by-overdose; it is the quiet
  severing of the self that watches from the self that lives.
- **Comedy that curdles.** The house scenes are genuinely funny — paranoid, digressive, warm — and that
  warmth is the trap. The grief is earned because we laughed first.
- **The cold coda.** The book ends by collapsing into documentary flatness and turning the whole thing
  into an indictment — and into a love letter to the lost. *Smaller than the idea* (ANTI_PATTERNS §20).
- **The unreliable two-column reader contract.** The reader holds the truth the protagonist has lost,
  and the prose never captions it.

## What we invent (everything that is *expression*)

- **Every name, character, relationship, and backstory.** Vance Roan is not Bob Arctor. Dora is not
  Donna. Renko and Hutch are not Barris and Luckman. Northway is not New-Path. They share a *function*
  in the engine; they do not share a life, a scene, or a line.
- **Every scene and beat.** The PLOT rhymes with Scanner's descent (false security → fracture →
  betrayal → erasure → the witnessing coda) the way any tragedy rhymes with any other. The actual
  events — the stakeout that goes wrong, the cipher-clear stupidity of the house arguments, the case
  that turns out to be a leash — are ours, set in places we built.
- **The drug, the tech, the institution.** **Tilt**, the **shroud rig**, the **Northway** campus are
  original mechanisms with their own grounded rules (see MYTHOS). They are not Substance D, the scramble
  suit, or New-Path with a coat of paint.
- **The substrate.** Near-now, realist, low-spectacle: real undercover narcotics work, a real-feeling
  stim epidemic, gig precarity. PKD-faithful in *grime and ordinariness*, not in gadgetry.

## The test, applied to every page

Before any scene ships, ask: *if PKD's novel did not exist, would this scene still be a real scene —
its own people, its own logic, its own surprise?* If it only makes sense as "the part where the
Scanner thing happens," it is taxidermy. Cut it and build the real one.

## Provenance, disclosed

Per the house thesis (*immersion is the craft obligation; provenance is disclosed, never hidden*): the
foreword names the debt to PKD plainly. We do not pretend the engine is ours. We pretend nothing.
