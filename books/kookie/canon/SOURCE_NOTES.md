# SOURCE_NOTES: *Kookie* (working title)

> **Subjects:** John McCown (d. 14 July 2011, complications from heart surgery) and his dog
> Kookie, a crossbreed who rode the gas tank of McCown's motorcycle through more than 300 desert
> races across the American Southwest in the 1960s–70s. Real people, real dog, real races. McCown
> raced for roughly six years, averaging near fifty races a year, including the Barstow-to-Vegas
> race. Also a butcher by trade and author of children's books.

**Note on the name:** AJ's prompt says "John McGown" — sources consistently spell it **McCown**
(and one forum thread spells it "McCowen"). Using **McCown** as the correct spelling throughout,
per Cycle News (the industry trade obituary, the most authoritative single source).

## Verified facts (binding)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Racing era | 1960s–1970s, Southwest US deserts (California) |
| Race count | Over 300 races together; ~50 races/year across roughly six years |
| Signature race | Barstow-to-Vegas |
| How Kookie rode | Perched on a small mat fixed to the motorcycle's gas tank |
| Kookie's "skill" | Learned to read terrain — stood up when ground went soft, backed away from approaching bumps; McCown said Kookie sometimes sensed a change before he did |
| Kookie's breed | Documented only as "crossbreed" — no further detail in any source |
| Cultural footprint | Appeared in the cult desert-racing documentary *On Any Sunday*; covered in specialist motorcycle press; subject of posters |
| Children's book | *Kookie the Motorcycle Racing Dog*, by Red Hallum & John McCown, illustrated by Leslie Lovett, Educational Activities Inc., 32 pages — a real, existing book, not our invention |
| McCown's day job | Butcher |
| 1981 quote (American Motorcyclist) | "I'll race for as long as there's a desert out there and the BLM will stay off our back and we can get out there and do it." |
| Posthumous honor | Inducted, with Kookie, into the Hot Shoe Hall of Fame, 2021 |
| Death | 14 July 2011, complications from heart surgery |

## Craft note
This is the warmest and least-tragic of the three books — a working partnership between a man and
a dog told straight, without over-sentimentalizing a story that's already disarming on the facts
alone. Resist making Kookie "speak" or narrate; he is rendered entirely through observed behavior
(the house's own embody-don't-state law applies doubly to an animal narrator problem — see
STYLE_GUIDE.md).
