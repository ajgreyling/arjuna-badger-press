# STYLE_GUIDE — *Kookie*

> A modern retelling of John McCown and his desert-racing dog Kookie — real riders, real dog, real
> races, 1960s–70s American Southwest. Warmest of the three books; keep it that way without going
> soft-headed.

## Register

Close third on McCown, plain American vernacular, sun-bleached and unhurried — think oral history
more than prose poem. Short paragraphs. The desert itself (heat, silence, the particular quality of
dust at speed) is a recurring sensory anchor, not scenery.

- **Kookie is never a narrator and never speaks.** He is rendered strictly through observed
  behavior — ears, stance, weight shift, the specific mat on the tank. The reader arrives at
  "this dog understands the desert" by watching him do it, never by being told.
- **No sentimentality inflation.** The facts (300+ races, a terrain-sensing dog, a butcher who
  wrote children's books, a Hall of Fame induction a decade after his death) are disarming enough
  undressed. Do not add a rescue-dog backstory, a dying-owner deathbed scene with Kookie present,
  or any other unsourced tearjerker beat. If it's not in SOURCE_NOTES, treat it as the single
  invented origin scene at most (flagged), not a pattern.
- **Comedy lives here more than in the other two books** — desert racing culture of the era (CB
  radios, garage bravado, BLM permit arguments, the 1981 quote) is naturally funny and should be
  let breathe.
- **The mat on the tank is the book's central image** — return to it physically (worn foam, the
  smell, how it's re-strapped each race morning) the way *Um Welt*'s bicycle frame is a returning
  image.

## Dedication (binding — front matter, before Chapter 1)

**Dedicated to AJ's own dogs, living and dead, and to dogs of the world.** See DEDICATION.md in
this same canon folder for the full source material (his dogs Awesome and Nienja by name, the
household detail, and the father's-funeral story) — read it before drafting the dedication page
and the Author's Note. The dedication page itself stays short and plain (e.g. "For Awesome and
Nienja, and every dog before and since — and for the dogs of the world"); the fuller, specific
material (the biltong line-up, Nienja going blind and becoming "a pork banger," the funeral
contrast) belongs in the Author's Note, told with total plainness and no added commentary
explaining what it means — the facts alone carry it. This personal material is separate from the
McCown/Kookie narrative itself; do not blend AJ's own dogs into the McCown chapters.

## Structural spine

Open on the one undocumented scene — how the pairing began — clearly imagined, then move into the
verified racing years anchored on Barstow-to-Vegas as the recurring fixture, McCown's butcher-shop
life as counterweight, the *On Any Sunday* cameo as the hinge into fame, and close on the 2021 Hot
Shoe Hall of Fame induction — ten years after McCown's death — as the book's actual ending beat
(a real, dateable, bittersweet fact: the honor arrived after he couldn't receive it).

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- No dog interiority ("Kookie knew this would be his last race").
- No invented rival characters given false specificity (no invented names presented as real racers).
- Don't let the children's-book-within-the-book become twee — treat *Kookie the Motorcycle Racing
  Dog* (the real 1970s book) as a documented artifact, referenced, not pastiched at length.
