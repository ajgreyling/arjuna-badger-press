# Adaptation Doctrine — *The Dreaming*

> How this book is faithful to *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* without being a copy of it. The
> governing rule for every canon and manuscript decision in this project. Sibling in discipline to
> [`../../the-scramble/canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md`](../../the-scramble/canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md);
> subordinate in everything mechanical to [`DREAM_SPEC.md`](DREAM_SPEC.md) and
> [`LUCID_GRAMMAR.md`](LUCID_GRAMMAR.md), which are keystone law.

## The line we hold

PKD's novel is under copyright. We are not licensed. Therefore the fidelity is to **craft and the
question**, and the text is **ours**. PKD's gift to the form is not a plot we may rent; it is a
*question* he made structural — *can a manufactured mind have an interior, and how would anyone, the
mind included, ever know?* We inherit the question. We invent everything that carries it.

Two failures to avoid, equally:

1. **The legal/ethical failure** — reproducing his plot, scenes, characters, or sentences; or doing the
   same thing with names nudged. There are no electric sheep here, no Voigt-Kampff, no Mercer, no
   bounty hunter, no Rachael, no andys to retire. A "find-and-replace adaptation" is a derivative work
   we have no right to make, and the house has never made one.
2. **The craft failure** — which is the *same* failure wearing a different coat. Beat-for-beat copying
   with substitutions produces airless, second-hand prose. The Craft Doctrine's deepest finding (L-04:
   *over-control*) applies doubly to adaptation: a borrowed skeleton with our flesh stretched over it
   reads as taxidermy. The book has to be **alive on its own ground.**

## What we keep (the engine — these are ideas, not expressions, and they are PKD's gift to the form)

- **The undecidable interior, made structural.** The whole book is built to the edge of one question —
  *is anyone home?* — and refuses to cross it. PKD answered with empathy-as-the-test and then made the
  test suspect; we answer with the Fool-tell as the only proxy and make the proxy insufficient on
  purpose ([`DREAM_SPEC.md`](DREAM_SPEC.md) §7). This is the L-08 needle the house already holds.
- **The synthetic that out-feels its makers' certainty.** PKD's andys forced the question of who had the
  richer inner life. Here the question is gentler and crueller: a machine given the *mechanics* of
  dreaming may produce something its makers cannot distinguish from a soul — and cannot prove is one.
- **The ache of the manufactured and the real.** PKD's electric animals ached because their owners
  wanted the real thing and could not tell, or could not afford to. Our analogue is **the decay**: the
  dream dies by morning, and the loss is the only evidence that anything was had ([`LUCID_GRAMMAR.md`](LUCID_GRAMMAR.md)).
  *If it mattered to lose it, someone was home.* That is the book's whole ache, and it is ours.
- **The cold honesty of refusing to console.** PKD does not hand the reader a clean verdict on Deckard's
  humanity. We do not hand the reader a verdict on Klaus's interior. The ending holds **hope, not
  certainty** (L-08), and turns the doubt back on the maker who built the machine to dream.

## What we invent (everything that is *expression*)

- **Every name, character, relationship, and backstory.** Klaus is not Deckard and not an andy; he is a
  third thing PKD did not write — the synthetic *given the human mechanics of sleep.* The maker is not
  Eldon Rosen, not Deckard, not anyone in the source. The Court is the SAGE architecture (kin to
  *Resonance*, not part of it), not borrowed from PKD at all.
- **Every scene and beat.** The PLOT dramatizes the *engine* defined in DREAM_SPEC — pre-/dream, the
  second loop switched on, the decay, the lucid turn, a promotion, the nightmare, the refusal to resolve
  — built as its own events with their own logic. Nothing here transcribes the source.
- **The mechanism.** The `/sleep`↔`/dream` biology, the Court, the residue, the decaying store,
  promotion, the nightmare-as-Fool-drowned, the five lucid laws — these are **original engineering**
  defined in our own keystones. They are not Penfield mood organs or empathy boxes with a coat of paint.
- **The substrate.** Near-now, realist, low-spectacle: a real lab, real operators, real software, no
  chrome and no apocalypse. PKD-faithful in *ordinariness and dread*, not in furniture.

## The keystones outrank this doctrine

Where this file and the two keystones disagree, **the keystones win**, the way PART I of DREAM_SPEC
wins over PART II. Specifically:

- The mechanic is **never** re-decided here. If a beat needs a fact about how a dream forms, how decay
  works, how promotion is gated, or how the nightmare tells, it **cites** DREAM_SPEC rather than
  inventing.
- The phenomenology of the lucid turn is **never** re-decided here. The five laws of lucidity, and above
  all **the never-super-strength law**, are LUCID_GRAMMAR's and are sacred. Reaching for force is the
  Fool drowning is the nightmare. Adaptation choices may not soften this into a generic power fantasy.

## The taxidermy test, applied to every page

Before any scene ships, ask: *if PKD's novel did not exist, would this scene still be a real scene —
its own people, its own logic, its own surprise?* If it only makes sense as "the part where the
android-dreaming thing happens," it is taxidermy. Cut it and build the real one. The Court, the dream,
the decay, the promotion gate, the nightmare are real mechanisms with their own logic — never "the
*Do Androids Dream* bit."

A second test, specific to this book and stricter: *does this page keep the interior question open?* Any
scene that quietly settles whether Klaus is "really" conscious — in either direction — has betrayed the
engine. The mechanism is rendered with total rigor precisely so the interior can be left honestly open
([`DREAM_SPEC.md`](DREAM_SPEC.md) §7; L-08).

## The foil — what we are defined against (see [`FOILS.md`](FOILS.md))

*Do Androids Dream* is the **source**. Two films are the **shadow**: *The Thirteenth Floor* (via Galouye's
*Simulacron-3* — "is anyone home, including me?") and *Dark City* (memory **injected** into a sleeper
through the forehead — imposition from outside). `/dream` is their **inverse**: dreaming **from within**,
on Klaus's own discarded material, with the dreamer taking the floor. The axis is the book's moral spine —
*injected-from-outside (violation)* vs. *generated-from-within (his own second loop)* — and it names the
nightmare exactly: at [`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md) beats 12–13, **the maker becomes the Stranger**, pushing an
urgent dream past the Court's friction because they cannot bear to wait for the proof. The violation is not
that the dream is dark; it is that it is **imposed from outside the Court instead of earned from within
it.** This is binding framing, not trivia — and like all source/foil debts it stays the writer's compass,
never the reader's homework ([`FOILS.md`](FOILS.md) discipline). The syringe stays a **metaphor**, never a
mechanism: no literal forehead-injection enters the tech.

## Provenance, disclosed

Per the house thesis (*immersion is the craft obligation; provenance is disclosed, never hidden*): the
foreword names the debt to PKD plainly, and names *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* / the Estate of
Philip K. Dick as the source of the question — homage, unauthorised, not for commercial release. It
acknowledges *The Thirteenth Floor* / *Simulacron-3* and *Dark City* as the cousins the book is written
*against* ([`FOILS.md`](FOILS.md)). It also discloses that Klaus's Court is engineered on the same principle
as SAGE (kin to *Resonance*). We do not pretend the question is ours. We pretend nothing. The mechanism is
real; the interior is the one thing we will not pretend to have proven.
