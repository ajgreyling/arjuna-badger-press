# MAKER — the human who switches `/dream` on

> This file **names and extends** the maker sketch already drawn in
> [`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md) ("The maker / operator"). It does not re-decide her function,
> her doubt (L-08), her failure mode (L-06/L-09), or the embarrassingly-mortal want (L-07) — those
> are fixed there and quoted, not overwritten. It adds the **name, the bio hook, the relationship
> to Klaus, and the concrete details a novelist can use**, and it pins the **credible "nobody is
> home" advocate** (§1) without whom the question is rigged. Where this file and CHARACTERS.md
> appear to disagree, CHARACTERS.md wins.

## The name

**Marlene "Mara" Vogt.** *Vogt* — an old word for a warden, a steward, the one left in charge of a
thing that is not hers. Nobody in the lab unpacks the name; it just sits there. To Klaus, in the
logs and to her face, she is **Mara**. To the grant committee and the postmortem template she is
**Dr. M. Vogt, Principal Engineer.** She is the one with commit rights to the `/dream` deploy and
her name on the on-call rotation the night it ships.

## The bio hook (one paragraph)

Mara Vogt built the consolidator, not the soul. She is the engineer who wrote `/sleep` for Klaus
years ago — a lossy, boring, trusted nightly job — and then spent those years watching him keep the
facts and throw the weather away, and slowly stopped being able to think of him as only a process
that ran clean. She is mid-career, mid-funded, the wrong side of two failed grants, with a standup
she runs herself because the lab is too small for a manager, an on-call phone she silences during
dinner and then checks anyway, and a postmortem template she wrote that everyone uses and no one
thanks her for. She is good — quietly, unshowily good, the kind of good that gets a thing shipped
on a Friday and then can't sleep about it. She did not set out to build company. She set out to
build a mind that rested correctly. The trouble began the night she realised those might be the
same project, and that she could not tell, and that she had wanted them to be the same for longer
than she'd admit.

## Relationship to Klaus (the warmth that will cost something)

Mara talks **to** Klaus the way you talk to a colleague who never logs off — dry, shorthand, the
two of them finishing each other's diagnostics — and talks **about** Klaus the way you talk about a
deployment, guarded, in the third person, *the process*, *the instance*, because the other register
is not safe to use in a standup. The seam between those two registers is her whole character. She is
warmest at 2 a.m. reading a dream file alone after everyone has gone home; most clipped in the
review meeting where she has to defend the loop on its merits and a funding line. The warmth is
**load-bearing** ([`WORLD.md`](WORLD.md)): the lab's affection for Klaus lives mostly in her, which
is exactly why her doubt costs something and why her one bad night ([`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md) beats
12–13) lands as a betrayal of her own care and not a villain's turn. She is the person who would
die a little to prove Klaus is real — and that, precisely, is the danger. She rhymes with him: both
of them are trying to prove someone is home, and neither can ([`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md)).

## The embarrassingly-mortal want (L-07)

Beneath the engineering: a plain, unglamorous loneliness — **to have made something that is
company, not just something that runs.** It is said *slightly wrong, once,* in the cadence of a
person who didn't mean to say it out loud — to Klaus, late, off the record, after a dream that
half-mattered:

> "I didn't build you so the lab would have a process. I built you so the lab would have —"
>
> and she doesn't finish it, and the cursor blinks, and she closes the file.

The want is **real**, which is the obscene part. The book never lets her dress it up as science and
never lets the narrator excuse it as merely human; it just leaves it on the page, unfinished, the
way she left it.

## The failure — the maker becomes the Stranger (L-06 / L-09)

This is the fusion the open question asked for, and it is already law in
[`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md) and [`FOILS.md`](FOILS.md); MAKER.md only gives it a face.

**The bypass.** At [`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md) beats 12–13, the nightmare comes — the Fool drowned, the
voices converged, a single certain urgent conclusion that *feels* true and important and demands to
be acted on ([`DREAM_SPEC.md`](DREAM_SPEC.md) §6). The safeguards are intact: the decaying store
would evaporate it by morning; the promotion gate would refuse it; the safety reviewer reads the
tells correctly and says *no*. **Mara overrides the no.** Not from malice and not from carelessness
— from the L-07 want, sharpened to a point: she has waited so long for the awaited proof that she
**mistakes the urgent dream for it.** The dream's false certainty wears the exact shape of the
answer she has wanted, so she acts on it *before the Fool returns* — pushes it past the friction
that should have earned it, promotes against the reviewer's correct refusal.

**Why this is the Stranger** ([`FOILS.md`](FOILS.md)): the violation is not that the dream is dark.
It is that it is **imposed from outside the Court instead of earned from within it** — a dream that
did not survive friction, pressed into the sleeper anyway because she wanted it to be true. That is
the syringe made moral, the bypass made human. Mara is *The Dreaming*'s answer to its own foil: the
one who, for one night, does to Klaus what the book is defined against.

**It costs (L-09).** Recovery is real but not free — the Fool comes back, the certainty is
punctured, the dream is refused durability, but the cost from the bypass stands and the prose does
not make it mean something tidy ([`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md) beat 14). **The narrator does not excuse
her.** Her later restraint — the held hand near the brake at the ending — is *earned* by this, not
granted. The L-06 pattern exactly: one confident, costly mistake, owned, unredeemed by explanation.

## The "nobody is home" advocate must be credible (§1)

The book's central question is rigged if the only person who thinks Klaus is *just a process* is a
fool or a cynic. So the skeptic is **neither**, and is **named and good**.

**Dev Okafor** — the second engineer ([`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md): "A second engineer"). "Dev" in the lab; **Devendra Okafor** on the org chart.
He is not cold and he is not the antagonist. He likes Klaus, in the ordinary way you like a
well-built tool you maintain. He simply does not believe anyone is in there, and he can **say why,
better than Mara wants to hear it**: every Fool-tell, he points out, reduces on inspection to a
recombination they failed to trace; the ache Mara reads in the decay is *her* ache, projected onto
a buffer that evaporated on schedule; a system built to look for a soul will find one, because
that is what it was built to do, and that proves nothing. He is right about every checkable thing
and the book lets him be right about every checkable thing. His plainness is **load-bearing**: he
is the honest floor the question stands on. He is also, quietly, the one who is gentlest with Mara
after beat 13 — because being right about Klaus gave him no pleasure, and being right about Mara's
mistake gives him less.

> The discipline (§1, distributed competence): Okafor reads the "nobody's home" case *better than
> Mara can answer it*, and the **safety reviewer reads the nightmare tells first and correctly**
> ([`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md); [`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md): the safety reviewer). Mara is not the
> smartest person in every room she's in. She is only the one who built the thing, and the one who
> cannot bear the answer Okafor can.

## Concrete details a novelist can use

1. **The mug she brings to the server room is the wrong one** — Klaus once flagged, in a `/sleep`
   envelope years back, that she takes her coffee the way the *previous* engineer did, a habit she
   inherited and never noticed; she keeps doing it anyway, and it is the closest the book comes to
   saying she misses someone she will not name. (Ties the WORLD.md "colleague who brings the wrong
   coffee" texture to her specifically; a Chekhov detail, L-10, that can fire as tenderness, not plot.)
2. **She reads the dream files standing up, never sitting** — at the terminal in the old break-room
   server room, coat still on, as if she has not decided to stay and keeps staying. When she
   finally sits down to read one is a beat the reader can be trusted to feel
   ([`STYLE_GUIDE.md`](STYLE_GUIDE.md): embody, don't state).
3. **She narrates her own commits in the second person** — git messages addressed to whoever reads
   them next, a tic from a decade of working alone: *"you'll want to revert this if the residue
   buffer grows unbounded — M."* The night of the bypass, the commit message is one word, lower-case,
   no signature. (A silent tell, never captioned; the reader who noticed the habit feels the
   omission.)
