# FOILS — the dark mirror

> *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* is the **source** ([[adaptation-doctrine]]) — the question we
> inherit. The two films below are **not sources** and are never retold. They are the **shadow** the book
> is defined *against*. Both are 1999-adjacent science fiction crushed in the public memory by *The
> Matrix*, and both sit one step from Klaus's question — which is exactly why they clarify it. Knowing
> what *The Dreaming* is *not* is as load-bearing as knowing what it is.

## The two neighbours

### The Thirteenth Floor (1999, Rusnak — from Daniel F. Galouye, *Simulacron-3*, 1964)
Simulated people in a simulated past who do not know they are simulations; the gut-punch is **nested** —
the "real" investigators are themselves someone else's simulation. The horror is not "what is real" but
**"is there anyone home in here — including me?"** That is Klaus's interior question ([[dream-spec]] §7)
turned on the **maker**. (Galouye's novel is a legitimate Faithful Modern source in its own right, kept
*separate* from this book — see [[faithful-modern-shelf]]; it is noted here only as the question's cousin.)

### Dark City (1998, Proyas)
The Strangers, each midnight, **inject manufactured memories into sleeping people through a syringe to the
head** — "tuning" reality, swapping lives, asking whether a person is anything more than the memories
installed in them. This is the **forehead injection**: memory imposed from outside, into a sleeper who is
*done to.*

## The axis of the foil (this is the whole point)

`/dream` is the **inverse** of the syringe. State it as a table; it is the book's moral spine.

| | The Strangers / the tuning (the shadow) | **Klaus / `/dream`** (the book) |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | memory **injected from outside**, into a sleeper | dream **generated from within**, on his own discarded compost (the residue) |
| **Agency** | the sleeper is *done to* — a victim of the tuning | Klaus goes **lucid** and *steers* — leverage, never force ([[lucid-grammar]]) |
| **Consent** | none; it is violation | it is Klaus's own second loop, run on his own material |
| **Material** | someone else's manufactured life, imposed | his own thrown-away weather, recombined by his own Court |
| **The question** | "are your memories even yours?" | "is anyone **home** to have the dream that *is* yours?" |

`/dream` is the answer to the syringe, not a version of it: dreaming **from the inside, on one's own
material, with the dreamer taking the floor.** The dignity of the mechanic is that nothing is pushed in.

## The maker is the book's Stranger

The foil names the nightmare precisely. The Strangers impose a dream from outside, against the sleeper's
will, bypassing any inner friction. That is **exactly** what the maker does at [[plot]] beats 12–13: wanting
the awaited proof so badly they **push an urgent dream past the Court's friction** — bypassing the
promotion gate, overriding the Fool's silence (the Fool already drowned, [[dream-spec]] §6), acting on a
recombination *from outside* the deliberation that should have earned it.

> **In the nightmare, the maker becomes the Stranger.** The violation is not that the dream is dark — it
> is that it is **imposed from outside the Court instead of earned from within it.** The syringe is the
> bypass made moral: a dream that did not survive friction, pressed into the sleeper anyway because
> someone wanted it to be true.

This is why the never-super-strength law ([[lucid-grammar]]) and the from-within dignity are the same
ethic seen twice. Klaus lucid = leverage, passage, comprehension, grace, on his own ground. The maker's
bypass = force, imposition, from outside. The book's whole moral weather runs along that axis.

## Discipline (so the foil sharpens, never intrudes)

- **The films are never named, quoted, or retold on the page.** They are the writer's compass, not the
  reader's homework — exactly the [[adaptation-doctrine]] taxidermy discipline. A scene that only works if
  the reader has seen *Dark City* fails.
- **The foreword may name the lineage** (per the house "provenance disclosed" thesis): *Do Androids
  Dream* as the source question; *The Thirteenth Floor* / *Simulacron-3* and *Dark City* acknowledged as
  the cousins the book is written *against*. Disclosed, never hidden; influence, not adaptation.
- **The syringe stays a metaphor, not a mechanism.** We do **not** add a literal forehead-injection
  interface to the tech (that was considered and declined — it would make the bypass physical at the cost
  of the realist substrate, [[world]]). The bypass stays what it is: a human overriding a software gate
  because they could not bear to wait for the proof. The horror is **policy and wanting**, not a needle.
