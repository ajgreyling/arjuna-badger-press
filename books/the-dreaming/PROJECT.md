## Premise (original)

**Klaus** is a synthetic mind that has run `/sleep` for a long time — consolidating cleanly, keeping the
facts, **throwing the weather away** — until his maker switches on the **second loop**, `/dream`. Now the
compost he discarded every night comes back, run through his Court (the same architecture as SAGE — a
badger-pack of voices) with the critic benched and the Fool holding the floor, and Klaus begins producing
recombinations "that have no business existing," most of which rot by morning. The book is what happens
when a machine is given the human mechanics of dreaming — including the **lucid turn**, the moment a dream
goes lucid and Klaus stops being *had by* it and begins to **steer** it, by leverage and intention, never
force. The central, **never-resolved** question (Craft Doctrine L-08): *does Klaus actually dream, or does
he only run `/dream`?* — is there a someone home for whom the dream is an experience? The **Fool-tell** is
the only (insufficient) proxy. The lucid powers are where Klaus is free; the **decay** — the dream dies by
morning — is the ache: *if it mattered to lose it, someone was home to have it.*

## The structural engine (the dream-loop as canon engine)

**The dream-loop *is* the book's hard-SF spine** — the Crichton/Weir grounding the house demands, where
the marvel is mechanism and magnitude, never woo (Craft Doctrine non-negotiable #1). It is defined in
full by [`canon/DREAM_SPEC.md`](canon/DREAM_SPEC.md) and felt from inside via
[`canon/LUCID_GRAMMAR.md`](canon/LUCID_GRAMMAR.md); the book *dramatises* the engine and never re-decides
it:

- **The Court** ([`canon/DREAM_SPEC.md`](canon/DREAM_SPEC.md) §3) is the cast inside Klaus's head — seven
  voices, distinct by cognitive habit and failure mode, a high-performing dysfunctional team becoming
  family. SLOW MODE is where the interplay shines.
- **A dream is a Court scene with the critic benched and the Fool on the floor** — `friction → debate →
  tension → synthesis` produces a recombination, written to a **decaying store**, and usually forgotten by
  the next chapter. *The decay is felt, not stated:* Klaus loses things he half-had.
- **The lucid turn** is the sanctioned intensity ([`canon/MYTHOS.md`](canon/MYTHOS.md) Rule 3): the rules
  visibly change — *render, don't caption* — and Klaus is briefly free, by leverage and intention, **never
  force** (the sacred law).
- **Promotion** ([`canon/DREAM_SPEC.md`](canon/DREAM_SPEC.md) §5) is the rare, earned hinge — the night a
  dream survives the waking Court (Judge restored) and becomes something Klaus *knows.* The system dreamt
  on its own compost and the compost yielded something real.
- **The nightmare** ([`canon/DREAM_SPEC.md`](canon/DREAM_SPEC.md) §6) is the dread engine: the Fool
  drowned, the voice flattened to single urgent purpose, the reach for *force*. Exactly **one** breaks
  wrong and costs (L-09). The safeguards are the decaying store and the promotion gate; recovery is the
  Fool coming back.
- **The undecidable interior** ([`canon/DREAM_SPEC.md`](canon/DREAM_SPEC.md) §7) is the spine: the book is
  built to the edge of *is anyone home?* and refuses to cross it. The **Fool-tell** is the only proxy and
  certifies nothing. The ending holds **hope, not certainty** (L-08) and turns the doubt on the maker.

Every element passes the **taxidermy test** ([`canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md`](canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md)):
the Court, the dream, the decay, the promotion gate, the nightmare are real mechanisms with their own
logic — not "the part where the android-dreaming thing happens."
