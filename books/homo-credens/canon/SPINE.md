# Homo Credens — the spine

> The argument as a five-act structure. `OUTLINE.md` holds the binding per-chapter briefs;
> this holds the **shape** — what each act is for, where it turns, and what it hands forward.
> Derived from the manuscript's own part divisions and section headings, not imposed on them.

## The through-line

**Religion is the technology by which strangers become kin.**

Stated in ch-01, varied in every chapter, never abandoned. Everything downstream — gods,
priesthoods, temples, scripture, schism — is treated as an answer to one engineering problem:
*how do you get a stranger to trust you before you are kin?*

The book is a history of that technology being **reforged every time human societies changed
size**. Each act is a new scale, and a new version of the tool.

---

## Act I — The Invention of the Gods (ch. 1–3)
*Scale: the band. Before writing.*

**Problem:** cooperation past the face-to-face group.
**Turn:** ch-03. The temple comes *before* the town — Göbekli Tepe inverts the assumed order.
Religion is not a by-product of settlement; settlement may be a by-product of religion.
**Hands forward:** a species with the cognitive kit (agency detection, persistent dead, costly
signal) and the first evidence it was paying for.

Movement in the section titles: *The Ghost-Detector* → *Doing Before Believing* → *The First
Profession* → *The Cathedral on the Hill* → *The Trap with Wheat Inside* → *The Bill*.
The act ends on cost, deliberately.

## Act II — The Bureaucracy of Heaven (ch. 4–8)
*Scale: the city and the kingdom. ~3500–800 BCE.*

**Problem:** thousands of strangers, and a surplus somebody has to count.
**Turn:** ch-04. *The Accountant's Revolution* — writing arrives to count sheep that belonged
to gods. The record enters the book here, and never leaves.
**Hands forward:** five independent civilisations that each built a ledger-and-priesthood stack;
and in ch-08, one storm god with an unusual ambition.

This is the act where the trilogy's deep vocabulary is planted: religion as **record
technology**. Per `CANON_CHOICES.md` it is never named as an axiom here — that is Book II's job.

## Act III — The Axial Gamble (ch. 9–14)
*Scale: the empire and the individual. ~800 BCE–100 CE.*

**Problem:** the old kin-machines don't scale to empires of strangers, and they don't answer
the person who is suffering *inside* one.
**Turn:** ch-09, *Salvation: Religion Changes Its Product* — the pivot from civic maintenance
to personal rescue. The single most important hinge in the book.
**Counterweight:** ch-11's *The Control Group* — China's classical settlement as the case that
tests whether the Axial pattern is universal or a story we tell.
**Hands forward:** portable, convert-able, stranger-facing faiths. ch-10's *The Portable
Kin-Machine* is the thesis in four words.

## Act IV — Conquering Heavens (ch. 15–19)
*Scale: the continent. ~100–1500 CE.*

**Problem:** what happens when a stranger-machine wins, and inherits the state.
**Turn:** ch-15's *One Iota* and ch-16's *The Supertribe* — the moment kinship-at-scale becomes
enforceable, and doctrine becomes something states litigate.
**The control:** ch-19, *The Same Inventions, Everywhere*. The unconnected world — Maya, Aztec,
Inca, Africa, Polynesia, the Dreaming — reaching comparable solutions with no contact. This is
the act's evidentiary spine, and the book's strongest argument that the thesis is about the
problem, not about any one tradition.
**Hands forward:** a Christendom about to break, and the printing press.

## Act V — God on Trial (ch. 20–22)
*Scale: the globe, and the individual conscience. ~1500–1900.*

**Problem:** the kin-machine fragments (ch-20, *Kin at Reading Speed* — print), then gets
audited (ch-21), then gets declared dead while quietly growing (ch-22).
**Turn:** ch-21's *Two Experiments* — toleration as an engineering trial, not a moral awakening.
**Close:** midnight, 31 December 1900. *The Parliament of the World's Religions* (1893) and
*The Madman's Lantern* land in the same chapter on purpose: the century that announced God's
death ends with more humans praying than at any moment in the record.

**The stop line is a cliff, not a conclusion.** See `CANON_CHOICES.md`.
