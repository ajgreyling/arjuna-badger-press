# Homo Credens — plants and payoffs

> Setups that pay off later — inside Credens, and across the *Homo Ter Probatus* box set.
> The trilogy's structure is **each volume is the falsifier for the one before it**, so the
> plants are load-bearing: cut one here and a later volume's argument arrives unearned.

## Within Credens

| Planted | Pays off | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| ch-01 *The Ghost-Detector* — the cognitive kit | ch-22 *The Madman's Lantern* | The same equipment that built the gods is still running when they're declared dead |
| ch-03 *The Bill* — religion's costs | ch-19 *The Debt of the Fifth Sun*; ch-20 *The Dark Twin* | Cost motif compounds; the book never sells the thesis without the invoice |
| ch-04 *The Accountant's Revolution* — writing for temple ledgers | ch-17 *The Server Farms of God* | Record technology scales from clay to cloister |
| ch-07 *The Coup That Invented the Audit* — Mandate of Heaven | ch-21 *The Toleration Workshop* | Auditing the sovereign's legitimacy, 2,500 years apart |
| ch-08 *The God Who May Have Had a Wife* | ch-13 *How to Kill a God* | Monotheism as an achieved outcome, not a starting condition |
| ch-09 *Salvation: Religion Changes Its Product* | ch-14, ch-16, ch-18 | The pivot from civic maintenance to personal rescue — the hinge all of Act IV rests on |
| ch-10 *The Portable Kin-Machine* | ch-16 *The Explosion*; ch-18 *The Road of Silk and Sutras* | Portability is what makes conquest-by-conversion possible |
| ch-11 *The Control Group* | ch-19 *The Same Inventions, Everywhere* | Two independent tests of whether the pattern is universal |
| ch-20 *Kin at Reading Speed* — print | ch-22 *New Gods in the Gaslight* | The same technology that shatters Christendom builds the 19th c. religious marketplace |

## Across the box set

**These are the ones that must survive any cut.**

| Credens plants | Verificans / Benignus pays |
|---|---|
| "Record technology" vocabulary — ledgers, audits, archives, files (ch-04, 05, 07, 17) | **Verificans** names S/B/R/C. The reader should already be fluent, never having been told. See `CANON_CHOICES.md` #3 |
| The book's own honesty moves — *The Case Against the Axis*, *Is This a Religion?*, *The Neanderthal in the Room* | **Verificans** runs the audit **on the auditor**. Credens must establish that self-testing is this author's habit, or that chapter reads as a stunt |
| The moral floor observed but never systematised — reciprocity and the stranger fed recurring across unconnected traditions (esp. ch-11 *golden_rule*, ch-16 *charity*, ch-19) | **Verificans**: the one thing surviving every tradition's audit. **Benignus**: taken as an engineering spec |
| ch-19 *The Same Inventions, Everywhere* — convergence without contact | The empirical backbone of the whole trilogy. If convergence is real, the floor is not a borrowing |
| The 1900 cliff — Victorian confidence left hanging | Currently paid **nowhere**. Pilgrimage Era X gestures at it; no volume covers the 20th century |

## Debts outstanding

**1. The 20th century.** Credens stops at 1900 "deliberately before the twentieth century, which
gets its own treatment elsewhere" (`TRILOGY-SUMMARY-FOR-G.md`). *Verificans* audits traditions,
not the century. *Benignus* builds forward. **The gap is real** — the book sets up a cliffhanger
the box set does not resolve. Either a fourth volume, or the front matter should stop promising
a sequel it doesn't have.

**2. Chapter 16's title collision.** *The Recitation* is also a Jakobus/HBT novel. Every
catalogue surface needs the disambiguation.

**3. Cross-chapter drift is unmeasured.** Twenty-two single-shot agents on one outline
(`CANON_CHOICES.md` #16). The identical setup produced 120 duplicate sentences in *Prophet*.
A repeated phrase here may be motif (§1 of `THEMES.md` sanctions five stranger-problem
variations) or drift — and nobody has looked.
