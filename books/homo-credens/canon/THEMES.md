# Homo Credens — themes and recurring engines

> The motifs the book actually runs on, traced through the chapter section headings so each one
> is evidenced rather than asserted. Use for continuity checks, plate briefs, and catalogue copy.

## 1. The stranger problem (the master theme)

Every act restates it at a new scale: band → city → empire → continent → globe. The phrasings
already in the manuscript:

- ch-04 *The Problem with Ten Thousand Strangers*
- ch-09 *The Century of Strangers*
- ch-10 *The Portable Kin-Machine*
- ch-16 *The Supertribe*
- ch-20 *Kin at Reading Speed*

**Continuity rule:** state it, vary it, never abandon it — and never in the same words twice.
These five are the sanctioned variations; a sixth chapter reaching for the same phrasing is
drift, not motif.

## 2. Religion as record technology

The trilogy's load-bearing vocabulary, planted here and detonated in *Verificans*.

- ch-04 *The Accountant's Revolution* · *The Liver Is a Spreadsheet* · *The Flood in the Files*
- ch-07 *The Crack Factory* · *The Coup That Invented the Audit* · *The Archive Resurfaces*
- ch-05 *The Last Examination* (the weighing of the heart as an audit)
- ch-17 *The Server Farms of God* · *The Argument Machine*

Ledgers, audits, archives, files, spreadsheets. **Never named as S/B/R/C** (`CANON_CHOICES.md`
#3) — the reader should arrive at Book II already fluent without having been told.

## 3. The bill — religion's costs, paid honestly

The book refuses to sell the thesis without the invoice.

- ch-03 *The Trap with Wheat Inside* · *The Bill* (Act I closes on cost, deliberately)
- ch-02 *The Price of a Dead Child*
- ch-07 *The Price of the Banquet*
- ch-11 *Heaven Sends an Invoice*
- ch-19 *The Debt of the Fifth Sun*
- ch-20 *The Dark Twin*

## 4. Mechanism-delight (the *Knowledge* gene)

Open the hood on how a rite actually worked. *How to Raise a Ten-Ton God* (ch-03), *Seventy
Days* (ch-05, mummification), *How to Keep the Sun Rising* (ch-06), *The Machinery of Ecstasy*
(ch-02), *The Machinery of Pardon* (ch-17), *A Machine for Making a God* (ch-05).

## 5. Controls and counter-examples

The book keeps testing its own thesis rather than only illustrating it.

- ch-09 *The Case Against the Axis* — argues against its own framing
- ch-11 *The Control Group* — China as the test of Axial universality
- ch-11 *Is This a Religion?* — the definitional problem, faced not dodged
- ch-19 *The Same Inventions, Everywhere* — the unconnected world as natural experiment
- ch-01 *The Neanderthal in the Room* — the honest edge of the evidence

**This is the book's integrity engine.** It is also the through-line that makes *Verificans*
inevitable. Protect it in any cut.

## 6. Gods that die, and the ones that don't

ch-05 *The God Who Died First* · ch-13 *How to Kill a God* · ch-22 *The Twilight of the Gods* /
*The Madman's Lantern*. The closing irony — the death-of-God century opening at peak prayer —
is the payoff of a motif running since Act II.

## 7. Portability and conversion

The shift from a god you are born under to a god you can carry and be recruited into:
ch-10 *The Portable Kin-Machine*, ch-13 *Religion in a Suitcase*, ch-16 *The Explosion*,
ch-18 *The Road of Silk and Sutras* / *The Monsoon Road*.

## Anti-themes — what this book will not do

- Will not make one tradition the hero or the villain (`CANON_CHOICES.md` #4)
- Will not flatten disagreement into an average (#6)
- Will not present a contested reconstruction as settled (#7)
- Will not sneer at believers (#8)
- Will not import fringe claims as fact (#9)
