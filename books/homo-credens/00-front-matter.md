## How to read this book

This is a history of religion, not a theology and not an exposé. It runs from the first
deliberate graves to the last night of the nineteenth century, and it stops there — at the
doorstep of a century that deserves its own book.

Its method is the lucid.rodeo method: when the sources disagree, you get both citations,
not a smoothed-over average. Scripture is quoted verbatim from public-domain translations.
Where scholars argue, the argument is on the page. Where we simply do not know, the book
says so — *we do not know* is one of the most honest sentences in any language, and it
appears here often.

Its thesis is simple enough to state in a sentence and will take twenty-two chapters to
earn: **religion is the technology by which strangers become kin** — the oldest, most
widely deployed, and most successful tool our species has ever built for scaling
cooperation beyond the circle of faces we can actually know.

The plates are original engravings drawn for this edition.
