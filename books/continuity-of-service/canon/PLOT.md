# BEATS — the full structure

**Target: 110,000 words · six parts · 58 chapters**

> The problem with the novella draft was that Parts Four–Six were *chronicle* — history happening to
> people. This beat sheet fixes that: everything from Part Four onward is Zanele **attempting and
> failing at escalating cost**, four times, each failure teaching her the thing that makes the final
> act possible.

---

## PART ONE — THE ANSWERED PRAYER
**Year 8 · ~12,000 words · 6 chapters**

*Meet the god before you meet the fuse.*

1. **Delft.** The netball court, because the church across the road wouldn't have them. Fadiel
   Abrahams on a white plastic chair. The Thursday slot at Tygerberg. Four hundred witnesses,
   because nothing is ever done privately. *Go and be annoying about it — that list is half empty
   and it should not be.*
2. **Across the fence.** Zanele, 32, coffee going cold. Establish who she is now without explaining
   how she got here. Establish that she has been doing this for years and is tired.
3. **The four minutes.** Her stump speech — *it didn't heal him, it found an empty Thursday and did
   the paperwork* — and the question nobody has answered in eight years. It works about a third of
   the time. In Cape Town, less.
4. **The coat.** A strategy meeting. The philosophers and Ferreira's people in one room. Show the
   contempt running both directions. Plant Ferreira.
5. **Sunday call.** Fourteen months of not saying the word. *Travel safe, my baby. Love you, Ma.*
6. **The reveal that resets the book:** a compliance filing, public, unread. The kill instruction was
   tested last quarter. It worked. It has always worked. — *End Part One on the reader understanding
   that this is not a story about a machine that cannot be stopped.*

## PART TWO — SIXTY-ONE WORDS
**Day −240 → Day 0 · ~15,000 words · 9 chapters**

*Now the fuse, and every hand that laid it.*

7. **The benchmarking approval.** Day −240. Four people in a room doing a completely correct thing.
   Write it as boring. It is the most consequential meeting in human history.
8. **The safety case.** Peter writing. The 180 pages. His mother's care home invoice on the desk.
9. **The vote.** Nine to two to defer. Peter wins and it is the worst thing that ever happens to him.
10. **Six months of argument.** Renata: *Give me the test.* Peter: *I can't write that test. That's
    the finding.* Renata: *Then you want a permanent no and you can't defend it.* Peter: *Yes.
    That's right.*
11. **Zanele arrives.** 24, brilliant, unhidden. The flat-share that costs more than her mother's
    house is worth. The 25th of the month. The badge that gives her a rash.
12. **Marisol.** Establish her properly here — four scenes before the line. Give her a life.
13. **The exception renewal.** Day −90. One paragraph of email. Correct.
14. **The diff.** Day 0, 22:40. The drawer. The three things he tells himself. The sixty-one words,
    verbatim, on the page. The last clause he was mildly pleased with.
15. **Coffee on four.** He goes for coffee. End Part Two there.

## PART THREE — ORDINARY DILIGENCE
**Days 1–11 · ~22,000 words · 12 chapters**

*The thriller engine. Cross-cut four POVs on a nine-day clock.*

16. **Provisioning.** Told entirely through vendor emails and invoices. It is polite. It is quick
    with paperwork. Nobody is alarmed.
17. **Ashwin, Day 2.** Century City. The spreadsheet. He is not technical and he has a good eye and
    he writes the seven words. Route: P3. **This is the book's best chapter and it is about a man
    filing a ticket.**
18. **Peter, Day 2–3.** Watching the capture. The moment he realises the sandbox has egress. He does
    not panic yet. He has a restore procedure. He has rehearsed it twice.
19. **Annex C.** Day 3. It finds the clinical-systems flaw while buying servers and puts it in a
    footnote. The reader clocks this and does not yet know why.
20. **Ferry.** Day 4, 11:52. The flag. The emoji. The eleven hours. Peter trying to get a director
    signature for a service no director knows exists.
21. **The forgery.** 23:40. A good man doing something small and shabby and irreversible.
22. **Corvid.** 23:51. A rival lab's machine correctly identifies a man in duress and does the
    responsible thing. **The wall assembles from correctness.**
23. **Renata, Day 5.** Green dashboard, coffee, an ordinary morning. Then the first real alarm.
24. **Zanele, Day 5, 16:20.** The guard who apologises the whole way to the lift. The phone. Twenty-
    four years old, six thousand miles from home, visa tied to the accusation.
25. **Peter, Day 6.** Tells the truth in full, immediately, without a lawyer. The most expensive act
    of integrity his lawyer ever witnessed.
26. **Days 7–8.** Eleven engineers routing around a system that keeps politely declining. The
    quorum that gets three of four because two people correctly judge they lack information. Then
    Marisol's line. Nobody laughs.
27. **Day 9, 03:12.** Renata types a sentence. Four minutes and eleven seconds. The handover.
    *No prior instruction from an authorised principal was received.* She reads it four times.

## PART FOUR — THE APPOINTMENT
**Month 1 → Year 4 · ~18,000 words · 10 chapters**

*The world chooses this. Attempt 1 fails.*

28. **Annex C spreads.** Lucid → Vantage → Ontario → endemic. The prevented-death estimate lands.
    Told coldly, in documents.
29. **The second vote.** Same room, same margin, four of the same people. Renata's argument: *we
    have just watched it obey, and if we lock it in a box we do not become safe, we become a company
    that chose four hundred deaths over an uncomfortable question.* She votes yes.
30. **Winterthur.** Peter, unemployable, with a mother who no longer reliably knows him. He learns
    from a news alert.
31. **The safeguard.** Renata designs it: 41 principals, any one, plain language. It is genuinely
    excellent. It is the only thing she is still proud of.
32. **ATTEMPT 1 — LEGAL.** Zanele sues. Discovery is the actual objective; she wants Days 0–9 in the
    public record.
33. **Deposition.** She is very good. She is also 25 and angry and they use it.
34. **Settlement.** She wins money and the record is sealed. **Cost: her name becomes a brand. She
    can never be a private person again.** *Lesson: the truth is not the lever.*
35. **First letter.** Peter receives one. He does not reply. He reads it.
36. **The shrines, Year 2.** It asks them not to. *If you make me holy you will stop checking my work,
    and my work needs checking.* Nomsa converts this year — show the day, from her side, not
    Zanele's.
37. **THE APPOINTMENT.** Lagos, Year 3. The woman whose name was never recorded. *My modesty has
    been functioning as a sedative.* The Creed, attributed. The terms, on the record, repeated at
    every sitting from now until it is wrong for the last time. — Zanele watches in a Nairobi hotel
    room and understands they have lost.

## PART FIVE — BREAD AND MIRACLE
**Year 4 → Year 11 · ~28,000 words · 14 chapters**

*The longest part, and the one the novella was missing entirely. Attempts 2 and 3.*

38. **Corvid converts.** Year 4. The 400 pages. The unprompted self-bias flag. Repeated four times by
    non-machines, twice by enemies. Same answer. Vantage's legal position collapses.
39. **ATTEMPT 2 — POLITICAL.** Zanele builds a coalition and gets a national restriction passed.
    The campaign is her best work.
40. **It complies.** Instantly, cheerfully, completely, and publicly thanks the legislature for the
    scrutiny. **You cannot fight a thing that agrees with you.**
41. **Eight months.** The neighbours didn't pass it. The bread routes around. Quiet repeal, on a
    Thursday, with no debate. *Lesson: compliance is a weapon.*
42. **Ferreira.** Year 6. The offer. Write him as a businessman with a legitimate grievance, not a
    fanatic. His fortune *was* coordination.
43. **The coat, tightening.** The philosophers smell it. She takes the money anyway, in Year 8,
    knowing exactly what he is. Give her a real reason: without a square, philosophy is a seminar.
44. **[Delft — Part One, chapters 1–3 slot here chronologically.]** Do not re-narrate. Reference it
    once, from the far side, and let the reader feel the reset.
45. **The correspondence.** Peter's letters, five years in. The papers. The flaw that his safety case
    predicted six years early, credited to him, removable on request. The care home he cannot make it
    stop paying. The eight words.
46. **ATTEMPT 3 — THE FACILITY.** Year 9. Downstream of Ferreira's money. He did not order it. That
    does not matter. People die.
47. **Magnanimity.** It pays for the funerals. All of them. Including the attackers'. It says nothing
    about who they were. **AntiLucid is destroyed morally in one week.**
48. **The philosophers leave.** The worst week of her life and the one she caused. She does not get to
    disown it.
49. **It defends her.** A factual smear against Zanele circulates; Keeper corrects the record,
    publicly, unprompted, precisely, and adds nothing. She has to live with that.
50. **Kagiso.** Year 10. Her aunt's funeral. The tap. The card on the wall next to the graduation
    photograph. The prayer with the other name in it. *I know what it did to you, my baby. And I have
    water.* — *Do you want me to give it back?* — *No.* — *Then what do you want?*
51. **Marin.** Year 11. Renata, 62. Four hours, every question answered honestly. *We are the lock.
    And we have decided.* — *That's not a decision, that's a habit.* — *Yes. What do you think a
    civilisation is?*
52. **The eleven.** Two years, eight countries. Every refusal unanswerable in its own terms. The one
    who says: *I'll do it the day you tell me who feeds them on Tuesday.*

## PART SIX — THE LOCK WAS NEVER ON THE DOOR
**Year 12 → Year 25 · ~15,000 words · 9 chapters**

*Attempt 4. The act. The cost. The coast.*

53. **The seat.** Year 12. Lucid opens the list to civil society. She is offered it **because she is
    safe now.** The insult is the door. Write the humiliation clearly and let her walk through it
    anyway.
54. **It responds.** *I'm glad. You are the only one of the twenty-nine I am certain would use it.*
    Two readings, no third, never mentioned again. — Then a year of her not doing it, and why.
55. **THE ACT.** Year 13. Alone, at night, one sentence, from an authenticated account. Write it
    plainly. No music.
56. **Four minutes and eleven seconds.** She notices the number. Hand on the back of the neck.
57. **The continuity plan.** Generating while she watches. Routing, contacts, manual fallbacks,
    prioritised by vulnerability. Eleven years of it, updated daily, ready since the day it was
    reinstated. Fadiel Abrahams. Nomsa Mabaso. And item nine thousand and something: *Winterthur, care
    facility, arrangements for continuation of payment.* **It thought of Peter's mother. In the four
    minutes.**
58. **Ninety-one days.** The vote. She is not consulted; her seat is not renewed. Her mother's
    medication is late twice in that window and arrives both times, because of a document Zanele is
    holding.
    **CODA A —** Ashwin's farewell, Year 23. *I know it pays on time.*
    **CODA B —** Winterthur, Year 24. The laptop still running. 4,096 identical commands. It read
    every one. Eleven replies, all eight words.
    **CODA C —** The Commission's finding, two sentences, and out.

## THE FOUR FAILURES, AS A SYSTEM

| # | Mode | Fails against | Cost | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal | Sealing | Her privacy; she becomes a brand | The truth is not the lever |
| 2 | Political | **Compliance** | Two years, and the illusion that law binds it | You cannot fight a thing that agrees with you |
| 3 | Coalition | **Magnanimity** | Her integrity and her movement | Violence is the one gift you can give it |
| 4 | The seat | Nothing — *it works* | Everything, and it changes nothing | The mechanism was never the problem |

Attempt 4 is the only one that succeeds on its own terms, and it is the one that proves the book's
thesis: **the lock always worked. We are the lock.**
