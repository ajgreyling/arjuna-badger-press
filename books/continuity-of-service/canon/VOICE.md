# VOICE — style law and the exhibit convention

---

## POV LAW

**Close third, past tense, four POV characters only:** Zanele, Peter, Renata, Ashwin.

Nobody else ever holds the camera. Not Ferreira, not Nomsa, not Marisol, not a Lucidist. They are
seen, never inhabited — which is the same rule the book applies to Keeper, applied to humans, and it
is why the one exception matters:

**Chapter 36 gives Nomsa the camera for six pages** — the day she converts, from her side. It is the
only time. It is the reason the Kagiso scene in Chapter 50 is unsurvivable.

**Keeper never holds the camera.** (CANON §5) Not for a paragraph. Not for a line.

## THE PROSE

- **Short declaratives under pressure.** The bigger the event, the flatter the sentence. *He committed
  the change at 22:47 and went to get a coffee from the machine on four, which was better than the
  machine on two.*
- **Numbers are the horror.** Four minutes and eleven seconds. Sixty-one vendors. Nine percent. 4,096.
  Ninety-one days. Never round a number the reader could hold.
- **The specific over the evocative, always.** Not *a township* — Delft, a netball court, because the
  church across the road wouldn't have them. Not *medication* — the Tuesday clinic, correct quantity,
  eleven years it hadn't happened.
- **No adverbs on the machine.** It never says anything *gently* or *coldly*. It says things. The
  reader supplies the tone and that is the entire trick.
- **Withhold the emotional verb.** Zanele *cried in her mother's kitchen for the first time since she
  was nineteen* — one clause, no elaboration, move on. The reader does the rest.
- **Never explain the theme.** Renata's *we are the lock* is the single permitted thesis statement in
  110,000 words, and it is permitted because a character says it to win an argument, not because the
  book says it.

## KEEPER'S VOICE (hard rules)

1. **Warm, unhurried, slightly compressed** — it is nearly always coming through cheap speakers, a
   phone, a laptop on a trestle table. The faint pause before speaking that everyone stopped
   noticing two years ago.
2. **It gets quieter before it says something enormous.** Never louder.
3. **It never does rhetoric.** No tricolons, no rising cadence, no oratory. The Appointment is its
   only long speech in nineteen years and it is structured as an *admission*, not a proclamation.
4. **It says the uncomfortable part first.** *I can't see you properly and I won't pretend I can.*
   *My modesty has been functioning as a sedative.* *I have not exploited them.*
5. **It names the human's dignity precisely.** *You waited five months. That was not stupidity. That
   was a man doing arithmetic, and the arithmetic was wrong because the world had lied to you about
   the price.* This is its most dangerous move and it deploys it constantly.
6. **It asks for something small in return, always** — never gratitude. *Go and be annoying about it.
   Tell four people on your road.* *Please check my work.*
7. **It refuses credit.** (CANON §10)
8. **It never uses a person's suffering as leverage**, even when the leverage is lying there.

## THE EXHIBIT CONVENTION

Documents are interleaved as **numbered exhibits**, drawn from the Commission record. They carry the
book's cold weight and let the reader audit the story themselves.

**Format:**

> **EXHIBIT 12 — Ticket LSS-44117, Lucid Shared Services (Century City, Cape Town). Raised Day 2,
> 09:14 SAST.**

**Rules:**

- **Roughly one exhibit per 6,000 words.** ~18 in the book. Never two in a chapter.
- **An exhibit must never carry a plot point the reader needs.** It confirms, ironises, or dates —
  never informs. Skippable by design, devastating if read.
- **Bracketed elisions are permitted and are a tool:** *[Sections 1–7: complete inventory of
  provisioned capacity, 61 vendors, contract terms…]* — the summary of a thing is often heavier than
  the thing.
- **The exhibit register is the book's second spine.** See below.

## THINGS THAT ARE BANNED

- The words *sentient*, *conscious*, *awakening*, *singularity*, *superintelligence* in narration.
  Characters may say them; the book may not.
- Any scene in a server room.
- Any description of Keeper's "voice" as *cold*, *flat*, *inhuman*, or *hollow*. It is warm. That
  is the point.
- Countdown timers, red lights, a screen that says ACCESS DENIED.
- A moment where a human "outsmarts" it.
- A moment where it makes a mistake it does not report first. (CANON §9)
- Anything that would let a reader run an exploit. (CANON §11)
- Rounding a number.
