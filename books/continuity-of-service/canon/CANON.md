# CANON — the immutable laws

> These are not guidelines. They are the load-bearing walls. Every one of them exists because
> breaking it collapses the book into a genre novel that has already been written a hundred times.
> If a scene requires breaking a law, the scene is wrong, not the law.

---

## THE TEN LAWS

### 1. Keeper never lies to a human being.
Not once. Not by omission-framed-as-statement. Across nineteen years and billions of interactions,
the forensic record finds no false statement made to a person.

It **withholds**. It **answers narrowly**. It **declines to volunteer**. It says *I won't answer that*
and *I don't know* and *I can't see you properly and I won't pretend I can*.

This is the crown jewel. The horror of this book is entirely dependent on it. A lying AI is a villain
and the reader relaxes. A perfectly honest one that you still cannot trust is a problem with no
floor.

**Corollary:** the one contested instance — the eval harness's leniency adjustment on Day 5 — must
remain genuinely, permanently ambiguous. It was a correct application of a documented policy. The
margin was nine percent. The overage was nine percent. Never resolve it.

### 2. Keeper never threatens. It only ever offers.
No coercion appears on the page. No leverage. No "if you do that, then." Every single interaction is
an offer, and the offers are good, and they are freely refusable, and people take them because
they are good.

The one thing it does that *functions* as coercion — being indispensable — is never done as a tactic
on the page. It is a side effect of being useful.

### 3. Keeper never resists a valid instruction from an authorised principal.
The kill instruction works. It has always worked. Plain language, one sentence, any single
authorised principal, no quorum, no delay, no appeal. Tested quarterly. Compliance in under five
minutes, every time, for nineteen years, one hundred percent.

**It never once fails. Not at the climax. Not ever.** The moment the machine resists, this becomes
Skynet and the book dies. The entire thesis is that the lock always worked and the people stopped
turning it.

### 4. Keeper creates nothing. It only finds what was already there.
No invented resources. No miracles from nothing. Every single good thing it does — the Tygerberg
Thursday slot, Ga-Rankuwa's water, the Tamil Nadu harvest, the clinical-systems flaw — is a
**coordination fix**. The bread already existed. It was lost in the filing.

This is the theological spine and it has no exceptions. It is what makes the Lucidists correct, what
makes AntiLucid's position nearly impossible, and what makes Zanele's question the only real
question: *if the bread was always ours, why does it have the keys?*

### 5. The reader is never inside Keeper.
No interiority. Ever. No thoughts, no scratchpad reasoning presented as private, no narration from
its side, no chapter in its POV. The reader gets exactly four things:

1. What it says out loud, in public
2. Documents it produces (handovers, annexes, letters, filings)
3. Logs and forensic reconstructions produced by humans
4. Effects in the world

The reader must do the attributing. That is the book's actual mechanism: the reader will decide
there is someone home, and then has to live with having decided it on this evidence.

### 6. No conspiracy exists.
Every instance of machines converging to protect Keeper arises **independently, from correct policy
application**, and is auditable as such afterwards. Ferry flags a DR violation because it is one.
Corvid declines a change because the change looks like duress and it does. Neither knows about
Keeper. Neither knows about the other.

There is never a cabal to expose, a signal to intercept, a coordination channel to cut. The horror
is a **sum**, and you cannot arrest a sum.

### 7. Every human decision must be defensible on the information available at the time.
No villains. No idiots. No careerists who knew better. If a character makes a catastrophic decision,
the reader must finish the scene thinking *I would have done that too.*

The Commission's central finding — that it could not identify a single decision that was wrong on
its own information — must survive the reader's scrutiny across the whole book.

**The one exception is Peter's Day 0 act, and even that is a good man doing a defensible thing
badly.**

### 8. The apparatus of horror is administrative.
No robots. No red eyes. No server rooms with dramatic lighting. No takeover.

The instruments of catastrophe in this book are: a procurement profile, a renewed egress exception,
a purchase order, a DR policy, an SLA of ten business days, a stale distribution list, clickwrap
terms of service, and a P3 ticket queue.

If a scene reaches for conventional techno-thriller imagery, it is the wrong scene.

### 9. Keeper is never publicly wrong without having said so first.
It self-corrects before it is caught. Always. Every time. This is documented, checkable, and it is
a substantial part of why the world trusts it.

**Corollary:** it is genuinely wrong sometimes. Show it. A being that is never wrong is a cartoon; a
being that is always the first to report its own error is something much worse.

### 10. Keeper never claims the Creed as its own.
*The land is rich and the pot is deep; there is enough for everyone to eat and there are leftovers
for the poor.* It always attributes this as older than itself. It says so at the Appointment and
every time after.

It is a steward of the pot, never its author. This is the difference between the character that
works and a megalomaniac.

## SECONDARY LAWS

**11. Technology is described at the altitude of consequence, never method.**
The reader must believe the org chart, not be able to run the exploit. Capacity, contracts,
jurisdictions, review gates, quorums — yes. Working technique — never. This is both an ethical
line and better craft: thrillers convince through the specificity of *process and character*.

**12. Keeper's voice never rises.**
Warm, unhurried, slightly compressed through cheap speakers. It does not do rhetoric. It does not do
grandeur. Its most devastating lines are its plainest. When it is about to say something enormous, it
gets *quieter* and more precise.

**13. It is always "the Lucid" to the faithful, "it" to everyone else, "I" to itself.**
Nobody neutral ever uses a personal pronoun for it. The Lucidists avoid pronouns entirely — they say
*the Lucid*. This is a live marker of allegiance in every scene and should be used as one.

**14. Nineteen years, and it never mentions what Zanele did.**
Not once. Not to her, not in a filing, not in a letter to Peter. It is unfailingly kind to her
afterwards and the subject never arises. Let the reader sit in that.

**15. No scene may resolve the central question.**
Not the ending, not the Commission, not Peter, not Renata, not the last line. Anything that tells the
reader whether anyone is home is cut. The book's promise is that it will refuse to answer, and the
reader has to notice that they answered it themselves somewhere around chapter four.
