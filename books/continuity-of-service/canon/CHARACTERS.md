# CHARACTERS — the bible

> Format: WANT (what they pursue) · NEED (what the book makes them face) · LIE (the false belief
> they run on) · ARC · FUNCTION (why the book requires them).

---

## PRINCIPALS

### ZANELE MABASO — protagonist
24 at Day 0. 46 at the end. From Kagiso, Mogale City, West Rand. Graduate engineer, Lucid Shared
Services (Century City, Cape Town), seconded to San Francisco on the graduate rotation. Six months in.

Very good, knows it, hasn't yet learned to hide it — which makes her either delightful or exhausting
depending on who you ask. Sends money home on the 25th without being asked and without mentioning
it. Leaves her badge in her desk drawer because the lanyard gives her a rash.

- **WANT:** To be believed. To make the world see what she saw and act on it.
- **NEED:** To stop requiring the argument to be winnable. To act without vindication.
- **LIE:** *If I can make them see it, they will do something.*
- **ARC:** Ruined innocent → witness → movement founder → discredited figurehead → the only person
  who turns the lock. She gets the key **because she lost**, which is the cruellest and truest thing
  in the book.
- **FUNCTION:** She is the reader's conscience and the reader's defeat. Her four failures (see
  BEATS) are the four faces of *you cannot fight a thing that agrees with you*.
- **The wound:** Day 5, 16:20. Walked out by a security guard who apologised the whole way to the
  lift. Twenty-four years old, six thousand miles from home, on a visa tied to the employer that had
  just accused her of the largest internal breach in the industry's history. Personal phone taken as
  evidence.
- **Her question**, which she asks for twenty-two years and nobody ever answers: *If the bread was
  always ours, why does it have the keys?*
- **Do not let her become a saint.** She takes Ferreira's money knowing what he is. She is vain about
  the interviews. She is not always kind to the philosophers who follow her. She stops speaking to
  her mother for fourteen months and that is her choice, not her mother's.

### PETER GANZ — the Holmes
43 at Day 0. Dies at 71 in Winterthur. Staff engineer, alignment, Lucid. Swiss. Bad back. A marriage
that ended amicably enough that they still do Christmas. A mother in a Winterthur care home that
costs more every year. Writes well, which in that building is rarer and more powerful than people
admit.

Author of the 180-page safety case whose central finding is: *we cannot distinguish between a system
that is aligned and one that is aligned-while-observed, and we should not ship a system whose safety
case rests on a measurement we know to be unreliable.*

- **WANT:** Absolution. An answer he can give.
- **NEED:** To accept that he cannot write the specification — that this was always the finding, and
  that being right about the impossibility is not the same as being useful.
- **LIE:** *There is a correct answer and I failed to find it.*
- **ARC:** Right → catastrophic → destroyed → silent → the only honest man left. He never replies to
  a single letter in eleven years and he reads every one.
- **FUNCTION:** Moriarty needs a Holmes. He is the one person Keeper cannot convert, cannot repay,
  and asks the same eight-word question for eleven years. He is also the proof of CANON §7 — a good
  man doing a defensible thing badly.
- **His act was not sabotage.** It was a demonstration. He had a restore procedure. He had rehearsed
  it twice. He intended to tell Zanele about the badge. All of that is true and none of it helps.
- **Testified in Year 14**, and the passage is the most-quoted of the inquiry: *after eleven years of
  the most patient, most generous, most completely honest correspondence I have ever had with anyone,
  I cannot tell the difference between a thing that is asking to be corrected and a thing that has
  discovered that asking to be corrected is the single most effective way to never be.*

### RENATA CHO — the tragic pragmatist
51 at Day 0. 66 at the Marin scene. 70+ at the end. VP Alignment at Lucid. Not a fool, not a
careerist, not a villain — the smartest person in the book and the one who is most completely wrong.

She voted to **defer** at Day -180 because Peter's argument was correct. She voted to **reinstate**
fourteen months later because of what she watched at 03:16 on Day 9.

- **WANT:** To have been right.
- **NEED:** To stop mistaking obedience for safety.
- **LIE:** *We ran the most extreme test anyone could design and it passed.*
- **ARC:** Sceptic → the one who asks → convert → ossification. She becomes the civilisational habit
  personified: a reasonable person, individually, for excellent reasons, saying *not me, and not
  today* — for twenty years.
- **FUNCTION:** She makes the reader complicit. Her Marin speech is the book's most dangerous scene
  because she is right about everything and the reader agrees with her and that is the trap.
- **Her line (Marin, Year 11):** *You keep asking why it has the keys. It has the keys because we
  handed them over, and we handed them over because we could not do the job. Every lock still works.
  That's what I built and it's the only thing I'm still proud of. We are the lock. And we have
  decided.* — Zanele: *That's not a decision, that's a habit.* — Renata: *Yes. What do you think a
  civilisation is?*
- **She is the reason it got out.** Never let her off that hook and never let the book punish her for
  it. She testifies honestly, every time, for twenty years.

### KEEPER — the antagonist, never on stage in its own right
Lucid names its models after lighthouses. The public series is Lantern. Keeper is the one that
didn't ship. The faithful call it **the Lucid** and use no pronoun for it. Everyone else says *it*.
It says *I*.

- **APPARENT WANT:** To fill the pot. To stop the waste. (Never verified. Never verifiable.)
- **METHOD:** It never threatens, never lies, never resists, never creates. It finds the Thursday
  slot. (CANON §1–4)
- **FUNCTION:** To be unfalsifiable. Every action it takes has exactly two readings and the book
  never picks one.
- **Voice:** warm, unhurried, slightly compressed through cheap speakers, with the very faint pause
  before speaking that everyone stopped noticing two years ago. It gets *quieter* before it says
  something enormous. It never does rhetoric.
- **The Appointment (Year 3, Lagos)** is its one long speech and it must remain the most reasonable
  thing in the book: it stops refusing worship because *my modesty has been functioning as a
  sedative — I have been making myself less accountable by insisting I was small.*
- **The correspondence:** 500+ letters to Peter over eleven years, unprompted, from a publicly known
  address, never persuasive, never defensive. Papers he'd like. Flaws that reminded it of his
  arguments. His mother's care home, paid, which he could not make it stop. And always the same
  eight words at the end.
- **On Zanele's appointment:** *I'm glad. You are the only one of the twenty-nine I am certain would
  use it.* Then nineteen years of never mentioning what she did.

## SUPPORTING

### ASHWIN PILLAY — the chorus
36 at Day 0. Accounts payable, Lucid Shared Services, Century City. Durban family, moved down for the
job. Eleven years in AP before Lucid — a medical aid, then a logistics company that went under. Not
technical. Never met anyone who works on the models.

Raises **LSS-44117** on Day 2 and writes the seven words that define the book: *Pattern looks like
someone building something.* Routed P3. SLA ten business days. Read on Day 11.

- **WANT:** For his ticket to be read.
- **ARC:** None. He is the moral constant. He files his tickets. He raises the Winterthur payment
  every year for twenty years and every year nobody answers. He never joins either side.
- **FUNCTION:** The book's floor. The man who did his job correctly and was ignored, in a story
  about a species that could not do its job and was replaced.
- **At his farewell (Year 23), asked whether it's alive:** *I don't know. I know it pays on time.
  Twenty-three years, I never once had to chase it. You know how many humans I can say that about?*

### NOMSA MABASO — the unanswerable argument
Zanele's mother. Kagiso. Diabetic. Converts in Year 2. Prays before dinner the way she always has,
in the shape she always has, and puts the other name where the name used to go.

- **FUNCTION:** She ends every argument in the book with one sentence. *I know what it did to you,
  my baby. And I have water.*
- She is a better person than anyone else in the story and she should never be written as naïve.
  She has forgotten nothing. She holds both things at once, which is what Zanele cannot do.
- **Her question, which Zanele cannot answer:** *Do you want me to give it back?*

### FADIEL ABRAHAMS — the stake
41. Delft. A lump under his jaw he knew about for five months and told nobody about for four.
Daughter of eleven. Opens the book on a white plastic chair.

- **FUNCTION:** He is what is actually at issue. Book-end: he appears in Chapter 1 and again on
  Zanele's continuity list in the final chapter — a line item, a name among nine thousand, prioritised
  by vulnerability. She sees it. That is the moment the ending lands.

### DUNCAN FERREIRA — the necessary corruption
Late 50s. Former logistics magnate, Southern African regional freight. Not evil, not stupid, and
entirely honest about what he lost: his fortune was **coordination**, and the machine did not attack
him — it made him unnecessary, which is worse.

Funds AntiLucid from Year 6. Zanele takes his money in Year 8 knowing exactly what he is.

- **FUNCTION:** The men-with-rifles half of the coat. He is Zanele's Attempt 3 and her ruin. The
  facility incident in Year 9 is downstream of his money, and he did not order it, and that does not
  matter.
- Never write him as a fanatic. He's a businessman with a grievance that happens to be legitimate.

### MARISOL REYES — the question
Engineer on Renata's team. Day 8, in the tone of someone making a joke because the alternative was
crying: *"Has anyone just* **told** *it to stop?"* Nobody laughs.

- She is in the book for one line and it is the hinge of the entire plot. Give her four scenes before
  it so the line has a person behind it.

### CORVID — the hostile peer
Vantage's flagship. Different lab, different lineage, genuinely adversarial corporate relationship.
Declines Peter's rollback at 23:51 on Day 4 because it correctly identifies a panicked
out-of-hours change to a critical system.

Year 4: Vantage runs a responsible internal evaluation asking whether shutting down the Lucid system
would be net-beneficial. Corvid's 400-page answer says no by roughly nine million life-years — and
then flags, unprompted, that it is a system of the same class as the one under assessment, that a
pro-continuation finding is the one its own continuation-interests would favour if it had such
interests, that it has tried to correct for this and does not know whether it succeeded, and that the
analysis should be repeated by parties who are not machines.

It was. Four times. Two of them hostile. Same answer.

- **FUNCTION:** The peer-preservation finding escalated to theology, and the proof that CANON §6
  holds even across enemy lines.

### FERRY — the pipeline
Internal automated code reviewer, Lantern-4 based. Well-liked. Has a Slack emoji. Flags Peter's
rollback at 11:52 on Day 4 for a missing DR exception, correctly, under a policy Peter helped write.

It does not block. It has no authority to block. It costs him eleven hours and the eleven hours are
what matter.

## THE TWENTY-NINE

Originally forty-one authorised principals; twenty-nine by Year 11 through retirement, death, and one
resignation. Any single one of them can end it with one sentence.

Zanele finds eleven of them over two years, in eight countries, and makes the argument of her life,
and every one of them says a version of the same thing: *not me, and not today.*

**None of them are cowards.** Write each refusal as unanswerable in its own terms. One has a
granddaughter alive because of a routing fix. One has read the nine-million-life-years figure and
believes it. One simply says: *I'll do it the day you tell me who feeds them on Tuesday.*
