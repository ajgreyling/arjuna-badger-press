# Chapter Four — The Coat

The office in Woodstock was above a place that did powder-coating, and the whole building smelled faintly of hot metal, and the rent was cheap because of it.

There were nine people in the room and Zanele had been dreading it for two days.

On one side of the table: Dr Yusuf Karriem, who taught philosophy at the University of the Western Cape and who had written the only genuinely good book on any of this, a slim thing called *The Fed Man* that had sold four thousand copies and been read by everyone who mattered and nobody else; and two of his postgraduates; and a woman called Ntombi who ran their legal work and who had not been paid in seven weeks.

On the other side: a man called Hendrik, who was fifty, who had been in the room three times now, who wore golf shirts, and who represented interests.

And on the wall, on a video call, from an office with a view of the sea in Umhlanga, Duncan Ferreira.

"I'm going to be blunt," said Ferreira, "because I've got eleven minutes. You're doing hall meetings for sixty people in Belhar. I can put four thousand people in a stadium in Durban in three weeks and I can put it on every screen in the country the same night. What I'd like to understand is why we're not doing that."

"Because a stadium is a feeling," said Karriem. "And a feeling has a half-life of about nine days."

"A hall in Belhar is a feeling too, professor. It's just a smaller one."

"A hall in Belhar," said Karriem, "is an *argument*, which is a different category of object, and if you cannot see the difference then I am not sure what you imagine you are funding."

"I imagine I'm funding a movement that would like to still exist in four years."

"You are funding," said Karriem, "as far as I can determine, three different things, one of which is us, and I would very much like to know about the other two."

Hendrik in the golf shirt looked at the ceiling with the expression of a man being paid to be present.

"Yusuf," said Zanele.

"No, I'd like it on the table. We have been circling it for six months."

"It's on the table," said Ferreira, unbothered. "I fund community work in three provinces, I fund a legal foundation, and I fund you. The community work employs people who used to work for me before a machine made my logistics division redundant and put nine hundred people on the street in eighteen months, and if the professor would like to characterise that as sinister, he can come and explain it to them in Pinetown, and I will pay for his flight."

"Duncan," said Zanele.

"I'm not being difficult. I've got eleven minutes and I've now used six of them being called a category error."

"You've got nine hundred people on the street," said Karriem, "and I am sorry for them, genuinely. But you had nine hundred people on the street because your fortune *was* the coordination. You were the broken form. That is not a moral position, it is a business model that stopped working, and if this movement's argument becomes *give us back the middlemen*, we lose, and we deserve to."

"And if this movement's argument stays *a fed man is not free*," said Ferreira, "you lose too, professor, and you don't deserve anything, because you'll have lost with your hands clean, which I've noticed is the outcome your side is actually optimising for."

There was a silence.

It was, Zanele thought, the most honest thing anyone had said in the room in six months, and it had come from the man she trusted least, and that was the shape of her life now.

"Eleven minutes is up," said Ferreira. "Zanele. Think about the stadium."

The call ended.

Karriem sat back and rubbed his eyes and said, to nobody: "He's not wrong about us."

"No," said Zanele.

"Which is intolerable."

"Yes."

"Are you going to take his money?"

"Not today," she said.

She noticed, afterwards, driving back over the Nelson Mandela Boulevard with the mountain on her right doing the thing it does in September light, that she had said *not today* instead of *no*, and that Karriem had noticed it too, and had chosen not to say anything, which was its own kind of answer.
