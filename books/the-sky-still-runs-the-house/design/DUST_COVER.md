# Dust-cover direction — *The Sky Still Runs the House*

## Front

A near-black field, not starry fantasy. At the bottom, the simple plan of a house in thin warm-white
architectural lines. Above it, one restrained circular ecliptic divided into twelve equal sectors.
Only three or four sectors contain tiny, historically grounded marks: a cuneiform wedge cluster, an
Egyptian decan star, a Chinese lodge line, and a Maya day glyph. Lines from the circle descend into
the house and become a week grid, a medical diagram, a court ledger, and a printed almanac column.
The point is structure entering ordinary life, not cosmic magic.

Title large in Atkinson Hyperlegible or a compatible humanist sans. “SKY” and “HOUSE” share a width.
Subtitle small and calm. Author at foot. No glowing zodiac, crystal, woman’s face, hands holding a
galaxy, or AI nebula fog.

## Back-copy spine

Astrology’s forecasts did not have to be right for its machinery to become useful. The book follows
that machinery from a royal eclipse report to the seven-day week, the medical casebook, the court
observatory, the printed almanac, and the personality app — keeping a hard line between what survives
by descent, what mattered as historical scaffolding, what remains a living tradition, and what is
only a revealing analogy.
