# SOURCE_NOTES: *The Long Road* (working title) — an African overland compendium

> A compendium of real journeys by cyclists, hikers, motorcyclists, and overlanders across South
> Africa and the length of the African continent. Grouped **by road/route, with countries nested
> inside** — Sani Pass, the Cape-to-Cairo corridor, the Skeleton Coast, the Tanzam Highway, and
> others — because the road, not the nationality of the traveller, is what a cyclist, a hiker, a
> biker, and a 4x4 overlander crossing the same ground actually share.

## Confirmed anchor journeys (binding — do not alter these facts)

### Johan C. Bakkes — the book's dedicatee, threaded throughout (South African)
**AJ owns Bakkes's complete works in print and has dedicated this book to him.** Bakkes is not a
single-chapter subject like the others below — his journeys are the closest thing this compendium
has to a recurring companion voice, and he should be woven through multiple Parts wherever his real,
sourced travels cross the roads this book covers, in addition to a standalone tribute in Part I.

- **Full name:** Casparus Johannes Bakkes. Born 21 October 1956, Stellenbosch; raised Saldanha Bay,
  eldest of four. Father Cas Bakkes, a military historian; mother Margaret, an author (relative of
  Hennie Aucamp) — a literary household he first resisted, then answered.
- **Died 24 June 2025, age 68,** of cancer, at a care facility in Paarl — one year and one month
  before this commission, not five days as with Stücke, but still recent enough that the dedication
  is a live act of memory, not distant homage. Survived by wife Nancy (clinical psychologist and
  artist — sourced as "Nanna Vorster-Bakkes" on one page, "Nancy" on the obituary; use **Nancy**,
  the obituary's spelling, and flag the discrepancy in the Author's Note), children Cara and Marc,
  two grandchildren.
- **Parallel careers:** qualified chartered accountant (1981), own auditing firm (1982); lectured
  applied accounting at Unisa (1983–2000) then University of the Western Cape (2001 onward, full
  professor); qualified tour guide (1990) and ran Induku Safaris; two years as a captain in the
  former South African Defence Force. Diagnosed with colon cancer in 2011; kept travelling and
  writing after treatment.
- **Travel modes (the reason he belongs in THIS compendium specifically):** on foot, by train
  (rode the Trans-Sahara and the Trans-Siberian; his stated ultimate ambition was to ride every
  train in the world), by motorcycle, and by public transport through remote regions. Slept under
  the stars; used local taverns as his research method ("a town's soul"). Travelled with over
  twenty copper bangles collected across Africa as a personal signature, and with an inflatable
  mannequin companion nicknamed "Pop," who had her own passport — a real, documented detail, use it
  once, precisely, never played for cheap whimsy.
- **African ground relevant to this book's roads:** Namibia (Fish River Canyon — completed **19
  times**; the Kuiseb River), Kamanjab near Namibia's Skeleton Coast, Angola, Botswana, Zambia,
  Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Ethiopia (Danakil salt mines, the Blue Nile source, Erta Ale volcano — "the
  hottest place"), Mali (Timbuktu), Ivory Coast, Senegal, Mauritania, Morocco, Egypt, Sudan, Kenya,
  Malawi. This is the single most extensive African itinerary of any traveller in this book —
  **Fish River Canyon (19 crossings) and Kamanjab/Skeleton Coast are the sourced, named material
  that closes the Part II gap** flagged elsewhere in this file.
- **Signature quote on travel:** "A journey is not merely something to endure to reach a
  destination." On his own identity: "I'm not a writer; I'm a storyteller, a traveller, a
  professional hobo." His father's word for him: a *pallieter* — a life-liver.
- **Bibliography (binding — use these titles and years exactly, do not invent others):**
  1. *Moer toe die vreemde in* (2001) — debut
  2. *Nou's ons in ons donner in* (2006) — English translation *To Hell and Gone* (2008)
  3. *Norrevøk* (2008) — Siberian tales
  4. *Samoe(r)sa reis* (2010) — India-centred
  5. *Oepse Daisy* (2011) — includes his cancer memoir material
  6. *Openbaring: 'n reisjoernaal* (2016) — expeditions to the earth's temperature extremes
  7. *Erfgeld is swerfgeld* (2020) — subtitled "reis na binne, die -ste -ste plek"; a tribute to
     his late father
  8. *Hou die kruit droog* (2021, Protea Boekhuis) — collected Facebook posts and reflections
  All republished/revised by Protea Boekhuis, 2022–2024.
- **Recognition:** won first prize, 2006 Mondi Awards (Travel, Sport & Entertainment category) for
  the article "Hel toe." Published in *De Kat*, *Insig*, *Huisgenoot*, *Weg!*, *Rapport*, *Rooi
  Rose*. Stories translated into English by Elsa Silke. Critic Justin Fox noted his use of teaser
  openings, flashbacks, and historical parallel as recurring craft moves — worth studying as a
  structural model for this compendium's own narrator-guide voice (see STYLE_GUIDE.md).
- **What is NOT documented:** no source gives a systematic country-by-country date log for his
  African travels (his books are impressionistic essay collections, not a travel ledger) — treat
  his appearances in this compendium as **anchored, dated where a source gives a date, and
  otherwise placed by region only** (e.g. "Kamanjab, on the Skeleton Coast" without inventing a
  specific year unless one surfaces). Never invent dialogue or private thought for him beyond what
  his own quoted words (above) support — he is real, recently deceased, and his own family may read
  this.

### Riaan Manser — circumnavigation by bicycle (South African)
- Departed Cape Town September 2003; returned end of 2005 (~2 years, 2 months)
- First person to circumnavigate the African continent by bicycle
- 36,500 km (one source) / ~37,000 km (another) through 34 countries, every coastal country
- Averaged ~90 km/day; lost 14 kg over the journey
- Jailed in Equatorial Guinea by border police; taken hostage by armed teenage rebels in Liberia
- Book: *Around Africa on My Bicycle*

### Keegan Longueira — solo Cairo-to-Cape record (South African), then broken weeks later
- Departed Cairo 2 January 2015; arrived V&A Waterfront, Cape Town, 2 March 2015
- 59 days, 8 hours; ~10,526 km, averaging ~175 km/day; broke Dutchman Robert Knol's 2011 record
  of 70 days, 3 hours
- Ridden partly in aid of Operation Smile (cleft palate surgery fundraising)
- Guinness confirmed the record 23 April 2015

### Mark Beaumont — broke Longueira's record the same year (Scottish)
- 10 April – 21 May 2015: 41 days, 10 hours, 22 minutes; 10,812 km
- Beat Longueira's 59-day record by roughly 18 days, only weeks after Longueira set it
- Book: *Africa Solo: My World Record Race from Cairo to Cape Town*
- **Craft note:** the back-to-back record chain (Longueira breaks a 4-year-old record in March,
  Beaumont breaks Longueira's in May, same year) is a genuinely dramatic real beat — use it as
  the compendium's closing hinge in Part VI, not just a footnote.

### Kingsley Holgate — "Africa's Outside Edge" (South African)
- Born 28 February 1946; described as "the most travelled man in Africa," Fellow of the Royal
  Geographical Society, Land Rover ambassador, humanitarian expedition leader (40+ expeditions
  across his career, via the Kingsley Holgate Foundation)
- "Africa's Outside Edge" expedition traced the entire coastal outline of the African continent:
  33 countries, 449 days
- Distinct from Manser (circumnavigation by bicycle alone) — Holgate's outline expeditions are
  vehicle-based, team humanitarian journeys, not solo human-powered crossings; keep these two
  clearly distinguished in the manuscript, do not conflate "traced the outline" claims

### McKenzie Barney (+ partner Jim) — Cairo to Cape Town by bicycle
- 10 countries, ~11,000 km, 5 months
- Explicitly cites Riaan Manser as inspiration — a usable braid point in the compendium

### Mario Rigby — "Crossing Africa," solo walk
- Departed November 2015; 12,000 km on foot through South Africa, Mozambique, Malawi, Tanzania,
  Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia, Sudan, Egypt
- Completed January 2018 (~2 years, 3 months)

### Modern Cape-to-Cairo motorcycle/4x4 route (composite, not one traveller)
- ~18,000 km, 12 countries: South Africa, Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya,
  Ethiopia, Sudan, Egypt
- Recurring hazards across accounts: border strikes, landslides, road closures, local conflict

## Craft note
Manser, Rigby, Longueira, Beaumont, Barney, and Kingsley Holgate are all, as far as sourced,
living, and none have been contacted or granted permission to appear — treat them with the same
"no invented dialogue or private thought" discipline as *Um Welt* and *Kookie*. **Johan C. Bakkes is
different: he is deceased (24 June 2025) and this book is dedicated to him.** That earns him fuller,
warmer treatment than the living subjects — more of his own real quoted words, more space — but
NOT invented dialogue or invented scenes either; his family may read this book, and the same
factual discipline applies, just applied with the extra care due a dedicatee rather than a profile
subject. The Author's Note must say plainly that Bakkes is the dedicatee, name the source of that
material (his own eight books, the LitNet and Maroela Media tributes), and note that — unlike the
living travellers — his own published words are quoted directly where sourced above, not merely
summarized.
