The Real Place(s):  
Newark, Ohio — the surviving Octagon Earthworks and Observatory Circle, plus the Great Circle a few miles away. The High Bank Works near Chillicothe, 53 miles south. The land is suburban and ordinary, ringed by roads, and for decades the Octagon was leased to a private golf club.

Site Map:  
- The Octagon: eight 550-ft earthen walls, 50 acres, gates aligned to the Moon’s standstills.
- Observatory Circle: 1,054-ft diameter, sub-4-ft tolerance; joined to the Octagon by a causeway.
- Great Circle: 366m diameter, interior ditch, 8-ft walls.
- High Bank: the only other Hopewell octagon, same standard unit, same alignments.
- The city: Newark’s residential sprawl, the golf course, the Ohio History Connection’s offices, tribal representatives’ meeting grounds.

Texture of the Wonder:  
Frost on morning grass where the Moon rises through a gateway; the hush of standing inside a geometric figure you can see only from above; the sound of a distant highway over the walls. The sense of a patient record, basket by basket, handed forward by people who never saw the finished pattern — and the shock of realizing the math is real, not a story.

Chase Geography:  
The crew moves between Newark’s earthworks, OHC’s archive rooms, the golf course’s restricted grounds, and a trip south to High Bank. The chase is as much through records and permissions as through physical sites.

Living People Near the Place:  
- Tribal representatives and THPOs (Tribal Historic Preservation Officers), some living in Ohio, some returning for consultation.
- The Ohio History Connection staff, navigating public trust, legal battles, and stewardship.
- Golf club members, some resentful, some curious.
- Local townspeople, many unaware of the monument’s scale or meaning.
- The clean-handed adversary: a foundation or think tank seeking to “secure” the record for research/commercialization before full tribal stewardship is restored.
