The ND crew (at least one load-bearing ND lead):  
- **Naila** — the spine-lead, analytic, language-obsessed, the one who most needs the maybe to become a yes; her arc is the struggle to let the mystery stand, and the cost of not forcing closure. Her wiring is her lens: compulsive pattern-seeking, restless, not a wound but her way of being.
- **The LENS** — ex-war photographer, PTSD, not ND but the recurring outside eye; their camera is the series’ question (“lower the lens; what’s really here?”), always inviting the reader to see what’s missed.
- **Other crew** (detail in full cast doc): each with a distinct lens (e.g., the tactile engineer, the pattern mathematician), all marginal, all seeing what others miss because they could never stop watching the room; their wiring is dramatized in action, never caricatured.
- **Jakobus** — the weathered fixer, never a POV, always the road, able to read a made thing’s function but here meets his limit. He defers to the crew and the local/custodian, hands the moment away; his gift fails hardest here.
- **THE LOCAL / CUSTODIAN** — not a living indigenous person (the makers are 600 years gone), but the Beinecke codicologist/conservator (a composite of Lisa Fagin Davis, Zandbergen, Bowern), who out-reads the crew on the object’s reality and the limits of what can be known. They own the “what is it for?” moment — their answer, or their refusal, is the book’s turn.
- **THE CLEAN-HANDED ADVERSARY** — a plausible, well-funded group (think: private collector’s agent, a foundation seeking to “solve” and brand the MS, or a crypto-art start-up wanting to “unlock” it for profit), never a cabal, always human, motivated by ownership or ideology, not evil. Their chase is to tidy the question away, to claim the book or the story for themselves.
Voices: All dialogue and inner life is shaped by wiring-as-lens; close-third rule: the camera is always with the crew, never omniscient, never in the adversary’s or custodian’s head.
