# *Lacework*

An **unauthorised homage** to Shirley Conran's novel *Lace* (1982) — a craft exercise under the Arjuna
Badger Press house tooling, on the **Faithful Modern** shelf. **Not licensed. Not for commercial
release.** The book keeps the *arcs and the engine* — four schoolgirls bound by a hidden pregnancy and
a pact; the child placed and lost; four working lives built on the page across three decades; the
grown child, now famous, summoning the four; the father whose standing the secret protects — and every
name, world, scene, and sentence is **original**. Conran's famous summons line is hers and does not
appear in this book.

Provenance is disclosed, never hidden — same standing as the press's other Faithful Modern homages
([`../the-sealed-finding/`](../the-sealed-finding/), [`../the-long-silence/`](../the-long-silence/),
[`../the-scramble/`](../the-scramble/)). See
[`canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md`](canon/ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md) for the line we hold: **take the
engine, never the text.**

> **The 1984 miniseries is not a source and not a model.** We are after the novel — the work, the
> money, the survival craft — not the camp.

## The three craft mandates (why this book exists)

1. **DNA killed the whodunit; culpability survives.** The daughter arrives with the lab result already
   in her pocket. The question is not *who* — it is *which of you decided.*
2. **The work is on the page.** Conran showed women working; we keep it at full calibre — four careers
   in post-94 South Africa, procedural, never montage.
3. **The child is a person, not a prize.** The daughter's chapters carry equal weight; the placement
   economy and the talent industry are indicted as machines — never the people inside them.

## Substrate & structure

**South Africa, 1994–2026.** Weylandt House girls' school in the Cape winelands, matric class of 1996
— the first democratic cohort — then Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, London. Two timelines
**interlaced**: the school years and working decades, and the six weeks around the 2026 summit at the
Marchmont Hotel. Every present-day chapter circles a hole; every past chapter fills it in — for the
reader only. Openwork: a fabric made of holes, held by thread.
