## The three craft mandates (why this book exists)

1. **DNA killed the whodunit; culpability survives.** In 1982 "which of you is my mother?" could carry
   a novel. In 2026 a cheek swab answers it in three weeks, and any retelling that pretends otherwise
   is a costume drama. So Lelethu Grace arrives at the summit **already holding the lab result.** The
   question she brings is the one no laboratory can answer: *which of you decided — who paid, who kept
   paying, who stopped, and who let the trail go cold?* Maternity mystery retired; **anatomy of a pact**
   installed. (MYTHOS hard line.)
2. **The work is on the page.** Conran's radical move — the thing that made *Lace* more than a
   blockbuster — was showing women *working*: the trade, the invoice, the shift, the negotiation. Kept
   at full calibre. Four careers built in post-94 South Africa in procedural detail, never montage.
3. **The child is a person, not a prize.** The source's Lili spends much of the book as a symbol the
   others contest. Here the daughter's chapters carry equal weight from the first part, and the machine
   that swallowed her — the informal placement economy, the talent industry that monetises a beautiful
   orphan — is indicted as a machine. Never the people inside it. (House all-sides law.)

## Premise (renamed, modernized, original)

**Matric class of 1996** — the first democratic cohort — at Weylandt House, an elite girls' boarding
school in the Cape winelands suddenly required to be what its prospectus always claimed. Four friends
from four South Africas: **Thandi Mokoena**, scholarship girl from Katlehong; **Elsabé "Els" Roux**,
dominee's daughter who wants to write; **Priya Naidoo**, Durban hotel dynasty, sent to be finished;
**Marguerite "Mags" de Villiers**, old-money wine estate, the heir with nothing to inherit but debt.

In their final year one of them is pregnant. The four swear a pact: the school never finds out, the
family never finds out, the father — a golden student politician with a future the whole country has
already assigned to him — never finds out. The baby is placed through **Mrs. April**, a private
"placement aunty" who takes a fee and promises a good home, and the four pool money into an envelope
every month for a child they are never allowed to visit. In 2004 the envelopes start coming back
unopened. Mrs. April is dead. The trail is cold. Each of the four privately decides the others are
handling it.

**2026.** Lelethu Grace — foster child of Belhar, talent-show survivor, now the most famous woman the
continent has produced — books the Marchmont Hotel's royal suite in Cape Town and sends four
invitations. She has the DNA result in her pocket. She has had it for a year. The summit is not a
question. It is a reckoning — and, before it ends, the father's name will surface into a country that
has built a statue of him.

## The structural engine (what makes it *this* book)

**Openwork made structural.** Lace is a fabric made mostly of holes — the pattern *is* the absence,
held by thread. Two timelines interlace: the school years and working decades (1994–2004) and the six
weeks around the summit (2026). Every 2026 chapter circles a hole — a thing the four never told each
other — and every past chapter, arriving after, fills it in for the reader alone. **The reader always
holds one more truth than any woman in the room.** That gap is the dramatic-irony engine; the source's
central mystery converted from a reveal into a shape.
