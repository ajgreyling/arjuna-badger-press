# CANON_CHOICES — *Lacework* (LOCKED)

> The decisions the whole book leans on. Change these only by conscious amendment, never by drift.

## CC-01 — The mother is Mags.

**Marguerite de Villiers** is Lelethu's biological mother. Locked reasons: (a) the old-money Afrikaans
estate girl bearing a mixed-race child in 1996 makes the concealment *historically load-bearing*, not
merely social — the pact forms in the exact year the country is renegotiating what her family name
means; (b) it inverts the reader's lazy guess (the scholarship girl); (c) it makes the 2026 estate
rescue arc retroactively devastating — Mags spent thirty years saving the land instead of finding the
child, and knows it.

## CC-02 — The father is Kagiso Marumo.

Student leader at Stellenbosch in 1996, twenty-one, magnetic, already being groomed as the face of the
new country. In 2026 he is a continental statesman-philanthropist with a foundation, an airport
lounge named for him, and a statue. **He never knew.** The pact protected him *unasked* — that is the
four's real sin against Lele: they decided a man's myth outweighed her name. The book indicts the
machinery of myth-making, not Marumo, who is given his own honest reckoning scene.

## CC-03 — Lele has known for a year.

The DNA match (a genealogy-site cousin cluster, then a discreet lab confirmation) landed twelve months
before the summit. She spent the year having the four *investigated* — the paper trail of the
envelopes, Mrs. April's estate file, the school's fire-damaged records. The summit is an inquest
staged by someone who already holds the answers and wants to watch which of them lies first. Her arc
in Part III is discovering she wants something other than the punishment she planned.

## CC-04 — Who decided (the culpability ledger)

- **The pact itself:** proposed by **Thandi** — the girl with the most to lose from scandal by
  association and the sharpest grasp of how the world actually works. She has carried the authorship
  of it, silently, all her life.
- **The placement:** arranged by **Priya**, whose family network produced Mrs. April; she vouched, and
  the vouching failed.
- **The stopping:** **Els** was the last to post an envelope and the first to learn Mrs. April was
  dead — and told no one, because by 2004 telling the others meant admitting the story she had chosen
  not to publish (a placement-economy exposé that would have found Lele) — see PP-07.
- **The silence:** **Mags** never once tried to find her. This is the hole in the centre of the book,
  and its answer — shame metastasised into paralysis, the belief that the child was better off without
  the name — is spoken only in the final session.

## CC-05 — Retirements (hard)

- No maternity whodunit anywhere in the text. The reader may guess early; Part III confirms without
  theatre.
- No paraphrase of Conran's summons line, ever.
- No villainised individuals in the placement chain: Mrs. April was competent and kind and *died*;
  the system had no successor protocol. Machine, not people.

## CC-06 — The ending

No prosecution, no press conference. Lele takes the estate's guest cottage for a summer — not
forgiveness, *proximity on her terms*. The four speak the pact aloud in full, together, once. Marumo
is told privately, by Lele, alone; what he does is left off the page. Last image: five women, one
table, the pattern finally showing all its holes and holding anyway.
