# SEED_STORY — *The Openwork*

## One sentence

Thirty years after four schoolgirls swore a pact to hide a pregnancy and share the child, the child —
now the most famous woman on the continent, with the DNA result already in her pocket — summons the
four to a Cape Town hotel suite to ask the only question a laboratory can't answer: *which of you
decided?*

## The structural spine

- **Part I — THE PATTERN (1994–1996).** Four girls from four South Africas at Weylandt House; the
  bond; the pregnancy; the pact; the birth; the placement. Interlaced with 2026 chapters: the
  invitations land in four working lives, one per chapter, each refusal-then-acceptance shaped by what
  the woman has to lose.
- **Part II — THE HOLES (1997–2004 / 2026).** The working decades: four careers built in procedural
  detail, the envelope posted monthly, the marriages and losses — and the 2004 catastrophe: Mrs. April
  dead, envelopes returned, four women each silently certain another is handling it. Interlaced: Lele's
  own decade, foster system to talent machine to fame, told as *work*. The 2026 strand walks the four
  toward the Marchmont.
- **Part III — THE THREAD (2026, six days).** The summit. Lele reveals she has known her mother's name
  for a year; what she runs is not a reveal but an inquest — who decided, who paid, who stopped. The
  father's name surfaces into a country that built a statue of him. The pact is finally spoken aloud
  by all four at once, for the first time ever — and the fifth chair is filled.

## The title

Openwork: the lace-maker's term for fabric patterned by its holes. The four women's story is held
together by everything they never told each other. The pattern *is* the absence, carried by thread.
