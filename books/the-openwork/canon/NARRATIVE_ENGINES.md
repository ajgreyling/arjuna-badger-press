# NARRATIVE_ENGINES — *The Openwork*

## 1. The interlace (the irony engine)

Two timelines braided: 1994–2004 and the six weeks of 2026. Every 2026 chapter opens a **hole** — a
thing the four never told each other — and a later past-chapter closes it *for the reader only*. The
reader therefore always holds one more truth than any woman in the room, and the summit plays as
tragedy watched from above rather than mystery solved from below. This is the source's central
mystery converted from a reveal into a shape (ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE).

## 2. The envelope clock

Part II's metronome: the monthly posting, whose mechanics never vary — until they do. Seven years,
eighty-four envelopes, the ritual carrying the pact's whole emotional load precisely because it is
the only form the four's love for the child is permitted to take. The clock stops in ch-form at the
2004 return-to-sender; the silence after it is the loudest beat in the book.

## 3. The inquest engine (Part III)

Six days, five sessions, run by Lele on the structure of the legal deposition she has sat through too
many of. She holds all the answers and asks anyway — the engine is not *what happened* but *watching
each woman choose, in real time, between the lie that protects her and the truth that costs her.*
Each session is keyed to one of the four; the fifth session is Lele's own, unplanned, when the
inquest turns on its convenor.

## 4. The vise

Both truths, never resolved: the pact was an act of love executed with total competence by
sixteen-year-olds, **and** it was the theft of a woman's name from her for thirty years. Every
engine above must keep both jaws engaged; any chapter where one truth wins outright has failed.
