# CHARACTERS — *The Openwork*

> Original cast. Each carries a *function* from the source engine; none shares a life, scene, or line
> with Conran's people. Voice laws at the foot of each entry.

## The four

### Thandi Mokoena (b. 1978, Katlehong) — function: the self-made media builder
Scholarship girl, arrives at Weylandt House 1994 with one blazer and a complete map of how power
actually moves. Freelance TV researcher → current-affairs producer → founds **Sizwe Pictures**
(production house, Joburg) → by 2026 the continent's biggest independent factual studio; she has
turned down Lele's people twice without knowing why the name pricked her. **Authored the pact.**
*Voice: short declaratives, numbers remembered exactly, jokes delivered deadpan as facts. Never
explains herself twice.*

### Elsabé "Els" Roux (b. 1978, Bredasdorp) — function: the writer who pays for the truth
Dominee's daughter. Court reporter (Cape Argus years) → investigations desk → editor of an
independent weekly by 2019, circling extinction. In 2003 she had the placement-economy exposé that
would have found Lele — and spiked her own story (PP-07). Last to post an envelope, first to know
Mrs. April was dead, told no one. *Voice: precise, self-deprecating, Afrikaans cadence under English;
quotes her father's sermons only when she's lying to herself.*

### Priya Naidoo (b. 1979, Durban) — function: the trade inherited and transformed
Fourth-generation hotel family, sent to Weylandt to be polished and married. Instead: hotel school in
Lausanne on her own application, fifteen years working other people's front desks, then back to take
the family's tired flagship and build the **Marchmont, Cape Town** — where the summit happens, in a
suite she comped before she knew who was asking. Vouched for Mrs. April. *Voice: hospitality-smooth
until it snaps into Durban directness; measures people by how they treat staff.*

### Marguerite "Mags" de Villiers (b. 1978, Simondium) — function: the aristocrat who saves the land herself
Heir to **Weylandt's Rust** (the estate the school leases its land from — the school's name is her
family's), which is to say heir to debt. **Lele's mother** (CC-01). Thirty years of the unglamorous
rescue: replanting, bank meetings, selling the Strandveld parcel, the first bottling under her own
label 2011. Never searched for her daughter. *Voice: fewest words of the four; farm metaphors used
accurately; long silences the others have learned to read wrong.*

## The fifth

### Lelethu "Lele" Grace (b. Dec 1996, Worcester) — function: the lost child, sovereign
Named by a foster mother in Belhar; six placements by twelve; a talent-show second place at sixteen
that the machine monetised harder than any win. By 2026: two continental tours, one film, a beauty
line she actually controls, and a legal team that exists because of what the industry did to her at
nineteen. Has known her mother's name for a year (CC-03). Runs the summit as an inquest and discovers
mid-way she wants proximity more than punishment. *Voice: performs warmth flawlessly and switches it
off audibly; asks questions she knows the answers to; Belhar Afrikaans surfacing when she stops
performing.*

## The men and the machine

- **Kagiso Marumo** (b. 1975) — the protected father (CC-02). Statesman, foundation, statue. Never
  knew. Gets one honest scene and no villain edit.
- **Mrs. Ivy April** (1941–2004) — Wynberg "placement aunty," competent and kind; took a fee, kept a
  ledger, died without a successor. The system's hole, not its villain.
- **Hendrik de Villiers** (1946–2009) — Mags's father; the debt, the name, the reason shame won.
- **Sisanda Grace** (b. 1958) — the Belhar foster mother who named Lele and is the only person in the
  book who never once failed her; alive, present at the ending, the moral counterweight.

## Voice law (binding)

No character is a transcription target: if a line could be lifted into *Lace* and fit a Conran
character, it fails review. Functions rhyme; people don't.
