# WORLD — *The Openwork*

## Weylandt House (1994–1996 strand)

Elite girls' boarding school, Cape winelands, on land leased from the de Villiers estate
(**Weylandt's Rust** — the school bears the family's old farm name). Founded 1923; in 1994 it is an
institution whose prospectus promised a South Africa it never practised, suddenly required to become
it. The matric class of 1996 is the first intake actually to mix the country's four worlds in one
dormitory. The school's own scramble — new anthem, old staff, a board that panics in both directions —
is background weather in Part I, never a lecture.

Key rooms: the chapel loft (the oath); the san with the matron who chooses not to see; the fire of
July 1999 that later conveniently thins the records Lele's investigators need.

## The placement economy (the machine indicted)

Between the formal adoption system's waiting lists and the family's need for silence sits the
informal layer: church networks, "aunties" with reputations, fees called donations, ledgers kept in
exercise books. **Mrs. Ivy April, Wynberg** was one of its competent, kind operators — perhaps two
hundred placements over thirty years, most of them well. The machine's flaw is structural: no
succession, no records escrow, no oversight, so one death (2004, aortic, sudden) orphans every open
file at once. Lele's placement failed not through malice but through a foster placement that
dissolved in 1999, re-entry into the state system under the foster mother's surname, and the paper
trail's three broken links. The book indicts the architecture. Every person inside it is drawn whole.

## The envelope (1997–2004)

R400 a month at the start, adjusted upward yearly — four contributions to a numbered savings book
Mrs. April administered, posted physically because a paper trail with names was the one thing the
pact could not allow. The envelope's mechanics (who collects, who posts, the code phrase on the slip)
are the pact's liturgy and Part II's clock. Total paid over seven years: R61,200. Amount that ever
reached Lele after 1999: R0. The reconciliation of that ledger is a Part III set piece.

## The four industries (the work on the page)

- **Television / production** (Thandi): 1990s SABC freelance economy → indie factual boom →
  streaming-era commissioning; the invoice-to-broadcast pipeline shown truly.
- **Print journalism** (Els): court reporting mechanics, the investigations desk economics that kill
  stories, the 2010s collapse, the survival weekly.
- **Hotels** (Priya): front-desk craft, the family-flagship politics, the rebuild of the Marchmont —
  rooms division arithmetic, not glamour.
- **Wine** (Mags): replanting cycles, the bank, the co-op exit, a first own-label bottling; the
  estate rescue in real agricultural time.
- Plus **the talent machine** (Lele's strand): talent-show contracts, 360 deals, the recovery of her
  own masters at twenty-six — her career told with the same procedural respect as the four's.

## 2026 strand

Cape Town, six weeks, late summer. The Marchmont Hotel (Priya's), the royal suite floor. Real city,
real logistics: load-shedding exemptions, the security detail a person of Lele's fame actually needs,
the tabloid stakeout that forces the summit's final session to the estate at Simondium — where the
book began.
