# SOURCE_NOTES — *Lacework*

> **SSOT for fidelity.** What the source *is*, what we keep, what we retire, and the **arc ledger**:
> every load-bearing arc of the original, listed, with the transposition that carries it. If a beat is
> not in the source's *engine*, it is ours to invent; if it is in the engine, our version must
> **rhyme**, never transcribe.

## The source

Shirley Conran, ***Lace*** — novel, Simon & Schuster, 1982. A four-decade saga of four women who meet
at a Swiss finishing school in the late 1940s, the illegitimate child one of them bears and the four
jointly hide and support, and the grown child — film star Lili — who summons the four to ask which of
them is her mother. Famous for taking women's *work* seriously at blockbuster scale, and for its
unembarrassed treatment of women's sexuality and survival.

**We do not own this and are not licensed.** Conran died in 2024; the rights are the estate's and her
publishers'. See [`ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md`](ADAPTATION_DOCTRINE.md).

**The 1984 miniseries is a different work and is not our source.** Its compressions and its camp are
its own. We adapt the *novel's* engine: the working lives and the pact.

## The engine we keep (ideas, not expressions — Conran's gift to the form)

| Engine element | One-line statement | Our transposition lives in |
|---|---|---|
| **The pact** | four girls close ranks around one girl's crisis and it binds their whole lives | PLOT I, CANON_CHOICES |
| **The shared child** | all four pay, none may visit; the child belongs to the pact, not a person | WORLD (the envelope), PLOT |
| **The intermediary's failure** | the paid go-between dies/fails and the child is lost into the machine | WORLD (the placement economy) |
| **The working lives** | decades of real labour on the page — trade, invoice, negotiation, survival | every chapter; STYLE_GUIDE |
| **The summons** | the lost child, now powerful, convenes the four | PLOT III (the Marchmont summit) |
| **The protected father** | the father's public standing is the reason the secret armed itself | CHARACTERS (Kagiso Marumo) |
| **The friendship that survives** | the pact damages all four and still holds; the book ends on the four, not the reveal | PLOT III coda |

## What we retire (and why)

| Source element | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **The maternity whodunit** | **RETIRED** | DNA testing killed it. Lele arrives knowing. The mystery becomes culpability: *which of you decided.* (MYTHOS hard line.) |
| **The Swiss finishing school** | replaced | Weylandt House, Cape winelands, matric class of 1996 — the first democratic cohort; the school's own reinvention is a subplot with real teeth. |
| **The prince / Middle East strand** | replaced | Kagiso Marumo — student politician then, continental statesman now; the secret protects a national myth, not a throne. |
| **The 1980s publishing/film milieus** | replaced | SA television and tabloids, hotel trade, wine trade, and the streaming-era talent industry. |
| **Conran's scenes, dialogue, and the famous line** | never used | Her expression, her IP. Ours are built from our world. |

## Fidelity tests

1. If a chapter can only be described as "the part where the *Lace* thing happens," it has failed.
2. A reader who never heard of *Lace* must be able to describe every scene without reference to it.
3. The work test (this book's own): every chapter must contain at least one thing a woman *does for a
   living*, shown truly enough to be used as instructions.
