## 0. THE PREMISE — one true story, four frightened children

South Africa, **1837–1838.** The same events — the Great Trek into the interior, **Piet Retief's
embassy to Dingane and his murder (Feb 1838)**, the killings at Weenen, the months of fear, and the
**Battle of Blood River / Slag van Bloedrivier (16 Dec 1838)** — told from **four 17-to-18-year-old
points of view**, plus a **narrated factual frame.**

- **The protagonist (POV 1): a Voortrekker young woman**, ~17. The Vrouemonument given a *pulse*.
- **POV 2: a young Zulu man**, ~17–18 — one of Dingane's *amabutho*, conditioned from boyhood.
- **POV 3: a young British soldier/settler**, ~17–18, at the Cape / Port Natal edge of empire.
- **POV 4: the narrated frame** — the documented history, the wide shot the children can't see.

**The thesis, in the author's own words:** *"Fuck the Crown — but that young troop was brave and scared."*
Separate the **institution** (villain: empire, conquest, the fear-driven adult machinery on every side)
from the **human the system used** (every child brave and scared, hardened by a machine they didn't
build). The villain is not any of the three teenagers. **The villain is the thing that turns frightened
children into enemies — fear itself, weaponised by power.** The book teaches a reader to aim their anger
at *systems* and their mercy at *people.*

**The spine (the universal):** *scared of change and the unknowns.* Three teenagers coming of age into a
world that is ending and beginning at once. The historical convulsion **is** the adolescence. This is a
**coming-of-age novel** first, a historical novel second.

## 1. THE FOUR — same age, three worlds, three machines

Each child was **forged by a system** — and the rhyme is deliberate:

- **The Voortrekker girl** — forged by **necessity.** On the trek there is no luxury of gendered roles:
  she cooks, cleans, sews — *and* rides and shoots with the best of the men, because a lion or an impi
  can come at dawn. **"Poor shots went hungry."** Competence is not a virtue here; it is *eating*. She is
  the one nobody reads correctly — the Zulu warrior and the British boy both underestimate her, and both
  are wrong, which makes her the perfect eyes for a book about *misreading the other.*
  - **CRAFT LAW (binding): NOT a girlboss cardboard cutout.** Competent AND terrified AND tender AND
    fierce AND devout AND grieving — the *whole* woman the monument was built for. Her strength means
    more *because* she is afraid. "Poor shots went hungry" — and she still weeps when she has to shoot
    the horse. Feeling deeply is not the opposite of strength; it is where strength gets its weight.
- **The Zulu youth** — forged by the **impi.** One of those conditioned to **run barefoot over
  thornfields until the soles turned to hide** (flinchers culled; the making of a weapon at a boy's
  age). The thorns are *his* "poor shots went hungry." The book's question on his side: **did they make
  his *heart* hard too, or is the scared boy still in there under the leather?**
- **The British boy** — forged by **empire.** Likely a poor English/Scottish/Irish kid himself *used* by
  the Crown — shipped to the bottom of Africa for a flag he barely understands. Humanised by being, like
  the others, the powerless instrument of a power. He carries (for the reader who knows where it goes)
  the cold shadow of the **concentration camps sixty years on** — the same empire, the boys who became
  the men who built the wire. Held human; the machine indicted.

## 2. THE GATE — the two voices that are NOT the author's to write alone (BINDING)

The book's entire moral claim is *all four humanised, all four true.* That claim **collapses** if one
side is the author's *imagining* of the other (the colonial move — speaking *for* the other).

- **The Voortrekker girl** = written from inside the author's own Afrikaner heritage. His to write.
- **The Zulu youth** = **provisional / pending Zulu sensitivity read by a named colleague** (committed,
  2026-06-21). His interiority — the thorns, the culling, what it *felt* like to be made a weapon at
  twelve, his pride/fear/belonging/cost — is rendered *with* that read, never claimed solo. The more
  vivid + specific the Zulu material, the MORE it needs that hand (specificity is where outsider-writing
  goes wrong). **Any Zulu-POV prose drafted by the engine is DRAFT-PROVISIONAL, not canon, until the
  read clears it.** Ideal: the colleague co-*creates*, not only checks-after.
- **The British boy** = **provisional / pending real research** into the lived experience of a young
  imperial soldier (class, conscription/poverty, why a poor kid wore the red coat). Drafted-provisional
  until grounded.

**Framing law (binding):** relentlessly humanising-ALL, NEVER "our-history-was-erased" grievance. The
same sentence ("our history is vanishing") is the far-right's cry; the multi-POV **is** the firewall
against the author's own heritage's worst current. Lean into it: by humanising the Zulu and British
boys equally, the book is impossible to weaponise.

**Facts law (binding):** every contested event told as **honest divergence** — *here is what the record
says; here is how each side has read it; here are frightened children inside it.* Retief's murder is
treachery from one side and a king defending his land from invaders who already showed bad faith from
the other — **both readings have factual support, and that duality IS the point.** Stay on the
documented frame; render the divergence, never a verdict.

---

## 3. HOUSE VOICE & TONE

Grounded, sensory, *Sawubona* attention to land and people. The land is a character (the veld, the
Drakensberg, the Ncome/Blood River, the laager, the kraal). Unsentimental about violence — render its
cost, never its glory. The set-piece (Bloedrivier) is **horror from inside**, not triumph: a river that
ran red, frightened boys dying in numbers, and a girl behind a wagon-wheel who will never be the same.
Theme is never spoken; it is *embodied.* Machine-tells banned; lean.

---

## 4. WHERE IT FEEDS

This book is **curriculum-bound**: it is exactly what the [[congosky]] **VO Klass** school should teach
SA history *from* — Bloedrivier rendered so a Zulu child and an Afrikaans child can learn it **in the
same book**, each seeing the other as a scared teenager rather than a monster. Audio-first, home
languages, the both-sides-humanised history nobody has told. A small act of healing for a country still
raw with all of it.
