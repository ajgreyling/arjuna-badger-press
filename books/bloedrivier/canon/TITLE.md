# *BLOEDRIVIER* — Title Workshop

> STORY_BIBLE binding: the working title **"Bloedrivier" is WRONG** — it telegraphs Boer triumph /
> one-side victory, which contradicts the all-sides thesis. Final title must hold the **all-sides
> truth** (fear / the unknown / coming of age / the year the ground tilted), never a battle cry, and
> must NOT be weaponisable as "our-history-was-erased" grievance. Tagline register:
> *three frightened children, one ground that ran red / brave and scared.*

## The brief, distilled

A title that says: **three teenagers, three machines, one year the world ended and began** — fear,
not glory; people, not flags. It must survive being read by a Zulu reader, an Afrikaans reader, and
an English reader and feel like *their* book, equally. Bonus if it works in a VO-Klass classroom and
on a stark relics-cover (COVER.md).

## Shortlist (with the reasoning, strongest first)

### ★ 1. **The Year the Ground Tilted**
*Recommended.* Takes the bible's own phrase. Names the *convulsion* (1837–38) without naming a
winner — the ground tilts under *all three* children, none of them chose it. "Ground" carries the
soil-as-character spine and the river-ran-red without saying "blood." Coming-of-age and historical
cataclysm in one image. Classroom-safe in three languages; impossible to weaponise.
- Tagline: *Three frightened children. One year. The ground that ran red.*

### 2. **Brave and Scared**
Straight from the author's own thesis line ("that young troop was brave and scared"). Tiny, human,
disarming — the whole moral claim in three words, applied equally to all three. Risk: maybe *too*
plain / could read YA-soft; but plainness is also its power, and it sits beautifully under a stark
cover.
- Tagline: *Three children. Three machines. One river.*

### 3. **The Ground Between** *(or* **The Ground Between Us***)*
The neutral soil all three stand on and fight over; "between" = the space the book lives in (the
crossing, the mercy-for-the-enemy beat at its heart). Quiet, dignified, no triumph. Slight risk of
genericness.
- Tagline: *Brave and scared, all of them, on one stretch of red earth.*

### 4. **What the River Took** *(or* **The River Took Us All***)*
Holds the Ncome/Blood River as *loss*, not victory — the river took boys from every side. Grief, not
glory. Risk: still foregrounds the river/battle; weaker on the coming-of-age spine; "Blood River"
adjacency could still read as the battle-book.

### 5. **The Unknowing Year** / **All the Frightened Children**
Honourable mentions. The first names the fear-of-the-unknown spine but is a touch abstract; the
second is on-thesis but a little on-the-nose / sentimental.

## Subtitle / series lockup (works with any title above)

- Series strip (cover top): **HISTORY LIKE YOU'VE NEVER HEARD IT**
- Optional subtitle for catalog/metadata: *A novel of Blood River, 1838 — from all sides.*
  (Catalog only — lets a reader find it by the event; the *title* itself never says "Blood River.")

## Recommendation

**Lead with #1 — *The Year the Ground Tilted*** — series-strip above, *A novel of Blood River, 1838*
as the catalog subtitle so discoverability isn't lost. It's the bible's own language, holds all
sides, carries the soil spine, and cannot be turned into a grievance flag. Keep **Brave and Scared**
as the strong alternate (and, either way, *brave and scared* stays the tagline DNA).
