## Concept — the three headpieces

Not faces. **What each child's world made them wear.** Three headpieces, three machines that forged
three frightened teenagers — the cover humanises without portraiture, and sidesteps the diorama/
caricature trap entirely. Show the dignity through the object, not the pose.

1. **Voortrekker girl (the protagonist) — a *kappie*** (the deep Voortrekker bonnet). Worn, dust on it,
   practical — domesticity *and* the frontier. Hers is the one the eye lands on first.
2. **Zulu youth — a *leopard-skin head-band*** (the *amabutho* warrior band — status, the boy made a
   weapon).
3. **British troop — a *pith helmet*** (the colonial sun helmet — the empire's stamp on a poor boy's head).

## Mood — STARK + POWERFUL

High-contrast, bold, museum-still. The three headpieces against deep darkness, lit hard — like
artifacts laid out, or relics recovered from a battlefield. Not a war-action cover (no figures, no
fighting, no glory); the power is in the *stillness and the weight*. A whisper of the Ncome/Blood River
— a thin dark-red gleam at the base, or the suggestion of red earth/water — present but restrained,
never lurid. The emotional note underneath the starkness: *three kids, three machines, and the ground
that ran red.* Empty, charged space top-third + bottom-quarter for type.
