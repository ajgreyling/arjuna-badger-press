# People's Plants — how Codex Medica relates to it

> **Cite. Do not copy.** This note is binding for every session that expands the Southern
> African atlas.

## The book on the desk

**van Wyk, Ben-Erik & Gericke, Nigel.** *People's Plants: A Guide to Useful Plants of Southern
Africa.* Briza Publications. Expanded 2nd edition (ISBN 9781920217716). Photographic ethnobotanical
guide; traditional and contemporary uses of more than ~700 plants across **20 chapters** in three
parts (Foods & drinks · Health & beauty · Skills & crafts).

The steward owns a physical copy. A private shelf photograph lives at
`design/reference/not-for-publication/` and **must never** enter EPUB, PDF, site, or the image
compendium. Cover design and book contents are copyright.

## What we may take from it

| Allowed | Forbidden |
| --- | --- |
| Bibliographic citation as secondary literature | OCR, photocopy, or typed transcription of entries |
| The publicly stated 20-chapter use taxonomy (marketing/catalogue) as an atlas *agenda* | Lifting species lists, anecdotes, dose notes, or photo plates from the book |
| Using it to *check* whether an open-source taxon belongs in a use class | Treating a People's Plants page as a Codex use report |
| Contacting authors/publisher about formal partnership later | Publishing their photographs or cover |

## How a People's Plants mention becomes a Codex record

1. Taxon identity must be resolvable against an open taxonomic backbone (POWO / SANBI / IPNI).
2. At least one **independent** public use report or published paper must exist (or a living
   custodian under consent). Ten websites quoting van Wyk & Gericke = **one lineage**, not ten
   (Book VI independence test).
3. Preparation / dose detail from the book stays out. Non-clinical notice still binds.
4. Mind & mood, women's reproductive, intoxicant, and high-toxicity taxa default to
   **caution-metadata** or **hold** until safety + consent review (Book VIII / XV exclusions).
