# Chapter 8

The compile finished at 01:25 and nobody looked at it until the following afternoon.

Aviva was at a family thing in Somerset West that she could not get out of and did not particularly want to; Thandi had driven to a funding meeting in town and come back with a headache and gone home at three. The instance, having nothing else in the queue, ran its own housekeeping and idled.

The output was five lines long.

```
JOB 2036-04-12-0011  compile/typecheck [HARD SCOPE: no evaluation]
strings ................ 31,509 well-formed
type-check passed ...... 31,509
unbound arguments ...... 0
free variables ......... 0
status ................. COMPILES
```

Aviva read it at 15:40 on the Monday and her first reaction was that it was wrong.

That was, she maintained afterwards, the correct reaction, and she never apologised for it. Zero unbound arguments in thirty-one thousand strings written by hand over eighteen years is not a result, it is a symptom. Real hand-produced operational material has gaps. People forget parameters. People write down half a thing meaning to finish it later and then do not. She had seen the working notebooks of two Fields medallists and both were full of things that did not close.

Zero was the number you got when your pipeline had quietly discarded everything that failed.

So she spent nine days trying to break it. It is the only part of the story she was ever proud of.

She re-ran the parse with the tear-damaged sheets included and unrepaired, and the count went up by twenty-nine ill-formed strings, exactly as it should, and the other thirty-one thousand five hundred and nine still type-checked. She deliberately corrupted four hundred strings at random and confirmed that the checker rejected three hundred and ninety-one of them and, correctly on inspection, accepted nine, because the corruption had by chance produced another legal string. She took the two genuinely ill-formed originals and looked at the physical sheets under magnification and found that one had been written over a fold and the other had a full stop that was a fly.

She wrote all of this up. It ran to fourteen pages and it is the reason the Institute’s account of the affair survived contact with the outside world at all.

On the twenty-first she took it to Thandi.

“It compiles.”

“I read that nine days ago.”

“You read a line that said it compiles. I’m telling you it compiles, which is a different sentence, because I’ve spent nine days trying to make it not.”

Thandi read the fourteen pages properly, which took her forty minutes, and Aviva sat and looked out of the window at the wind for the whole forty minutes and did not once check her phone.

“All right,” Thandi said. “What is it, then?”

“I don’t know.”

“Guess.”

“No.”

“Aviva.”

“No, genuinely, no. That’s not modesty, it’s the whole of my professional training. I have a well-formed closed operational grammar with no free variables and no idea what its terminals denote, and the number of things it could be is not small, it’s unbounded. It compiles like a recipe. It compiles like a knitting pattern.”

“A knitting pattern with two hundred and fourteen terminals.”

“A complicated jumper,” said Aviva.

Thandi didn’t laugh, but her mouth moved.

“Here’s where I am,” she said. “We have gone as far as I can defend. Everything to this point has been a physical question about marks on paper and I would stand up in front of anybody and say so. The next question in the sequence is *what does it do*, and that is not a physical question about marks on paper, and there is no version of it I can authorise, because the answer requires the machine to interpret a dead man’s private notation without his consent, and that is precisely and exactly the thing we exist not to do.”

“I know.”

“Do you agree?”

Aviva took a while.

“Yes,” she said. “I agree. And I want to know, and I’d do it tonight if it were my building, and you’re right and I’m wrong.”

“That’s the most useful thing anyone’s said to me this year.”

So they stopped.

It is worth saying plainly, because of everything that came afterwards and everything that was written about the Institute in the eighteen months that followed: *they stopped*. On the twenty-first of April 2036, having established that a dead American’s marginal notation was a well-formed operational grammar that type-checked without a single free variable, the two people in a position to take the next step looked at the protocol, agreed that it forbade them, and put it down.

Aviva wrote the description note. It was four hundred words, it used the phrase *closed operational grammar, purpose undetermined*, and it was, by the standards of the profession, an extremely good note. She uploaded it to the catalogue on the Thursday.

Then she went back to the Wolraad backlog, because there were thirty-eight other boxes and the money had to be spent inside eighteen months, and she worked on shipping manifests from Walvis Bay for the rest of the week and quite enjoyed it.

Nobody in the building thought about Ted Owens again for four days.
