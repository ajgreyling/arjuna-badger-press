# Chapter 38

Thandi flew to Philadelphia in November and hired a car and drove to Lancaster County, because Constance had asked to be told before anybody else and Thandi was not going to do it on a telephone.

The house was small and extremely tidy and there was a photograph on a shelf in the front room of a man laughing with his head back, which Thandi looked at twice and did not ask about.

It took two hours.

She did it in order, with the documents, and she did not editorialise, and when she got to draft one and the burn residue she put the two scans side by side on the coffee table and let Constance look at them for as long as she wanted, which was about four minutes.

Then she did sheets two to eight, and Marius's eleven pages, and the delay curve with its three lumps, and the recipient rule, and what it meant to move a receiver, and the only lever available in a rented room in 1985.

Then she said the date.

Constance Owens-Hale sat with her hands in her lap and did not move for a while.

"Say the last part again."

"The original execution timestamp on segment four was the twenty-eighth of December 1987," said Thandi. "It was overwritten, in his hand, in the same notation, and sheets two to eight are the derivation. He finished the arithmetic on the eleventh of November and posted it on the twelfth and died on the twenty-eighth."

"And the letter in 1985."

"Sheets seven and eight are him working out what would push the window. The only variable he could reach was whether he would ever be taken seriously again. He wrote eleven drafts. The first one is four pages and it's the best thing he ever wrote and he burned it in a sink."

"And the mansion."

"The mansion is the instrument," said Thandi. "The nine hundred thousand is the instrument. The whole letter is the instrument. He made himself impossible on purpose, and it worked, and it cost him everything he had left, and it is the only reason anybody has ever heard of him."

Constance got up and went and stood by the window with her back to the room, which is what her father did in a kitchen in 1972 and which Thandi did not know and would not have said if she had.

"Do you believe it?" she said.

"I believe every document I've shown you is what I say it is. I believe the dates. I've stopped trying to have a view about the rest and I'd be lying if I said that was a principled position rather than exhaustion."

"That's an honest answer and I'd like you to know I noticed."

She stayed at the window a long time.

"You've made him sane," she said.

"Mrs Hale—"

"No. Let me." She did not turn round. "Do you understand what you've brought me? You have brought me a document in which my father, who sat at a kitchen table for four months while my mother went to work in a laundry, and who did a thing to an airport in 1977 that I read about in your basement and have not slept properly since — you have brought me a document in which that man was *doing arithmetic*. In which every one of it was on purpose. In which he chose it."

"Yes."

"For fifty-two years I have had one sentence," said Constance. "One. *He was ill.* It is the only thing that has ever held both halves of him, and I built it when I was fourteen years old and I have carried it up a hill every day since, and it works. It lets me love him and it lets me not forgive him and it does not require either of those to win."

She turned round.

"And you've come four thousand miles to take it off me and replace it with a hero."

"That isn't what I—"

"I know it isn't what you meant. It's what it is." She sat down again, entirely composed, and put her hands back in her lap. "Ms Mokoena, I am seventy years old. I am not going to spend what I've got left believing that my father was a man who saved the world from a rooming house in Philadelphia while I learned to be quiet in the kitchen. I would rather he was ill. Do you understand that? Not *I think he was ill* — I would rather. Those are different and I know they are different and I have known it for about eleven minutes."

Thandi said nothing at all.

"So no," said Constance. "I don't believe it. I am going to go on not believing it, deliberately, for the rest of my life, and I want that recorded in your file next to everything else, in my words, because I have watched eight people put words in his mouth and I am not having anybody put one in mine."

"I'll record it exactly."

"Thank you." She smoothed her skirt. "Now. You've come a long way and I've been unkind, and neither of those is your fault. There's a Woolworth's cake in the kitchen because I didn't know what else to do with myself this morning. Would you like some?"

They had cake for forty minutes and talked about the flight, and about Pieter, whom Constance had liked, and about a hospital administration system that had gone wrong in 2019 in a way that Thandi found genuinely funny.

At the door Constance said: "The young man in 1983."

"Yes."

"He wrote and said he'd been told in advance and was right, and my father never answered him again."

"Yes."

"That's the only part I'll think about," she said, and shut the door.
