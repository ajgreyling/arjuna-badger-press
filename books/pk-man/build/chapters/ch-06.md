# Chapter 6

The parse job ran on the twelfth of April and returned in eleven minutes, and Aviva was in a meeting about shelving.

```
JOB 2036-04-03-0006  parse/wellformedness [HARD SCOPE: no semantics]
strings extracted ......... 11,902 sheets → 31,540 strings
parsed .................... 31,540
well-formed ............... 31,509  (99.90%)
ill-formed ................ 31
  of which truncated at
  page edge / tear ........ 29
  of which genuine .......... 2

grammar recovered:
  type ................ context-free, 3 productions deep
  terminals ........... 214
  non-terminals ....... 19
  ambiguity ........... none detected

NOTE grammar is not natural-language-shaped
     (no recursion on the left, no optionality,
      fixed arity per production).
     it is OPERATIONAL in shape:
     each production takes arguments.
     scope prevents evaluation.
```

Marius Kotze, who did not work at the Institute and had never heard of it, was at that hour in a departmental meeting four hundred metres from a different mountain, being told that his funding line had been folded into somebody else's. Constance Owens-Hale was asleep in Pennsylvania. Thandi was in the laundry room with an electrician, discussing a fault that had been intermittent since January.

The output sat in the queue history for two hours and eleven minutes before anyone read it, and this is the fact that the subsequent inquiry found hardest to accept, and it is also the plainest evidence anybody ever produced that nothing in that building was being rushed.

Aviva came out of the shelving meeting at 14:30 and read it on the stairs.

She sat down on the stairs.

A grammar is a much larger claim than a notation. A notation is just a set of marks with habits. A grammar means the marks combine, and that the combinations have structure, and that the structure is regular enough that a machine given thirty-one thousand examples can recover the rules from the examples alone. Nineteen non-terminals. Three productions deep. No ambiguity anywhere in thirty-one thousand five hundred and nine strings.

She had spent twenty years describing the papers of people whose minds had come apart. She knew what those looked like on the page and she knew what they looked like in aggregate. They did not do this. Ambiguity was the thing that always gave them away: a private system built by one person under pressure always ends up with two rules that contradict, because the person is not a committee and does not keep minutes.

Thirty-one thousand five hundred and nine strings and no ambiguity.

And then the last note, which she read four times.

*It is operational in shape. Each production takes arguments.*

She went and found Thandi in the laundry room, standing on a chair with a torch.

"I need you to come and look at something."

"I'm holding a torch."

"I need you to come and look at something."

Thandi came down off the chair.

They read it together in the office with the door open, because Thandi had a rule about the door, and when they had both read it twice Thandi said: "Say the thing you're not saying."

"I'm not saying it because I don't want to be the person who says it."

"Say it anyway. I'll be the person who heard it."

Aviva put the tablet down. "It's not a language. It's not a diary code or a mystical alphabet or a filing system. Languages recurse and they're ambiguous and they have optional bits, and this doesn't and isn't and hasn't. This is shaped like something you *run*."

"It's shaped like a lot of things," said Thandi. "Chess is shaped like something you run. So is knitting. You said that yourself, three weeks ago, and you were right."

"I know."

"So say the honest version."

"The honest version is that there are perhaps forty classes of human activity that produce a closed operational grammar and thirty-nine of them are dull, and I still cannot tell you which of the forty this is, and neither can the instance, because we have not asked and we are not going to."

"Good," said Thandi.

"But I want to say the other thing too."

"Go on."

"He was mailing these," Aviva said. "That's the bit I can't put down. Whatever this is, it isn't private. A man does not invent a stock-control system for a hardware shop and then post pages of it to a professor of psychology in Wyoming and ask him to keep the envelope. The notation and the letters are in the same envelopes, Thandi. Physically the same envelopes. He was sending it to people."

Thandi turned that over for a while.

"That's a provenance observation," she said at last.

"It is."

"That's genuinely a provenance observation. I'm not being clever. Who a document was sent to is provenance."

"Yes."

"All right." Thandi picked up a pen and did not write anything with it. "Here is what I will authorise and it is the last thing I authorise on this lot without a great deal more thought. Evaluate the grammar for *well-formedness under its own rules* — that's already done — and then tell me whether the strings, taken as operations, are internally consistent. Do the arguments type-check. Not what they do. Whether they'd run at all if there were anything to run them on."

"That's a compile."

"That's a compile," Thandi agreed. "A compile is not an execution. There is nothing here to execute *on*. It is thirty-one thousand strings of ballpoint from the nineteen-seventies and the answer is going to be that half the arguments are missing, and then I will write a very good description note and you will shelve it and we will both feel silly."

"Probably."

"Say *probably* again, with your face doing something else."

"Probably," said Aviva.

The compile was submitted that afternoon. Because it was cheap and because the queue had emptied over the school holidays, it ran the same night, at 21:14, and took four hours and eleven minutes.
