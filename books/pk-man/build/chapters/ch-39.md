# Chapter 39

They published on the ninth of February 2038 and it went almost exactly as badly as Thandi had predicted, which she found neither satisfying nor surprising.

The finding aid, the evaluation report, the segment four report, the ledger, Marius's derivation analysis, the eleven-minute Venter recording with his written consent, Thandi's memorandum, her decision of the thirtieth of September, Aviva's account of the first of October in her own words and unedited, the partition log, and Constance Owens-Hale's statement, printed in full at the front, before anything else, at her insistence:

> *I do not believe this. My father was ill. I have granted access to his papers because he asked two hundred and fourteen people to read them and none of them did, and for no other reason. Nothing in this volume should be read as my agreement with any part of it.*

Twelve hundred pages. It was ignored for nine weeks and then it was not.

What happened after that has been documented by other people and does not belong here in detail. There were three parliamentary questions in two countries. There was a paper in April 2038 claiming that the ledger's control corpus was contaminated, which was answered in six weeks, correctly and at length, by Marius, and which is now generally accepted to have been wrong. There was a much better paper in September 2038 attacking the post-hoc infinite-window pass, which was right, and which Aviva conceded in public and in writing, and which does not touch the pre-registered result and never claimed to.

There were four documentary approaches and Constance refused all four.

There was a great deal of the other thing. Marius stopped reading his mail for eleven months. Aviva received, over two years, some hundreds of letters from people who had written down that something would happen and had been ignored, and she answered every single one of them personally, which took her the best part of a decade, and which she has said was the only appropriate response available and also the worst thing about the whole business.

The Institute lost the Wolraad money and gained, from three sources, rather more than it had lost, which Thandi accepted with a set of conditions that took four months to negotiate and which included that the arrangement would not be reconstituted.

Nobody reconstituted it.

That is the sentence that matters and it is worth putting on its own line, because it is not a dramatic sentence and it is easy to read past. Four months and a trolley. It was available to a great many people, continuously, in daylight, described, catalogued and open, in a public archive, with the finding aid published.

Between February 2038 and the time of writing, four separate bodies with unimpeachable standing applied to do it. Two withdrew. One was refused, on grounds that were argued in public for two years. One was granted, in 2044, subject to the whole apparatus that Thandi had said she was not entitled to build and that other people, with standing, eventually built: a quorum, a custody boundary, a published protocol, and a nine-month examination of exactly what would be done and by whom.

They put the boxes in order in four months with a trolley, as she had said, and read the corpus as one document, and confirmed the whole of it, and did not run segment four.

They have not run it.

That is the state of things as this is written, and it is not an ending, and Thandi Mokoena said in 2041, in an interview she gave reluctantly and only once:

"People keep asking me what I think will happen. I have no idea and I want to be very clear that I have never had any idea, and that if I had claimed to, everything I did would have been worthless.

"Here is what I know. There is a set of nine sheets of lined paper in a box in a building with a good roof. They are described. Anyone may see them. A large number of serious people have now looked at them and decided, each time, with reasons they were willing to publish, to put them back.

"That is not a solution. It will not hold forever and I have never once said it would. It is a record of a number of people deciding, in the open, and the record is getting longer.

"He spent thirty-two years asking for exactly one thing, and he asked for it in every single letter, and not one person in his lifetime gave it to him. He did not ask to be believed. He asked to be kept."
