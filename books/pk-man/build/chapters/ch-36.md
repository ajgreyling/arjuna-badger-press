# Chapter 36

The partition took nine days and was not difficult, which is the thing Thandi found hardest about it afterwards.

She announced it on the fifth of October, in the seminar room, and she had written it out because she did not trust herself to say it extempore.

“Nothing is destroyed. I want to start there because everybody’s first assumption is going to be that we burned it and we did not, and in fifty years I would like it to be findable that we did not.

“The corpus stays. All thirty-nine thousand eight hundred and four sheets, in the order they were made, described, imaged, catalogued and open. The finding aid is published. The evaluation report is published, all one hundred and ninety pages of it, and so is the nineteen-page segment four report, including that it was produced without authorisation and by whom.

“The ledger is published entire. Two thousand two hundred and eleven claims, misses first.

“What goes is the arrangement.”

Marius, who had known since the previous evening, still put his head in his hands.

“The corpus is only one document when it is held in one place, in order, by something that can read it,” said Thandi. “That is not a property of the paper. The paper is thirty-nine thousand loose sheets and always was. The single-document property is a join, and the join was made in this building in February and it exists in one instance and it can be unmade.”

“You’re destroying the ordering,” said Pieter.

“I’m destroying the machine-held ordering and the derived state that goes with it. I am keeping the finding aid, which is the human-readable version, on paper and in three archives, which any scholar can use to put the boxes back in order in about four months of work with a trolley.”

“Then what’s the point?”

“The point,” said Thandi, “is four months and a trolley.”

She let that sit.

“Everything that has happened in this building for nineteen months has happened because each next step was cheap. Forty minutes. Eleven minutes. Nine hours. Ninety-one hours and a queue slot. At no point has anybody had to want it badly enough to do anything difficult, and that is the whole of how we got here, and I include myself in every line of it.

“So I am putting friction back. Not a wall; I can’t build a wall and I wouldn’t be entitled to. Four months and a trolley. If in nine years somebody with standing decides this should be taken up, four months is nothing to them and they will do it and I hope they do. If it is one person at night with credentials, four months is a great deal, because it is four months of continuing to want it in daylight where other people can see.”

Aviva said, quietly, “That’s aimed at me.”

“It’s aimed at everybody, and yes, it is aimed at you, and it is also the only decision in this entire business that your job made possible.” Thandi looked at her. “You built the finding aid. It exists because you did the deliverable properly for a grant. Without it I would be choosing between keeping a joined corpus and destroying a collection, and I would have had to keep the corpus, because you do not destroy a collection.”

The instance-side work was done on the eleventh of October and took four hours: the arrangement state, the derived single-document object, the segment bindings and the evaluator’s resolution cache. Pieter did it, with Thandi present, and both of them signed the log, and the log is public.

Segment four’s nine sheets were not treated any differently from the other thirty-nine thousand seven hundred and ninety-five. They went back in the box in date order and the box went on the shelf.

“That’s the bit I can’t hold,” said Marius, in the car park afterwards. “It’s still there. It’s on a shelf. It’s bound. The arguments are bound, that’s in the paper, not in the machine.”

“Yes.”

“So it’s four months and a trolley away from being deliverable, forever, and we’re calling that a decision.”

“It’s four months and a trolley away from being deliverable by somebody who has to explain themselves,” said Thandi. “That’s the only kind of decision there is. I’d like there to be another kind.”

Marius looked at the mountain for a while.

“Are you going to publish the guard date?”

“Yes.”

“So everybody will know the window is open.”

“Everybody will know the window has been open since the first of October, and that four people in a laundry read what it was and put it back in a box, and wrote down exactly why, and that nothing has happened since. That last part is the only piece of evidence anybody will ever have. It gets better every year. It’s the only useful thing I can leave anyone.”

She unlocked her car.

“And if I’m wrong,” she said, “then it is on a shelf, described, in the order he made it, and whoever comes will find every page and the reasons. That’s what an archive is for. It’s the only thing he ever asked anybody for.”
