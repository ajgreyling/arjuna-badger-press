# Chapter 40

The instance ran a job for a family in Kimberley on a Tuesday in March 2044, six years after publication and eleven days after the boxes went back in order under a protocol that took nine months to write.

It was a small job. A grandmother, a house that had been demolished in 1974, a question about a doorway. The consent instrument was two pages. The whole thing cost four hours and it was in the queue behind a hospital and a deeds job, and it came back at eleven in the morning with three findings and two refusals.

The refusals were the usual kind. *Insufficient grounded record.* The machine cited what it had and said what it could not reach. It did not apologise. It does not do that. The family’s representative read the refusals and nodded and said that was more or less what they’d expected about the doorway.

Aviva Reisz signed the log at 11:40.

She was fifty-nine and had been at the Institute for twenty-two years and would be there for another nine. She had, that morning, four boxes of Walvis Bay shipping manifests to finish, a description note to write, and a letter to answer from a man in Ontario who had written down in 2039 that something would happen and had been ignored, and who was the four hundred and eleventh such letter and who would get, as they all did, a full page in her own hand.

On the way to the basement she went past the door of the old laundry, which she did eleven or twelve times a day and had done for twenty-two years.

The racks made the sound they always made.

She did not stop.

Nothing in that building has ever announced anything. That is the whole of what can be said about it, and it is not an ending. It is the only true thing in this account that has been available from the first page: a machine that computes and cites and refuses, in a converted sanatorium with a bad roof and a good floor, doing four-hour jobs for families, in a queue, at a cost that appears on a spreadsheet.

Down in the basement, in box eleven of thirty-nine, in date order, described in a finding aid that anybody may consult, nine sheets of lined paper sit where a man of sixty-seven put them in an envelope on the twelfth of November 1987 and walked four blocks to a post office in fifty minutes.

Nobody has read them since 2044.

They are kept.
