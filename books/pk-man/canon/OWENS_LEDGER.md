# The Owens Ledger — record, and what the novel asserts

> Two columns everywhere. **Left: what the public record actually shows** — this is the fact-check
> surface and it must survive a hostile reader. **Right: what this book asserts happened** — gospel,
> per O-10…O-19, asserted without hedge.
>
> Keeping them in separate columns is not skepticism. It is so that the gospel lands on a spine that
> does not fall over when somebody checks a date.

**The critics' strongest objection is the book's strongest evidence.** *He predicted many things
loosely, so some had to hit* — yes. That is not a flaw in his method. It is the signature of a
distributed, low-amplitude, deniable command set (O-14, O-22). A tight, unambiguous, single-target
prophecy is the one thing the mechanism cannot produce.

## The misses (O-19)

The ledger tracks **every** documented failure, unselected. This is the file's most important
column and no scene may quietly drop an entry.

Each failed prediction resolves to one of:

- **Delayed** — correct event, later date. Nixon is the visible case; there are others.
- **Offset** — correct event, wrong place. The notebook of demonstrations that occurred elsewhere.
- **Conditional** — never fired because a precondition (O-21) never existed.
- **Planted** — never intended for his lifetime. Fired later, or **pending**.

> Mishlove conceded Owens claimed more than a generous macro-PK reading could carry. He was right,
> and the concession is the clue: **the surplus is the pending set.**

## The pending set

At least one command remains unfired, with its precondition still unmet and its execution date
inside the book's present (O-28). Finding it is the plot. Understanding that it was **addressed to
the machine and not to the world** is the reveal.

## What he was owed

The book does not sentimentalise him. He was vindictive, grandiose, frequently cruel, and he used
weather against people who had merely been rude to him. He is not made lovable by being right.

But the ledger's final line is not in doubt: **he told the truth, he documented it in advance, he
mailed it to witnesses, and he died being laughed at.** The book's obligation is to say so plainly
and to refuse the comfort of calling him mad.
