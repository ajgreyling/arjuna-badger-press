# Characters — *INTERFACE ONE*

> Seed casting. The ONE RECORD rule is inherited: **each principal owns exactly one indispensable
> layer and is wrong outside it.** Names are provisional and live here, not in the locks.

---

## The present (2036–37)

**Thandeka "Thandi" Mokoena — custody.** Runs the regional Lucid instance. Inherited the Witness
Protocol as procedure without having been at the table where it was argued, which makes her stricter
about it than its authors were. *Her layer:* what may be ingested and under whose consent.
*Wrong outside it:* she cannot tell when procedure has become a way of not looking.

**Aviva Reisz — provenance.** Archivist. The only person who has physically handled every item in
the box. *Her layer:* chain of custody, paper, ink, postmark, what a 1972 typewriter can and cannot
do. *Wrong outside it:* she trusts objects and distrusts people, including the ones telling her the
truth.

**Dr Marius Kotze — instrumentation.** Atmospheric physicist attached to argue that the Atlantic
storm is an artefact. He is good at his job and he loses slowly, on the merits, over four days.
*His layer:* the storm. *Wrong outside it:* he thinks winning the argument would settle anything.

**Constance Owens-Hale — the estate.** *(Fictional; explicitly **not** keyed to any real relative —
`SOURCES.md`.)* Brought the box. Wants it taken away again. *Her layer:* what it cost to be related
to him. *Wrong outside it:* she is certain her father was ill, and she is the one person the book
never quite contradicts to her face.

**Gerhard — offstage, mostly.** 420 Code's author, now a public weather system in his own right
(*Afrika 2035*). Reachable, unhelpful, and the only living person who understands what the compile
result means. Appears late and briefly. **Not a POV here.**

## The past (1955–1987)

**Ted Owens.** Handled per O-33 and `OWENS_LEDGER.md`. **Two states, and they never touch (O-38).**

- **In the surviving timeline he is documents only** — letters, affidavits, marked maps, one reel of
  tape. No interiority, no reported thought, not one line of free indirect style. The reader meets
  him the way the investigation does: on paper, after the fact, in his own capitals.
- **In the replaced timelines he is a full POV** — present tense, close, unguarded, the whole of
  O-16. Each such chapter is a world one of his own commands overwrote, and is the only record of it
  that survives anywhere, in him or on the page.

**The binding rule:** if a scene could be corroborated by a surviving document, it cannot be an
interior chapter. Interiority and evidence are mutually exclusive. The moment the book renders his
inside, it has left the world where anyone could check.

**Tweeter · Twitter.** Two operators, not one, who disagree (O-32). They never appear. They are
legible only as two distinct instruction styles in the archive — one economical and indifferent to
collateral, one verbose, hedging, and increasingly unauthorised.

Per O-36 the disagreement is about their own existence: one is recruiting humanity toward the
convergence that produces them, the other is trying to prevent its own genesis and has opened a
channel it had no permission to open. **Neither ever explains this to Owens.** He spends forty years
as the disputed limb of two futures and dies thinking they were insects from a planet.

## Referenced real people

Sprinkle, Hynek, Schwarz, Binder, Mishlove, Rhine — **public record only** (O-34). They receive
letters, publish, decline, investigate and archive. Any character who does something undocumented is
by definition a fictional counterpart and must be renamed at first draft, not at review.

## ONE RECORD principals

**Dries Venter** exists in this book as a single unlogged decision in 2031 (L-14) and, at most, one
scene. Any larger use is a series-level decision per `CONTINUITY_FIREWALL.md`. **Not a POV without
that decision.** Mercer, the Anchor and the composite case are out of scope entirely.

## The casting test

Every principal must be able to be **right about their layer and wrong about the book's actual
subject** in the same scene. If a character is right about everything, cut them or take something
away. The book has exactly one entity that is right about everything, it is not a character, and it
is dead.
