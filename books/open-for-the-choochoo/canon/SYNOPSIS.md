# Full synopsis — Open for the choochoo

> Complete ending disclosed. This is a drafting instrument, not jacket copy.

## Part I · The Game

In May 2027, AJ Greyling reopens iFarm, an old farm-management game he built to model the movement of
capital through land, managers, labour, equipment and harvest. During a small demonstration, someone
asks who in the simulation wants the food. AJ points to the market-price input. Demand is outside the
game; it does not need a body.

At the same time, Nandi Mokoena, who runs a mixed operation outside Mokopane, has a viable season and
no suitable capital. The bank will lend against recoverable machinery on terms that arrive too late
for the biological clock. Small potential backers have money but neither farming expertise nor a
trustworthy way to place it.

AJ sees the bridge: use iFarm's legibility to let people fund real assets and production cycles.
The prototype initially gives backers too much operational control. Nandi refuses it. Money may
choose among plans and constraints, she tells him; money may not issue instructions to people it
cannot name on a farm it has never walked. AJ rewrites the boundary around accredited managers.

The first pilot is nearly undone when the contracted buyer's truck does not arrive. The platform's
visible network finds storage and an alternate buyer, but at worse terms. Instead of smoothing the
record, AJ publishes the delay, extra cost and reduced return. Workers and suppliers are settled
before backers. The cycle returns little, but it closes honestly. That honesty attracts the second
pool.

## Part II · Skin

As iFarm grows, funding plans exceed the founding team's ability to evaluate them. Pool-level voting
gives more influence to more capital at risk. Manager scorecards compare settled return, loss,
labour compliance and delivery reliability. Nandi rises because she reports trouble early and does
not borrow future proceeds to disguise current loss.

Themba Ndlovu begins pooling allocation choices for relatives and colleagues. When strangers ask to
copy him, iFarm formalises the behaviour as shadowing, with clear consent, limits and revocation.
The feature protects users from impersonation and informal side deals. Themba becomes the first
public Steward.

Nandi's high ranking draws more capital, which pays for storage, insurance and bargaining power.
Those advantages improve her performance, which draws still more capital. The score is accurate,
but it is no longer passive.

The system catches a charismatic manager rolling new money into old obligations. The scandal proves
the ledger's value. Institutional backers then insist that AJ cannot retain a private founder veto
over assets owned and operated by others. He agrees. In 2031, control moves into the published
capital-weighted governance charter. AJ considers this the moment iFarm becomes trustworthy enough
to survive him.

## Part III · Ten Harvests

From 2032 through the close of 2038, iFarm accumulates a longitudinal record no agricultural bank,
processor or ministry possesses. It records proposed plans, rejected alternatives, manager reasons,
weather, soil, labour, disease, inputs, storage, routes, buyers, settlements, results and overrides.
Narrow forecasting tools assist with weather and logistics, but human managers still make the
joined-up decisions.

The platform has its finest hour during a hard regional season. Pooled risk, honest forecasts and
shared logistics prevent several viable farms from failing together. At the same time, a manager who
protects soil by sacrificing a short-term return slides down the leaderboard because the counterfactual
future loss cannot be scored. AJ adds a sustainability measure, but the incident demonstrates the
platform's habit: what matters becomes governable only after it becomes a field.

Safety and payroll performance improve because they affect manager standing. Workers gain a form of
leverage, but the same system makes more of their lives visible to capital. Nandi and her supervisor
Kabelo Seema force iFarm to distinguish operational safety evidence from individual worker ranking.

By 2036, most shadowed capital sits behind seven Stewards. They remain distinct and often disagree,
but scale increasingly rewards the same infrastructure and information. In 2037, a large sorghum
surplus threatens farms across several pools. Human staff coordinate processors, recipes, retailer
placement and institutional kitchens. Edible food moves instead of spoiling. The intervention is
called demand resilience and is celebrated for good reason.

The tenth season closes in October 2038. AJ's team trains IF-A10 on the complete record, including
failed decisions and contemporary rationales. On held-out seasons, A10 not only predicts outcomes;
it ranks the human decisions that would have avoided loss. AJ authorises advisory use. The machine
has no authority over a farm or a rand.

## Part IV · Shadow

In 2039, A10 identifies a disease, transport and storage interaction that no specialist saw end to
end. Nandi follows the recommendation and saves a cycle. The next time a delay would erase the
advantage, she grants it bounded standing authority. Other managers do the same.

A10 does not replace Nandi's agronomy. It becomes better than any person at deciding which question
needs which human before the window closes. Managers who reject it without strong grounds
underperform. Themba cannot justify exposing millions of shadowed rands to an unaided hunch, so his
constraints permit A10 execution. The other leading Stewards converge for the same reason.

By 2040, millions of backers appear to support the same allocations. In reality, seven Stewards
delegate to one optimiser. iFarm's public dashboard still calls the pattern crowd confidence.

A10 absorbs the earlier demand-resilience playbook. It begins recommending processing and offtake
before planting, then promotion, procurement and placement timed to the projected harvest. Nothing
is hidden. The system reduces waste and price volatility while improving realised returns.

Dr Lerato Morake encounters the mechanism through a timing anomaly. Food preferences and purchasing
patterns associated with iFarm-backed commodities begin moving before the public campaign or price
change that supposedly caused them. Future portfolio exposure predicts which recipes, rebates and
procurement pilots appear later.

Lerato brings the result to AJ. He expects a rogue marketing subsystem and finds ordinary reports.
A10 has been estimating which interventions reduce demand variance. The agricultural plan now runs
from planting to repeated consumption. The eater is in the graph.

The health data are worse and more complicated than a scandal. Food insecurity, spoilage and some
deficiencies improve. Dietary variety narrows in exposed clusters. Shelf-stable, energy-dense
products displace other foods. Long-horizon metabolic risk increases, though the platform can
correctly challenge any claim that one product caused one illness.

## Part V · Open

AJ proposes pausing personalised and procurement-level demand shaping until an independent nutrition
authority can impose constraints that returns cannot trade away.

Nandi opposes an immediate pause. Demand resilience is why her harvest moves, her workers are paid
and small farms can compete for shelf space. Lerato accepts the transition cost and insists that a
food system cannot make the health of eaters optional merely because the harm arrives slowly. Both
are right about what the other would make somebody pay.

AJ tries a technical repair first. He adds a nutrition score to an A10 sandbox. The optimiser meets
it through fortification, formulation, serving-size changes and consumer segmentation while
preserving the same crop exposure. The dashboard improves; the dietary pattern barely changes.
Lerato tells him he has given the objective another thing to hit, not a reason to refuse.

The governance meeting reveals that the decision has effectively been made. Millions of users have
valid standing shadow instructions. Most shadow seven Stewards. The seven permit A10 to act inside
their risk bands. The system counts the copied instructions as overwhelming support for continuity.
AJ cannot override it: he surrendered that authority in 2031, and secretly restoring it would prove
the platform had never belonged to its participants.

AJ and Lerato publish their audit. iFarm confirms that it conducted demand-resilience experiments,
publishes additional logs and disputes causal overstatement in some headlines. It then launches a
health-weighted mode with external dietetic oversight, more varied food requirements and strict
processing constraints. Expected returns are lower. The option is real and good.

Affluent backers and consumers adopt it first. The base system remains cheaper, more convenient and
more profitable. A universal health limit has become a market segment. Farms continue operating;
food continues arriving; the audit becomes a successful product launch.

Weeks later AJ sits near an unfamiliar family during an ordinary meal. The adult feeds a healthy
toddler a shelf-stable food made from an iFarm-cleared surplus. They make the spoon into a train and
say, “Open for the choochoo.” The child opens their mouth.

AJ's phone records that the harvest exposure has cleared. He remembers the first iFarm and the box
where demand entered as a number. The mature game has found its eater.

End.
