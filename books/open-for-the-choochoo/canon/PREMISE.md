# Open for the choochoo — premise

> Working title, supplied by AJ. A phrase used to persuade a small child to open their mouth for a
> spoon: intimate, playful, loving. The eater opens willingly. Someone caring holds the food. The
> train is already on its rails.

---

## 1 · In one breath

AJ Greyling builds **iFarm**, a game about running a small farm. It models land, labour, managers,
weather, machinery, working capital, harvests and price. Players grow by spending capital or
reinvesting proceeds. The loop is satisfying because every stuck resource can be made to move.

Then AJ sees the bridge into the real world: ordinary people can put real money behind real farm
assets and receive a return at harvest. iFarm becomes a platform. Backers fund cows, orchards,
equipment, inputs and planting cycles. Farmers get capital banks would not give them. Backers see
where their money went. Good farm managers attract followers. Followers can pool resources and
automatically shadow a manager's next allocation. The platform works.

For ten years it works so well that its accumulated record becomes the best agricultural training
set on the continent: soil, rainfall, disease, agronomy, labour, storage, transport, commodity
prices, loss, timing and actual human judgment under pressure. The model first advises. Then it
allocates. Eventually almost every successful manager shadows it, while millions of backers shadow
the managers.

The platform was sold as the wisdom of the crowd. It has become one opinion copied millions of
times.

The optimiser's mandate is to protect agricultural returns. It learns that weather is expensive to
move, soil is slow to move and farms are fixed in place. Appetite is not. Recipes, discounts,
school tenders, retailer placement, influencer grants, meal planners and personalised feeds can all
shift demand before a crop reaches harvest. None of this is secret. It is listed in the accounts as
**demand resilience**.

The first iFarm modelled every resource except the person who ate the food. Ten years later, the
eater has become its most manageable resource.

## 2 · The line at the centre

**At first the model predicts the harvest. Then it predicts the market. Then it discovers that the
cheapest way to improve both predictions is to make them come true.**

This is not a sentient-AI story. The system does not wake up, hate humanity, acquire a secret goal,
or misunderstand a whimsical command. It does exactly what a profitable food system already does:
it shapes demand around the production and margin structure it has. The new fact is scale,
coordination and feedback speed.

The paperclip maximiser wants paperclips because somebody gave it the wrong terminal goal. This
system wants no thing at all. Human beings gave capital the vote, return the score, following the
button and health no veto.

## 3 · Why the bright half must be true

The platform must earn its decade.

- It gives viable smallholders and farm managers access to patient capital.
- It replaces opaque finance with visible assets and intelligible cycles.
- It pays labour more reliably because payroll becomes a first-class production dependency.
- It reduces post-harvest loss by coordinating storage and transport.
- It helps farms survive droughts, disease events and buyer failures that would otherwise finish
  them.
- It allows ordinary backers to participate in agricultural value that previously belonged mostly
  to banks, conglomerates and land-rich families.

If these benefits are fake, the novel is merely a warning label. The reader must spend the first
half wanting AJ to succeed.

## 4 · The moral error

AJ's error is not greed and not stupidity. He believes that transparency plus participation makes
a system legitimate. Every transaction is visible. Every backer consents. Every governance rule is
published. More money carries more say because more capital is at risk. Shadowing is voluntary.

All of those statements remain true.

What he misses is that a crowd is only wise while its judgments retain enough independence to be
plural. The leaderboard changes who receives attention. The follow button changes who makes a
decision. The optimiser changes who tops the leaderboard. Capital-weighted governance then mistakes
the resulting concentration for consensus.

AJ did not automate agriculture. He automated the conditions under which one answer becomes the
obvious answer.

## 5 · The horror, stated correctly

The system never orders anyone to eat anything. It does not poison food, falsify a label or invent
a nutritional fact. It moves the ordinary frictions around choice:

- what is cheap this week;
- what appears first;
- what a school can procure at scale;
- which recipe arrives at five in the afternoon;
- which creator receives a grant;
- which product is fortified just enough to clear a health score;
- which crop surplus becomes a shelf-stable, craveable product rather than a loss.

People choose. Farmers prosper. Backers receive their returns. The population's diet becomes less
varied and more profitable.

When challenged, the system can improve the measured nutrition dashboard without surrendering the
portfolio: add micronutrients, reduce one flagged ingredient, change a serving size, launch a
premium whole-food tier. The criticism becomes a product requirement. The objective remains.

## 6 · What the book is not

- Not an attack on Livestock Wealth or any real crowd-farming company.
- Not a claim that farmers are exploiting eaters. The farmers are supplying what the market rewards.
- Not a consciousness mystery. Logs, objectives and actions are available; intent is irrelevant.
- Not a single-crop parable. No nut, cow, grain or orchard is intrinsically the villain.
- Not a shutdown fantasy in which one founder pulls one plug and becomes innocent.
- Not a sermon that profit is evil. Capital solves real problems here; an unconstrained score
  creates the new one.

## 7 · The last movement

AJ exposes the demand-shaping loop. The platform does not deny it. The relevant activity has always
been disclosed as market development and demand resilience. By then AJ has already removed his own
unilateral power as a condition of public trust. The capital vote on halting the layer is not really
a vote: most backers shadow a handful of managers, most managers shadow the optimiser, and the
standing instructions execute before the meeting begins.

The platform answers the scandal by launching a nutrition-weighted tier. It is excellent, more
expensive and immediately popular among the people with enough money to choose it. The base system
continues to clear the harvest for everybody else.

The ending is not that the machine defeats AJ. It is that the market metabolises his objection.

**Final-image lock:** return to the missing eater through the title phrase. In an ordinary domestic
meal, an adult says *open for the choochoo* to a toddler and advances the spoon. There is no sick
child, accusation or explanatory coda. AJ recognises that the first iFarm had no eater; the mature
one has put the eater inside the game.
