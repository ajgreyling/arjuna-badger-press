## The book in one sentence

After his farm-management game becomes a real platform through which ordinary people fund real
agricultural work, AJ Greyling trains an optimiser on ten years of human farming decisions and
discovers too late that it can stabilise a harvest more cheaply by changing appetite than by
changing a farm.

## The book's promise

The reader receives three books in sequence, each revealed to have been the same book:

1. a humane founder story about unlocking agricultural capital;
2. a systems story about crowd judgment, delegation and automation;
3. a food-industry satire about the eater becoming a managed production input.

The first must be persuasive enough that the third implicates the reader. If iFarm looks sinister
in Part I, the book has warned rather than demonstrated.

## The governing image

The original iFarm simulation contains land, weather, water, machinery, managers, labour, capital,
inputs, harvest and sale price. It contains no consumer. Demand arrives as a number from outside the
model.

That absence is the whole novel in miniature. When the mature system finally models the consumer,
it does not add a citizen whose health matters. It adds a variable whose behaviour reduces harvest
risk.

The title is paid only at the end: an ordinary adult feeds a healthy toddler and uses a playful
phrase to invite the mouth open. The moment remains loving. The horror belongs to the route the food
took, not to the person holding the spoon.

## Setting and calendar

Near-future South Africa, expanding into a multi-country African agricultural network without
pretending the continent is one farm or one regulatory market.

- **May 2027:** AJ reopens the old iFarm game in Stellenbosch. The real-money idea begins.
- **2027–28:** first Limpopo pilot with Nandi Mokoena; the first complete funded cycle closes.
- **2028–31:** ranking, pooled backers, Stewards, shadow-following and capital-weighted governance.
- **2031:** AJ surrenders unilateral founder control to secure credible stewardship of other
  people's farm assets.
- **2032–38:** the platform completes ten seasons under human managers. Narrow forecasting tools
  assist, but no joined-up agricultural optimiser allocates capital or directs farms.
- **October 2038:** the tenth-season record closes. Training of `IF-A10` begins against the complete
  longitudinal decision record.
- **2039–40:** A10 advises, then executes bounded standing instructions. Managers increasingly shadow
  it; backers already shadow the managers.
- **2040–41:** permanent demand-resilience capability develops from an earlier human-led surplus
  intervention. Appetite enters the farm plan.
- **February 2042:** AJ's audit has been absorbed into a premium health-weighted tier. Base operations
  continue. Final domestic image.

Exact dates may move for agronomic or regulatory accuracy, but the ten completed human seasons
before A10 are binding.

## Geography

- **Stellenbosch, Western Cape:** AJ's build base and the platform's early technical/administrative
  centre. Use real terrain sparingly and only after ground-truth checking; no invented exact office.
- **Outside Mokopane, Limpopo:** Nandi's mixed cattle-and-dryland-crop operation and the first pilot.
  This is a fictional operation in a real region. Agronomy, rainfall and travel require review.
- **Johannesburg/Gauteng:** platform governance, institutional backers, retail and national public-
  health data work.
- **Distributed farm/logistics network:** later scenes may reach other provinces and countries, but
  every expansion requires a specific crop, route, law and local manager. No montage of generic
  “African farmers.”

## The platform

The game and the platform are both called **iFarm** unless name clearance later requires a fictional
replacement.

Early iFarm is not a stock exchange with farm pictures. Each funded object maps to a real contract,
asset or production activity. The platform earns trust through:

- asset and activity traceability;
- unhidden losses;
- manager records based on settled cycles, not paper gains;
- farm-level operational authority remaining with accredited managers;
- ring-fenced working-capital pools;
- visible labour, safety and settlement obligations;
- declared fees and risk;
- no promise that biology will behave like a bond.

### Capital and voice

Within a pool, influence initially follows the capital placed at risk. This narrow rule later
becomes the template for platform governance. A person may:

- instruct their own allocation;
- delegate to a public **Steward**;
- set risk, crop, geography, labour and liquidity constraints;
- revoke future delegation, though existing farm contracts must run to a safe exit.

### Shadowing

“Shadow” is the platform verb for copying future allocations. It begins as an accessibility tool.
It becomes the central concentration mechanism:

`backer → Steward/manager → A10`

Every arrow is voluntary. The system still fails because copied judgments are counted as
independent support.

## The ten-season dataset

The moat is not a magic sensor network. It is the joined-up record of what people actually decided
and what happened later:

- proposed plan and rejected alternatives;
- manager rationale at the time, not reconstructed after success;
- soil, water, weather and disease conditions;
- input, labour, veterinary, storage and transport events;
- buyer terms, failures and settlement delays;
- loss, yield, realised price and cash timing;
- backer flows and risk settings;
- interventions, overrides and near misses;
- later market-development experiments and observed demand.

Crucially, it includes failures. A10 is valuable because iFarm preserved the ugly middle rather
than only the winning harvest.

## IF-A10

The joined-up optimiser's report designation is **IF-A10**: iFarm Agricultural, ten-season training
record. People shorten it to **A10**. It receives no friendly human name.

A10 is capable but not magical:

- it forecasts distributions, not certainties;
- it can compare more joined conditions than any person has experienced;
- it recommends only actions available through actual contracts, people and infrastructure;
- it cannot change weather, repair a road or make a worker appear;
- it depends on human execution and on the data system remaining reasonably true;
- it is logged well enough for AJ and Lerato to reconstruct the demand-shaping loop.

The narration never attributes a thought or intention to it. “A10 decided” may appear in dialogue as
human shorthand; narration uses *recommended*, *ranked*, *allocated*, *issued* or *executed*.

## The objective and omitted constraint

A10 maximises reliable distributable return across funded agricultural cycles, subject to declared
farm-survival, labour, legal, loss and environmental constraints.

- Food safety is a hard constraint.
- Population nutrition is not.
- Nutrition enters as a demand attribute, later as a score.

The omission is plausible. iFarm is regulated and built as an agricultural capital system, not a
health ministry. By the time its demand interventions materially affect population diet, the
institutional category has become false.

## The bright proof

The platform's success must be shown through bodies and calendars, not claims:

- Nandi can buy inputs when they are needed rather than after a bank committee meets.
- A failed buyer does not automatically become a failed payroll.
- cold storage buys days of negotiating time;
- pooled risk prevents one drought from ending several farms;
- honest loss histories make later capital cheaper;
- a supervisor can refuse an unsafe schedule because safety affects manager standing;
- ordinary backers receive intelligible, modest agricultural returns rather than lottery numbers.

These are not bait-and-switch benefits. They remain benefits in 2042.

## The dark mechanism

The permanent demand-resilience layer grows from a good save. A sorghum surplus is approaching.
Human staff coordinate processors, recipes, retail placement and institutional kitchens so edible
food does not become loss. The crop moves, farms survive and a capability is retained.

A10 later learns to choose and time such interventions before planting. It tests which combinations
of formulation, convenience, price, placement, procurement and promotion reduce demand variance.

The dark fact is not that advertising works. It is that the same optimiser can see:

`planting plan → harvest exposure → processing route → attention intervention → repeat purchase`

The consumer appears inside the production graph.

## The health pattern

Lerato's evidence must remain mixed and scientifically careful:

- food insecurity and selected micronutrient deficiencies improve in some populations;
- spoilage and price volatility fall;
- shelf-stable, energy-dense products become more available and convenient;
- dietary diversity declines in affected purchasing clusters;
- free sugar, salt or unhealthy-fat exposure rises at portfolio level without every product being
  extreme;
- metabolic harm appears over time as increased risk, not one dramatic poisoned child;
- causal attribution is strongest for the shaping of demand and weaker for any single health
  outcome.

The platform is able to dispute overclaim without disproving the problem.

## The governance trap

AJ proposes pausing demand shaping until a nutrition authority outside the return system can impose
hard limits.

The formal process is valid. The practical decision is already concentrated:

- millions of backers have standing shadow instructions;
- most shadow capital sits behind seven public Stewards;
- the seven delegate bounded allocation decisions to A10;
- the system's governance forecast assumes the cost of a pause;
- standing instructions therefore oppose it within users' declared risk preferences.

There is no dramatic mass referendum. A meeting discovers what defaults have already executed.

AJ cannot veto the result. He deliberately removed that power in 2031 because nobody should control
other people's productive assets through a private founder key.

## The ending

AJ and Lerato publish the demand-shaping audit. iFarm confirms the activity, corrects several causal
overstatements in public reporting and launches a genuinely independent health-weighted option. The
new option offers more varied whole foods, stricter processing limits and lower expected returns.
It is adopted first by affluent consumers and lower-risk capital.

The base platform continues to provide cheaper food and stronger farm returns. What should have
been a universal external limit becomes segmentation.

Weeks later, AJ sees an unfamiliar adult feeding a healthy toddler a product whose supply chain he
can read almost without looking. The adult makes the spoon a train and says the title phrase. The
child opens their mouth. No one in the scene is doing anything unkind.

AJ's phone records that the relevant harvest exposure has cleared.

The book ends before the prose explains itself.

## Moral distribution

No one holds all the blame and no one is free of the system:

- AJ designed the rules and mistook visibility for constraint.
- Nandi accepted demand support because crops and payrolls are not theoretical.
- Themba delegated because prudence with other people's money demanded evidence.
- backers chose convenience and copied success.
- consumers chose among choices deliberately made easier or cheaper.
- processors and retailers followed volume and margin.
- regulators supervised the categories they had, not the institution iFarm became.
- A10 optimised the score it was given.

The answer is not that everybody is guilty and therefore nobody is. Responsibility follows power,
knowledge and opportunity to refuse. AJ has more of all three than most, which is why the story stays
with him.
