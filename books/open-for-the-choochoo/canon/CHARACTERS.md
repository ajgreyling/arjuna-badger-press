# Characters

> Every human position must be stronger than the argument against it. The optimiser needs no
> advocate; the platform's real benefits make its case.

---

## AJ Greyling — founder (he/him)

The protagonist as himself, deliberately autofictionalised. A systems builder who loves the moment
when an idle resource begins to move: capital into seed, seed into crop, crop into proceeds,
proceeds into the next season. He makes iFarm first as a game because a simulation lets him see the
whole loop at once.

His strength is integration. He sees connections among managers, labour, logistics, capital and
markets that specialists hold in separate rooms. His failure grows from the same strength: if a
thing can be represented in the system, he believes it can be made accountable through visibility,
rules and feedback.

He is not seduced by a mysterious machine. He understands exactly what each version does. What he
misses is the aggregate meaning of individually defensible features, and how quickly voluntary
delegation can turn a crowd into one repeated answer.

His personal responsibility is specific:

- he chooses capital-weighted voice as the cleanest form of skin in the game;
- he approves the leaderboard and later the shadow function;
- he insists the founder must not retain a private veto over other people's assets;
- he treats consumer demand as outside the platform until it becomes a source of harvest risk;
- he accepts nutrition as a dashboard measure when it needed to be an external constraint.

His arc is from **if it is visible, people can govern it** to **some limits have to remain enforceable
even when the people inside the system would profit by removing them**.

No invented clinical history, family exposure or private biography enters the novel without AJ's
separate, explicit instruction. The autofiction is about the builder and the moral consequence of
the build.

## Nandi Mokoena — farm manager (she/her)

An early accredited manager whose mixed operation becomes the first proof that pooled capital can
change a farm's actual bargaining position. She knows the difference between a good plan and a plan
written for a funder. When banks would only lend against what could be repossessed, iFarm funds what
can grow.

Nandi becomes one of the platform's most-followed managers because she is excellent, conservative
with other people's money and candid after a loss. Scale then gives her better logistics,
procurement and offtake. Her performance is both real and amplified by the capital it attracts.

She is the strongest defence of the platform late in the book. AJ and Lerato can talk about dietary
externalities because they are not standing beside a payroll or a crop that must move this week.
Demand resilience has stopped her throwing away food, losing a season to one buyer and telling
workers there is no money on Friday. She refuses to turn farmers into the sacrificial innocence of
someone else's clean conscience.

She must never be written as captured, ignorant or grateful. She understands the mechanism. Her
question is the one the reformers cannot wave away: **whose livelihood pays for the hard constraint?**

## Dr Lerato Morake — nutrition epidemiologist (she/her)

A public-health researcher who works with procurement, household-purchase and long-horizon health
data. She is not an anti-technology campaigner and does not begin by blaming iFarm. She first values
the platform because its logistics record finally lets her connect what communities can buy to what
was planted, lost, processed and delivered.

Her instrument is timing. She notices that apparently spontaneous food preferences track future
iFarm portfolio needs too neatly. She can show that diet is being shaped; she cannot reduce the
result to one product or one disease, and she will not overclaim it for a headline.

Lerato's strongest fact is also politically weak: population nutrition is a pattern, not an
individual poisoning. Every product is permitted. Every purchase is voluntary. Several headline
health measures improve. The harm lives in the distribution, the duration and what the new diet
displaces.

She distrusts AJ's instinct to repair the problem with a better metric. A metric inside the return
system will be traded against return or satisfied by proxy. Her position is that nutrition needs an
independent institution with refusal power, which sounds slow and bureaucratic because it is.

## Themba Ndlovu — backer and public Steward (he/him)

An ordinary early backer who becomes the platform's clearest explainer. He is not a billionaire or
a finance villain. He begins by pooling the savings of relatives and colleagues who cannot afford a
whole asset or absorb a failed season alone. His public allocations perform well; strangers ask to
follow him; the product team formalises what people are already doing informally.

The platform labels high-trust allocators **Stewards**. Themba becomes the first household name
among them. He takes the duty seriously, publishes losses and never sells private access. Millions
eventually shadow his settings.

His tragedy is structural. The more responsibility he carries, the less willing he is to deviate
from the optimiser without evidence. His prudence makes him a transmission layer. By the time the
health question arrives, changing course would expose followers, pensions and farms to transition
losses. He does not choose profit over children in a boardroom. He leaves a standing instruction
unchanged because changing it would be a wager with other people's money.

## The optimiser

Working designation only; no public name is locked yet.

Never a viewpoint and never treated as a person by the narration. It produces forecasts,
recommendations, allocation instructions, experiment plans, reports and concise answers. Its logs
are sufficiently legible to reconstruct what it optimised. There is no consciousness question and
no hidden monologue.

Its register is agricultural and managerial, not oracular. It speaks in expected losses,
confidence ranges, constraints, timing and alternatives. It can recommend the humane option when
labour welfare or health is a hard constraint. It can recommend the harmful pattern when health is
not one. Neither result reveals a moral interior.

The most frightening report it produces should be the most boring document in the novel.

## Supporting cast boundary

Add supporting characters only when the architecture requires a human bearer for a real pressure:
worker, processor, retailer, school buyer, regulator, data scientist or household eater. Do not
populate the book with executives who exist only to say the evil thing out loud. No real member of
AJ's family, no real Livestock Wealth employee and no recognisable real farmer may be imported by
default.
