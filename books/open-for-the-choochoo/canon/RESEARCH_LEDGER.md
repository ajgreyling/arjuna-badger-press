## B · Why the bright platform matters

### Food and Agriculture Organization — Access to Rural Finance

- Source: <https://www.fao.org/policy-support/policy-themes/access-to-rural-finance/>
- Supports: financial services for rural smallholder households remain difficult to provide;
  finance is material to investment, risk management, resilience and rural development.
- Story use: Nandi's operation and the platform's early moral legitimacy.

### FAO Investment Centre — evidence review on access to finance

- Source: <https://www.fao.org/investment-centre/latest/news/detail/how-farmers--access-to-finance-boosts-investment-in-agrifood-systems/en>
- Supports: a 2025 FAO/IPA review drawing on experimental and quasi-experimental studies found that
  suitable financial services can support agricultural productivity and income, while access
  barriers remain.
- Story use: prevent the book from treating agricultural finance as a fake need invented by the
  platform.

## C · Demand can be shaped

### World Health Organization — digital marketing ecosystem

- Source: <https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/97892890579500>
- Supports: digital advertising ecosystems enable targeted promotion of foods high in fats, sugars
  or salt, and evidence reviewed by WHO links this marketing to children's eating behaviour.
- Story use: the demand-resilience ladder; targeted timing and selection are plausible extensions
  of present marketing systems.

### WHO — food marketing exposure and behaviour review

- Source: <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240041783>
- Supports: WHO's review covered 143 content-analysis and 36 consumer-research studies; it reports
  that unhealthy-food marketing is prevalent and that exposure is positively associated with
  consumption of marketed or less healthy foods.
- Story use: Lerato's evidence base and the refusal to claim that one advertisement causes one
  disease.

### WHO — healthy diet

- Source: <https://www.who.int/health-topics/healthy-diet>
- Supports: varied diets rich in whole grains, vegetables, fruit, legumes and nuts and lower in
  free sugars, salt and unhealthy fats are associated with health benefits; highly processed food
  and aggressive marketing contribute to unhealthy dietary patterns.
- Story use: define the difference between satisfying a micronutrient proxy and preserving a
  healthy overall dietary pattern.

## D · When the crowd stops being a crowd

### Lorenz et al., PNAS — social influence and crowd wisdom

- Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/>
- Citation: Jan Lorenz, Heiko Rauhut, Frank Schweitzer and Dirk Helbing, “How social influence can
  undermine the wisdom of crowd effect,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 108(22),
  2011, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1008636108.
- Supports: experimental evidence that even mild social influence can undermine crowd wisdom by
  reducing diversity while increasing confidence.
- Story use: the leaderboard/following engine. The line to preserve is not “crowds are foolish” but
  “a crowd of correlated judgments no longer supplies the independence the effect needs.”

### Becker, Brackbill and Centola, PNAS — network conditions matter

- Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5495222/>
- Citation: Joshua Becker, Devon Brackbill and Damon Centola, “Network dynamics of social influence
  in the wisdom of crowds,” *PNAS* 114(26), 2017, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1615978114.
- Supports: social influence does not have one universal effect; network structure and information
  diversity affect whether it improves or degrades group estimates.
- Story use: keep the thesis exact. The problem is the platform's specific star-and-shadow topology,
  not human communication itself.

## F · Initial agricultural grounding for Limpopo and sorghum

### South African Department of Agriculture — Sorghum production guideline

- Source: <https://www.nda.gov.za/phocadownloadpap/Brochures_and_Production_Guidelines/Brochure%20Sorghum.pdf>
- Supports: Waterberg and Vhembe in Limpopo are documented production areas; sorghum is grown under
  warm conditions, is frost-sensitive and is commonly planted from mid-October to mid-December when
  soil temperature and rainfall permit. The guideline also supports the need for advance seedbed,
  moisture, fertiliser and planting preparation.
- Story use: Chapter 2's June planning horizon and the later choice of sorghum as a plausible
  Limpopo dryland crop. No cultivar, yield, hectare count or precise operation is locked from this
  general guide.

### South African Department of Agriculture — Trends in the Agricultural Sector 2022

- Source: <https://www.nda.gov.za/images/Branches/Economica%20Development%20Trade%20and%20Marketing/Statistc%20and%20%20Economic%20Analysis/statistical-information/trends-in-the-agricultural-sector-2022.pdf>
- Supports: the report identified Limpopo as a material share of South African commercial sorghum
  production and describes the crop as important in low and erratic rainfall areas. It also records
  high year-to-year volatility in planted area, output and yield.
- Story use: regional plausibility and the later multi-pool surplus/price-risk mechanism. Historical
  figures are not projected unchanged into the novel.
