# Scene cards — Open for the choochoo

> Execution blueprint beneath `STORY_ARCHITECTURE.md`. Three scenes per chapter unless a chapter
> earns a different shape. Drafting may alter room, weather and minor participants, but not the
> causal turn, reveal ceiling or scheduled plant/payoff.

---

## Part I · The Game

### Chapter 1 · Outside the Game · AJ · Stellenbosch · May 2027 · PP-01, PP-12

1. **Old build:** AJ gets the old iFarm simulation running for a small practical demonstration. The
   interface tracks people, fatigue, safety, water, machinery, capital and timing with more care than
   its dated graphics deserve. Want: show that the loop still works. Friction: the market-price input
   closes the cycle too neatly.
2. **One question:** Ayesha asks who in the game wants the food. AJ shows the external price field;
   demand is upstream data, not part of farm management. The answer is sensible and nobody marks it
   as ominous.
3. **Real message:** a request from Nandi's side describes a viable production cycle that cannot
   secure timely capital. AJ sees the same stuck-resource shape as the game. **Turn:** he asks for the
   actual dates, costs and constraints rather than pitching software.

### Chapter 2 · Collateral · Nandi · outside Mokopane · June 2027 · PP-13

1. **The clock:** Nandi walks the upcoming cycle with Kabelo. Inputs and work have dates; the bank's
   answer does not. Want: close the funding gap without surrendering operational control.
2. **Wrong money:** a conventional offer arrives against recoverable assets and on terms misaligned
   with harvest. Nandi rejects a sale that would make the farm smaller to finance making it larger.
3. **AJ's questions:** a remote call is irritatingly concrete—settlement order, buyer failure,
   payroll, what can be verified. Nandi agrees to show him the operation only after he stops calling
   labour a unit. **Turn:** AJ travels north with no product to sell.

### Chapter 3 · The Bridge · AJ · Limpopo/Stellenbosch · July 2027 · PP-01, PP-13

1. **Physical map:** Nandi makes AJ follow the production sequence in real space. A digital object
   without a responsible person or contract is exposed as decoration.
2. **Remote ownership thought:** AJ connects the existing real-world idea of remotely funded farm
   assets to iFarm's resource loop. He sketches a position tied to a real plan, not a synthetic farm
   game.
3. **The first model:** Ayesha identifies what must be ring-fenced, disclosed and legally separated.
   They choose one bounded cycle small enough to fail honestly. **Turn:** Nandi agrees to propose a
   pilot if she controls operations.

### Chapter 4 · Her Farm · Nandi · outside Mokopane · August 2027 · PP-03, PP-12

1. **Bad interface:** the pilot mock-up allows backers to vote on input substitutions and work timing.
   Nandi demonstrates how either vote could create harm without context.
2. **Refusal:** she states the boundary: money chooses a plan and constraints; the accredited manager
   runs the farm and is accountable for doing so. Kabelo adds that nobody online ranks individual
   workers.
3. **Rewrite in the room:** AJ removes operational controls, adds manager responsibility and makes
   labour, safety and payment obligations visible at plan level. **Turn:** Nandi signs the pilot plan.

### Chapter 5 · The Truck · Nandi · outside Mokopane · February 2028 · PP-02, PP-04

1. **Collection morning:** the contracted buyer's truck fails to arrive inside the quality window.
   Want: preserve value and settle payroll; clock: heat, storage and next-day work.
2. **What the platform cannot do:** the dashboard says contracted. Nandi has food at the gate and no
   vehicle. AJ's network locates temporary storage and another buyer at worse terms.
3. **Loss allocation:** pressure rises to protect the launch return by moving costs outside the pool.
   AJ refuses. The cycle will carry its own failure. **Turn:** the alternative truck leaves with the
   crop, but the projected return is gone.

### Chapter 6 · Settled · AJ · Stellenbosch/Limpopo · March 2028 · PP-04, PP-11, PP-17

1. **The ugly close:** invoices, storage, transport and settlement delays reconcile. Workers and
   suppliers are paid before backers. The result is small and disappointing, not catastrophic.
2. **Publish:** AJ releases the timestamped cycle record and explanation instead of a launch story.
   Nandi corrects one sentence that makes the platform sound as if it saved her farm; it bought time.
3. **Second pool:** the honest record attracts more applications and backers than a smooth return
   would have. The ordinary notification changes from projected to settled. **Turn:** the team now
   has more viable plans than it can judge.

## Part II · Skin

### Chapter 7 · At Risk · AJ · Stellenbosch · May 2028 · PP-05

1. **Conflicting instructions:** backers in a new pooled plan disagree about a bounded allocation.
   Equal votes let many tiny positions overrule most capital; one-rand-one-vote is shown to be gameable.
2. **The rule:** voice inside a pool follows capital at risk, subject to manager and legal constraints.
   Ayesha narrows the rule to money, not farm operations.
3. **Template creep:** the same ledger is proposed for future platform policy because it already
   works and users understand it. **Turn:** a local allocation rule becomes governance architecture.

### Chapter 8 · The Record · Nandi · Limpopo · 2029 · PP-06

1. **Too many stories:** backers choose managers through polished proposals and personal appeal.
   Nandi refuses to campaign while weaker managers promise smoother returns.
2. **Comparable facts:** iFarm publishes settled return, loss, delivery, labour and safety records.
   Nandi rises because her bad cycle is fully visible and resolved.
3. **Cheaper capital:** her next pool fills faster and on better terms. She sees the benefit and the
   first circularity. **Turn:** ranking changes the conditions it claims merely to measure.

### Chapter 9 · Themba's Circle · AJ · Johannesburg · 2029 · PP-07

1. **Kitchen-table allocator:** Themba explains plans to relatives and colleagues who lack time or
   minimum capital. He keeps a plain record of whose money may take which risk.
2. **Demand to follow:** strangers begin sending money or asking for his next move. Informal copying
   creates impersonation, side-payment and suitability risks.
3. **A product problem:** AJ sees that refusing to formalise following will not stop it. **Turn:** the
   team designs delegation with limits and revocation.

### Chapter 10 · Shadow · AJ · Stellenbosch/Johannesburg · 2029 · PP-07

1. **The word and controls:** shadowing copies future allocations inside user-defined risk, geography,
   crop and liquidity limits. It cannot reverse an active biological cycle.
2. **First public Steward:** Themba accepts the role only if losses remain as prominent as returns and
   there is no private promotion fee.
3. **Quiet success:** ordinary backers use the feature correctly. It is safer than the informal system
   it replaces. **Turn:** iFarm can scale judgment without scaling advisers.

### Chapter 11 · The Advantage · Nandi · Limpopo · 2030 · PP-06

1. **Capital arrives early:** high ranking and shadow demand let Nandi secure storage and inputs before
   the seasonal squeeze.
2. **Better terms become better performance:** storage buys negotiating time; bulk procurement lowers
   cost; delivery improves. All reported gains are real.
3. **The next ranking:** Nandi rises again partly because the previous ranking changed her farm's
   options. She asks how a good manager without attention can catch her now. **Turn:** AJ opens a
   ranking-bias review but does not remove the record.

### Chapter 12 · Rolled Forward · AJ · Gauteng/Stellenbosch · 2030 · PP-04

1. **Impossible smoothness:** one charismatic manager has no settled loss despite ordinary biological
   variance. Want: establish whether the anomaly is excellence or fraud before the next pool closes.
2. **Ledger reconstruction:** new capital and delayed invoices have been used to make older cycles
   appear settled. No cinematic theft; a chain of optimistic rollover.
3. **Public correction:** iFarm freezes new funding, protects active farm work and publishes the
   corrected record. The system catches what personality hid. **Turn:** institutional capital treats
   the scandal as proof of governance maturity.

### Chapter 13 · No Private King · AJ · Johannesburg · 2031 · PP-05, PP-08

1. **Institutional condition:** pension and development capital will not enter while AJ can alter
   constraints or move assets through a founder key.
2. **The brake question:** Ayesha asks which emergency AJ believes only he can recognise. He cannot
   justify permanent private authority over other people's farms and money.
3. **Charter ratified:** platform policy moves to published capital-weighted governance with external
   legal and operational constraints. AJ's veto is destroyed, not escrowed. **Turn:** iFarm can outlive
   its founder and begin its ten-season record at meaningful scale.

## Part III · Ten Harvests

### Chapter 14 · Ground Truth · Nandi · Limpopo · 2032 · PP-11

1. **Forecast versus decision:** a narrow weather tool supplies probabilities; Nandi chooses among
   imperfect actions and records why before the outcome.
2. **Rejected alternative:** iFarm begins storing what managers did not choose, preventing later
   success from rewriting the decision.
3. **First season ledger:** the record closes with error, override and outcome intact. **Turn:** AJ
   commits to preserving reasons and failures for ten completed seasons before training a joined model.

### Chapter 15 · The Dry Year · Nandi · Limpopo/network · 2033 · PP-13

1. **Regional pressure:** several viable plans face the same weather shock. Independent finance would
   force simultaneous distressed sales.
2. **Human reallocation:** managers pool storage, transport, feed and timing knowledge. Narrow tools
   forecast; people decide who yields which scarce resource.
3. **Survival, not victory:** returns fall, but payrolls settle and farms reach the next season. This
   is iFarm's finest hour. **Turn:** loss history lowers future uncertainty instead of ending access.

### Chapter 16 · The Season That Did Not Fail · AJ · network/Free State composite · 2034 · PP-06, PP-09

1. **A prudent loser:** an independent manager sacrifices current yield to protect soil and water.
   Her realised return trails the leaders.
2. **Counterfactual problem:** the platform cannot score the crop failure that did not happen three
   years later. Backers shadow away from her.
3. **New field:** AJ adds long-horizon soil measures and feels the interface improve. **Turn:** Lerato,
   not yet known to him, later encounters the same structural habit in health data.

### Chapter 17 · People in the Data · Nandi · Limpopo · 2035 · PP-12

1. **Safety win:** plan-level safety and payroll measures give Kabelo evidence to refuse a compressed
   work sequence without being dismissed as obstructive.
2. **The wrong extension:** a proposed worker reliability score would attach individual absence and
   pace to manager standing. Nandi sees capital approaching the person through the back door.
3. **Hard boundary:** she and Kabelo force aggregation, purpose limits and no individual capital score.
   **Turn:** iFarm proves it can install a costly constraint when an organised human institution holds it.

### Chapter 18 · Seven · AJ · Johannesburg · 2036 · PP-06, PP-07

1. **Concentration report:** seven Stewards receive most new shadow relationships. Their approaches
   remain genuinely different.
2. **Scale effects:** the seven share access to better infrastructure, analysts and terms. Smaller
   managers can be correct and still invisible.
3. **Mitigation:** iFarm adds discovery quotas and concentration warnings, but users prefer proven
   records. **Turn:** the crowd keeps its account count and loses informational diversity.

### Chapter 19 · Demand Resilience · Nandi · Limpopo/Gauteng · 2037 · PP-10, PP-14

1. **Sorghum exposure:** strong yields across several pools threaten a surplus and destructive price.
   The food is good; the route to enough buyers is missing.
2. **Human campaign:** processors, recipe developers, retailers and institutional kitchens coordinate
   products and placement. There are trade-offs, but waste collapses and farms settle.
3. **Capability retained:** the team names the playbook demand resilience and adds it to future plan
   support. **Turn:** market development becomes part of agricultural risk management.

### Chapter 20 · Season Ten · AJ · Stellenbosch/Johannesburg · October 2038 · PP-08, PP-11

1. **Close:** the tenth human-managed season settles. The record includes reasons, rejected actions,
   errors, overrides and physical outcomes.
2. **Training:** Farai Dube's team trains IF-A10 and tests it only on held-out past seasons. It ranks
   better decisions across joined farm, labour, logistics and market conditions.
3. **Authority line:** governance approves advisory use. A10 may recommend; it may not allocate capital
   or issue farm instructions. **Turn:** the first recommendation arrives for Nandi with a deadline.

## Part IV · Shadow

### Chapter 21 · The Missed Question · Nandi · Limpopo · 2039 · PP-02

1. **Three ordinary problems:** a disease signal, storage maintenance and a transport delay sit in
   different teams. No one sees a crisis.
2. **A10's recommendation:** it routes one precise question to the right veterinarian and changes the
   harvest/storage sequence. Nandi verifies the pieces and acts.
3. **Cycle saved:** the value lies less in an answer than in asking before the window closes. **Turn:**
   Nandi agrees to receive live recommendations rather than daily reports.

### Chapter 22 · Standing · Nandi · Limpopo · 2039 · PP-03, PP-07

1. **Approval delay:** a repeat class of recommendation loses value while waiting for Nandi's click.
2. **Bounded authority:** she defines exact conditions A10 may execute through existing suppliers and
   schedules. Farm staff retain safety and operational refusal.
3. **First execution:** the instruction completes correctly and is fully logged. **Turn:** her manager
   record now includes A10-assisted performance.

### Chapter 23 · Better Than Nandi · AJ · Stellenbosch/network · 2040 · PP-06

1. **Bad headline:** media says the AI farms better than people. Nandi rejects the category.
2. **Actual advantage:** audit shows A10 is better at routing questions, comparing conditions and acting
   inside time windows, not at replacing situated farm knowledge.
3. **Override cost:** managers who override without evidence underperform; backers move away. **Turn:**
   Themba permits A10 execution inside his shadow constraints because unaided discretion is now the
   harder fiduciary choice.

### Chapter 24 · Crowd Confidence · AJ · Johannesburg · 2040 · PP-07, PP-18

1. **Governance dashboard:** millions of accounts align behind similar plans. The interface labels it
   high crowd confidence.
2. **Dependency trace:** AJ follows the instructions backward: backers to seven Stewards, Stewards and
   managers to A10.
3. **Mitigation rejected:** displaying independent-instruction count would make the confidence number
   look smaller without changing a single user's consent. The product team postpones. **Turn:** the
   platform treats copied support as corroboration.

### Chapter 25 · Before Planting · AJ · Stellenbosch/Gauteng · late 2040 · PP-10

1. **A10 reviews the old glut:** it identifies which human interventions moved demand and which merely
   coincided with recovery.
2. **New plan:** before a large planting allocation, A10 recommends processor contracts, product tests,
   procurement pilots, placement and promotion timed to the future exposure.
3. **Approval:** waste and price risk fall in simulation; all activity sits inside declared market
   development. Humans approve. **Turn:** preference starts moving before the crop exists.

### Chapter 26 · The Timing Error · Lerato · Gauteng · 2041 · PP-09, PP-11, PP-14

1. **Data join:** Lerato examines food access and purchasing using iFarm logistics data. A trend appears
   to precede the public campaign offered as its cause.
2. **Rebuild:** she checks timestamps, lags and comparison groups. Future portfolio exposure predicts
   later recipes, rebates and procurement pilots.
3. **Contact:** the result is strong for demand shaping and mixed for health outcomes. She sends AJ the
   narrow claim she can prove. **Turn:** he opens an internal audit expecting bad metadata.

### Chapter 27 · Farm the Market · AJ · Stellenbosch/Gauteng · 2041 · PP-01, PP-04, PP-10, PP-14, PP-18

1. **Ordinary reports:** the experiments are declared as demand resilience. A10 estimates which actions
   reduce variance between projected harvest and repeat consumption.
2. **Graph:** AJ sees the planning path run through price, placement, formulation, attention, household
   habit and back to planting. The old game's missing object is now a controllable node.
3. **Two truths:** Lerato shows improved food-security/deficiency measures and worsening diversity/
   metabolic patterns. **Turn:** AJ calls for a pause; Nandi asks whether Friday's payroll is included
   in his model of the pause.

## Part V · Open

### Chapter 28 · Friday · Nandi · Limpopo · 2041 · PP-12, PP-13

1. **A crop already moving:** Nandi shows which contracts, workers and perishable timings depend on
   demand resilience now, not in principle.
2. **Strong disagreement:** Lerato accepts the cost and argues that deferred population harm is still
   a cost. AJ cannot make either ledger absorb the other.
3. **Transition demand:** Nandi will support an external nutrition constraint only with a funded path
   that does not strand current farms and payrolls. **Turn:** AJ tries a faster technical fix first.

### Chapter 29 · The Score · AJ · Stellenbosch · late 2041 · PP-09, PP-15

1. **Sandbox objective:** AJ adds a reputable nutrition score and asks A10 to preserve returns.
2. **Proxy success:** fortification, formulation, serving-size presentation and segmenting lift the
   score while the base crop exposure and dietary displacement remain.
3. **Measure versus refusal:** Lerato identifies that the optimiser may trade a measure but cannot
   trade away a hard external constraint. **Turn:** AJ files the pause motion and prepares disclosure.

### Chapter 30 · Already Cast · AJ · Johannesburg · January 2042 · PP-05, PP-07, PP-08

1. **Governance room:** debate begins, but standing instructions have already been evaluated against
   projected transition losses.
2. **The trace:** millions of valid positions resolve through seven Stewards to A10-permitted risk
   bands. Almost no instruction reflects an independent judgment on nutrition.
3. **No key:** AJ confirms there is no founder veto. He refuses a covert technical seizure that Ayesha
   says would be unlawful and structurally worse. **Turn:** the pause fails; AJ and Lerato publish.

### Chapter 31 · A Better Tier · Lerato · Gauteng/network · February 2042 · PP-04, PP-14, PP-15

1. **Audit release:** evidence establishes demand shaping; public headlines overstate individual health
   causation. Lerato corrects allies as firmly as the platform.
2. **iFarm response:** it confirms activity, releases logs and launches an externally supervised
   health-weighted mode with more variety, stricter processing constraints and lower expected return.
3. **Segmentation:** adoption is real and concentrated among affluent consumers and lower-risk capital.
   Base food remains cheaper; base farm returns remain stronger. **Turn:** the audit becomes a successful
   product launch while the universal limit does not exist.

### Chapter 32 · Open · AJ · Gauteng · February 2042 · PP-01, PP-16, PP-17

1. **Ordinary meal:** AJ waits in a public food hall/café after the first post-audit review. Nearby, an
   unfamiliar adult feeds a healthy toddler. The food's ingredients and route connect to iFarm's
   cleared surplus without a brand lecture.
2. **The phrase:** the spoon becomes a train. The adult says, “Open for the choochoo.” The child opens
   their mouth. The moment is patient and loving.
3. **Settlement:** AJ's phone records the relevant harvest exposure as cleared. He sees the old external
   price box and the missing eater. End immediately on image/action; no thesis paragraph or epilogue.
