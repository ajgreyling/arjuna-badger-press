# CONTENT_RATING — *The Firmament* sequence

> The content ceiling, binding on every drafting pass. **Rating: New Adult / 18+ literary.** Adult themes
> — sex, violence, trauma, grief — handled with **literary restraint.** Frank about the human truth,
> tasteful about the depiction. Not shelved as children's/teen YA; adult fiction with taste.

## The one-line

**Sex is real and present; it is not the genre and not explicit on the page.** Competent, traumatised
adults thrown together after shared danger will reach for each other — that is true to the people, and
pretending otherwise would be a lie. But this is not a romance and not erotica: the *fact*, the *charge*,
and above all the *aftermath and its politics* carry the weight — **the camera cuts before the act.**

## The heat ceiling (LOCKED — fade-to-black / implied)

- **What's on the page:** the reaching-for-each-other, the tension and the charge, the decision, the
  closing door or the hard cut — and then the **morning-after**, which is the part that matters (who
  reached for whom, what it costs, how it re-wires the house). Frank that it happened; unsqueamish;
  adult.
- **What's NOT on the page:** choreography of the act; clinical or pornographic detail; the camera
  lingering on bodies. Implication and restraint, not anatomy. (Register: *Station Eleven*, *The Road*,
  good literary post-apocalyptic — adults are adults, the reader is trusted, the camera has taste.)
- **Desire is mutual, embodied, and never leering** (inherits THE_COURT_TWELVE): when attraction is in
  frame it's adult and reciprocal, never the narration ogling a body. The "Lara Croft with a real chest"
  brief = capable AND embodied AND never a fantasy object — that rule governs every sensual beat.

## Violence & trauma (matched restraint)

- **Violence:** real and consequential, not gratuitous or gore-lingering. The horror is *cleanness,
  dread, and cost* (STYLE_GUIDE: trust the image, don't caption the awe), not splatter. Deaths land
  emotionally, not viscerally-for-its-own-sake.
- **Trauma & grief:** unflinching but not exploitative — the SA galgehumor register (DARK_HUMOUR) is
  exactly how these people carry the unbearable; the pain is honoured, never wallowed in.
- **Language:** frank, earthy, adult — profanity where the people are profane (the *oom*, the soldier),
  never for shock. SA idiom and code-switching welcome (register is character).

## Why this rating fits the book

The book's real content is **epistemic, spiritual, and political** — the reveal, the fulfilment, the
house politics, the choosing-each-other. Sex and violence are *textures of adult life under pressure,*
not the draw. A fade-to-black ceiling keeps the focus where the book lives (the people, the SynergyEngine)
while being honest that these are grown, embodied humans. It reads as *literary adult with restraint,*
accessible to an 18+ readership without tipping into either coyness or exploitation.

## Binding notes

- **The SynergyEngine, not the sex scene, is where intimacy pays off** (SYNERGY_ENGINE §3): a coupling
  matters for what it *does to the house* — the shifted alignments, the jealousy, the betting pool, the
  morning-after politics — far more than for the act itself. Draft the *consequences*, imply the act.
- **Consistent ceiling across the series.** Book I (the sleeper) is barely on this axis (Neva's arc is
  interior); Book II (the ensemble) is where it lives — same ceiling throughout.
- **No content involving minors, ever, in any sexual frame.** All romantic/sexual currents are between
  adult members of the Court. (Jorik the Innocent is a young *adult*; his arc is faith and hope, not a
  sexualised one.)
- **When in doubt, cut earlier.** The restraint is the taste. If a beat feels like it's tipping toward
  choreography or leering, it's past the line — pull the camera back.
