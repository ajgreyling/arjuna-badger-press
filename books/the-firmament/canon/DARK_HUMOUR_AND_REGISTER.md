# DARK_HUMOUR_AND_REGISTER — *The Firmament* sequence

> A binding voice law across all books. **The register is South African galgehumor: _"Ons lag, want
> anders huil ons."_ — We laugh, because if we don't, we cry.** This is not comic relief bolted onto a
> serious book; it is the *worldview* of people who cope with catastrophe by making it funny so they can
> carry it. The humour rides *on top of* the pain, never instead of it.

## What it is (and where it comes from)

- **SA dark humour / galgehumor.** The joke told over the grave; the braai lit next to the ruin; the
  swearword that is really a prayer. It is how competent, traumatised, resilient people actually survive —
  they take the hardest thing in the room and make it *laughable* precisely because it is unbearable.
- **It is love, not cruelty.** The best of this humour is aimed at *the situation* and at *oneself*, and
  it draws people together. When it punches, it punches *up* — at fate, at the machine, at death — never
  down at the vulnerable. (Ties to the house all-sides law: mock the situation, not the wounded.)
- **It is a specifically South African gift AJ brings.** The dryness, the understatement, the
  gallows-wit under pressure, the code-switch between languages mid-joke, the way a *dorp* funeral holds
  weeping and laughter in the same hour. Bring it authentically; it is one of the book's truest textures.

## The binding rules

1. **The humour is load-bearing on top of the pain, never a substitute for it.** The same scene must be
   allowed to hold a man weeping and a man laughing. If a joke lets the reader (or the writer) *off* the
   pain, it's the wrong joke. If it lets them *carry* the pain, it's right.
2. **Earn the moment the joke fails.** The Fixer (HUMAN_SPINE) jokes at every funeral — so the page where
   the joke finally *won't come*, where he cracks and cries, is the most devastating in the book. Humour
   as armour only works dramatically if the armour is shown to fail once, at the right cost.
3. **Never at the expense of the vulnerable.** Galgehumor mocks death, the machine, the absurdity, and
   the self. It does not mock grief, faith, the frightened, or the dead. (All-sides law; SCRIPTURE
   reverence rule extends here.)
4. **It keeps the tone off both cliffs.** Book II's reboot-optimism (Bregman) could tip into *saccharine*;
   the catastrophe could tip into *grimdark.* The dark humour is the exact instrument that holds the
   middle — practical hope, earned, with the ache always visible under the laugh.
5. **Register is character.** Different people carry it differently: the Fixer's open gallows-jokes, the
   Sergeant's dry deadpan, the Nerd's mordant technical wit, the Bushcrafter's near-silent one-liners.
   Humour is a fingerprint (HUMAN_SPINE friction map), not a house style pasted on everyone.

## Book I vs. Book II

- **Book I (the sleeper):** the register is present but *submerged* — dread's version of galgehumor. The
  sealed people joke blackly about the wall, the reassignments, the ration, in the low voice of people
  who know not to be overheard. It humanises the flat world without breaking its dread. Lean, dry, rare.
- **Book II (the reboot):** the register comes into full voice — the ensemble's coping mechanism, their
  bonding-language, and the tonal engine that keeps the rebuild warm without going soft. The **vinyl
  party** (SET_PIECE_VINYL_PARTY) is its purest expression: grief and joy in one room, dancing to dead
  people's music at the end of the world.

## The one-line to keep on the wall

> *Ons lag, want anders huil ons.* — Everything in this book that is funny is also, if you look one
> inch to the left, unbearable. That is the point. That is the people.
