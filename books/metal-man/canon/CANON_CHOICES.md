# CANON_CHOICES — *The Metal Man* (LOCKED)

> The decisions the whole book leans on. Change these only by conscious amendment, never by drift.

## CC-01 — It is gospel, and the book never litigates it.
Not "was he a magician." Not both-sides-and-you-decide. It happened. The ambiguity is relocated to
*gift vs. infestation*, not *real vs. fake*. [`THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md`](THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md)

## CC-02 — The power is a reading before it is a bending.
He does not command metal; he receives its history, and occasionally writes to it. "Bending" is what
other people call the smallest visible symptom. [`THE_MEMORY_OF_METAL.md`](THE_MEMORY_OF_METAL.md)

## CC-03 — Metal only, and every apparent exception is findable.
Drawings, seeds, dice — there is always a metal object doing the work, and an attentive reader can
locate it in every scene. This is a promise to the reader and it is kept 100% of the time.

## CC-04 — The spoon is camouflage, and by 1973 he is in on it.
Carson is a decision, not a failure. Everything vulgar in his later career is maintained camouflage.
The reader learns this in Act III and re-reads Act I differently.

## CC-05 — Randi is honoured, and never confirmed.
His work is real and good; his method is sound; his blind spot is structural. The suggestion that he
knew is **Uri's inference, held as unproven to the last page.**
[`THE_CONTAINMENT.md`](THE_CONTAINMENT.md#3-what-randi-is-binding--get-this-wrong-and-the-book-is-worthless)

## CC-06 — The intelligence is not a spacecraft and not a god.
It is a pattern metal can hold, which industrial civilisation accidentally gave a nervous system. No
craft, no occupants, no abduction, no religious vocabulary in the narration.
[`THE_INTELLIGENCE.md`](THE_INTELLIGENCE.md)

## CC-07 — Uri is the antenna, not the prophet — and also a boy who stood in the wrong place.
Both, held simultaneously, from chapter two onward.

## CC-08 — Hanna is the anchor and has no secrets.
The only person for whom he is not the metal man. She has never once asked him to do it. She solves
the one thing that gets solved.

## CC-09 — The confederate allegation is not adopted, rebutted, or raised.
In a book where the powers are real it has no work to do, and Shipi is a private person.
[`LIVING_PERSONS.md`](LIVING_PERSONS.md#2-the-five-hard-rules)

## CC-10 — He cheats twice, publicly, and is caught.
Non-negotiable. It is the credibility mechanism for everything else, and the two most human chapters
in the novel.

## CC-11 — The flesh border is refused for four acts and crossed once.
Metal inside a living body. Planted in 1967, detonated in ch. 39, and it is what the ending costs.

## CC-12 — The ending resolves the plot and refuses the question.
The world does not understand what it saw and calls it a stunt by nightfall — the last and kindest
containment. Both readings stand undamaged. [`PLOT.md`](PLOT.md#the-ending-posture-binding)

## CC-13 — Danny Ofir is fictional and gets the last words.
No real "mini-Geller" child is identified, named, or characterised.

## CC-14 — The date is 20 December 2026, his eightieth birthday.
Drawn at Menlo Park in 1973 and meaningless for fifty-three years. This fixes the novel's climax in
the immediate near-future, which is deliberate: the book is published into the month it describes.

## CC-15 — Title: *The Metal Man*.
Pulpy on purpose — the title *is* the containment strategy in three words, and the book earns it back
by Act IV. Subtitle deferred to cover stage. [`SEED_STORY.md`](SEED_STORY.md#the-title)
