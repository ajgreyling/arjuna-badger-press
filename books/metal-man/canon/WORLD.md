# WORLD — *The Metal Man*

> Six eras. Each gets a light, specific, non-nostalgic palette: one or two true details, never a
> costume parade. The world is always seen at domestic scale, because that is where the book lives.

## I. Tel Aviv, 1946–1957 — *the small kitchen*

A young state, poor and loud. Rationing austerity, tzena. Kerosene, primus stoves, tin. An
apartment on Betzalel Yaffe Street with a shared stair. His mother's sewing machine — a **Singer**,
black and gold, the most complicated metal object in the house and the first thing that ever
answered him. The Arab garden across the road, overgrown, with an iron gate.

The palette: whitewash, dust, orange peel, the smell of hot metal and sewing oil. Everything is
mended rather than replaced, which means everything in the house has a **history in it**, which
means the child is standing in a room full of voices.

**Key fact for the prose:** in 1952 in Tel Aviv, metal is scarce and precious. A spoon is not
disposable. Bending one is not a party trick; it is breaking something the family owns.

## II. Nicosia, 1957–1963 — *the hotel*

His mother's remarriage; the Ritz, a small hotel; Terra Santa College. English arrives here and
never quite fits.

The palette: shutters, cheap cutlery in a hundred identical sets, keys on a board behind a desk —
**a wall of keys**, each carrying the history of every hand that ever turned it, and a boy who has to
learn not to touch the board. The first time he understands the gift as something to be *managed*.

Cyprus in these years is a place with soldiers in it and things being blown up. He learns what
military metal feels like before he ever wears a uniform.

## III. Israel, 1963–1971 — *the uniform and the stage*

Paratroopers. June 1967, and shrapnel. **The central fact of his life that the world does not know:
the thing does not work on the metal that is inside people.** It never has. He lies in a field
hospital surrounded by young men full of steel and cannot move a single fragment, and the failure
makes him, for the first time, a man rather than a phenomenon.

Then: modelling, and Shipi, and Hanna, and the discovery that in a nightclub in Jaffa the thing that
was a curse in a classroom is worth money.

The palette: khaki, dust, hospital enamel, cigarette smoke, a stage the size of a table, and metal
folding chairs.

**Binding:** the 1967 war is rendered from inside a nineteen-year-old with no politics and no
overview. No strategy, no geopolitics, no commentary. A boy, a noise, and afterwards a ward.

## IV. America and Britain, 1972–1975 — *the sealed room and the studio*

The two rooms the act alternates between, and they are the same room with different lighting:

**Menlo Park.** Institutional carpet, a shielded chamber, a Faraday cage, sealed envelopes, an
oscillograph. Men in short sleeves being extremely careful. The pleasure of these chapters is
*procedure*.

**The studio.** Burbank and then Shepherd's Bush. Hot lights, a floor manager's hand, a table of
props somebody else has selected, and beyond the camera an audience of forty million people whose
cutlery drawers are all, briefly, in play.

The palette: brown, orange, formica, cigarette smoke indoors, the specific 1970s ugliness of both
science and television.

## V. The long middle, 1976–2015 — *the sofa*

Reading, England. Sonning-on-Thames, a house by the river. Four decades in which the most
extraordinary man alive appears on daytime television with crystals.

The palette softens and cheapens: chat-show sofas, motivational paperbacks with his face on them,
merchandise, a Cadillac welded over with thousands of bent spoons. This era must be written **with
affection and without embarrassment** — the reader is watching camouflage being maintained, and only
finds that out later.

Also here: the litigation, the dowsing contracts, Michael Jackson, the Isle of Lamb, the museum in
Jaffa. And the quiet accumulation of newspaper clippings about metal fatigue that nobody notices him
collecting.

## VI. The present — *the near future*

2026. He is seventy-nine, turning eighty on 20 December. Sonning and Jaffa. Grandchildren.
A converted soap factory full of spoons.

The world of Act IV–V is our world, one small step on: an aging global infrastructure of metal —
bridges past design life, reactors extended past licence, satellites, centrifuges, an internet with
its skeleton in the ground — and a rate of unexplained material failure that is, if you plot it,
very slightly and very steadily rising.

The palette returns to the first one. A kitchen. A drawer. A woman drying a bowl.

## The through-object

**The spoon from the soup, 1952.** His mother keeps it, bent, in a drawer, for fifty-one years. It
passes to him in 2003. It is in his pocket in the last chapter. It is the only object in the novel
present in all five acts, and the last sentence of the book is about it.
