# THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE — *The Metal Man* (BINDING)

> The single posture decision that makes this book itself. Everything else in canon serves it.

## The rule

**We treat Geller's story as gospel.**

Not "was he a magician." Not "can spoon-bending be replicated." Not "here are both sides, dear
reader, you decide." Those are the questions of the literature that already exists, and that
literature has been arguing with itself since 1973 without moving an inch.

In this book, **it happened.** All of it. The garden, the light, the spoon in the soup, the compass,
the watches, the drawings, the laboratory results, the thing in the Negev. The novel does not
adjudicate because the novel is not in doubt.

## Why this is a craft decision, not a credulity decision

A book that keeps asking *is it real?* has to keep its protagonist at arm's length, because the
moment you go inside his head you have to know the answer. The ambiguity that looks like
sophistication is actually a refusal to commit — and it produces a cold book about an argument.

Commit, and something better becomes available: **you get to write the inside of a miracle.** What
it costs. What it is like to be five and have the world go soft. What it is like to be nineteen and
find out that the thing in your hands does not work on shrapnel. What it is like to be believed by
the wrong people, and disbelieved by everyone else, and to understand — slowly, and then all at once
— that the disbelief was arranged, and that you agreed to it.

That is a novel. The other thing is a documentary with a shrug at the end.

## The house all-sides law still applies — but it moves

The press does not flatten opposing positions. That law is not suspended here; it is **relocated**.
The book is not evenly balanced between *psychic* and *fraud*. It is evenly balanced between:

- **the reading that this is a gift** — a man given the keys to matter, who spends his life trying to
  give the world something wonderful and is punished for it; and
- **the reading that this is an infestation** — a man used as a doorway by something that does not
  care about him, whose "gift" is a symptom, whose whole biography is an antenna being tuned by
  something patient.

Both readings are fully alive at the end. Neither is arbitrated. The ending resolves the plot and
refuses the question. (House posture — see `../../verdigris/canon/THE_TWO_READINGS.md` for the
sibling implementation.)

## What the doubters get

**Everything except the last word.** This is binding, and it is a matter of respect.

- **James Randi is never a fool and never a villain of convenience.** He is a serious, brave,
  genuinely honourable man who spent his life protecting vulnerable people from predators, and who
  was *correct* about a very large number of them. His method is sound. The book gives him his best
  arguments, in his own register, and lets them land. See [`THE_CONTAINMENT.md`](THE_CONTAINMENT.md).
- **The failures are real and are not explained away.** When nothing happens, nothing happens, and
  the book sits in the silence rather than inventing an excuse. This is a rule with teeth: a gospel
  in which the miracle is always on tap is a cartoon. The power is unreliable, and its unreliability
  is characterisation.
- **The vulgarity is kept.** The crystals, the merchandise, the celebrity, the self-promotion, the
  Cadillac welded over with cutlery. He is not sanitised into a saint. A man can be an authentic
  miracle and an exhausting self-promoter in the same afternoon, and the book finds that funny and
  human rather than embarrassing.

## The one thing the book never does

**It never winks.** Not once. There is no line, no image, no arch narrator's aside that lets a
sceptical reader off the hook with "of course, this is all a bit of fun." The moment the book winks,
the spell breaks and it becomes the cowardly version.

The reader may disbelieve. The *book* may not.
