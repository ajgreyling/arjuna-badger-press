# LIVING_PERSONS — *The Metal Man* (BINDING)

> The house already runs a consent register for protected identifiers. This book is the hardest case
> the press has taken on: a novel whose protagonist is alive, is eighty, and has litigated over his
> own portrayal for fifty years.

## 1. The posture, and why it is defensible

The novel's central claim about Uri Geller is that **his abilities are real**. He is the hero of it.
He is brave, generous, frightened, vain, funny, and ultimately self-sacrificing. It portrays him as
telling the truth for seventy years while the world called him a liar.

This is worth stating plainly because it inverts the usual risk. The dangerous book about Geller is
the one that asserts fraud — that is the defamatory shape, and it is the shape of most of the
existing literature. **This book asserts the opposite of the injurious claim.**

That is not a licence. It is a starting position, and the rest of this file is the discipline.

## 2. The five hard rules

1. **No invented public statement.** Nothing Uri Geller is shown saying in a documented public
   setting — a broadcast, an interview, a court, a published book — may be fabricated. Quote the
   record or stage the scene without the quote. This rule is absolute and applies to every real
   person in the book.
2. **Private scenes are signalled as invention by the front matter, not by hedging in the prose.**
   The Author's Note does the disclosure so the novel can be a novel. (House pattern: the Road Books
   carry exactly this note — see `books/um-welt`, `books/the-long-road`.)
3. **No crime, no cruelty, no vice that is not in the record.** He is never shown committing a
   fraud, betraying his wife, harming anyone, or doing anything a reasonable reader would think less
   of him for and that did not happen. His documented vulgarities — the merchandising, the
   self-promotion, the litigiousness — are fair game and are drawn with affection.
4. **The family is treated with more care than the man.** Hanna Geller and Shipi Shtrang are private
   people who did not choose public life. They are drawn warmly, minimally, and never given a secret,
   a betrayal, or an interior failing. **The persistent published allegation that Shipi acted as a
   confederate is not adopted, not rebutted, and not raised** — in a book where the powers are real,
   it has no work to do, and repeating it would be gratuitous.
5. **The children are invented.** Uri Geller's real children are named in public sources and are
   otherwise private adults. Any child character with a role in the plot is fictional and clearly
   not them.

## 3. The dead

**James Randi** (1928–2020), **Andrija Puharich** (1918–1995), **Johnny Carson** (1925–2005),
**Wernher von Braun** (1912–1977), **Michael Jackson** (1958–2009), **David Bohm** (1917–1992),
**John Taylor** (1931–2012), **Eldon Byrd** (1939–2002).

Defamation claims do not generally survive the person in the jurisdictions that matter here, but the
duty does. Rules:

- **Randi gets the fullest care of anyone in the book.** The Act III reading of him is *Uri's
  inference*, held explicitly as unproven, and the novel never states as fact that Randi knew. His
  real work is honoured at length. Anything that would read as calling him a liar in his own cause
  is cut. See [`THE_CONTAINMENT.md`](THE_CONTAINMENT.md#3-what-randi-is-binding--get-this-wrong-and-the-book-is-worthless).
- **Puharich is the tragedy, not the fool.** The easy version makes him a crank whose alien-contact
  book sank the science. The book's version is that he was a serious physician who received
  something real and had only the wrong vocabulary for it — and lost everything. He gets dignity.
- **Michael Jackson appears only where the record puts him**, briefly, and the novel does not
  litigate anything else about his life. Nothing about the allegations. He is a friend at a wedding.

## 4. The living

**Russell Targ** and **Harold Puthoff** are alive. They are working scientists with reputations they
have defended for fifty years. They are portrayed as rigorous, careful, and ultimately correct —
which is what they have always maintained — and they are given no private failings and no invented
misconduct. Their published methodology is described accurately.

Everyone else in the laboratory and agency scenes is **composite or invented**, and named so as not
to collide with a real post-holder. Check every invented officer, editor and producer name against
the historical record before drafting.

## 5. Contact

The house standard, and it applies here at full strength: **this is a living subject and the press
writes to living subjects.** Before any publish decision, the recommendation on file is that Uri
Geller — or his representatives — be written to, told exactly what the book is, and offered the
manuscript.

Not for permission. The book does not require permission and does not ask for it. For the reason the
press does this: because writing seventy years of a man's life and not telling him is a thing done
*to* someone, and this house does not do that.

> He is also, by every account, a man who answers his post. There is a version of this where he is
> delighted, and a version where he sues, and the only way to find out is the honest one.
