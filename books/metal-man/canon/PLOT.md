# PLOT — *The Metal Man*

> Five acts, forty chapters, ~115k. Beat sheet with PP-ID crosswalk to
> [`PLANTS_AND_PAYOFFS.md`](PLANTS_AND_PAYOFFS.md).

## OVERTURE

**1. — London, 23 November 1973.** The BBC. *Dimbleby Talk-In*, live.

He asks the country to fetch its broken watches and hold them near the set. Across Britain, stopped
watches start. The switchboard takes so many calls it fails. **[PP-001 clock ledger opens]**

Three things the broadcast does not know it has done:

- In a house in Lincolnshire, a watch that has been in a box of a dead RAF officer's effects since
  **1947** begins to run — and its hands are going **backwards**. **[PP-002]**
- In a locked government archive in the Home Counties, a clock nobody has wound since 1961 does the
  same. **[PP-003 — the corridor]**
- In a warehouse of recovered wreckage, **every clock and instrument taken from one particular
  aircraft crash starts together**, and they all read the same wrong time. **[PP-004]**

Closing image: Uri in the hospitality room afterwards with his hands shaking, being told he was
wonderful, hearing the high granular crackle that nobody else can hear, coming from every direction
at once.

## ACT I — THE BOY WHO BROKE THE SPOON *(ch. 2–10 · 1946–1971)*

**2.** Tel Aviv, 1952. The apartment, the mother, the Singer sewing machine **[PP-005]**. The Arab
garden across the road and its iron gate. The flash of light — rendered flatly, from a five-year-old's
eye-level, with no interpretation whatsoever. He comes home and does not mention it.

**3.** The soup. The spoon folds in his mouth at the table. His mother's face. The spoon is not
thrown away because in 1952 you do not throw away a spoon — it goes in the drawer, bent.
**[PP-006 — the through-object; present in all five acts]**

**4.** School. He is not good at it. The compass in the geography lesson that turns to follow him
across the room, and the teacher's decision to be angry rather than curious — **the first time the
world chooses ridicule over enquiry, and it is a child's classroom.** The high crackle: he assumes
everyone hears it, mentions it once, and learns not to.

**5.** His mother's card games, and the winnings he should not know about. The father who is not
there. The beginning of the private arithmetic: *this is useful, and it makes people frightened of
me, and those two facts are the same fact.*

**6.** Nicosia, 1959. The hotel. The board of keys behind the desk **[PP-007]**, and the discipline
of not touching. Terra Santa College; English arriving badly. A boy learning to be charming as a
form of camouflage — the first draft of the man.

**7.** Israel, 1965. Paratroopers. The uniform, the noise, the ordinariness. Metal everywhere and
none of it interesting: rifles are annealed, mass-produced, historyless. He is, for the first time
in his life, in a place where the crackle is quiet, and it is nearly a holiday.

**8.** June 1967. The war, from inside a twenty-year-old with no politics. He is wounded.
**The central private fact of his life:** in the ward, surrounded by young men full of shrapnel, he
cannot move a single fragment. Not one. He tries for three nights. **[PP-008 — the flesh border;
detonates in ch. 39]** The failure makes him a man instead of a phenomenon.

**9.** 1969. Modelling. Hanna, and the way she does not react. Shipi, aged fourteen, who reacts more
than anyone ever has and never stops. The first paid performance, in a hall, for money, and the
discovery that a curse is a career. **[PP-009 — "the metal man" used as a poster line, contemptuously]**

**10.** 1970–71. Fame in Israel, which means suspicion in Israel. The newspapers, the accusations,
the first debunkings. A. Meir attends a show and does not introduce himself. Puharich arrives at the
end of the chapter, and the last line of Act I is an American saying *I have come a long way to
watch you do that again.*

> **Metal register I** — heat, folding, dark. Pure record.

## ACT II — THE LABORATORY *(ch. 11–19 · 1971–1974)*

**11.** Puharich in Israel. Testing in hotel rooms. The man's seriousness, his equipment, his
kindness — and the first flicker of the vocabulary problem: he keeps asking Uri *who is sending it.*
The tapes that erase themselves. **[PP-010]**

**12.** Menlo Park, November 1972. Targ and Puthoff. The pleasure of procedure: the shielded room,
the protocol, the controls. First results. The scientists' care is the most attractive thing that has
ever happened to him — **for the first time in his life, people are looking at him and not at the
spoon.**

**13.** The controls tighten. And tighten. Each chapter-beat here is a tightening, and each time,
something still happens. The magnetometer. The sealed containers. The double-blind drawings.

**14.** **Eldon Byrd and the nitinol.** Short, technical, cold. Memory metal is given a new memory.
Byrd understands immediately what this means about a firing pin, goes quiet, and files a report that
is not believed. **[PP-011 — the escalation from parlour to ordnance]**

**15.** The envelope failures. Uri draws things that were not in the envelope:
a **metallic corridor** **[PP-003 payoff begins]**; a **black sun** **[PP-012]**; **three figures
without faces** **[PP-013]**; **coordinates in the Negev** **[PP-014]**; and a date in a hand he does
not use — **20.12.2026** **[PP-015]**. All filed as noise. Targ's careful note: *subject produced
non-target material of high internal consistency.*

**16.** The agencies fall out. Asset, weapon, or **contagion**. The word that changes everything is
contagion, and it is introduced in a memo about *children*. **[PP-016]**

**17.** Erasure by demonstration — he works it out at a bench, alone, holding a spoon he has just
bent and finding it empty. **The only way to prove what he is, is to destroy the proof.**
He tells Targ. Targ already knows and has not said.

**18.** London, June 1974. Birkbeck. Hasted, Bohm. A serious physicist saying a serious sentence
about the certainty of matter, and Uri understanding perhaps a third of it and recognising all of it.
The high point of his intellectual life. He is never in a room like this again.

**19.** October 1974: the *Nature* paper. Two weeks of legitimacy. Then Puharich's book is published —
*Spectra*, *Hoova*, the spacecraft — and the door closes on the whole field in a month. The scene is
Uri reading the book in a hotel room and understanding, page by page, that the most loyal man he
knows has just killed him. **[PP-010 payoff]**

> **Metal register II** — record, with the first repetition in it.

## ACT III — THE GREAT DEBUNKING *(ch. 20–28 · 1973–1995)*

**20.** **Carson, 1 August 1973 — replayed from inside.** *(Deliberately out of sequence; the reader
has had it as a famous failure for two hundred pages.)* He can feel every object on that table. He
understands what happens by morning if he performs. He chooses twenty-two minutes of nothing.
Afterwards, the hotel bathroom, and Shipi running the tap so the sound is covered, and not asking.
**[PP-017 — the decision]**

**21.** Randi, properly introduced, at full strength and entirely sympathetic. His childhood, his
escapology, his rage at people who take money from the bereaved. **The book makes you like him.**

**22.** Randi's method, demonstrated: he bends a spoon by trickery and shows you exactly how, and it
is dazzling. And the careful, scrupulous thing he never quite says. **[PP-018]**

**23.** The children. Thousands of them. Danny Ofir, aged eleven, in a kitchen in Reading, bending a
fork in front of his mother and being smacked for it. **[PP-019 — Danny, beat 1 of 3]**
The testing programmes. Some of the children are cheating. **Some are not**, and the ones who are not
get letters, and then visits.

**24.** Uri is shown the file on the children. This is the chapter where he consents. Nobody threatens
him. A reasonable person explains a reasonable position over a good lunch, and by the coffee he
understands that as long as he is a joke, they are safe. **[PP-016 payoff]**

**25.** The cheating chapters. Twice, publicly, needing the money, he does it with his thumb, badly,
and is caught. **The most humiliating and most human material in the book** — and the reason the
reader trusts everything else.

**26.** The eighties. The reinvention. Dowsing contracts, crystals, motivational paperbacks, the
Cadillac **[PP-020]**. Written with affection. Hanna, watching him build the camouflage, saying
nothing, once putting her hand flat on his back in a car park.

**27.** The litigation years. He sues Randi and loses and sues again. **The counter-reading is fully
armed here:** a man who cannot let it go, whose "containment" story may simply be the kindest
available explanation for a career that became a punchline. The novel gives the counter-reading its
best chapter.

**28.** **The corridor, 1993.** Ninety seconds, a conference hotel, both of them, no witnesses. What
is said is banal. What is not said is the whole act. They shake hands. Randi's hand is dry and warm
and he does not let go quite quickly enough. **[PP-018 payoff — and never confirmed]**

> **Metal register III** — record, with anticipation in it.

## ACT IV — THE METAL WORLD *(ch. 29–35 · 1995–2026)*

**29.** Puharich dying, 1994–95, entirely lucid and entirely wrong and knowing it. The one apology in
the novel. **[PP-010 closes]**

**30.** The long quiet. He is a brand. He is on daytime television. He is sixty. And he has, for
twenty years, been cutting things out of newspapers: a bridge hanger, a turbine disc, a reaction
wheel, an airframe crack in a place the analysis says is impossible. **[PP-021 — the clippings]**

**31.** Dr. Frances Okonjo-Hale, metallurgist, agrees to look at the clippings as a favour and to be
polite. She stops being polite about forty minutes in. **The geometry.** Not a map of anything human.
Refining. Interval shortening.

**32.** The unbearable inference, arrived at alone, over two chapters: **it is learning from him.**
Every demonstration since 1973 has been a lesson in controlled deformation, delivered to a student he
did not know was in the room. He taught it. On chat shows. For money. For fifty years.
*(Both readings live: he taught it to be gentle / he taught it to be precise.)*

**33.** The Negev **[PP-014 payoff]**. The 1952 recovery. The report, read at seventy-eight, and the
metallurgist's sentence: *regular to a degree I am not able to explain by cooling alone.* The
Widmanstätten figure that is not a cooling figure. Okonjo-Hale, very quietly: *this is writing.*

**34.** **Hanna and the date.** A kitchen. She is drying a bowl. He is telling her about the drawings
for the hundredth time. She says, without looking up, *that's your birthday.* Four seconds. Thirty
years. **[PP-015 payoff]** The quietest scene in the book.

**35.** The corridor, entered. A. Meir, very old, does the one decent thing: he gets him in.
**[PP-003 payoff]** The object. The black sun **[PP-012 payoff]**. And the three figures resolve —
not aliens: **the shape of the socket, with whoever was in it worn away.** The fourth is him.
**[PP-013 payoff]** He is seventy-nine years old and he finally looks at the picture he drew in 1973.

> **Metal register IV** — the first thing that could be called intention.

## ACT V — THE LAST BEND *(ch. 36–40 · December 2026)*

**36.** Criticality. The mass reaches the state the black sun was a picture of. What is required is
beyond any parlour demonstration and beyond any one person — which the book has been saying, in the
smallest possible way, since chapter one, every time a viewer at home reported a watch.
**[PP-022 — the audience amplification rule, planted ch. 1, rule 5 of the power]**

**37.** He asks for a camera. Nobody will give him one — he is a joke, and being a joke was the
point, and the camouflage he maintained for fifty years is now the thing preventing him from saving
anyone. **The cost of the containment, presented as a bill.** It is Danny Ofir, sixty, who gets him
on air. **[PP-019, beat 2]**

**38.** **The broadcast.** He asks the world to fetch its spoons, its keys, its broken watches. He
has asked this before, in 1973, and the world laughed and did it anyway. It does it again — not as
spectators. **As a distributed circuit.** The trivial objects are the mechanism. Every clock in the
ledger stops at once. **[PP-001 payoff]**

**39.** **The border.** Rule 9 — metal *inside* the body — the door he has refused for four acts
**[PP-008 payoff]**. The iron in blood. He has to go through it, and the cost is exactly what the
escalation ladder has promised since chapter fourteen. For one minute, everywhere on Earth, metal is
soft. Held in the intimate register, at kitchen scale: a woman in a kitchen watching a bowl sag.

**40.** **Then the clocks restart.** The world does not understand what it has seen and never will —
by nightfall it is a hoax, a stunt, a mass hysteria, a good bit of television. Which is the last and
kindest containment of all, and he is the only one who knows it, and it means his grandchildren are
safe.

Hanna, in the kitchen. The spoon from 1952 in his pocket **[PP-006 closes]**. Danny Ofir gets the
last words. **[PP-019, beat 3]**

The final line is the clock ledger closing.

## The ending posture (BINDING)

**The plot resolves. The question does not.**

At the last page both readings stand undamaged:

- **The gift** — a man given the keys to matter, disbelieved for seventy years, who saved everyone
  and was laughed at for it by nightfall.
- **The infestation** — a man used as a doorway by something patient that needed a nervous system
  and found a boy at a gate, whose entire biography was an antenna being tuned, and whose great
  sacrifice was the last thing it required of him.

Every fact in Act V supports both. Nothing in the novel arbitrates. See
[`THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md`](THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md#the-house-all-sides-law-still-applies--but-it-moves).
