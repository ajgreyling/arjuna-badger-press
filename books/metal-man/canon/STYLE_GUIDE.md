# STYLE_GUIDE — *The Metal Man*

## Two registers

**1. The intimate human (default, ~95% of the book).** Close third on Uri, past tense. Warm, exact,
domestic. The prose lives at the scale of a kitchen table, a hotel room, a dressing room, a
laboratory bench. **The impossible must always arrive inside an ordinary frame** — a woman drying a
bowl, a boy waiting for his mother, a producer checking a running order — because the whole
engine of this book is that the miraculous comes in through the cutlery drawer.

Sentence-level: plain, concrete, unhurried. Nouns doing the work. The bigger the event, the flatter
the delivery. **When something genuinely impossible happens, the prose goes quieter, not louder.**

**2. The metal (rationed to six or seven appearances in the whole novel).** Present tense. No
first-person pronoun for itself, ever — the constructions are impersonal, sequential, physical. It
does not describe; it *records*: force, temperature, duration, order. It never names a person, never
states an opinion, never uses a metaphor.

Model:

> Heat, and then the folding. Heat again. A hand, and the hand is warm on the left side only. Dark.
> Dark for a length that has no name. A hand again, colder. The turning of the edge against
> something that gives. Salt. Dark. Dark.

Every appearance must be deniable as Uri's synaesthesia. **If a metal passage ever tells the reader
something Uri could not know, it has broken the book.**

## The prose laws

1. **Never wink.** No arch aside, no knowing narrator, no line that lets a sceptical reader off the
   hook. The book believes. See [`THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md`](THE_GOSPEL_PREMISE.md#the-one-thing-the-book-never-does).
2. **The household object is the delivery mechanism.** Every act of power in this novel enters
   through a spoon, a watch, a key, a compass, a ring, a nail, a coin, a fork, a pair of scissors. No
   abstractions. No energies. No auras. **If you cannot put it in a kitchen drawer, it does not
   belong in a scene.**
3. **Understate at the point of greatest power.** *(House north-star: flat arithmetic stated as
   plain fact; the reader does the maths and feels the dread. See
   `memory/arjuna-badger-press/action-kinetics-northstar.md`.)* Not "the spoon writhed in his grip
   like a living thing." Rather: *"It went soft in the middle, the way a candle does, and then it
   was two pieces, and the ends were cold."* The most frightening sentence in Act III is a budget
   line in a memo.
4. **The failures are written straight.** When nothing happens, the prose sits in the nothing for as
   long as it actually took. The Carson chapter is twenty-two minutes long and the reader feels every
   one of them. No excuse is offered, then or later.
5. **No trippy passages.** The visionary material — the envelope drawings, the Negev, the corridor —
   is rendered with total clinical control. Flat, specific, and rendered as *drawing*: pencil on
   paper, a hand moving, the sound of the graphite. Psychedelia would let the reader dismiss it as a
   state of mind, and dismissal is what the book exists to refuse.
6. **The technical vocabulary is exact and unglossed.** Annealing, work hardening, residual stress,
   hysteresis, Barkhausen noise, Widmanstätten, nitinol, martensite, lattice. Real terms, used
   correctly, never explained in an aside. Trust the reader; the meaning arrives from context.
   **"Annealed" is the book's most important word and appears at exactly three turning points.**
7. **Period texture, lightly.** 1946 Tel Aviv, 1959 Nicosia, 1969 Jaffa, 1973 Menlo Park, 1973
   Burbank, 1974 London, 1990s Reading, the present. Each era gets a light, specific, non-nostalgic
   palette — one or two true details, never a costume parade. See [`WORLD.md`](WORLD.md).
8. **Hebrew, Hungarian and Yiddish are used without italics or translation** where the meaning is
   carried by context. He thinks in Hebrew and performs in English, and the gap between those two
   facts is a running, unstated ache.
9. **Never say "psychic."** Nobody serious in this book uses the word. The scientists say *effect*
   and *anomaly*; the agencies say *capability*; Randi says *claim*; Hanna says *it*; Uri, in
   private, says nothing at all — he has no word for it, which is rule 10.
10. **He has no vocabulary and the prose must honour that.** When Uri tries to explain what he does,
    he is inarticulate — genuinely, painfully so — and the narration does not rescue him with a
    beautiful metaphor he would never reach for. **The narration is only allowed to be eloquent
    about metal, never about his interior.**

## The title word

"The metal man" is a phrase used *about* him by other people, in contempt or affection, four times
across the novel. He never uses it about himself. The last time it appears, it is not contemptuous
and it is not affectionate, and it is not a metaphor.

## Chapter form

Numbered, headed with the word (`# One`), average 2,800–3,500 words. Scene breaks marked with a
centred em-dash rule. Each of the five acts opens with a dated place-stamp line and closes on the
metal register or on a household object.
