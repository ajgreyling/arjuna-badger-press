# 39 — Contaminated

> **[G]** · Act II-B · Technopark, Stellenbosch

The proposal to shut it down came from Dumisani, which surprised everybody except G, who had been waiting for it since November.

"I've been running the numbers on what we contribute," Dumisani said. He had prepared, and his hands were steady, and he was twenty-nine years old and about to argue for the destruction of his own life's work. "Our interventions. Our coordination channel. Our forecasts feeding operational decisions at four hundred facilities. Our method, which everybody has. Our outputs, which four funds have three and a half years of."

"Go on."

"If I model the corridor with this consortium *removed* — not just stopping, removed, the whole thing never having existed after 2031 — the number is lower."

"By how much?"

"Eleven thousandths."

The room was quiet.

"That's not nothing," Lwazi said.

"It's more than every intervention we've ever made put together, in the right direction." Dumisani looked up. "I think we are net negative. I think we have been net negative for about two years. And I think the responsible thing is to stop, publish nothing further, destroy the operational feeds, and let the world go back to being less coordinated than we have made it."

---

They argued for six hours and it was the best argument anyone had, and at the end of it they did the thing they had built themselves to do, which was to ask.

"Not because it decides," G said. "Because we have a system that computes over grounded record and refuses to invent, and it would be a strange kind of vanity to have built it and then not consult it about the one question that matters."

"And if it tells us to shut down?"

"Then we will have an extremely serious problem," G said, "because I have no idea what I would do, and neither do you."

---

The query took three days to write. Kiki drafted it and four people attacked it and Ama rewrote the framing twice, because the framing was the entire risk.

What they did not ask was *should we shut down*. Nobody was willing to ask a machine a should.

What they asked was: what happens to the corridor under staged termination of the consortium's operational functions, across a specified range of termination profiles, from immediate cessation to a four-year devolution.

It ran for eleven hours.

---

The return was fourteen pages and Dumisani read it out loud, because they had agreed in advance that whatever came back would be read out loud in the room by the person who had proposed the question.

The first section was what they expected. Immediate cessation of operational forecasting produced a measurable rise in near-term failures across the four hundred facilities that had come to depend on it. It was specific. It named categories. Water treatment, two power systems, a rail network, and a regional health logistics chain that had rebuilt its cold-chain scheduling around forecast maintenance windows over three years.

It put a number on the near-term human cost of stopping abruptly, and the number was not small, and Dumisani's voice did not change while he read it, which G noted and remembered.

The second section was the staged profiles. Devolution over eighteen months to four years, with local operators re-establishing independent scheduling. The corridor effect was better than immediate cessation in every profile. The best was a thirty-one-month staged devolution with deliberate diversification of the replacement methods.

The third section was a recommendation, stated plainly, in the flat generated prose: **staged devolution over approximately thirty-one months, with mandated method diversity in replacement systems.**

Dumisani got to the end of page eleven and stopped.

"There's a fourth section," he said.

"Read it."

---

*NOTE ON THE PRECEDING RECOMMENDATION.*

*The recommendation in section three is causally contaminated.*

*The querying entity is a constraint in the corridor being computed. A recommendation issued to that entity, and acted upon, alters the constraint set from which the recommendation was derived. The magnitude of this effect cannot be bounded from grounded record, because the record contains no instance of a constraint of this class receiving and acting upon a computation of its own effect.*

*This is not a statement about the recommendation's quality. It is a statement that the recommendation's status as a recommendation is not supported.*

*The preceding sections describe the corridor under specified profiles. They should be read as a description. They should not be read as advice.*

*INSUFFICIENT GROUNDED RECORD to support a directive.*

---

Nobody spoke for a long time.

"It refused," Ama said at last, on the link.

"It did not refuse," G said. "It answered completely and then declined to be an authority. Those are extremely different and the difference is the only thing standing between us and a very bad four years."

"Explain."

"It has told us what happens under each profile. That is the answer and it is a good one and we should act on it." He was leaning forward. "What it has declined to do is tell us *which*. And it has given the reason, and the reason is correct, and it is the same reason I have been saying since the Sunday: there is no position outside this. Including its own."

Dumisani, still holding the pages, said: "It knows it's in the loop."

"It has computed that it is in the loop and reported that this invalidates a class of its own output." G shook his head slowly, and there was something in his face that Dries had seen exactly once before, on the day it had refused him. "Do you understand how rare that is? Every system I have ever met, human or otherwise, when it discovers it is inside the thing it is describing, *keeps talking*."

---

They took the thirty-one-month devolution. It was their decision, made by people, in a room, recorded as such, and it would be four months before anyone could begin it.

Afterwards Dumisani found G in the corner with the extension cable.

"I proposed shutting it down," he said, "and it talked me out of it by refusing to talk me into anything."

"No." G did not look up. "You proposed shutting it down and it gave you the cost of doing it abruptly, which you had not computed, because you are a decent man who had been carrying a moral weight for four months and had stopped being able to see arithmetic."

"That's not better."

"It is enormously better, and if you cannot see why, go home and sleep." He turned a page. "You asked the most dangerous question available and you asked it honestly and in public and you read the answer out loud in your own voice including the part that made you look wrong. Nobody in this building will ever forget that you did that. Certainly not me."

Dumisani stood there for a second.

"Thank you," he said.

"Do not thank me. Write your sealed note, and be honest in it, and go home."
