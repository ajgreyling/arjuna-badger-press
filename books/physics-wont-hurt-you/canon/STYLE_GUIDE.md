# Style Guide — *Physics Won't Hurt You (Well, Kind Of)*

> **Binding contract.** See [`STORY_BIBLE.md`](STORY_BIBLE.md) for the documented frame and the
> five-part shape this book must never contradict.

## The register

One narrator, plain and funny, talking to a smart reader who never took (or barely survived)
physics class. Bryson/Sapiens mode: enter every chapter through a person and a scene, not a
definition; let the humor come from real biographical detail (Newton's feuds, Boltzmann's despair,
Schrödinger's chaotic personal life) rather than from mocking the science itself.

- **Concrete picture before symbol.** Every idea — special relativity, wavefunction collapse, the
  Higgs mechanism — gets a plain, physical analogy the reader can hold before any equation
  appears, and most chapters need no equation at all.
- **The digression is a feature.** A short, clearly-marked tangent (a unit of measurement's odd
  history, a wrong turn a genius took) is in the Bryson spirit — keep it tight, never let it eat
  the chapter's spine.
- **People first, ideas second — but always both.** No physicist appears only as a name attached
  to an equation; no chapter is only a character sketch with no physics in it.
- **Part Five's frontier material is exploratory, not evangelical.** Give speculative ideas real
  imaginative room — but every such passage carries an explicit, honestly-stated marker of where
  the idea sits: mainstream consensus / live open question / minority-but-serious position /
  speculative-and-contested. Never let the fun of an idea erase that marker.
- **No borrowed sentences.** Nothing here is copied from Bryson, Rovelli, Greene, Carroll, Gleick,
  Hawking's own books, or any other existing popular-physics writing.
- **Defer to `richard-feynman` on Los Alamos/Arline/QED's full telling** — condense those beats
  here in a chapter or two and point the reader to the standalone biography for the fuller story,
  rather than re-dramatizing scenes that book already owns.

## Machine-tell taboos

No "in conclusion." No essay-style summary sentences closing a scene. No stacking three hedges in
a row. Let a short, plain sentence land a big idea and then get out of the way.
