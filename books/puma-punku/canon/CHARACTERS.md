THE CREW: 
- Naila (ND metallurgist/maker, lead POV): sees the world in flows and fits, obsessed with the physical “how”; wiring is pattern-locked, hyperfocus on process, no social smoothing; her arc is learning to let the question stay open, not force closure.
- “Specs” (crew engineer, ADHD, always building test rigs, never finishing the paperwork): voice is kinetic, questioning, quick with a joke or a hack; their flaw is impatience with “just-so” stories.
- Rani (crew historian, autistic, loves footnotes and sources, never looks you in the eye): the memory of the crew, always chasing the original, never the retelling.
- The LENS (ex-war photographer, PTSD, not ND; recurring; sees what others miss, but the camera is a shield): their arc is learning when to lower the lens and let the place speak.
JAKOBUS: the fixer, the thread; never POV, never leads, always hands the moment to crew or local; reads the made thing, asks “what is it for,” fades back.
THE LOCAL: Dr. Lucía Quispe (Aymara/Bolivian archaeologist, site custodian): owns the answer, out-reads the crew, never a puzzle-box for outsiders; her voice is measured, dryly funny, proud and tired; she holds the ceremonial and ancestral meaning of the site, and chooses what to give.
ADVERSARY: The Fundación Viracocha, led in the field by Dr. Ethan Rourke (clean-cut, charming, sincerely convinced of a lost super-civilization, but plays by the rules; their flaw is confirmation bias, not malice). The adversary is never a cabal: just people with funding, a story to prove, and a willingness to take more than they give.
CLOSE-THIRD: All scenes lived through crew perspective, never omniscient, never the local’s inner voice; the local’s knowledge is always a gift, never appropriated.
