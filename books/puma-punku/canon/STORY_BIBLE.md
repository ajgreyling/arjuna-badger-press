A small, odd, neurodiverse crew travels to Puma Punku, the high Andean ruins famed for impossibly-precise stones and mysterious bronze cramps. They chase the official archaeological story — Tiwanaku genius, not aliens — and try to physically replicate the marvels, only to find the "easy" version is hard: their best attempt makes a potato. The one real, checkable mystery (the bronze cramps, cast in situ at altitude, never fully duplicated) is the engine; the crew digs for the craft answer while a well-funded, agenda-driven adversary races to “prove” a lost super-civilization. The local Aymara/Bolivian archaeologist holds the meaning of the place, out-reading the outsiders and grounding the climax: the cramps are not just technical, but ceremonial — a bond of stone, people, and memory. The Why Files register governs: play the official story dead straight, then find the one hole, then wink. Let the reader decide, never confirm or deny. Theme is never spoken. Intended reader: the rabbit-hole wonderer, the one who never quite believes the debunk but never swallows the myth whole. Close-third POV: crew-owned, never omniscient, always seeing through ND wiring. Content bounds: all claims checked and cited; fringe labelled as such; lived local knowledge never overwritten. Creative driver: the “how did they DO that?” of real craft, and the gift of being allowed to say “maybe.”
