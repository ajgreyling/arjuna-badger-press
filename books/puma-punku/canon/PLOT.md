ACT I (ARRIVE): The crew arrives at Puma Punku, following reports that the Fundación Viracocha is about to secure key artifacts and launch a media campaign for a lost super-civilization. They meet Dr. Lucía Quispe, the site’s Aymara custodian, who sizes them up with dry amusement. Jakobus is already on the ground, quietly mapping joinery patterns. The official story is presented: Tiwanaku genius, no aliens, the marvel is the cast bronze cramps.

OBSTACLE: The adversary is ahead — they’ve already staged a high-profile demo (using modern tools) “proving” the site is impossible without lost tech. The crew is challenged to do better: replicate the cramps, full scale, at altitude, with period methods. Locals are wary, the air is thin, materials are scarce. Specs’ test rig leaks, Naila’s bronze cools too soon, Rani’s research finds only partial answers. The potato: their “cramp” is ugly, the fit leaks, the groove cracks.

READ: The crew digs deeper — interviews with local craftspeople, nights in the cold, trading stories with elders. Jakobus finds a pattern: the best cramps are where the stones fit not just physically, but symbolically — in ceremonial lines, where offerings are left. Lucía, seeing their sincerity and failures, opens up: the cramps are more than engineering, they are acts of joining — people to place, ancestors to now.

COST: The adversary, frustrated by lack of “proof,” tries to remove a cramp block for analysis. Lucía confronts them, risking her position. The crew steps in, exposing the adversary’s agenda but refusing to claim the answer for themselves. The place is defended, but not “solved.”

KEY: In the site’s heart, Lucía walks the crew through a ceremonial joining, showing how the act of binding stone is a binding of community — the making is the marvel. The crew’s final attempt, guided by local method, still falls short, but they see the beauty in the failure.

HANDOFF (CLIMAX): The “what is it for” is given: The cramps are a key — technical, social, ceremonial — locking stones and people, surviving earthquakes and centuries, a visible sign of mastery and memory. The crew leaves with wonder, not closure.

BACK MATTER: Every official claim is sourced; the unresolved marvels are real, the fringe labelled. The last page is a wink: “If it’s so easy, why can’t we do it?” The reader is left with maybe.
