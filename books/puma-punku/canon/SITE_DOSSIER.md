SITE: Puma Punku / Tiwanaku (Bolivia)

REAL (binding, sourced facts):
- LOCATION: Puma Punku is part of the Tiwanaku archaeological complex, near Lake Titicaca in Bolivia.
- DATING: Consensus archaeological dating puts the main construction phase at ~500-950 CE (Tiwanaku Classic period); some carbon dates from organic material in the fill run as early as ~536–600 CE, but the megalithic phase is generally placed after 500 CE.
- MATERIALS: The site features both andesite (a volcanic rock, very hard) and red sandstone (softer, but some blocks extremely large — up to ~130 metric tons).
- ARCHITECTURE: Famous for large, precisely cut stone blocks, including the iconic "H-blocks" that interlock; faces are remarkably flat, with sharp angles and tight joints.
- TOOL MARKS: Some blocks show evidence of stone and bronze tools; no evidence of steel or iron tools found at the site.
- METALWORK: Real evidence of bronze cramps (U- or I-shaped), poured molten into carved channels in the stone, to hold blocks together. Metallurgical studies confirm the alloy as arsenical bronze. Some cramps found in situ; casting channels visible in blocks.
- JOINERY: Blocks feature complex joinery — interlocking notches, T-shaped grooves, and mortise-and-tenon arrangements.
- NO EVIDENCE of lost advanced technology or ancient power tools; the precision, while impressive, is within the range of what can be achieved with hand tools, templates, and abrasives, given enough time and skill.
- LOCAL CULTURE: The Tiwanaku culture, ancestral to the Aymara, is the builder; oral traditions connect the site to the Aymara people.
- MODERN FRINGE CLAIMS: The site is frequently cited by "ancient aliens" proponents for its precision and scale, with claims of machine tools, laser cutting, or lost advanced technology — these are unsupported by material evidence.
- CRITICAL DEBATES: Some archaeologists debate the exact construction sequence, the logistics of moving the largest blocks (especially the andesite from a quarry ~90km away), and the function of the site (ritual, calendrical, civic).
- REFERENCE: See Jean-Pierre Protzen & Stella Nair, "The Stones of Tiahuanaco: A Study of Architecture and Construction," 2013; Alexei Vranich, "Reconstructing ancient architecture at Tiwanaku, Bolivia: the potential and promise of 3D printing," Heritage Science, 2018; John Janusek, "Tiwanaku and Its Precursors: Recent Research and Emerging Perspectives," Journal of Archaeological Research, 2008.

CLAIMED (not binding, clearly labelled):
- CLAIM: "Impossible precision" or "machine-flat" faces are evidence of advanced lost technology — NOT supported by direct evidence; achievable with pecking, grinding, and polishing.
- CLAIM: The site is 12,000+ years old, predating known civilizations — NOT supported by stratigraphy, radiocarbon, or material culture.
- CLAIM: The blocks were "poured" like concrete — NO evidence of geopolymer or concrete techniques at Puma Punku.
- CLAIM: Only advanced beings (aliens or Atlanteans) could have built Puma Punku — fringe theory, not supported by the archaeological record.

HOLE (unresolved, real, checkable):
- The in situ bronze cramps: While similar cramps are known in other ancient sites (Egypt, Greece), the precision casting and the logistics of pouring molten bronze into carved stone channels at high altitude, with consistent alloy and fit, remain under-studied. The full workflow (smelting, transport, pouring, cooling, fit) is not fully reconstructed or experimentally duplicated at full scale in modern publications.
- The largest andesite blocks' transport (from quarries up to 90km away) remains logistically challenging; no direct evidence of the exact methods (e.g., sledges, rollers, reed mats, water transport) has been found, though several are plausible.
