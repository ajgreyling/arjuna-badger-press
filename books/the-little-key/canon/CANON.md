# The Little Key — Canon

> A children's picture book for Arjuna Badger Press.
> Original work. Inspired-by, not derived-from: the *medicine* of *The Indian in the Cupboard*
> (Lynne Reid Banks) — an ordinary object, a small life suddenly in a child's hands, and the
> weight of being responsible for someone smaller than you. No characters, names, or text are
> borrowed. This is its own thing.

## The one-sentence spine
A girl finds an old key and an old cupboard, and learns that the most powerful thing in the
world is not making something small obey you — it is **listening to it**.

## The story-under-the-story (the *What-a-Mess* layer)
On the surface: a magic cupboard wakes a carved wooden animal.
Underneath: this is a book about **power held gently** — about what you owe anything that is
smaller, newer, and more afraid than you are. A child reading it learns she is *big enough to
matter to someone*. (This is the same medicine that runs under the whole press: the small one
turns out to be the one who counts.)

## The reader
Read-aloud age 4–8; reads-alone age 6–9. One adult, one child, one lamp. The illustrations
must carry the feeling even before a child can read the words — they kept a parent sane once,
and they should be able to do it again.

## Cast
- **Thembi** — the girl. Seven. Curious, a little lonely, kind under the curiosity.
  (A South African name on purpose — this book is going out in eleven SA languages + Swahili,
  and the child on the page should look like the children reading it.)
- **Ouma / Gogo** — her grandmother. Off-page mostly; the keeper of the cupboard before Thembi.
  We learn at the end she *knew*. (Called "Ouma" in the Afrikaans edition, "Gogo" in the Nguni
  editions — the translation layer handles this; English master uses "Gogo".)
- **Nkwe** — the little carved creature who wakes. A hand-carved wooden **honey badger** the
  size of a teaspoon. Stubborn, brave, frightened, proud — does not want to be a pet, does not
  want to be a toy, wants to be *asked*. (Honey badger = the press's own animal. The mark on the
  cover of every book in the library is a badger. This is a quiet wink, not a label.)
- **The other little ones** — a carved bird, a carved tortoise, a carved girl no bigger than
  Thembi's thumb. They wake later. They are not Thembi's. They belong to themselves.

## The cupboard & the key
- An old wooden bathroom cupboard with a little mirror door, paint worn at the handle.
- A small brass key on a red thread. It does not lock the cupboard *shut* — it locks something
  *awake*. Turn it once with a carved thing inside, and by morning the carving is breathing.
- **The rule Thembi works out herself:** the key can wake them, but it cannot *un*-wake them
  into being toys again. Once something is alive, it is alive. You can't put it back. That is
  the whole weight of the book in one object.

## The Gruffalo beat (the middle turn)
Thembi's first instinct is the wrong one — she treats Nkwe like a toy soldier: *march, fetch,
do a trick.* Nkwe runs, and gets cornered by the real danger of a small thing in a big house —
not a monster, but **the world at child-of-a-creature scale**: the cat, the drop off the table,
the cold of an open window at night. Like the mouse in the deep dark wood, Nkwe survives the
first night not by being strong but by being *clever and stubborn*. And Thembi survives her own
worst impulse by finally doing the one brave thing a big person can do: she gets down on the
floor, makes herself small, and **asks** instead of commands.

## The turn that lands the medicine
The cat traps Nkwe. Thembi could grab Nkwe and "save" him — own him, again. Instead she opens
the window, opens the cupboard, opens her hand, and lets him choose. He stays. Not because he
must. Because he was *asked*.

## The ending
Gogo finds them in the morning. She isn't surprised. She had the key first, long ago, and the
little carved girl — the thumb-sized one — was hers, once, and is *still her friend*. The key
passes to Thembi properly now, with the one rule that was always the point:

> *"You may wake anything you like, my girl. But you may never own it.
> A thing you wake is a thing you must listen to — for as long as you both shall live."*

## The closing image
Thembi asleep, the cupboard door open a crack, four tiny creatures keeping watch on the
windowsill in the moonlight. The key on its red thread around her neck. Nkwe — the little
badger — sitting on the pillow, awake, chosen, free, and staying.

## Tone & rules for the prose
- Read-aloud rhythm. Short lines. A repeated refrain a child can say back:
  **"You can wake a thing. You can't own a thing."**
- No villain. The danger is scale and loneliness, not evil.
- Never cute about the weight. The book is gentle but it is *honest* — that's the house rule.
- The honey-badger wink stays a wink. Never explained.

## Spread plan (14 spreads)
1. Title spread — the cupboard in the corner of Gogo's spare room, dust in a sunbeam.
2. Thembi finds the key on its red thread, and the cupboard with the little mirror door.
3. She finds the carved badger inside — the size of a teaspoon. She names him Nkwe.
4. Night. She turns the key once. The mirror door, the click. (No creature yet — just the turn.)
5. **HERO SPREAD.** Morning. Nkwe is *awake* — tiny, breathing, blinking up at an enormous girl.
6. Thembi plays him like a toy: march, fetch, do a trick. Nkwe's face: not having it.
7. Nkwe runs. The world goes huge — table-edge cliff, the cat's green eye in the dark.
8. **COMPOSITE SPREAD.** The same room, now seen small — and *the other little ones are awake
   too*: the bird, the tortoise, the thumb-girl, all superimposed into the hero scene, each one
   a life of its own among the giant furniture.
9. The long night. Nkwe, clever and stubborn, outlasts the cold and the cat. (Gruffalo beat.)
10. Morning danger: the cat corners Nkwe. Thembi could grab him.
11. The turn: she gets down on the floor, opens window, cupboard, hand. *She asks.*
12. Nkwe chooses to stay. The refrain lands.
13. Gogo in the doorway, unsurprised, the thumb-girl waving to her from the sill.
14. Closing image — Thembi asleep, key at her throat, four watchers in the moonlight, Nkwe on
    the pillow, awake and free and staying.

## Art direction (house-consistent)
- Warm, painterly, storybook gouache feel — the Joseph-Wright-illustration warmth AJ named: a
  little chaotic, a little funny, deeply tender. Lamplight palette: honey, ochre, deep night-blue.
- The badger mark of the press is literally a character here, but drawn as a hand-carved wooden
  toy, not the logo.
- Every spread must read emotionally with the sound off — a tired parent at 8pm should *feel*
  it before reading a word.
- Child and grandmother are Black South African. This is non-negotiable house a11y/representation.
