# CHARACTERS.md

## Dr. Leila Aziz
- **Age:** 34
- **Origin / heritage:** Ethiopian — **Tigrayan, Ethiopian Orthodox (Christian) heritage**, secular-
  scholarly by practice. The surname *Aziz* is a cross-Horn name that crosses the Christian/Muslim
  line — fitting for a scholar whose life's work refuses the separation of the traditions. That she is
  Christian-heritage while investigating *all three* Abrahamic faiths is deliberate and load-bearing:
  her inquiry reads as even-handed, not as in-group self-critique of any one faith. Surface this early
  and lightly in the prose (a detail of upbringing, a church in Lalibela, a remembered liturgy) — never
  as a label.
- **Role:** Protagonist; a brilliant linguist and historian specializing in ancient texts.
- **Want:** To uncover the truth behind her father’s mysterious death, believing it is linked to the Brotherhood of Abraham.
- **Wound:** Leila carries the guilt of not being able to save her father, feeling she failed to heed his warnings about the corrupting power of religion.
- **Flaw:** Her growing paranoia leads to impulsive decisions, causing her to mistrust those who genuinely want to help her.
- **Voice:** Analytical, passionate, and driven, with a tone that shifts from hopeful to desperate as she uncovers truths that shake her faith.

## Father Samuel El-Ghazali
- **Role:** Ally; a moderate Muslim scholar deeply entrenched in interfaith dialogue.
- **What They Want:** To foster peace between the three Abrahamic religions and expose the suppression network — the institutional actors who have altered scripture for political control.
- **Collision with Protagonist:** Father Samuel becomes a mentor to Leila, guiding her through the complexities of religious texts. However, his idealism sometimes clashes with her darker, more paranoid outlook, creating tension as they navigate their dangerous quest together.

## Anton Devlin
- **Role:** Antagonist; a powerful high-ranking official within the Vatican — the organizing intelligence of the suppression network. He is not a cartoon villain but a curator of "managed truth" who believes controlled interpretation prevents civilizational collapse.
- **What They Want:** To maintain the status quo of religious power and suppress any truths that could destabilize his influence.
- **Collision with Protagonist:** Anton sees Leila as a threat to his plans. He manipulates events to make it appear as though she is unhinged, using her paranoia against her to keep her from discovering his true intentions.

## Miriam Asher
- **Role:** Relationship anchor; Leila's childhood friend and a journalist for a prominent international magazine.
- **What They Want:** To write an exposé on religious corruption, which she believes will make a significant impact on society.
- **Collision with Protagonist:** Miriam’s ambition sometimes blinds her to the dangers Leila faces, leading to friction in their friendship. While she supports Leila's quest, her eagerness to publish stories can inadvertently put them both in peril.

## Ibrahim Nassar
- **Role:** Antagonist; a charismatic leader of a radical sect that misinterprets the teachings of Islam.
- **What They Want:** To incite a holy war that he believes will purify the faith and eliminate the influence of what he calls the "corrupt West."
- **Collision with Protagonist:** Ibrahim sees Leila as an obstacle to his cause, believing that her research could undermine his manipulation of religious texts. He actively seeks to eliminate her as she gets closer to unveiling the truths that threaten his agenda.

## The Keeper (Rastafari elder — ch-16; load-bearing custody thread)
A barefoot elder of the Brotherhood's "outer line that keeps the roads," encountered at Lalibela.
Verified grounding (web-checked 2026-06-01, do not drift):
- **Grounded in Ethiopia via Shashamane** — the ~500-acre land grant Haile Selassie gave (1948) for
  diaspora repatriation, ~250km south of Addis, a real ~75-year Rastafari community ("Little
  Jamaica"). This is why a Rasta elder in Ethiopia is historically natural, not a transplant.
- **Presentation:** Bobo Shanti register — locks bound under a tightly-wound wrap, long robe.
  Sackcloth/hessian + barefoot = AJ's direct field observation of SA Bobo communities; rendered as
  THIS man's presentation, never claimed as universal Rasta doctrine.
- **Iconography:** authentically Rasta — Lion of Judah, red-gold-green. (Ankh deliberately NOT used —
  it is not Rastafari iconography.)
- **Livity/language:** I-and-I (the self and Jah not two things), "overstand", "Babylon", One Love,
  King/My Lord ("the Most High is in all of us"). Ital law as HIS line (no fruit of the vine; small
  fish). Cannabis deliberately omitted. Marcus Garvey / repatriation lineage.
- **Haile Selassie:** the narrative honours the faith WITHOUT asserting divinity, using Selassie's
  OWN verified words (1967 CBC interview, Bill McNeil): "I am a man, I am mortal, and I will be
  replaced by the oncoming generation... never assume a human being is emanated from a deity." The
  "replaced by the oncoming generation" line is the thread's keystone — it ties to the Ark guardian-
  monk's deathbed succession: the keeping is the HANDING-ON, not the holder.
- **Theme function:** the fourth voice in the trilogy's custody-not-text spine (rock churches /
  king-carried-home / texts-in-a-cave / the handing-on). Ties to ch-09's rebound Covenant + held-back
  coin. The Ark told AS legend only (no literal supernatural — mythos rule).
