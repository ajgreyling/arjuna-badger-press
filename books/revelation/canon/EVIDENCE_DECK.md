## The governing principle (from the review)

> Convert the manuscript from an **exposition-led** conspiracy novel into an **evidence-led**
> pursuit thriller. The conspiracy is real — **but evidence is being manipulated from multiple
> sides.** The Brotherhood is partly right and partly self-serving. The deepest tension is not
> "truth vs lies" but **"who gets to mediate destabilising truth?"**

Fictional procedural credibility, not historical validity. Anchor everything in invented
artifacts so the book reads as a thriller, never as a real-world claim.

## THE DECK — 8 artifacts

Each: **what it proves · who knows · where it lives · what can be misread · risk of finding it ·
plant → payoff chapters.**

### 1. The Lalibela Codex discrepancy — *"with them" → "over them"*
- **Proves:** a single preposition was altered to manufacture hierarchy. The oldest copy reads a
  relational "with them" (mutual obligation); the received/printed text reads "over them"
  (dominion). One word; six centuries of commentary rearranged around it.
- **Who knows:** Abebe found it; the Brotherhood holds the old copy; Devlin's network knows which
  page it is.
- **Where:** the hidden library beneath the Lalibela church complex; a photograph in Abebe's notes.
- **Misread as:** scribal error / dialectal variant (Miriam and Devlin will both push this).
- **Risk:** the moment Leila photographs it, basement access is revoked (ch-1/ch-3 beat).
- **Plant → payoff:** seeded ch-1 (the note's idiom) → Leila *herself* catches the preposition
  **ch-3 / ch-5** → it becomes **Proof 1** at the summit **ch-13**.

### 2. The tri-faith alteration pattern
- **Proves:** the SAME rhetorical shift (relational → hierarchical) appears in one Christian, one
  Jewish, and one Islamic manuscript branch, via three different institutions → common **political
  logic**, not isolated error. This is the anti-supersessionist heart: not "one true religion," but
  "a shared editing-for-power across all three."
- **Who knows:** Brotherhood scholars (Tewodros); partially in Abebe's comparative notebook.
- **Where:** three manuscripts — the Lalibela Gospels, a Torah Genesis-1.28 marginal, a Qur'an 2.62
  variant (the draft already names these folders: `Torah_Gen_1.28_marginal`, `Qur_2.62_variants`).
- **Misread as:** cherry-picked coincidence.
- **Risk:** assembling all three in one place makes that place a target (ch-10 safe-house raid).
- **Plant → payoff:** glimpsed ch-5 → assembled ch-9 → **Proof 3** at the summit ch-13.

### 3. The Council Directive — *"harmonization"*
- **Proves:** institutional intent. A memo/marginal instruction ordering doctrinal "harmonization"
  / "alignment" — the word **Abebe** underlined six months before he died: *"Not error.
  Harmonization."* His later phrase for the whole system: **"administrative theology."**
- **Who knows:** Abebe; Kebede (who heard him change the phrase); Devlin (it's his apparatus).
- **Where:** referenced in the Office of Doctrine and Faith memo (draft ch-8); the directive itself
  is the thing Leila must authenticate.
- **Misread as:** a forgery / out-of-context bureaucratic boilerplate.
- **Risk:** authenticating it requires the seal/ledger cross-check (item 4) — slow work under pursuit.
- **Plant → payoff:** named ch-2/ch-8 → authenticated by Leila ch-8 → **Proof 2** at summit ch-13.

### 4. The seal anomaly (TCP-ALT-03.7 / the 12th-c Gospels)
- **Proves:** later institutional handling. The wax composition / thread / a removed-staple
  pressure mark on the 12th-century Gospels manuscript dates the *handling* to a later political
  archive, not the ecclesiastical one it claims — chain-of-custody is broken. (Draft ch-1 already
  cites "a specific wax seal on the 12th-century manuscript of the Gospels that doesn't match the
  registry," filed under call number **TCP-ALT-03.7**.)
- **Who knows:** Abebe logged it; the Brotherhood can image it; Devlin's people know it's the weak
  seam.
- **Where:** the restricted Institute archive (drawer **B-74**); imaged again in the library ch-3.
- **Misread as:** ordinary conservation / restoration.
- **Risk:** it's physical — it can be *swapped*. The page Leila photographs in ch-3 does **not
  match** the page still bound when she looks back (the "evidence is moving" reveal).
- **Plant → payoff:** ch-1 (Leila finds the call number) → ch-3 (substituted page) → **ch-5 ANCHOR:
  Leila retroactively concludes the swap was a *staged tactic* — a prepared replacement leaf / a
  folio turned in the dark, meant to destabilise her witness confidence, not a hallucination** →
  corroborates Proof 1 at summit. (review-005: the swapped page must be *metabolised*, not left as
  an unexplained eerie beat. Anchor it once, in scene, as deliberate menace.)

### 5. Abebe's hidden comparative notebook
- **Proves:** method, not panic. NOT a frantic journal — a working scholar's tool: cross-references,
  collation tables, unresolved questions, and a growing worry that **the Brotherhood wanted to use
  selective revelation strategically**. Contains his operating principle:
  > *"Never trust a conclusion written before the variants are collated."*
- **Who knows:** only Leila can fully decode his cross-reference system (her agency engine).
- **Where:** a hidden safe in Abebe's apartment (ch-11); a fragment hidden in Leila's childhood
  graduation journal fifteen years earlier (the ch-5 emotional hook — the erased line beneath the
  approved text matches a fragment he tucked into her journal).
- **Misread as:** confirming the Brotherhood's thesis — when it actually **complicates** it.
- **Risk:** it reveals a Brotherhood proof-text (item 6 below / Covenant) may be **derivative**,
  destabilising her own allies.
- **Plant → payoff:** ch-1 (the unused graduation journal planted) → ch-5 (fragment match) →
  ch-11 (notebook found; Brotherhood overreach discovered) → ch-12 (Leila removes the weak proof).
- **review-005 / ch-11 is now a HEAVY beat (was MOD).** The notebook must read in three movements:
  (A) the safe and the *working* notebook vs the fear-journal (procedural intimacy, not sentiment);
  (B) **show Abebe's method** — 1–2 sample collation entries where he *questions the Brotherhood's
  own assumptions*, so the reader meets his working mind before the Covenant lands; (C) the Covenant
  forensic catastrophe (see item 6). Deepen Abebe through *temperament*: scruple, refusal to
  overstate, disappointment in allies, the discipline of writing no conclusion before the variants
  are in.

### 6. The Covenant of Equals — and the curation problem
- **Proves (two-edged):** the Brotherhood's central text — "predates all three religions as they
  exist today," holds the original relational teaching. BUT Leila discovers (via item 5) that the
  copy they treat as **original** was actually copied from a **corrupted branch** → someone in the
  Brotherhood has *also* been curating truth. Makes Samuel/the Brotherhood morally ambiguous, per
  the review.
- **Who knows:** Samuel presents it as bedrock; Tewodros has private doubts; Leila proves the flaw.
- **Where:** the deep chamber library (draft ch-9 already names "the Covenant of Equals").
- **Misread as:** unimpeachable foundation (the Brotherhood's blind spot).
- **Risk:** disproving it weakens the public case if released unguarded → drives the ch-12 decision
  to **rebuild the reveal on stronger proofs**.
- **Plant → payoff:** ch-9 (presented as bedrock) → ch-11 (Leila finds it derivative) → ch-12
  (cut from the public case to make the case *stronger*).
- **review-005 — the ch-11 derivation must be a forensic SET-PIECE, not a told conclusion.** Leila
  follows the apparatus on the page so the reader *watches the inference*: the Brotherhood's primacy
  claim → the variant pattern → at least **two concrete tells** that prove downstream descent:
  (1) a copyist **eye-skip / homoeoteleuton** reproduced faithfully (a line the "original" could not
  contain unless copied from a source that already had the slip); (2) a **regional spelling or
  letterform that postdates the claimed date by ~two centuries**; optionally (3) a "corrected" form
  that betrays it was copied *from* a corrupted exemplar, after the corruption, not before it.
  Then the **explicit practical stakes** (state them, don't imply): a hostile expert seizes the
  Covenant as the weak stone, proves it derivative in an afternoon, and uses *that* to discredit the
  three sound proofs and brand the whole movement romantic fabrication → therefore Leila **cuts it**
  from the public case. It should land as **a betrayal by evidence itself**, not as technical intel.
- **ch-9 charge (review-005):** before it can fall, the reader must *feel* why the Brotherhood
  cherishes it — oaths sworn near it, generations of protection, a reverence and conservation care
  reserved for this codex alone. Samuel/Tewodros speak of it as they speak of nothing else.

### 7. The planted FORGED Brotherhood document (the salting trap)
- **Proves:** the suppression network is **salting archives with false "confirmations"** so that
  later truth claims collapse under fraud accusations. A document stamped "Brotherhood of Abraham"
  uses a **transliteration convention a non-Arabic speaker would use** — wrong by one letter. Leila
  spots it instantly. *"He didn't just want the truth buried. He wanted it made ridiculous."*
- **Who knows:** Leila catches it (ch-2), recognises the pattern repeated in Hakim's office (ch-6);
  Miriam grasps the strategy.
- **Where:** planted among Abebe's restricted files (ch-2) and in Hakim's office (ch-6).
- **Misread as:** genuine Brotherhood support evidence (the trap's whole point).
- **Risk:** if Leila had trusted it, the public case would have detonated on contact.
- **Plant → payoff:** ch-2 (first spotted) → ch-6 (pattern confirmed across sites) → informs the
  ch-12 strategy (separate proof from interpretation; expect a discredit campaign).

### 8. The metadata / chain-of-transmission trail
- **Proves:** the antagonist is **already inside**, intelligent, and close. The `coordinates.jpg`
  was forwarded through dead servers and **one active local machine**; the last IP belongs to the
  **Institute basement**. The airport "brass handle" photo is hardware from **Archive B-74** —
  *we were there before you, and still are.* The obvious route to Lalibela has been **staged**.
- **Who knows:** Leila reads the raw metadata; the sender intends her to understand the threat's logic.
- **Where:** the image file itself (ch-1); the airport photo (ch-1/ch-4 boundary).
- **Misread as:** generic "you're being watched" menace (the OLD, weaker version).
- **Risk:** acting on the staged route walks her into the trap; aborting it is her first smart move.
- **Plant → payoff:** ch-1 (metadata + handle = antagonist's *mind* present in Act 1) → recurs as
  the texture of every surveillance beat (verification, not paranoia).

### 9. The gold trail (the Brotherhood's untraceable wealth)
- **Proves:** the Brotherhood is real, old, and **off every modern ledger** — it banks and pays in
  physical gold precisely because the suppression network reads metadata and follows money (the
  material twin of #8). And it gives Abebe's death a *forensic* anomaly: in his last months he moved
  value that appears on **no** bank record. Two layers of gold: (a) the order's **own ancient minted
  coin** — a one-ounce piece with tri-faith iconography and a Ge'ez/Arabic motto, struck by the
  Brotherhood, traceable to no national mint (the deep reserve / mystique); (b) **Krugerrands and
  generic bullion** it converts to for modern liquidity (fungible, unserialised, anonymous). The
  historical spine the coin invokes: Aksumite gold coinage (King Endubis, c. 270 CE), Solomon's gold
  (Kebra Nagast / Ophir), Mansa Musa's gold hajj — gold as scripture's measure of value.
- **Who knows:** the Brotherhood (Samuel administers it); Abebe used it to fund work off-grid; Devlin's
  people know the order is unbanked and have been trying to find the physical cache; a fence/assayer
  who once handled a Brotherhood coin can read its mint-marks.
- **Where:** Brotherhood coins surface as objects (one among Abebe's effects; the cache near the deep
  library); the Krugerrand conversions surface as a transaction trail Leila reconstructs.
- **Misread as:** ordinary collector's bullion / a dead academic's quiet savings (Devlin will push
  "he was just hoarding gold like a paranoid"). The **knowing beat:** someone clocks the irony of a
  Krugerrand — a coin named for a Boer president — now carrying a secret interfaith order's wealth.
- **Risk:** the cache is a *physical* target (drives part of the ch-10 raid); tracing the gold can be
  spun as Leila "following the money like a conspiracist," a discredit angle (ties to #7's strategy).
- **Plant → payoff:** a Brotherhood coin glimpsed among Abebe's effects **ch-8** (the no-ledger
  murder anomaly) → the order's gold economy shown when Leila reaches the Brotherhood **ch-9** → the
  physical cache is part of what the **ch-10** raid targets → the gold's untraceability informs the
  **ch-12** "separate proof from interpretation, expect a discredit campaign" strategy.
- **Keep distinct in-world:** the Brotherhood's *own* coin ≠ the Krugerrand (the John Wick "Continental
  coin" is fictional — the Brotherhood coin is its own invention; the Krugerrand is the real bullion it
  converts to). Institutional/material power, never occult (STYLE_GUIDE DON'T #4).

## RESTORED-DISTRUST MAP (review-005 — the allies must feel riskier)

The thriller pass sorted people too cleanly into enemy / flawed ally / trustworthy helper. Re-open
the gray *selectively* — not by making everyone duplicitous (that muddies the action line), but by
giving each ally-supplied truth **intentional pressure**. Acceptance test for any helpful-character
scene: *What are they withholding? What do they want Leila to conclude? What would a more suspicious
Leila verify before accepting?*

- **Abdi (ch-4):** trust is earned **later**. One early correct instinct should read as possibly
  *manipulative*; he withholds one practical truth a beat too long; Leila clocks that his "test" may
  also be assessing whether she can be *used*, not only protected. "After a fashion" = loyal to the
  texts, not to the order's governance. Aftershock on "he told us not to trust you": her father
  subjected trust to method too — her inheritance, not a wound.
- **Tewodros (ch-5):** tests her **incorruptibility**, not just her skill (can she resist a reading
  that flatters her own side?). His "including us" doubt attaches to something **concrete in the
  room** — a withheld file, a conservation label that doesn't match the Brotherhood's claim.
- **Miriam (ch-7):** right, but **ambitious** — alive to the career-defining story. "The story is
  *you* as instrument" should *disturb* Leila: being made legible-as-persona is itself a curation.
- **The Vatican memo (ch-8):** real **and** curated. Leila asks who *selected* this page, what was
  on the removed stapled sheet, why this is what the Brotherhood wants her to see *now*. A true
  document is not the same as an un-managed one.
- **Samuel (ch-9):** candor is **tactical**. He answers one question too elegantly; his admissions
  arrive in the order most likely to preserve her cooperation; the effect is to make her trust the
  *information* and distrust the *intention* in the same breath. Nearly as dangerous as Devlin, in a
  different register — never let Devlin be the sole articulate voice of institutional complexity.
- **The leak (ch-10):** narrower than betrayal — a hole between the order's most trusted office and
  the mountain (the slow/timing faction's floor-plan access), not a named Judas.

## VOICE GUIDE (so the cast stops blurring — from review-004 §10)

- **Leila** — precise, analytical, dry; interrupts when facts are wrong; emotion leaks sideways
  into *analysis* ("She counted the exits before she realised she'd stopped listening").
- **Abdi** — plainspoken, practical, cutting; not omniscient; withholds for reasons. Lives by:
  *"Truth is a blade. I care who's holding it."*
- **Miriam** — fast, skeptical, journalistic compression; least reverent. *"Every dead academic
  gets called brilliant. What did he find that made someone hurry the obituary?"*
- **Samuel** — controlled, layered, withholds by omission; may be Devlin with better intentions.
  *"The Brotherhood exists because every institution eventually decides God needs editing."*
- **Devlin** — concise, elegant, managerial; weaponises understatement. *"Texts do not govern
  people. Interpreters do."*
- **Kebede** — old-academic diction, regret under discipline. · **Amara** — cool, economical,
  unreadable until late. · **Tewodros** — exacting archivist, weary, not reverent.
