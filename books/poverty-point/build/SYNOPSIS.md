# Ninety Days — SYNOPSIS

## Logline

A documentary crew races a heritage consortium to uncover the meaning of Poverty Point, Louisiana’s vast, enigmatic earthworks—built in a season by foragers with no kings—before the site is commodified, discovering the power and cost of collective action and the dignity of what can never be fully known.

## Book Synopsis

In the heat of a Louisiana summer, a close-knit documentary crew arrives at Poverty Point, summoned by rumors that a well-funded heritage consortium plans to “develop” the ancient site. The crew—skeptical, curious, and fiercely respectful of local sovereignty—finds themselves in a contest not just for access, but for the story itself. As the consortium woos state officials and the community with promises of jobs and preservation, the crew attempts to reconstruct the site’s marvels: six concentric ridges and a bird-shaped mound, raised by hunter-gatherers three thousand years ago, without kings, crops, or written records.

The crew’s investigation unfolds through muddy reenactments, interviews with local archaeologists, and a deep dive into the soil science and trade networks that bound the continent together. They encounter both the limits of their own methods and the wisdom of those who have lived alongside the ridges for generations. As the consortium’s pitch grows more aggressive—offering funding in exchange for narrative control and restricted access—the community faces a choice: profit or preservation.

In the end, the evidence points to an extraordinary collective act: thousands gathered, built a mountain in a season, then walked away, leaving no tombs, no heirs, and no official explanation. The local custodians choose what to share and what to hold back, and the community votes to preserve the site on its own terms. The crew leaves with the story still open, the wonder intact, and the right to say “maybe” preserved for those who come next. The final chapters ground every claim in real, checkable evidence, label the extraordinary as such, and leave the function of Poverty Point unresolved—a residue of awe and a challenge to easy answers.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Landing on the Floodplain
The crew arrives at Poverty Point at dawn, sizing up the landscape and the consortium’s proposal. The fixer reads the grift in the offer; the local archaeologist makes it clear that outsiders are often unimpressed before they’re impressed. The crew is introduced to the site’s subtlety and the challenge of seeing what’s truly monumental.

### Chapter 2 — The Official Story
At the trailhead, the crew reviews the state’s polished narrative: foragers, baskets, engineering marvels. The custodian gives the “official” tour but invites skepticism. The crew’s POV centers on reading between the lines—who paid for the narrative, what the small print omits.

### Chapter 3 — Basket Math
Theo, the engineer, attempts to reconstruct a tiny fraction of the mound-building process using replica baskets. The crew quickly discovers the scale is overwhelming; their calculations and efforts go sideways, exposing the gap between theory and muddy reality.

### Chapter 4 — Reading the Layers
In a trench cut into Mound A, the LENS and the MONEY interrogate the microstratigraphy with the site custodian. The soil itself is the record—layers mixed on purpose, resistant to erosion, evidence of a deliberate, rapid build. The official story is told again, but the “why” remains withheld.

### Chapter 5 — The Consortium’s Pitch
At a community center meeting, the consortium presents its development plan: interpretive centers, curated experiences, promises of economic uplift. The crew observes the subtle tactics of persuasion and reads the underlying extraction in the proposal.

### Chapter 6 — Mud and Memory
Working in the sticky “gumbo” clay, the crew learns firsthand how the soil’s properties made the earthworks possible. The custodian watches, guiding and correcting, reinforcing that local expertise and memory outlast any outsider’s analysis.

### Chapter 7 — The Gathering
Theo models the logistics: how thousands could have gathered to build the site in a single season without kings or crops. The crew debates food, labor, and coordination—always circling back to the impossibility of certainty, the marvel of collective action.

### Chapter 8 — Lines in the Earth
Using a magnetometer, the engineer maps post-ring structures beneath the plaza. The LENS documents the tension as the consortium updates their proposal to optimize “visitor flow”—a euphemism for controlling the narrative and access.

### Chapter 9 — Erosion and Endurance
After a storm, the engineer and custodian walk Mound A, reflecting on the math of endurance: the mound should have eroded away, but the deliberate mixing of soils and collective maintenance has preserved it for millennia.

### Chapter 10 — Trade and Distance
In the collections room, the archivist and engineer examine lithic artifacts sourced from hundreds of kilometers away. The evidence of vast trade networks complicates the image of an isolated community, suggesting Poverty Point was a gathering place on a continental scale.

### Chapter 11 — Negotiations
Over catfish at a local diner, the fixer deconstructs the consortium’s financial promises and the subtle ways they seek to buy legitimacy. The crew reads the room, aware that every negotiation is also a performance.

### Chapter 12 — The Impossible Season
The crew assembles the data: basket loads, timelines, microstratigraphy. The impossibility of building Mound A in 90 days becomes a window into the extraordinary—thousands working together, children included, with no evidence of coercion or hierarchy.

### Chapter 13 — No Kings, No Heirs
The archivist searches for the “guy”—the leader, the king, the tomb—and finds only the residue of collective action. The site stands as a rare monument with no clear owner, heir, or organizing elite.

### Chapter 14 — The Walk-Away
At sunset, the LENS and the local archaeologist walk the ridges. The marvel is not just what was built, but that the builders left—no conquest, no succession, just a memory held in earth. The crew starts to accept the limits of their own storytelling.

### Chapter 15 — Pressure and Pride
At a packed community meeting, the consortium makes its final pitch. The crew reads the body language and the subtext, sensing the community’s pride and the pressure to choose between profit and sovereignty.

### Chapter 16 — The Hole
Theo obsesses over the logistics—more hands means more mouths, more food, more questions. The “hole” at the heart of the story is not just physical but conceptual: the function of the site remains unresolved, the wonder undiminished.

### Chapter 17 — The Wink
Marisol chases a persistent footnote: “the function remains unresolved.” The local archaeologist reminds her that sometimes the honest answer is to let the official story stand, unembellished, with an affectionate wink at the unanswerable.

### Chapter 18 — The Custodian’s Choice
On the eve of the community vote, the local custodian walks the outer ridge, choosing what to share and what to hold back. The crew learns that the right to wonder includes the right not to be told everything.

### Chapter 19 — Handoff
As the crew packs to leave, the fixer reflects on the outcome: the community has voted to preserve, not commodify, the site. The story remains open; the crew respects the boundaries set by the locals and the land.

### Chapter 20 — The Residue of Wonder
After the storm, the crew sits with what they cannot answer. The site’s wonder is not in its solved mysteries, but in what remains unresolved—an enduring challenge to the desire for closure.

### Chapter 21 — Real / Debated / Invented: A Gazetteer
The archivist compiles a final inventory: what is real, what is debated, what is invented. Every claim is cited, every extraordinary fact labeled, and the reader is left with the residue—the right to say “maybe,” and the awe that comes from not knowing.
