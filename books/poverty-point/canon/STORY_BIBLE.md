A fevered summer on the Louisiana floodplain: the crew races a well-funded heritage consortium to read what Poverty Point was for, before the site is commodified and its story repackaged for profit. The premise is not “how did they build it?” but “how did a gathering of foragers, without kings or crops, raise a mountain in a season and then walk away?” The theme is the power and cost of collective action, the residue of wonder left when the official story is played straight, and the dignity of the unknown. Always the Why Files register: rabbit-hole curiosity; the official story played dead straight, affection for the skeptics and the builders; then the one checkable hole; then the wink; then the function — never the answer. Intended reader: the one who still wants to say “maybe,” who wants both the rigour and the awe. Close-third POV: the ND crew own the telling, but never the place; the local’s knowledge is sovereign. Content bounds: real-world facts, cited and checkable; fringe labelled as such; no supernatural confirmation or debunk. The creative driver: the tension between the engineered earth and the impossibility of certainty; the chase to read, not to own.
