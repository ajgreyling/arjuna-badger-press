SITE: Poverty Point, West Carroll Parish, Louisiana

**REAL (peer-reviewed, binding):**
- Monumental Late Archaic earthwork complex, c. 1700–1100 BCE; majority of construction (ridges) 1600–1300 BCE.
- Built by hunter-fisher-gatherers: no evidence of agriculture, no evidence of ranked chiefdom or permanent leadership.
- Recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2014).
- Earthworks: six nested C-shaped ridge embankments, outermost ~1.2 km in diameter.
- Mound A: ~22 meters tall, ~238,000 cubic meters of fill.
- Construction method: fill moved entirely in hand-carried baskets (30–50 lb loads); microstratigraphy shows men, women, and children participated.
- No wheel, no draft animals, no metal tools.
- Mound A construction: Tristram Kidder (WashU) microstratigraphy finds no weathering/rain-wash surfaces between fill episodes → no significant pauses in construction. Built in "a matter of months, possibly 30–90 days."
- No archaeological evidence of a chief or central authority; leadership interpreted as situational/ritual, dissolving post-event.
- Continental trade network: stone raw materials sourced from up to ~1,000 km away (caveat: some "Great Lakes copper" later identified as southern Appalachian; network is continental, but precise distances/links are debated in detail).
- Engineering: soils deliberately mixed to control shrink-swell and resist erosion; Mound A has held its form for 3,000+ years with minimal erosion. Kidder compares to Roman concrete / Chinese rammed earth.
- Poverty Point Objects: baked loess "cooking balls" used in earth ovens; hallmark of site’s material culture.
- Magnetometry: buried wooden post rings (25–63 m in diameter) discovered in the main plaza.
- Astronomy: proposed solstice-aligned aisles/ridges are contested; Haag and Purrington select different site centres. No consensus on sky alignments.

**CLAIMED / CONTESTED / FRINGE (labelled):**
- Some popular claims overstate the "impossible" scale or propose lost civilizations; scholarly consensus is that the achievement is extraordinary but within the capabilities of organized foragers.
- Claims of "Great Lakes copper" should be stated as "continental network, detail disputed."
- Claims of precise astronomical observatory function are not settled; alignments are debated among archaeologists.
- No evidence for agriculture, but some debate on the extent of wild plant management.

**CITATION HOOKS:**
