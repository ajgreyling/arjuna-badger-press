Every checkable thing is real: all measurements, construction math, and engineering feats are peer-reviewed and cited; the rapid-build evidence is open; the continental trade network is genuine but debated in detail. The “potato engine”: the crew’s attempts to reconstruct or model the “easy” official story (moving earth by basket, feeding a crowd, resisting erosion) always show the gap between plan and achievement. Wonder, not conspiracy: no aliens, no lost super-civilizations; the marvel is what real people did, not what’s invented. §3 in spirit: the local/keeper always out-reads the crew, the “what is it for” is theirs to give or withhold, the crew never own the place. Mysteries stay open: the speed of construction, the gathering, and the walk-away are never fully resolved in narration. The site is always real, honestly rendered, never a puzzle-box for outsider growth. For this book: the rapid-build of Mound A (Kidder’s microstratigraphy), the absence of chiefs or crops, the enduring earthworks, the open question of function — all binding and foregrounded.
