The real Poverty Point: a monumental earthwork city rising from the gumbo clay and wild cane of northeast Louisiana, six nested C-ridges and the vast bird-shaped Mound A — all built by hand, basket by basket, three millennia ago. The terrain is floodplain, the air thick with summer thunder, the river always threatening to reclaim what’s built. The site is living: home ground to the descendant community, a field station for archaeologists, a magnet for heritage investors and weekend wonder-seekers. The chase runs from the ridges to the mound, through the state office, the local diner, the backwater levees, and the storied trading rivers. The wonder is physical: the engineered soils, the basket-load math, the post rings underfoot, the cooking balls still found in the dirt. The modern stakes are visible: funding cuts, local pride, and the push-pull between preservation and extraction. The world is honest: not a lost city, but a place where people still walk and remember.
