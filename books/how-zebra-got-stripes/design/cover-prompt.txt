HOW THE ZEBRA GOT HER STRIPES — ART SPEC
Arjuna Badger Press · Children's Library · ages 4–8 · read-aloud picture book
File slug: "stripes" · Landscape 3:2, full-bleed, NO text in any image.

============================================================
STYLE  (apply to EVERY spread + the cover — keep it identical so the book is one world)
============================================================
Warm painterly storybook illustration in the classic read-aloud picture-book tradition —
soft gouache-and-coloured-pencil feel, visible brushwork, rounded tender shapes, a little
chaotic, a little funny, deeply warm. NOT cartoon-flat, NOT 3D-render, NOT photographic.
The whole book is built on LIGHT AND SHADOW: every scene is lit by a low honey-gold African
dusk sun pouring sideways through tall dry grass, throwing long bars of warm gold and deep
blue-black shadow across the ground — that dapple is the visual heartbeat of the book and must
appear in every spread. PALETTE: honey-gold, dry-grass ochre, warm amber, with deep blue-black
evening shadow and a glowing dusk sky of rose, apricot and violet.

SETTING — specific, dignified southern African bushveld/grassland (the veld), NEVER generic
"jungle": tall pale dry-season grass, flat-topped thorn (acacia) trees, a low rise, a single
old farm windmill far on the horizon, a shrinking muddy waterhole (pan). Big open sky.

RECURRING CHARACTERS (keep them consistent on every spread):
- {{CHILD}} = a young African herder-child of the veld, warm brown skin, short natural hair,
  a simple worn earth-coloured tunic and a herding stick, bare or sandalled feet. Gentle,
  watchful, unhurried face. DRAW THE SAME CHILD EVERY TIME — this is the personalised hero.
- ZEBRA = a real plains zebra in SHAPE, but her COAT CHANGES across the book. Spreads 1–9 she is
  PLAIN: one smooth grey-gold colour, NO stripes at all, soft and unmarked, proud-necked, a
  little lonely. From spread 10 onward she gains stripes (gold-and-dark bars). Keep her FACE and
  body the same animal throughout so the child sees the SAME zebra becoming herself.
- THE HERD = wildebeest and antelope (impala/kudu) with naturally dappled, mottled coats that
  blend into the striped grass. Gentle, ordinary, drawn with dignity.
- TORTOISE = an old leopard-tortoise, wrinkled wise head, patterned shell.
- WEAVER BIRDS = small bright golden-yellow weaver birds, quick and many.
- LION = a dignified golden lion, noble and calm — the honest danger of the open, NEVER a
  cartoon monster, never snarling-evil; majestic and a little sad.

No text, letters, logos, or watermarks inside any illustration.

============================================================
SPREAD PROMPTS
============================================================

spread-01-stripes.png
Wide establishing shot of the dry-season veld at golden dusk. A shrinking muddy waterhole,
tall pale grass laid through with long bars of gold light and blue-black shadow, the herd
grazing far off, a single thorn tree and a far windmill on the horizon. Small in all that gold,
{{CHILD}} the herder-child stands at the water's edge with a herding stick, watching over the
animals. Vast warm dusk sky. Mood: peace, openness, the whole world about to begin.

spread-02-stripes.png
Close on ZEBRA in the old days — PLAIN, no stripes, one smooth grey-gold coat, chin lifted,
standing proud and a little apart from the herd in the striped dusk grass. Beautiful and subtly
lonely. The dappled herd grazes softly behind her, blending into the grass while she stands out.

spread-03-stripes.png
Plain grey-gold Zebra lifting her chin and looking down her nose, vain and lonely, among the
dapple-coated antelope and wildebeest who graze together unbothered in the long striped grass.
Show how the herd's mottled coats melt into the light-and-shadow while plain Zebra does not.
Warm, gently funny — her pride is sweet, not nasty.

spread-04-stripes.png
The dry season biting: the waterhole shrunk to a small muddy eye, the grass tall and bleached
pale, the open ground wide and bare under a hot apricot dusk. {{CHILD}} kneels at the last pan
cupping water for the small thirsty animals first. Long blue shadows thrown across the gold.
Mood: tender care in a hard, exposed season.

spread-05-stripes.png  (HERO SPREAD)
Dusk on open ground. A dignified golden LION comes down a low rise, calm and noble, not
snarling. The dapple-coated herd is HALF-VANISHED into the striped grass — melting away, almost
gone. And alone in the open stands PLAIN grey-gold Zebra, lit up bright as a lamp, the single
clear shape the eye can find, exposed and frightened. High tension built purely on being SEEN —
no violence, no blood. The most dramatic, most beautiful spread in the book.

spread-06-stripes.png
{{CHILD}} stands bravely between the lion's golden gaze and plain Zebra, one arm flung out,
herding stick in hand, calling Zebra low and quiet toward the grass. But plain unmarked Zebra
stands out starkly against the striped dapple — nothing of the grass on her coat to hide in,
shining in the open like the moon on bare water. Brave child, exposed zebra, the lion at distance.

spread-07-stripes.png
After the danger passes. Soft deep-blue twilight. The herd safe and nearly invisible in the
long grass. Plain Zebra stands alone, trembling, head lowered — for the first time NOT proud,
turning to ask {{CHILD}} a quiet question. The child listens with a hand on her neck. Quiet,
honest, a little sad.

spread-08-stripes.png
Old TORTOISE lifts his wrinkled wise head in the striped dusk light, bars of gold and blue
shadow falling clean across his patterned shell — visibly showing Zebra and {{CHILD}} the idea:
that the evening lays down a coat of light and shadow, if you know how to wear it. Gentle,
hopeful, the turn of the story. {{CHILD}} and plain Zebra lean in to listen.

spread-09-stripes.png  (COMPOSITE making-spread)
Joyful working scene at dusk. {{CHILD}} catches a bar of low gold sunlight in one cupped hand
and a bar of blue-black shadow in the other; a flock of bright golden weaver birds carries
ribbons of light; the whole dappled herd brings the dark — arms, hooves and beaks full of
gathered light and shade. The dusk grass glows. Many hands, one happy song. Plain Zebra waits
in the centre, hopeful.

spread-10-stripes.png
The painting. {{CHILD}} and the animals lay the gathered dapple onto Zebra's plain coat STRIPE
BY STRIPE — a bar of gold, then a bar of blue-dark, then gold again — all down her sides. Zebra
(now HALF plain, half newly-striped) holds very still and trusting in the warm dusk light, the
herd gathered close and singing over her. The first spread where her stripes appear. Tender, magical.

spread-11-stripes.png  (THE PAY-OFF)
Fully-striped Zebra steps into the tall striped dusk grass and ALMOST VANISHES — her gold-and-
dark stripes melting perfectly into the bars of light and shadow, so you can barely find her.
In the distance the golden lion's eyes slide right past, seeing only grass. Wonder and relief.
The whole point of the book in one image: she has become the place she stands in.

spread-12-stripes.png  (THE BELONGING TURN)
Fully-striped Zebra walks back INTO the dapple-coated herd at twilight, and now you cannot tell
where Zebra ends and the herd's dapple begins — she is surrounded, inside, one of many, no
longer apart on a proud lonely edge. Warm, full, belonging. {{CHILD}} watches, smiling.

spread-13-stripes.png
{{CHILD}} and the now-striped Zebra at the waterhole in the last gold light, foreheads gently
touching, a quiet private thank-you between them. The herd grazes softly behind in the dapple.
Intimate, warm, the emotional landing of the story.

spread-14-stripes.png  (CLOSING IMAGE)
Night falling soft and violet over the veld, first stars out. The whole herd at rest in the
long grass; striped Zebra among them, barely visible in the dapple, safe and belonging. {{CHILD}}
curled asleep nearby under the thorn tree, the herding stick laid down, the windmill a quiet
silhouette. Deep peace. The story put to bed.

COVER  (cover-stripes.png — landscape 3:2, full-bleed, NO text in image)
============================================================
The signature image of the book: at golden dusk, the now-STRIPED Zebra stands in the tall
striped dry grass, her gold-and-dark stripes echoing the bars of light and shadow of the veld
so she half-melts into the evening — beautiful, safe, belonging. {{CHILD}} the herder-child
stands warmly beside her with a hand on her neck, both looking out across the glowing veld
toward the thorn tree and the far windmill. Honey-gold, ochre, blue-black shadow and a rose-and-
violet dusk sky. Warm, painterly, tender, instantly readable as a beloved read-aloud storybook.
Leave calm sky space at the top for the title to be set later. No text, letters, or logos in
the image itself.
