# How the Zebra Got Her Stripes

*{{DEDICATION}}*

<!-- spread:1 image="spread-01-stripes.png" alt="Wide dry-season veld at golden dusk. A shrinking waterhole, tall pale grass, the herd grazing far off, a lone thorn tree on the horizon. A small African herder-child stands at the water's edge in the low gold light." -->

Long ago, when the grass was older than the elephants,
the sun came down at evening
and laid itself across the veld
in bars of gold and bars of dark.

And at the edge of the last water,
small in all that gold,
stood {{CHILD}},
who watched over the animals
and loved them, every one.

<!-- spread:2 image="spread-02-stripes.png" alt="Close on Zebra in the old days — a plain, smooth, grey-gold coat with no markings at all, standing proud and a little apart from the herd, chin high in the dusk light." -->

In those days Zebra wore no stripes.

She was one smooth colour,
the soft grey-gold of the dry grass,
and she thought herself
the most beautiful thing in the veld.

So she stood a little apart,
the way the proud ones do,
where the others could see her best —
and where she could be lonely best, too.

<!-- spread:3 image="spread-03-stripes.png" alt="Zebra lifting her chin among the dapple-coated antelope and wildebeest; she looks down her nose while they graze together in the long striped grass, not minding her." -->

"Look at you," Zebra told the herd,
"all smudged and spotted and speckled.
But I am one pure colour,
clean as the morning.
I am the loveliest of all."

The antelope only swished their tails.
The wildebeest went on grazing.
For they knew a thing Zebra did not,
and the long grass kept it for them.

<!-- spread:4 image="spread-04-stripes.png" alt="The dry season biting — the waterhole shrunk to a muddy eye, the grass tall and bleached pale, the open ground wide and exposed. The child carries water to the thirsty animals at the last pan." -->

Then came the long dry.

The water shrank to a muddy eye.
The grass grew tall and pale,
and the open ground went wide and bare,
where anything that stood alone
could be *seen*.

So {{CHILD}} carried water to the pan,
cupping it for the small ones first,
and watched the gold grass
and the long blue shadows it threw.

<!-- spread:5 image="spread-05-stripes.png" alt="HERO SPREAD. Dusk on open ground. A dignified golden lion on the rise. The dapple-coated herd melts into the striped grass and vanishes — only plain grey-gold Zebra is left standing, lit up bright, the one shape the eye can find." -->

One dusk, on the open ground,
a lion came down the rise —
not cruel, only hungry,
the honest danger of the open,
who hunts whatever he can *see*.

And the dapple-coated herd
stepped into the striped grass
and were *gone* —
light and shadow swallowed them whole.

Only Zebra was left.
One pure colour. Bright as a lamp.
The single thing the eye could find.

<!-- spread:6 image="spread-06-stripes.png" alt="The child standing bravely between the lion's gaze and Zebra, one arm out, calling her low and quiet toward the grass — but plain Zebra stands out against the dapple, with nothing of the grass on her coat to hide in." -->

{{CHILD}} reached her first.

Stood between her and the gold eyes.
Called her low, called her quiet,
*come, come into the grass.*

But Zebra had no grass to wear.
No gold, no dark, no dapple —
just one smooth colour,
shining in the open
like the moon on bare water.

<!-- spread:7 image="spread-07-stripes.png" alt="After the danger has passed. The herd safe and hidden in the long grass at twilight; Zebra standing alone and trembling, no longer proud, head lowered, asking the child a question." -->

The lion turned away that night —
{{CHILD}} was brave, and the dark came kind.
The herd was safe in the grass.
Zebra was safe, this once.

But she was trembling,
and for the first time
she was not proud at all.

"Why," she whispered,
"can everyone disappear but me?"

<!-- spread:8 image="spread-08-stripes.png" alt="Old Tortoise lifting his wise wrinkled head in the striped dusk light, the bars of gold and shadow falling across his shell, beginning to explain the oldest gentle idea." -->

Old Tortoise lifted his wrinkled head.
He remembered the oldest things,
and the gentlest way of doing them.

"Look," said Tortoise,
"how the evening lays itself down —

*Light and shadow, shadow and light.*

That is a coat, little one,
if you know how to wear it."

<!-- spread:9 image="spread-09-stripes.png" alt="COMPOSITE making-spread. The child, a flock of bright weaver birds, and the whole herd gathering the dusk dapple — catching bars of low gold sunlight in one hand and bars of blue-black shadow in the other, arms and beaks full of light and shade. A joyful working scene." -->

So they gathered the dusk.

{{CHILD}} caught the low gold sun
in one cupped, careful hand,
and the blue-black shadow in the other.
The weaver birds brought bars of light.
The whole herd brought the dark.

And as they worked they sang it,
soft as the wind in the dry grass —

*Light and shadow, shadow and light —*
*now you'll never be lost again.*

<!-- spread:10 image="spread-10-stripes.png" alt="The painting. The child and the animals laying the dapple onto Zebra's plain coat stripe by stripe — a bar of gold, then a bar of dark, then gold — Zebra standing very still and trusting in the warm dusk." -->

They laid it on her, stripe by stripe.

A bar of gold.
Then a bar of dark.
Then gold again, and dark again,
all down her trembling sides,
the grass itself made into a coat.

And Zebra held so very still,
while over her the whole herd sang:

*Light and shadow, shadow and light —*
*now you'll never be lost again.*

<!-- spread:11 image="spread-11-stripes.png" alt="The pay-off. Zebra stepping into the tall striped dusk grass and almost vanishing — her stripes melting perfectly into the bars of light and shadow. The lion's golden eyes in the distance slide right past her." -->

Then Zebra stepped into the grass.

And the grass took her in.

Where she ended and the dusk began,
no eye on earth could say.
The lion's gold gaze slid right past —
he saw only grass, and grass, and grass.

She was not hidden away.
She had become the place she stood in.

<!-- spread:12 image="spread-12-stripes.png" alt="The belonging turn. Striped Zebra walking back into the dapple-coated herd at twilight, and now you cannot tell where Zebra ends and the herd's dapple begins — she is inside the herd, one of many, no longer alone on the proud edge." -->

And when she walked back to the herd —

*nobody could find the edge of her.*

Where Zebra ended and the others began,
where her stripes stopped
and their dapple started,
no one could tell at all.

So she stood not apart, but among.
Not the loneliest. One of many.
The thing that made her different
was the thing that brought her home.

<!-- spread:13 image="spread-13-stripes.png" alt="The child and the striped Zebra at the waterhole in the last light, foreheads gently touching, a quiet private thank-you between them, the herd softly grazing behind." -->

At the water, in the last gold light,
{{CHILD}} pressed a forehead to her cheek.

"You were always lovely," said {{CHILD}}.
"But now the lovely thing
is the very thing that keeps you safe.

What made you different
was a gift the whole time.
We only had to help you wear it."

<!-- spread:14 image="spread-14-stripes.png" alt="Closing image. Night falling soft over the veld. The whole herd at rest in the long grass, striped Zebra among them barely visible in the dapple, the child curled safe and sleeping nearby under the thorn tree and the first stars." -->

And that, the old ones say,
is why to this very day
every zebra wears the dusk upon her back —

bars of gold and bars of dark,
the grass and the evening, both at once,
so she is never seen by the wrong eyes,
and never, ever lost.

*Light and shadow, shadow and light —*
*now you'll never be lost again.*

— THE END —
