## The crew (the recurring ensemble — each reads a different layer)

### Frik (Frederik Basson) — *reads reads THE LIE*
- **Wiring/margin:** Tourette's — involuntary, exquisitely-timed truth
- **Want:** to stop apologising for the body that keeps blurting what the room is hiding
- In a country fluent in the polite face, his throat is the one mouth that can't keep tatemae.

### Naila Okonkwo-Sato — *reads reads THE SYSTEM*
- **Wiring/margin:** autistic pattern-reader
- **Want:** to be trusted with the machinery, not managed around it
- Hand her the glossy brief and the 1899 statute and she sees they're the same document, painting in opposite directions.

### Mira Vos — *reads reads THE UNSPOKEN*
- **Wiring/margin:** profoundly deaf, lip- and face-reader
- **Want:** to be the one who decides when her own face is read
- She watches the honne under the bowing mouth and knows the smile that means no before the no arrives.

### Sandile 'Sandi' Dube — *reads reads TONGUES*
- **Wiring/margin:** polyglot; learner's Japanese + Ainu phrases
- **Want:** to reach people in their own words without mistaking translation for permission
- He can half-disarm a room in its mother tongue — and is learning that some words aren't his to say first.

### Yael Brandt — *reads reads THE GRIFT*
- **Wiring/margin:** reformed ad-money fixer
- **Want:** to atone for years selling exactly this kind of postcard
- She knows precisely why the buyer wants THIS Japan, because she used to be the one who sold it to him.

## The local leads (the camera belongs to them)

### Rera Kawamura — *Ainu*
- **Role:** Hokkaidō tour-guide and tonkori player who quit the heritage-park circuit
- **Want:** to be a living Ainu woman, not a curated exhibit or a tragic footnote  ·  **Wound:** raised to pass as plain Japanese by a mother who hid the language to protect her
- **Done being:** done performing 'we Japanese' for cameras that need her to be either invisible or a costume
- She'll give the crew tea, jokes, and her grandmother's songs — but not the timeless-noble-savage shot, and not on their schedule.

### Tōru Maeda — *buraku(fictionalised)*
- **Role:** third-generation taiko-skin craftsman in a fictional western-Kansai workshop, now plays in a reclamation drum ensemble
- **Want:** to drum loud and named, in the open, the way his grandfather never could  ·  **Wound:** a marriage that collapsed when the other family ran his koseki; a leaked online 'area list'
- **Done being:** done with the proverb 'don't wake the sleeping child' — but on HIS terms, not an outsider's exposé
- He'll teach you how a drum is born from a hide and a life — and he decides who hears that, and what gets filmed.

### Sayaka Ishii — *keeper*
- **Role:** Bushido-as-construction historian; granddaughter of a war-drum maker, married into Maeda's community
- **Want:** to make people love the real, plural Edo before they grieve the myth  ·  **Wound:** watched a beloved uncle weaponise the invented 'code' to justify cruelty
- **Done being:** done letting the 1900 postcard pass as ancient law
- She'll show you the most beloved ancient code on earth was written in English, in 1900, for foreigners — and love the real thing more for it.

## The keepers (living-craft masters)

### Genzō Aoki — *swordsmith*
- His family forged swords until 1876; now he makes knives, the shokunin who outlasted the bushi, and he gives blades, never sells the good ones.

### Hori-Umi (Umeko Tanabe) — *horishi*
- An old hand-poke master who reads the man across the tea before she reads the skin, and chooses the design herself.

## The flattening force

### Daniel Reyes / 'KOKORO' (the commission) — *media/postcard-seller*
- **Wants:** a flawless luxury-streaming film — 'Soul of the Samurai: Timeless Japan' — sold on the most beloved, best-selling version of the lie
- **Not a cartoon:** Reyes genuinely loves Japan, treats the crew well, pays beautifully, and believes reverence is honour; he is the polite, well-meaning face of erasure, not a villain — the postcard sells because it's beautiful
- **How they lose:** not outgunned: the leads simply will not be spoken for; every postcard frame the commission needs requires erasing the actual person standing in it, so the only honest film is the one the brief forbids — and the crew chooses to hand back the mic instead of deliver the gloss
