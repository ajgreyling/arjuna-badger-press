# OUTLINE — *The Unheard: Japan* (*The Way That Was Invented*)

> A crew arrives to film the soul-of-the-samurai postcard and meets the people Japan's perfect picture was built to bury — an Ainu woman done performing a country that won't claim her, and a man whose family skinned its war-drums.

## ch-00 — Clay and Water
*POV:* Genzō Aoki, then a quick cut wide  ·  *beat:* arrive/cold-open  ·  *thread:* inversion  ·  *Jakobus:* none  ·  *place:* Genzō Aoki's forge; Ishi-no-Hōden, Takasago, Hyōgo  ·  *~700w*

In the blue dark Genzō paints clay along a blade's spine and plunges the steel into the quench; the metal curves itself in the fire — real metallurgy, the one thing the postcard gets right. At noon the camera cuts to a ~500-ton stone that appears to float on water at a Hyōgo shrine. A single line lands the inversion: the leather that wrapped the warrior's armour and the skin stretched over the drum that called him to war were tanned by hands this country still will not name.

**Continuity:** Genzō's family made swords until 1876 then knives; the float is an engineered effect (hidden central pedestal over a water basin), purpose genuinely unsolved by the 2005 laser survey; stone is welded tuff, NOT granite; establishes the braid (blade aesthetic / hidden hands / wonder)

## ch-03 — She Will Not Give You the Postcard
*POV:* Rera  ·  *beat:* read/arrive (the local who is DONE)  ·  *thread:* ainu  ·  *Jakobus:* guard  ·  *place:* Hokkaidō  ·  *~3000w*

The crew flies north for the 'Indigenous wonder' segment and meets Rera, hired as a guide. She's funny, modern, irritated by their schedule, and she flatly refuses to stand in the elm-bast robe by the river for golden hour. When Sandi tries an Ainu greeting he half-learned, she corrects his vowel, laughs, and tells him a museum is not a person. The crew's whole shimmering reverent brief, she points out, is the erasure: to film 'timeless homogeneous Japan' is to paint over her.

**Continuity:** Rera quit the heritage-park circuit; her mother hid the language to let her pass; Ainu = language isolate, animist (kamuy), recognised as Indigenous only 2019 (shallow, tourism-shaped); Rera plays tonkori; NO fresh traditional Ainu facial tattoos depicted

## ch-04 — The Word the Prime Minister Said
*POV:* Naila  ·  *beat:* obstacle/flattening force (institution)  ·  *thread:* ainu  ·  *Jakobus:* none  ·  *place:* Upopoy, Shiraoi, Hokkaidō  ·  *~3000w*

Reyes wants the gleaming national museum as the Ainu segment — clean, state-blessed, photogenic. Inside, Naila maps the architecture of the framing: how the memorial hall holds ancestral remains, how the oppression history is sanded soft, how 'preserved in glass' is the exoticising the book exists to reject. Rera walks them through it deadpan, points at a placard, and quotes a 1986 prime minister calling Japan a nation with no minorities. The crew's instinct — to film the beautiful museum as the answer — is wrong, and Rera says so.

**Continuity:** museum ≠ living people; 1899 assimilation law (banned language/customs) stood until repealed 1997; the 2019 Act gave recognition but NO land/self-determination/apology; ancestral-remains repatriation is live and painful; population contested (~11,450 self-ID 2023 vs far higher real number)

## ch-05 — The Humility Beat
*POV:* Mira  ·  *beat:* cost/the crew's instinct is WRONG  ·  *thread:* ainu  ·  *Jakobus:* guard  ·  *place:* Hokkaidō  ·  *~3200w*

## ch-06 — Changing to Remain Unchanged
*POV:* Sandi  ·  *beat:* key/arrive (keeper)  ·  *thread:* keeper  ·  *Jakobus:* guard  ·  *place:* Ise Jingū & a miyadaiku workyard  ·  *~3000w*

South again for the 'craft' segment. At Ise — rebuilt from scratch every 20 years for 1,300 years — a miyadaiku temple-carpenter joins wood without a nail and explains, dry as sawdust, that nothing here is ancient: it's all new, on purpose, to keep the craft alive. Sandi reaches him in careful Japanese and the man lets the crew film the joinery but not the inner rite. The crew's idea of 'timeless Japan' starts coming apart: the wonder survives only by being remade.

**Continuity:** Ise = 'changing to remain unchanged'; non-photography rules at Ise (verify/respect); craft handed body-to-body, not graved; sets up the keepers as proof against erasure

## ch-08 — The Code Written in English
*POV:* Sayaka  ·  *beat:* key/the inversion lands  ·  *thread:* inversion  ·  *Jakobus:* none  ·  *place:* Kyoto  ·  *~3200w*

Sayaka walks the crew through the central inversion as a real, alive scene, not a lecture: Bushido — the world's most beloved ancient code — was largely synthesised by Nitobe in 1900, written in English, abroad, for Westerners, shaped like European chivalry, then weaponised by WWII militarism. Most Edo samurai were bureaucrats, not duellists. She doesn't sneer; she loves the plural real thing more than the myth, and tells the crew their whole brief is selling a draft as scripture. Frik's throat, mid-tea, lands the truth no one will say about the commission.

**Continuity:** Bushido = real-but-CONSTRUCTED tradition (Nitobe 1900, English, for the West; WWII weaponisation; plural Edo reality); narrative voice NEVER adopts the homogeneity/timeless-code framing; the book loves the real, never sneers

## ch-09 — The Floating Stone
*POV:* Naila  ·  *beat:* read/the wonder, grounded  ·  *thread:* wonder  ·  *Jakobus:* none  ·  *place:* Ishi-no-Hōden, Takasago, Hyōgo  ·  *~3000w*

At the floating stone Naila reads the engineering: the basin, the hidden central pedestal, the welded tuff, the absence of woo. The 2005 laser survey found no tool marks and left who/when/why genuinely unsolved — a real mystery with no aliens in it. Rera and Tōru both come; standing at the rail, the leads and the crew feel the rhyme — a wonder whose makers are unknown, in a country that knows exactly whose names to leave off the plaque. Reyes wants it as the film's mystical centrepiece; Naila says the honest caption is 'we don't know,' and that's the part he can't sell.

**Continuity:** ~500-ton worked tuff, engineered float via hidden pedestal over water basin, NOT granite; purpose UNSOLVED (2005 laser survey, Harima Fudoki c.713 mention); NO Ancient-Aliens framing; the wonder rhymes with unnamed makers

## ch-11 — The Wave That Does Not Fall
*POV:* Mira / Jakobus from outside  ·  *beat:* cost/jakobus beat (peripheral)  ·  *thread:* keeper  ·  *Jakobus:* onsen  ·  *place:* Hori-Umi's studio; an onsen  ·  *~3000w*

In one hand-poked sitting with the nomi, Hori-Umi inks a great wave caught at the instant of its full weight — all crushing power, the moment before it breaks — and it does not fall. Power held soft, low where it'll never show above a collar. That evening the onsen door is shut to him: the most ink-revering country won't let the inked into its water. One quiet, earned line — the heaviest thing in the room, set down soft — carries the whole moment and reads as pure character to everyone in it.

**Continuity:** motif LOCKED = the great wave held at full weight that does not break (power held soft); inked low/hidden; onsen tattoo-ban shuts the bath to him all book; the single double-duty line must be earned, plain, embodied, once — never glossed; G-nod stays PURE story (no symbols/equation)
