## A. The "homogeneous Japan" myth — the engine of the inversion

- **The myth is modern, not ancient.** Scholarship dates the "one race, one language, one nation"
  ideology to the **post-WWII loss of empire**, and treats it as a constructed national self-image, not
  a historical fact — the *nihonjinron* ("theories of Japaneseness") discourse.
  [Chris Burgess, "Multicultural Japan? Discourse and the 'Myth' of Homogeneity," Asia-Pacific Journal](https://apjjf.org/chris-burgess/2389/article)
  (page 403'd on direct fetch; real and citable, corroborated below)
- **Politicians have said it out loud — the usable anchors.** PM **Yasuhiro Nakasone** called Japan a
  **"monoethnic nation"** in 1986 in which minorities "do not exist" — a remark that provoked Ainu
  protest and helped catalyse Indigenous activism.
  [Christian Science Monitor, Nov 1986](https://www.csmonitor.com/1986/1107/orace.html) ·
  [Springer: "Ainu: 'Homogenous' Japan's Indigenous People"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-6300-124-3_10)
  Deputy-PM **Tarō Asō** repeated the "one race / one nation" line in 2005 and again in 2020.
  [Tokyo Weekender, "The Mono-ethnic Myth"](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/news-and-opinion/the-mono-ethnic-myth/)
- **The erased layers:** **Ainu** (§B), **burakumin** (§C), plus — pointers only, other strands own the
  depth — **Zainichi Koreans** (ethnic Koreans resident for generations, rooted in the colonial period)
  and **Okinawans / Ryukyuans** (the formerly independent **Ryukyu Kingdom**, annexed 1879, distinct
  language, the wartime + US-base burden). [Burgess, APJ](https://apjjf.org/chris-burgess/2389/article)

## B. The Ainu — Japan's Indigenous people (the keel-anchor unheard people)

- **Who.** Indigenous people of Hokkaidō (historically also southern Sakhalin, the Kurils, northern
  Honshū). Distinct **language isolate** (no proven relation to Japanese or anything else),
  **critically endangered** — UNESCO's 2009 Atlas listed it critically endangered (~15 speakers then);
  fluent-speaker count today is tiny and **genuinely disputed** (Wikipedia: effectively no native
  speakers by 2021; Endangered Languages Project lists "two native speakers" as of 2025). State as
  contested, never a flat number.
  [Minority Rights Group: Ainu](https://minorityrights.org/communities/ainu/) ·
  [Wikipedia: Ainu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_language) ·
  [Endangered Languages Project: Ainu](https://www.endangeredlanguages.com/lang/ain?hl=en)
- **Worldview** is **animist** — *kamuy* (spirits) inhabit animals, fire, water, tools, weather. The
  most sacred rite is **iyomante** (the bear-sending ceremony). **Material culture:** *attus/attush*
  elm-bast robes (woven by women), carved/appliquéd swirl patterns (*moreu*), and women's **mouth/lip
  tattoos** (see firewall). [Facts and Details: Ainu religion](https://factsanddetails.com/japan/cat18/sub119/item638.html) ·
  [University of Oregon, *Windows to the Ainu World*: iyomante](https://glam.uoregon.edu/s/uo-ainu-collections/page/iyomante)
- **The erasure.** Hokkaidō annexed/renamed from "Ezo" 1869. The **1899 Hokkaidō Former Aborigines
  Protection Act** forced assimilation — redistributed Ainu land to settlers, banned traditional
  hunting/fishing, imposed Japanese schooling, suppressed ceremony; **tattooing banned by order in
  1871**. That 1899 law stood until **repealed only in 1997**.
  [Wikipedia: Hokkaido Former Aborigines Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_Former_Aborigines_Protection_Act) ·
  [Georgetown Journal of International Affairs](https://gjia.georgetown.edu/society-culture/culture-centered-indigenous-policies-in-japan/)
- **Recognition timeline (precise).** **6 June 2008** — Diet *resolution* recognising the Ainu as
  Indigenous (a resolution, not binding law). **2019 Ainu Policy Promotion Act** (in force **24 May
  2019**) — the **first statute** to legally recognise the Ainu as Indigenous and prohibit
  discrimination. **What it did NOT do:** no UNDRIP rights, no land/resource rights, no
  self-determination, no formal apology; critics call it **culture/tourism-focused and shallow**.
  [Al Jazeera, Feb 2019](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/02/japan-recognise-ainu-indigenous-people-time-190215053116742.html) ·
  [Int'l Journal of Human Rights analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13642987.2020.1811692) ·
  [IWGIA: Indigenous World 2025 — Japan](https://iwgia.org/en/japan/5664-iw-2025-japan.html)
- **Living culture today.** **Upopoy National Ainu Museum and Park**, Shiraoi, opened **12 July 2020**
  — Japan's first national Ainu institution. **The debate (flag it):** critics call it state-curated,
  soft on the oppression history, commodifying, and object to its **memorial hall** holding ancestral
  remains. Revival is real (Ainu-language broadcasts, lessons). **Food:** *ohaw* (salmon/venison soup),
  *sito* (pounded-grain dumplings), *sayo* (porridge). **Music:** *tonkori* (five-string zither),
  *mukkuri* (bamboo mouth-harp), *upopo* (round-songs).
  [Wikipedia: National Ainu Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ainu_Museum) ·
  [HURIGHTS Osaka, "Critiquing the Colonialist Origins of Upopoy"](https://www.hurights.or.jp/archives/focus/section3/2022/03/critiquing-the-colonialist-origins-of-the-new-national-museum-upopoy.html) ·
  [Wikipedia: Mukkuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukkuri)
- **Population reality.** Government self-identification surveys: Hokkaidō **~11,450 (2023)** — but the
  real number is unknown and almost certainly far higher (unofficial estimates reach **200,000+**)
  because of assimilation, concealed identity, and discrimination. The gap **is** the point about
  hidden identity; 2023 survey: perceived discrimination **rose to 31%**.
  [Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/communities/ainu/) ·
  [IWGIA 2025](https://iwgia.org/en/japan/5664-iw-2025-japan.html)

**Ainu — handle with care (sensitivity reader, veto):**
- **The slur to AVOID:** never pun "Ainu" (which means *human*) with Japanese *inu* ("dog") — a real,
  live racist trope (a 2021 Nippon TV show had to apologise for it). A bigot character could weaponise
  it; **never the narrative voice**, and signpost it as the slur it is.
  [Unseen Japan](https://unseen-japan.com/japanese-news-program-apologizes-for-calling-ainu-dogs/)
- **iyomante (bear ceremony):** sacred, not spectacle. Prefecture-banned 1955 as "cruel," **rescinded
  2007**; the live-cub form is essentially no longer practised. Present the Ainu theology *and* the
  outside "cruelty" framing, privilege the Ainu view, **do not stage a fictional live sacrifice as
  colour.** [Journal of Northern Studies 2018 (PDF)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1939841/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- **Women's mouth tattoos:** real, sacred (adulthood/protection), **suppressed by the 1871 ban**; the
  **last traditionally-tattooed Ainu woman reportedly died in 1998**. Treat as a near-lost sacred
  practice — **do NOT put fresh traditional Ainu facial tattoos on any character** (there is a live
  reclamation conversation a sensitivity reader must steer). **This is also why Jakobus's tattoo in this
  book is from a Japanese *horishi*, NOT an Ainu hand — see §G firewall.**
  [Lars Krutak: tattooing among the Ainu](https://larskrutak.com/tattooing-among-japans-ainu-people/)
- **Museum-vs-living-people tension:** don't let an Upopoy visit stand in for living people; the
  "preserved in glass" move is exactly the exoticising the book exists to reject. Ancestral-remains
  repatriation is an ongoing, painful issue. [IWGIA 2025](https://iwgia.org/en/japan/5664-iw-2025-japan.html)
- ***kamuy*** and the spirit-logic are **living belief** — render from inside, don't adjudicate true/false.

## C. The burakumin — the hereditary caste-outcasts (the MOST sensitivity-critical strand)

> **Read this whole section before writing a line of this strand. Sensitivity reader with veto is
> non-negotiable. The cardinal harms are (1) using the slurs and (2) naming/mapping real communities.**

- **Who.** Descendants of feudal-era outcast communities — **not** a racial/ethnic minority but a
  **caste-like minority among ethnic Japanese**. The Tokugawa order fixed two categories: **eta**
  (穢多, tied to butchery/leatherwork) and **hinin** (非人, "non-person"). Their work was deemed
  **kegare** (ritual pollution) under Buddhist/Shinto belief — anything touching **death and blood**:
  tanning, butchery, executioners, gravediggers, undertakers.
  [IMADR: Dalits & Buraku](https://imadr.org/dalits-buraku/) ·
  [Britannica: burakumin](https://www.britannica.com/topic/burakumin) ·
  [Wikipedia: Burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin)
- **TERMINOLOGY — load-bearing.** ***eta*, *hinin*, *yotsu*, *kawata* are SLURS** — never in narration
  except clearly-marked historical/quoted context. Respectful/current terms: **burakumin**, **hisabetsu
  buraku** (被差別部落, "discriminated communities"), plain **buraku**, **dōwa** (同和). Note even
  *burakumin* carries some discriminatory connotation; scholarship/activism prefer *hisabetsu buraku*.
  [Wikipedia: Burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin) ·
  [Unseen Japan: Japan's Burakumin outcasts](https://unseen-japan.com/japan-burakumin-outcasts/)
- **The craft inversion (the "hidden hands" — verify each; documentation varies SHARPLY):**
  - **(a) Leather for samurai armour — WELL DOCUMENTED.** *Daimyō* organised the outcaste leather-workers
    (*kawata*) "to ensure a stable supply of leather for military goods like armour"; armour, saddlery,
    and gear were a real economic base. [Wikipedia: Burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin) ·
    [Encyclopedia.com: Burakumin](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/burakumin)
  - **(b) Taiko drum skins — WELL DOCUMENTED, and a dignity-affirming beat.** "Without leather, there is
    no taiko drum"; most drum craftsmen were buraku of **Naniwa, Osaka**; the **Ikari** ensemble (founded
    1987) was the first to *openly* identify as buraku and reclaim taiko as activism.
    [Cal Poly thesis](https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1045&context=histsp) ·
    [LSE Human Rights blog: "Drumming Out Resistance"](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/humanrights/2015/03/04/drumming-out-resistance-in-japan-writing-back-burakumin-identity-through-music/)
  - **(c) Footwear/sandals — PARTIAL / folk-association.** Leather *setta* are real and Nara (a footwear
    capital) overlaps buraku leather geography, but I could **not** source a solid direct tie. Treat as
    plausible-but-thin; don't assert.
  - **(d) The SWORD trade (saya/handle-wrap/polish/leather fittings) — FOLK-ASSOCIATION, NOT VERIFIED.
    FLAG HARD.** Dedicated searches found only modern commercial sword sites, no caste framing. **Do NOT
    state in-world that burakumin wrapped/polished swords as fact.** Route the "hidden hands beneath the
    celebrated blade" through the *documented* **armour-leather** link, or mark it explicitly as a
    character's folk-belief — and clear it with the reader. *(This correction matters: the obvious
    "outcasts made the samurai's swords" beat is the one that's unsupported; the true, equally powerful
    beat is "outcasts tanned the leather of his armour and skinned the drums that called him to war.")*
- **1871 emancipation.** The **Senmin Haishirei / Kaihōrei** ("Emancipation Edict," 28 Aug 1871)
  abolished *eta*/*hinin* status. It did **not** end discrimination: it **stripped their guaranteed
  monopoly trades** (notably leather), worsening livelihoods; triggered anti-buraku violence; spawned a
  new slur, *shin-heimin* ("new commoners"); and the **koseki** family-registry kept ancestry traceable.
  [Wikipedia: Burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin)
- **Modern discrimination — the defining scandal.** The **Buraku Chimei Sōkan** ("Comprehensive List of
  Buraku Area Names"), exposed **1975** — a directory of buraku settlement locations sold secretly to
  **200+ major firms** (incl. Toyota, Nissan, Honda, Daihatsu) to screen job/marriage candidates. Never
  fully erased — copies still surface online (IMADR: lists "recently uploaded to the internet" with "no
  effective protection or remedy"). The **Buraku Liberation League (BLL)** descends from the 1922
  **Suiheisha** ("Levellers"). A **2016 Act** legally recognised the discrimination still exists. The
  topic stays **largely unspoken** — the proverb *neta ko o okosu na* ("don't wake the sleeping child").
  [IMADR HRC31](https://imadr.org/buraku-discrimination-japan-hrc31-2016-os/) ·
  [Wikipedia: Buraku Liberation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buraku_Liberation_League) ·
  [Japan Times: "Breaking the silence on burakumin"](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/community/2009/01/20/issues/breaking-the-silence-on-burakumin-2/)
- **Numbers + geography (contested).** No census tracks caste; most burakumin **pass / don't
  self-identify**. Estimates: official ~**1.2M**; commonly cited ~**1.5–3M**. Heavily **western Japan /
  Kansai-weighted** (Osaka, Kyoto, Hyōgo; also Fukuoka, Hiroshima, Nara); **none in Hokkaidō or
  Tōhoku**. Always caveat hidden identity + disputed figures.
  [Wikipedia: Burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin) · [IMADR](https://imadr.org/dalits-buraku/)

**Burakumin — handle with EXTREME care:**
- **Mapping = harm.** The entire injustice runs on *identifying where people live*. **Use fictionalised
  places; never name or geolocate a real present-day community.** A novel that pins real districts on a
  map reproduces the weapon (cf. the 2009 Google Earth Tokugawa-map controversy, recurring list-leaks).
- **"Outing" is the cardinal danger.** No genetic/physical marker; many concealed origin. A plot that
  hinges on *revealing* someone's buraku ancestry must be handled as the violence it is, **never a
  reveal-for-thrill.**
- **The scholarship is a live battlefield (the Ramseyer affair).** Harvard's J. Mark Ramseyer's 2019
  buraku paper drew a **special refutation issue** of the Asia-Pacific Journal (historians: "dangerous
  inventions"). **Don't lean on any single revisionist source;** favour IMADR, the BLL's research
  institute (**BLHRRI**), and peer critique. [APJJF special issue](https://apjjf.org/2021/9/toc) ·
  [Cambridge: "Doing Violence to Buraku History"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asia-pacific-journal/article/doing-violence-to-buraku-history-j-mark-ramseyers-dangerous-inventions/C6DD840896B49EE1D59D1EB39864F761)
- **Don't flatten to "Japan's untouchables."** IMADR does draw the Dalit parallel ("discrimination
  based on work and descent"), but burakumin are **ethnically Japanese**; the exoticising frame is part
  of what the book undoes.

## D. The "floating rock" and the worked-stone wonders (the witnessable wonder)

- **Ishi-no-Hōden (石の宝殿) — THE floating rock.** A colossal carved stone block at **Ōshiko Shrine,
  Takasago, Hyōgo** (Kansai, ~40 min west of Himeji) — a reader can stand at the railing. The
  **"floating" is real and grounded — an engineered visual effect, not woo:** the block sits in a
  rock-cut basin that fills with water, resting on a **single central pedestal invisible at eye level**,
  so the mass reads as hovering. It was **cut from the living bedrock** of the Tatsuyama quarry and (the
  sober reading) was likely **never separated from the ground beneath**. **~5.7 m H × 6.4 m W × 7.2 m
  D**, est. **~500 tons** if freestanding.
  **CORRECTION to common assumption: it is NOT granite** — it's **rhyolitic welded tuff** (workable
  quarry stone, used in Himeji Castle), which *strengthens* the grounded read.
  **The genuine mystery (use this):** a **2005–06 laser 3-D survey** (Takasago City + Otemae
  University) found **no tool marks** on the surface and concluded **who carved it, when, and why remain
  unknown**; earliest mention is the *Harima Fudoki* (c. 713 CE). Sober leading theory: an unfinished
  monument/sarcophagus abandoned at the quarry. **National Historic Site (1979).**
  [Wikipedia: Ishi-no-Hōden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishi_no_H%C5%8Dden) ·
  [Ancient Origins (use for the laser-survey fact only)](https://www.ancient-origins.net/myths-legends-asia/ishi-no-hoden-0015229) ·
  [The Heritage Trust](https://theheritagetrust.wordpress.com/2014/10/27/the-ishi-no-hoden-megalith/)
- **Masuda-no-iwafune (益田岩船) — the granite "rock-ship" (second stone node).** A vast carved
  **granite** block on a hilltop near Okadera Station, **Asuka, Nara**. ~**11 × 8 × 4.5 m**; weight
  **disputed** (~800 tons by volume; one widely-copied "~80 tons" figure is almost certainly an error —
  **give a range, don't state one number**). Two precise **~1 m-square holes** bored through the top, a
  carved lattice on the flank — **purpose lost to time**; the *precision of square cuts in hard granite*
  is the legitimate engineering wonder. Loosely 7th-c. (end of the Kofun period).
  [Daily Grail](https://www.dailygrail.com/2018/10/masuda-no-iwafune-the-enigmatic-rock-ship-of-japan/) ·
  [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/the-purpose-of-japans-huge-rock-ship-of-masuda-is-lost-to-time-68519)
- **The Asuka stones (a setting cluster).** **Ishibutai Kofun** — Japan's largest megalithic chamber
  tomb, its mound eroded to expose the bare granite skeleton; capstones **~60 t / ~77 t**; believed the
  tomb of **Soga no Umako (d. 626)**. (The "how did they move 77 t" question is real but the *answer* —
  rollers, ramps, labour — is known, so it's engineering awe, not unsolved.) **Sakafune-ishi** — an
  incised granite slab whose puzzle was reframed as a 7th-c. imperial **water-ritual** installation
  (good "mystery that got solved" texture).
  [Wikipedia: Ishibutai Kofun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishibutai_Kofun) ·
  [Nara iko: Sakafune-ishi](https://naraiko.pref.nara.jp/en/spots/mfo8utpdem)
- **Mt. Nokogiri / Nihon-ji (Chiba)** — saw-tooth quarry cliffs, the **Jigoku-nozoki** overhanging
  rock platform over a sheer drop, Japan's tallest pre-modern stone **Daibutsu** (31 m, 1783). **No
  unexplained-engineering angle** (documented Edo quarrying) — excellent **vertigo set-piece location**,
  weak as a "mystery wonder." [JeePe guide](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/nokogiriyama-travel-guide-1123)

**Fringe-claims to AVOID (so the grounding holds):** the Ishi-no-Hōden / Masuda stones are heavily
colonised by *Ancient Aliens* content (S12E14 "A Spaceship Made of Stone") and "unknown machines /
Mu / Yonaguni / lost-continent" framings. **Steer clear.** Avoid the "a 500-ton block could not have
been carved by hand" trope; the truthful, more interesting line is that it *was* hand-quarried tuff,
and what's genuinely unknown is the **purpose** and why it was **left unfinished** — not the physics.
*(This is the exact RELIC/grounded-mythos register: show the worked stone, let the open question
stand, never reach for aliens.)*

## E. Living keepers of the old ways (the "fear of loss" strand — culture handed on, not graved)

- **Miyadaiku (宮大工) — temple/shrine carpenters.** Build/restore with **kigumi** interlocking joints,
  **no nails or metal**; joints *tighten over time* as timber settles. Legend figure: **Tsunekazu
  Nishioka (1908–1995)**, last master carpenter of **Hōryū-ji** (father and grandfather held the same
  post). **Kongō Gumi** — founded **578 AD** (by a miyadaiku invited from **Baekje**/Korea by Prince
  Shōtoku to build Shitennō-ji); ran in the family **40 generations**; the **world's oldest documented
  continuously operating company** — with the caveat that strict independence ended when **Takamatsu
  Construction** absorbed it in **2006**; it still builds/repairs temples today.
  [Japan Woodcraft Assoc.: Nishioka](https://japanwoodcraftassociation.com/masters/tsunekazu-nishioka/) ·
  [Wikipedia: Kongō Gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kong%C5%8D_Gumi)
- **Shikinen Sengū (式年遷宮) — Ise Grand Shrine, rebuilt from scratch every 20 years for ~1,300 years.**
  The **2013 rebuild was the 62nd**; the next cycle of rites **began 2025** (concludes ~2033/2034 —
  sources differ on the end-year; flag). Everything is remade — buildings, treasures, divine apparel —
  deliberately to **transmit the craft to the next generation**. Official framing: **"changing to
  remain unchanged"** — eternal *because* renewed. **This is the strand's perfect load-bearing wonder:
  the literal embodiment of "handed on, not graved."**
  [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/this-japanese-shrine-is-torn-down-every-20-years) ·
  [Visit Iseshima: Sengū](https://www.iseshima-kanko.jp/en/highlights/sengu_eg)
- **Shugendō / yamabushi (山伏).** Mountain ascetics of a syncretic Shinto-Buddhist path (waterfall
  austerities, cliff rites). Living centres: **Dewa Sanzan** (Haguro = birth, Gassan = death, Yudono =
  rebirth), **Kumano**, **Ōmine**. **Near-extinction:** **banned by Meiji decree in 1872** under
  *shinbutsu bunri* (forced separation of Buddhism and Shinto) as "superstition"; **Haguro survived
  uniquely** by splitting into a Buddhist temple and a Shinto shrine; practice continues, small.
  [Wikipedia: Three Mountains of Dewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mountains_of_Dewa) ·
  [DIJ Tokyo: Shugendō & shinbutsu bunri at Haguro](https://www.dijtokyo.org/event/shugendo-and-the-separation-of-buddha-and-kami-worship-shinbutsu-bunri-the-case-of-hagurosan-1870-1875/)
- **Sōhei (warrior monks) — HISTORICAL ONLY** (the feudal past the living traditions descend from, not
  a living order). Enryaku-ji on Mt. Hiei and the Ikkō-ikki were crushed by **Oda Nobunaga** (razed
  Enryaku-ji **1571**). [Tokyo Weekender: warrior monks](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/art_and_culture/history/the-rise-and-fall-of-japans-warrior-monks/)
- **Pilgrimage as living practice.** **Kumano Kodō / Kii Mountains** — UNESCO-inscribed **2004**; one
  of only **two** pilgrimage routes UNESCO-listed (the other: the Camino de Santiago); still walked.
  **Shikoku 88-temple pilgrimage (ohenro)** — ~1,200 km, 88 temples tied to **Kūkai**; pilgrims in
  white receive **osettai** (alms/hospitality) from locals.
  [UNESCO #1142](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1142/) ·
  [Shikoku Tourism: ohenro](https://shikoku-tourism.com/en/about-shikoku/ohenro)
- **Witnessable living wonders.** **Hōryū-ji** (Nara; founded 607; **oldest surviving wooden buildings
  in the world**; Japan's first UNESCO site, 1993). **Mt. Kōya (Kōyasan)** — Shingon HQ; the
  **Okunoin** necropolis (200,000+ stupas); **devout belief:** Kūkai did not die but entered **eternal
  meditation (nyūjō)** in 835 — monks have brought him **meals twice daily for 1,000+ years**. **Render
  as living devotion, never debunk.** [UNESCO #660: Hōryū-ji](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/660/) ·
  [Nat Geo: Kōyasan](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/temples-and-meditation-with-monks-on-japans-holy-koyasan)

**Keepers — sacred / handle with care:** Kūkai's eternal meditation = devout belief, not a fact to
assert/disprove. **Ōmine / Mt. Sanjō still enforces *nyonin kekkai* (female exclusion)** — the only
major Shugendō peak that does; a 2004 petition to lift it failed; **genuinely contested and live**, not
settled colour. **Yudono-san is secret** ("what is seen here is not spoken of"; photography forbidden
in the inner sanctum) — verify the current rule before depicting any rite. Ise's inner sanctuaries are
screened and not photographed. Shrine etiquette: bow at the torii, purify at the *temizuya*, the centre
of the path is the kami's. [Buddhistdoor: women in Shugendō](https://www.buddhistdoor.net/features/women-in-shugendo-supporters-leaders-or-both-part-1/) ·
[Wikipedia: Nyonin Kinsei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyonin_Kinsei)

## F. Bushido & the samurai heritage — handled HONESTLY (the central paint-over)

- **"Bushido" as a single, timeless, codified "way of the warrior" is largely a MODERN INVENTION.** The
  word barely appears before 1900. **Basil Hall Chamberlain** argued bushido "as an institution or a
  code of rules, has never existed," and accounts of it were "fabricated out of whole cloth, chiefly for
  foreign consumption."
  [Columbia: "The Modern Re-invention of Bushido"](https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/chushinguranew/Bushido/reinvention.htm)
  (403'd on fetch; real, corroborated below)
- **The popular global image = Nitobe Inazō's *Bushido: The Soul of Japan* (1900)** — crucially
  **written in English, abroad, for a Western readership**, by a Christian internationalist who drew
  explicit parallels to European chivalry and codified "seven virtues." It "portray[ed] the samurai in
  terms of Western chivalry."
  [Wikipedia: *Bushido: The Soul of Japan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushido:_The_Soul_of_Japan) ·
  [Project Gutenberg full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/12096)
- **Scholarly consensus: an "invented tradition."** **Oleg Benesch, *Inventing the Way of the Samurai*
  (Oxford 2014)** — its modern theorists were "concerned less with Japan's past than the nation's
  future," assembling *multiple, inconsistent* versions **after the samurai class was already
  abolished**. Historians **Friday, Hurst, Conlan** independently: **no overarching warrior ideology
  existed in premodern times.**
  [transnationalhistory.net on Benesch](https://www.transnationalhistory.net/history/2020/10/24/modern-japanese-bushido-an-invented-tradition/) ·
  [Karl Friday interview, Budo Japan](https://budojapan.com/kenjutsu-iai/20210714kf/)
- **Real Edo ethics were plural & contested.** One pole: the **Hagakure** (Yamamoto Tsunetomo, ~1709–16)
  — *"The way of the samurai is found in death."* Against it: the **Confucian-administrator ethic** of
  the peaceful Tokugawa centuries (loyalty = good governance, not dying). Later the Meiji→Shōwa state
  **weaponised "bushido" for nationalism**, redirecting loyalty to the emperor and, by WWII, justifying
  militarism. [Wikiquote: Yamamoto Tsunetomo](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Yamamoto_Tsunetomo)
- **The real samurai = a hereditary military-administrative CLASS;** after ~250 years of peace **most
  Edo samurai were salaried bureaucrats**, not duellists. Class dismantled in the **Meiji Restoration**
  (stipends cut 1873; **Haitōrei sword-ban, 28 March 1876**). Death rattle: the **Satsuma Rebellion
  (1877)** under **Saigō Takamori** — both sides used **guns and Western tactics**; Saigō wore an
  **imperial officer's uniform**, not heirloom armour. **Cliché to avoid: Hollywood's *The Last Samurai*
  (2003)** (armour-vs-guns, ninja assassins, white-saviour mentor — all invention).
  [Wikipedia: Haitō Edict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hait%C5%8D_Edict) ·
  [Britannica: Satsuma Rebellion](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Satsuma-Rebellion) ·
  [HistorIQly: Last Samurai vs history](https://blog.historiqly.com/blog/last-samurai-vs-history)
- **Seppuku** — handle **soberly, factually**, never spectacle. A *kaishakunin* (second) typically
  beheaded the dying man to shorten agony; abolished as punishment **1873**.
  [Britannica: seppuku](https://www.britannica.com/topic/seppuku)
- **Living values worth using (don't over-mystify):** **gaman** (enduring with dignity — note the dark
  side, suppressing real distress), **giri** (duty, vs. *ninjō* human feeling), **kata** (perfected
  repeated form — the line from martial drill to **craft mastery**), **shokunin** (the artisan's
  devotion to craft, with a *social* obligation to do one's best — **load-bearing for the keepers
  theme**), **ichigo ichie** (one time, one meeting — from tea culture), **omotenashi** (selfless
  hospitality), **mono no aware / wabi-sabi** (pathos at impermanence; beauty in imperfection).
  [Kyoto Journal: Shokunin and Devotion](https://kyotojournal.org/culture-arts/shokunin-and-devotion/) ·
  [Wikipedia: Gaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaman_(term)) ·
  [Wikipedia: Mono no aware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_no_aware)
- **Castles/temples as witnessable wonders (bucket-list):** **Himeji Castle** ("White Heron"; the finest
  surviving feudal castle; UNESCO 1993; one of only **twelve original castles**), **Matsumoto**,
  **Nijō**; the temple-cities **Kyoto / Nara / Kamakura**.
  [UNESCO #661: Himeji-jō](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/661/)

## G. Edged steel + the blade made for him (Jakobus's nerd-out + acquisition) — grounded metallurgy & law

> Jakobus = the knife-man whose tribe's love-language is the blade ([[jakobus-knives-given-grok]]). Two
> beats here: (1) the **forge as a real wonder** + his steel nerd-out (the keeper is a *local master* —
> keel-safe); (2) the **blade made for his hands** (the favour-economy ascetic, for once, *buys*). Keep
> it Crichton-credible, never katana-mysticism.

- **Tamahagane & the tatara.** Iron-sand (*satetsu*) + charcoal smelted in a single-use clay **tatara**
  furnace; a run burns ~**10–13 t iron-sand + 12–13 t charcoal over 36–72 hrs**, directed by the
  **murage** master, yielding a 2–3 t bloom (*kera*) of which **~25%** is sword-grade. **Only ONE
  traditional tatara still operates: the Nittōhō tatara, Shimane**, run under the **NBTHK** (Society for
  the Preservation of Japanese Art Swords), fired **2–3×/year** for ~300 licensed smiths. *Why it
  matters metallurgically:* the low, slow process keeps contaminants (S, P) low and lets the smith
  **sort the bloom by carbon** (~0.6–1.5% C within one kera) — high-C for the edge, low-C for the spine.
  [Tokyo Nihonto: tamahagane](https://tokyo-nihonto.com/blogs/nihonto-blog/what-is-tamahagane) ·
  [Wikipedia: Tatara (furnace)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatara_(furnace))
- **The real science (the "let the data speak" geek-out).** **Folding** hammer-welds the steel to
  **expel slag, even out carbon, homogenise** — ~**8–16 folds** (each doubles layers: 10 folds = 1,024
  layers, 16 ≈ 65,000). **The hamon is real metallurgy:** clay coating (thin at the edge, thick at the
  spine) controls cooling in the quench → hard **martensite edge** (~58–65 HRC) + tough
  **pearlite/ferrite spine** (~40 HRC). **The curvature (*sori*) forms DURING the quench** (martensite
  is less dense; the edge expands). The hamon is a **crystallographic boundary, not etched decoration**
  — acid-etched fakes are how a literate eye spots a non-traditional blade.
  [Wikipedia: Hamon (swordsmithing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamon_(swordsmithing)) ·
  [Island Blacksmith: yaki-ire clay tempering](https://islandblacksmith.ca/process/yaki-ire-clay-tempering/)
- **The living craft.** Smiths are **licensed by the Agency for Cultural Affairs** after a **~5-year
  apprenticeship** + annual exam, and are **capped at ~2 longswords/month** (quality over quantity). A
  finished sword passes smith → **togishi (polisher**, a separate ~10-year craft) → fittings (*habaki /
  saya / tsuka*). Top honorifics: **mukansa**, **Ningen Kokuhō (Living National Treasure)**.
  [SoraNews24: apprenticeship](https://soranews24.com/2017/08/15/japan-is-running-out-of-swordsmiths-and-a-strict-apprenticeship-requirement-is-a-big-reason-why/) ·
  [Tozando: modern swordsmiths](https://japanesesword.net/blogs/news/modern-japanese-swordsmiths-a-living-tradition)
- **THE LAW (drives plot plausibility).** Governing statute: the **Firearm and Sword Possession Control
  Law** (*Jūtō-hō*, 1958). **Every genuine sword must carry a registration certificate (*torokusho*)**
  from the **Prefectural Board of Education**, kept *with the blade*; ownership change notified within
  **20 days**. A foreigner **can own** a registered sword **but it must stay in Japan** unless exported
  via the Agency for Cultural Affairs (one-month permit, photos + license); **National Treasures / some
  Important Cultural Properties cannot leave at all.**
  [JSSUS: Japanese sword laws](http://www.jssus.org/nkp/japanese_sword_laws.html) ·
  [Wikipedia: Firearm and Sword Possession Control Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearm_and_Sword_Possession_Control_Law)
- **THE CARRY CONSTRAINT (a gift for the knife-man).** Carrying a blade **over 6 cm** in public without
  a "justifiable reason" violates the Jūtō-hō; **over 8 cm** is effectively banned; even a sub-6 cm
  pocket knife needs a real reason (job/fishing/transporting a purchase) and must be **boxed/wrapped,
  not pocket-carried**. **Real and enforced — Jakobus genuinely cannot habitually pocket a knife in
  Japan.** (Story gold: the man defined by the blade on his belt, disarmed by the law of the most
  blade-revering culture on earth.)
  [Tokyo Weekender: knife law](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/cutting-chase-knife-laws-work-japan/) ·
  [OIST: carrying knives](https://www.oist.jp/resource-center/carrying-knives)
- **What he can REALISTICALLY acquire (the honest "blade made for him").** A licensed *sword* is
  impractical (cost, the cap, the export choke). The true, legal, beautiful version is a **hand-forged
  blade from the kitchen/tool lineage** — from a smith **whose family once made swords**:
  - **A custom kitchen knife from Sakai (Osaka)** — Sakai makes ~98% of Japan's pro chef's knives, still
    by hand across separate specialists (forge / sharpen / handle); a visitor can finish & personalise
    one in a session (~¥8,500) or commission a named smith (more, weeks of lead time).
  - **A higonokami** — a friction-folding pocket knife **born 1896 in Miki, Hyōgo**, *precisely because*
    the **Haitōrei** pushed smiths into pocket tools; the name is trademarked to **only Nagao Kanekoma**
    (now 5th-gen). Cheap, legal, deeply authentic.
  - **Knife/tool capitals:** **Sakai (Osaka)** + **Seki (Gifu)** for knives, **Miki (Hyōgo)** for tools.
  [LeeKnives: knife regions](https://leeknives.com/knife-making-regions-of-japan/) ·
  [Country Knives: Higonokami history](https://www.countryknives.com/brand-history/nagao-higonokami/)
- **The Haitōrei (1876) — the thematic rhyme.** Banning the wearing of swords collapsed the sword
  industry; smiths **redirected their metallurgy into kitchen knives, farm tools, scissors, cutlery**
  (Seki became one of the world's three great cutlery centres with Solingen & Sheffield). **The
  "unheard" hands kept the steel alive after the samurai romance ended** — the *shokunin* outlasting the
  *bushi*. [Wikipedia: Haitō Edict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hait%C5%8D_Edict) ·
  [KIREAJI: history of Japanese knives](https://kireaji.ca/pages/history-of-japanese-knife)

**Steel — myths to AVOID (debunk, keep the wonder):** "folded a thousand times" (no — ~8–16 folds,
thousands of *layers*; over-folding *burns out* carbon); "magical/spiritual super-steel" (tamahagane's
virtue is *clean, sortable carbon*, not magic — modern monosteel can equal it on hardness); "a katana
cuts through anything" (a hard edge is *brittle*; the soft spine exists so it won't shatter); "the hamon
is decoration"; "the curve is hammered in" (much *sori* forms in the quench).

## H. Tebori / the horishi — Jakobus's tattoo (authentic, and the firewalls)

> The big personal beat: an **old horishi** gives Jakobus ink — **tea first, the master chooses the
> design to suit the man, hand-poked in one sitting.** Ties to [[jakobus-tattoo-continuity]] (he
> acquires ink progressively, each from an artisan in *their own hand*) — this is his **5th tattoo**.
> The submission is the **humility lever** (the man who controls what people see, surrendering it).

- **What it is.** **Irezumi** (入れ墨) = Japanese tattooing; **horimono** (彫り物) = the respectful term
  for a full traditional piece; **tebori** (手彫り, "hand carving") = the method — ink hand-poked with a
  rod (*nomi*) tipped with a needle cluster (*hari*), no machine. Roots in **ukiyo-e** (Kuniyoshi's
  *Suikoden* warriors); the **hori-** prefix is the same *horu* ("to carve") as woodblock carvers — the
  trades overlapped. [Wikipedia: Irezumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irezumi) ·
  [Authentink: tebori](https://www.authentink.com/japanese-horimono/tebori-tattoo-traditional-japanese-hand-poked-ink/)
- **The master & who chooses (verify-answer — it's exactly the brief).** A master is a **horishi**
  (Horiyoshi, Horisumi…); the living touchstone is **Horiyoshi III** of Yokohama. **The client names
  the central motif (*shudai*); the master owns the composition and lays it on the body** — Wikipedia:
  "the artist [has] a greater level of control over the finished design." Horiyoshi III frames it as
  **mutual trust** ("the client offers his body as a canvas"; "one is not free to do whatever he
  wants") and insists he is a **craftsman, not an artist**. **So deferring to the horishi's judgment is
  real and expected** — especially background + how the motif fits the body. **The "he picks whatever
  suits you" beat is authentic.** [Wikipedia: Horiyoshi III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horiyoshi_III) ·
  [Zoom Japan: Horiyoshi III](https://www.zoomjapan.info/2016/11/16/no46-encounter-a-lifetime-to-achieve-excellence/)
- **The process reality (scene accuracy).** A **full bodysuit takes YEARS** (weekly visits, "up to five
  years," US$30k+) — **so a one-sitting piece must be a SMALL single motif, not a suit.** Outline
  (*sujibori*) is often one sitting; shading/colour follow over visits. Sessions ~3–5 hrs. Tebori pain
  is reported *different, often gentler* than machine (discrete pokes, **less bleeding, faster
  healing**). **Tea/relationship-first is real etiquette;** serious clients commit to **one** master.
  [Authentink: tebori](https://www.authentink.com/japanese-horimono/tebori-tattoo-traditional-japanese-hand-poked-ink/) ·
  [tatuagemblog: tebori guide](https://www.tatuagemblog.com/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-tebori-tattoos-traditional-japanese-hand-poked-art)
- **The yakuza stigma + onsen ban (a usable constraint for a tattooed Jakobus).** Tattoos are
  stigmatised via the **yakuza** link (reinforced by **Edo-period penal tattooing**). The
  **onsen/sentō/gym/pool ban is widespread and current** — tattooed people routinely refused entry
  (historically a quiet way to bar yakuza). A 2015 Japan Tourism Agency survey: ~30% of inns permitted
  tattoos. **Jakobus — covered in ink — *will* be barred from baths.** Lean into it.
  [JNTO: onsen & tattoos](https://www.japan.travel/en/uk/inspiration/onsen-hot-springs-on-tattoos-and-etiquette/) ·
  [Japan Today: tattoos still a problem](https://japantoday.com/category/spotlight/70-why-tattoos-are-still-a-problem-in-japan)
- **History of suppression.** Decorative tattooing **banned 1872 (early Meiji)** to clean Japan's image
  for the West — *even as* foreign royals (the future **George V**, Tsar Nicholas II) got tattooed *in*
  Japan; **legalised 1948** under the US occupation. **2020 Supreme Court (Taiki Masuda case):**
  tattooing ruled **not a medical procedure** — no medical licence required.
  [Nippon.com: tattoo history](https://www.nippon.com/en/views/b06701/) ·
  [Japan Times: 2020 tattoo ruling](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/opinion/2020/10/02/commentary/japan-commentary/court-win-tattoo-artists-medical-procedure/)
- **The foreigner (the firewall question — answered).** **Yes — foreigners are a real, growing client
  base** for many horishi. It is **contested** (some artists hold non-initiates should avoid certain
  imagery; appropriation critique is live). **The respectful frame is exactly Jakobus's posture:** revere
  the craft, learn the meaning, **accept the master's authority over design** (use the word *horimono*,
  don't haggle, don't dictate, don't call him an "artist" to his face). **Loaded imagery to avoid
  glibly:** **Fudō Myō-ō** and other religious figures carry real Buddhist weight; **kamon** (family
  crests) and syndicate-coded full-suit programs are insider-coded. **Safest/truest:** a **small,
  body-fitted animal/nature motif the master *chooses* to suit the man.**
  [daiyokai: tebori technique](https://www.daiyokai.com/en/post/tebori-japanese-hand-tattoo-technique)

**Tebori — for the scene (authentic vs. avoid):** AUTHENTIC = tea & talk first; the master sizing up
*the man* then choosing/laying out the design; near-silent patient hand-work; a **single small motif in
one long sitting** (outline first); mutual trust as the currency; the master speaking of *kokoro*
(heart) and craft. AVOID = a bodysuit in one sitting; the client dictating; a foreigner casually handed
a yakuza-coded back-piece; glib use of sacred Buddhist iconography. **Get a sensitivity reader with real
knowledge of the tattoo world** (and the Buddhist-iconography weight of any named motif).

**The locked motif — the great wave (well-grounded, firewall-safe).** The horishi chooses **a wave at
the instant of its full weight, held the moment before it falls** — power held soft (SEED_STORY §Jakobus
beat 2). Why this motif is the right call:
- **It's authentic irezumi.** The wave (*nami*) is core traditional vocabulary — the background-and-motif
  grammar of horimono is *built* on waves, wind-bars, clouds; a wave-as-subject is unremarkable, not
  exotic. (The Hokusai *Great Wave* lineage is the recognisable register, rendered by ear not as a
  reproduction.)
- **It dodges every firewalled image** — it is **not** Fudō Myō-ō or any religious figure (no Buddhist
  weight to mishandle), **not** a *kamon* crest, **not** a syndicate-coded program; it is exactly the
  "small, body-fitted nature motif the master chooses to suit the man" the safe-path line calls for.
- **It carries the meaning the scene needs** — a heavy, dangerous thing held gentle — without a single
  symbol or caption, so it reads as pure seascape to every reader. *(This is also, secretly, the **G-nod**
  — danger held soft; see SEED_STORY §Jakobus beat 2, the ⊕ block. The nod is in the MEANING only; NO
  symbols/equation/Ø ever appear. PRIVATE register; never glossed on the page.)*
- **Still run the named motif past the tattoo-world sensitivity reader** (above) — confirm a wave piece
  on a foreign client carries no reading we haven't anticipated.

> **Cross-firewall (important):** Jakobus's ink in this book comes from a **Japanese horishi**, NOT an
> **Ainu** hand — Ainu tattooing was women's sacred, near-erased practice (§B); a non-Ainu man receiving
> it would be appropriation. The horishi tradition has a real, contested-but-extant foreign-client
> practice; the Ainu one does not. Keep these two completely separate.

## I. Hospitality & etiquette a respectful crew must get right (texture, where trust is won/lost)

**Omotenashi** (selfless hospitality) animates the **ryokan** + **onsen**. **Shoes off** at the
*genkan*. **Onsen protocol:** wash thoroughly *before* entering; **fully naked** (no swimwear); towel &
hair **never touch the water**; voices low; no phones. **Tea ceremony** (*chanoyu / sadō*) — render with

give/receive **with both hands + a slight bow**. **Noodles** — slurping is appreciative. **Chopstick
taboos:** never stand upright in rice or pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (both = funeral rites).

## J. Contested / handle with care — gathered (run ALL past the sensitivity read)

1. **Burakumin = the highest-risk strand.** Slurs (*eta/hinin/kawata/yotsu*) never in narration;
   **never name/map a real community**; **never make a buraku "reveal" a thrill**; the sword-craft link
   is **unsupported** (use the documented armour-leather/taiko link instead); don't lean on revisionist
   (Ramseyer) scholarship; don't flatten to "Japan's untouchables." **Japanese sensitivity reader with
   veto, ideally buraku-literate.**
2. **Ainu sacred + linguistic care.** No *inu* slur in narration; iyomante = reverent, no fictional live
   sacrifice; **no fresh traditional Ainu facial tattoos on a character**; museum ≠ living people;
   speaker numbers + population are *contested* (don't state flat figures). **Ainu cultural consultant
   with veto.**
3. **The exoticising trap (the symmetric failure).** Inverting the "mystical samurai postcard" must NOT
   become a *different* single story — "mystical-ancient-Japan" awe is the same dehumanising coin as the
   cliché. Keep Japanese characters **modern, ordinary, funny, flawed, self-determining** — not only
   awe-inspiring. (Same lesson as Ethiopia's "glorious-ancient-Africa" trap.)
4. **Bushido = present it as a real-but-CONSTRUCTED, contested, partly 20th-c. tradition** (Nitobe's
   English-for-Westerners synthesis; WWII weaponisation). **Never let the narrative voice adopt the myth
   it exposes** (no "the Japanese are uniquely…", no "we Japanese," no timeless-mystical-code framing).
5. **Sacred-site rules.** Kūkai's eternal meditation = devout belief (don't debunk). **Ōmine/Mt. Sanjō
   female exclusion** is live & contested. **Yudono-san secrecy** + Ise inner-sanctuary
   non-photography: verify current rules before depicting.
6. **The floating rock is tuff, not granite; its "floating" is engineered; its origin is genuinely
   unsolved.** Use the real mystery (no tool marks, unknown purpose, left unfinished) — **avoid the
   Ancient-Aliens framing entirely.** Masuda weight is disputed (give a range).
7. **The law (drives plot).** Sword registration/export and the **6 cm/8 cm carry thresholds** come from
   reputable *summaries*, not the primary statute (amended 1993/1995/2008/2018) — **confirm with a
   Japanese lawyer / Japan Customs** before treating any number as exact.
8. **Zainichi Koreans + Okinawans/Ryukyuans** are further unheard layers — keep as respectful pointers,
   not caricature, unless a sensitivity-read deep-dive backs fuller treatment.
9. **Jakobus's tattoo = Japanese horishi, NOT Ainu** (appropriation firewall, §H).
10. **Source hygiene:** Wikipedia / studio blogs / Ancient-Origins used only as *pointers* or for a
    single flagged fact; load-bearing claims should be re-verified against the academic / museum /
    journalism / human-rights-org sources cited above before print. Several authoritative pages (Columbia
    bushido, Burgess APJ, LoC Ainu-law) **403'd on fetch** but are real and corroborated here — re-pull
    before quoting verbatim.
