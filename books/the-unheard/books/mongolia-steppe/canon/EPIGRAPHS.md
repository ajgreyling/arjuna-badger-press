# EPIGRAPHS — *The Felt and the Sky* (Mongolia / the steppe herders)

> Book-level epigraph(s) placed in the front matter, and the per-chapter epigraph policy. **Public
> cultural material only** (proverbs, attributed sayings) — **never a sacred song, rite, or closed
> knowledge** (MYTHOS Rule 1). All non-English text is **provisional pending native-speaker proof** in
> the sensitivity read (mark `⚠ pending native proof` in canon; strip the marker only after a Mongolian
> reader confirms script + transliteration). **Do not "correct" the transliterations to a different
> romanisation system without that read** — Mongolian Cyrillic ↔ Latin has several conventions.

---

## Book-level epigraph (front matter)

**Primary (a Mongolian pastoral proverb — public, well-attested in spirit; confirm exact wording/script in
the read):**

> *"A man without a horse is like a bird without wings."*
> — Mongolian proverb  ⚠ *pending native proof (Cyrillic + transliteration)*

*Why:* it carries the steppe's whole keel in one line — the horse as the backbone of the life (the
**five snouts**; PLACE_RESEARCH §1, §5) — and it quietly seats the book's Jakobus seam (the one man on
this ground who **won't trust the horse**, the wheel-man among horse-people). It reads as honour, not
exotica.

**Secondary (optional — the felt/sky title-pair, the fear-of-loss undertow):** a short attributed line on
the **open land / the moving home / the sky** (sourced in the read), OR a single grounded line of the
book's own (an in-world herder's saying) clearly marked as fictional. *Decide in drafting; keep ONE if the
primary already carries the weight — one true epigraph outweighs three.*

> **Candidate (in-world, fictional — clearly the book's own, no attribution risk):**
> *"The grass remembers the road. The fence does not."*
> — *(in-world; the district's saying)*

*Why:* states the theme (the land that looks empty is precisely known; the fence is the lie) in an image,
never a lecture — and is **invented**, so it can't misappropriate a real saying.

---

## Per-chapter epigraph policy

**Default: NONE.** The series voice is propulsive and earns its weight in the prose, not in chapter
ornaments (and chapter epigraphs risk either travelogue or appropriation). **If used at all**, restrict to:
- **Fictional, in-world** sayings of the district (clearly the book's own), OR
- **Public, attributed** Mongolian proverbs **confirmed in the sensitivity read** —
and **never** a sacred song, an ovoo prayer, a shamanic line, or any closed/sacred knowledge (MYTHOS Rule
1). Keep them rare and load-bearing; better none than a decorative one.

---

## Hard rules (binding)

1. **Public cultural material only** — proverbs/attributed sayings or clearly-fictional in-world lines.
   **No sacred song / rite / closed knowledge** as an epigraph, ever (MYTHOS Rule 1).
2. **Native-speaker proof gate** — any Mongolian-language epigraph carries `⚠ pending native proof` in
   canon until a Mongolian reader confirms script + transliteration; the render strips the marker only
   after. **Do not auto-"correct" romanisation** without that read.
3. **No conflation** — a Kazakh saying is **Kazakh** (Bayan-Ölgii), labelled as such; never presented as
   "Mongolian" (MYTHOS Rule 2, §4.2).
4. **One true epigraph outweighs three** — restraint over ornament.
