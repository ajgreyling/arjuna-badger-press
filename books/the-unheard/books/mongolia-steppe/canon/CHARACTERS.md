## THE LOCAL LEAD (primary POV)

### ◆ TUYAA (provisional given name) — the daughter who left
**The layer she reads: BOTH.** The city's survey-language *and* (re-learned) her father's grammar of the
land. The lens of the book.

- **Who she is.** Mid-late 20s/early 30s; born to a herding family on the steppe→Gobi edge; **left after
  the dzud** that killed most of the family herd in a week (the loss that broke the household's future).
  Took the road the young take now — **Ulaanbaatar**, a **veterinary / livestock-science degree**, the
  ger-district years, then a consultancy doing **"stakeholder engagement"** for a development concern. She
  is **nobody's timeless native** — she is the one who *got out*, which is exactly why she's the right lead.
- **Why she's sent back (the engine).** The mine's people want the **acceptable, Mongolian,
  daughter-of-herders face** for the survey that will fence her home district. She takes it telling herself
  it's the humane version — *better me than a stranger* — not yet seeing she's the soft edge of the erasure.
- **Her wound / want.** **Shame and longing** (THEMES "Tuyaa's theme"): she fled the loss and now her
  city-fluency is the weapon aimed at her father. Her want is *not* the city's approval; it's the thing she
  was taught to leave behind — **belonging again to a country and a father.**
- **Her gift (the real relay tool).** She can read **both ledgers** — the survey's spreadsheet *and* the
  land's. She's the only one who can translate the herders' undefendable use-right knowledge into the
  language the law and the press understand — *if* she chooses to. (Pairs with the crew: Naila reads the
  lie in the documents; Tuyaa knows which truth the documents are hiding.)
- **Her arc.** The daughter who left → the daughter who chooses her father's country → the one who hands
  the **telling** back (uses both her tongues *for* the herders, not the mine). She owns the book's heart;
  she is **not** rescued by the crew — they help her *see*, and then get out of her way.
- **Voice.** Dry, modern, code-switching (city Mongolian + the herder register she slips back into);
  guarded; the cynicism of someone who learned the consultancy game and hates that she's good at it; warming
  as the steppe re-claims her.

## THE HERDER WORLD (the experts — they lead, decide, own the win)

### ◆ BATU (provisional) — Tuyaa's father; the herder who won't leave
**The layer he reads: THE LAND.** Grass, water, wind, sky, the migration, the ovoo — the thousand-year
grammar.
- The keeper of the wonder: still moves the **five snouts** by season; reads the sky no instrument reads as
  well; circles the **ovoo** and **asks** the land before taking from it. Watching his world close — the
  fences, the drying **spring**, the dzud, the grandchildren gone to the city.
- **Not a noble-savage and not a victim** (§3): modern, stubborn, funny, proud, sometimes wrong, fiercely
  competent; uses a phone and a motorbike *and* the deep knowledge; grieving without saying so. His refusal
  to leave is **expertise and dignity, not backwardness.**
- **With Tuyaa:** the ache of the book — the father whose daughter left, who won't ask her to stay, who
  can't follow her into the city. Their reconciliation is *shown*, never speechified.
- **He leads the win** — the defence of the spring/route is **his** call and the district's, not the
  outsiders'.

### ◆ The wrestling champion (provisional name TBD) — the district's young spine
**The layer he reads: HIS OWN GROUND.** The bökh grammar; the land he's never left.
- A **young herder, the regional bökh champion** (a real, earned title — Falcon/Hawk/Elephant rank, won at
  Naadam; PLACE_RESEARCH §5). **Half Jakobus's fuss and twice his craft** on his own ground.
- **Renders with full dignity — an expert of his place, never a comic foil or a brute** (SSOT drafting
  guard; MYTHOS Rule 3). He is the one who **hands Jakobus the lesson** at the contest — and, the loss
  owned, becomes part of the district's spine in the back half: the young man who **stays**, who fights for
  the pasture with his body and his standing, the future the dzud and the city are trying to take. *(Per
  the author's call, the bout is a humbling, not the start of a recurring buddy-pairing — he out-classes
  Jakobus, mutual respect, then he's the local's strength, not Jakobus's sidekick.)*
- His arc rhymes with Tuyaa's inverse: **the one who didn't leave**, and what that costs and what it holds.

### ◆ The grandmother / long-song keeper (provisional) — the carrier of the fire
**The layer she reads: MEMORY & SONG.** The **urtiin duu** that carries the distance and the dead.
- An elder who holds the **long-song** — the book's "fire" (§7b). The win includes **the song handed on**
  (a grandchild learning it) — the culture carried, not mourned.
- **The sacred is hers to withhold** (MYTHOS Rule 1): she may sing a *fictional* long-song; the book never
  scripts a real sacred song, and the shaman's/ovoo's rite is shown at the threshold and looked away from.
- Jakobus joins **her** fire (the dance grace-note) — *with* her circle, on their ground.

### ◆ Supporting herders (provisional — keep distinct, name specifically)
The district as a living community, not a backdrop: a neighbour family already broken by the last dzud and
half-gone to the city (the loss, shown); a young mother weighing whether to take the children to UB for
school; an old man at the ovoo. **Name them; give them competence and complexity; never homogenise**
(§4.2).

## THE FLATTENING FORCE (the antagonist — the erasure, human-faced)

### ◆ The mine's fixer (provisional name TBD) — the "empty frontier" salesman
**The layer he reads: THE FRAME.** How to make the fence palatable.
- The **human face** of a composited Gobi **copper-and-water** concern (fictional name; real mechanism —
  WORLD/MYTHOS). Likely **Mongolian himself** — diaspora-polished or UB-elite — which is the point: the
  erasure isn't a foreign villain, it's the **"development / progress / underused frontier"** logic that
  recruits its own. Smooth, reasonable, genuinely believes the steppe is a postcard holding back a poor
  country.
- **Not a moustache-twirler.** His argument has teeth — jobs, GDP, a nation that needs the copper; the
  herders *are* poor; the law *is* on his side. The book beats him **on dignity and truth**, not by making
  him stupid. He's the one who hired Tuyaa as the soft edge — and her turning is his real defeat.
- **The law is his weapon** (use-right not title; "strategic site" exclusion) — the book's hardest,
  realest obstacle.

### ◆ (Optional) the absent corporate principal
The faceless foreign-capital majority owner (the real Oyu Tolgoi pattern: foreign major + state minority) —
kept off-page or glimpsed; the fixer is the human antagonist, the capital is the weather behind him.

## Casting law (binding — the keel)

1. **The local out-reads the visitor on the local's ground, every time** (§3, §5b.4). Batu and the champion
   are *more* capable than the crew and Jakobus in their domain; the visitors learn *from* them.
2. **No deficit framing, no noble-savage, no rescue** (MYTHOS Rule 3/5). The herders lead, decide, own the
   win. Hardship is a thing done *to* them by a system (mine + law + climate), never their defining trait.
3. **Distinguish peoples** (§4.2). Khalkha Mongol steppe-herders here; if Kazakh eagle-hunters appear,
   they're a **named, distinct people** (Bayan-Ölgii / Turkic / Muslim) — never blurred in.
4. **The camera stays with the unheard.** When a draft centres the crew's or Jakobus's feelings over the
   herders' story, it's wrong — pull it back (§11).
5. **Every name provisional** until the sensitivity read (§4.7).
