# WORLD — *The Felt and the Sky* (Mongolia / the steppe herders)

> Binding geography and the in-world "physics" of the wonder. **Real places and facts used honestly**
> (grounded in [`PLACE_RESEARCH.md`](PLACE_RESEARCH.md); MYTHOS_RULES Rule 6) — but the *specific*
> district, mine, spring, and migration route are deliberately **composited and unmappable** (guardrail
> §4.2). The land is the book's largest character.

## The shape of this world

Present-day Mongolia — **no fantastical layer.** The "wonder" is the *true* one: a **living nomadic
pastoral civilisation** that has read the open steppe for a thousand-plus years with a precision no
outsider instrument matches — grass, water, wind, sky, weather, the felt home that *moves* — most of it
hiding in plain sight as "emptiness." The extraordinary is not buried by aliens or lost to a dead
conqueror; it is **carried by the living herders**, and the modern world keeps arriving late to what they
already know. The threat is ordinary and real: **extraction** (copper, coal, and above all **water**) and
the **map-and-law erasure** of a people who hold their land by use and not by title — set against the
deep, indifferent time of the grass sea and the killing winters.

## The land (composited from the real steppe / Gobi edge)

- **The district** (fictional, unnamed-or-invented): an **aimag/sum on the steppe→Gobi transition**, where
  open grassland gives to semi-desert — the country of **migration**, of long horizons, of the **five
  snouts**. Far enough south that **water is scarce and decisive** (the mine's lever); far enough north
  that the **dzud** is the recurring killer. *(Composite, not mappable — but true to the real geography of
  the South-Gobi/steppe-edge where herding and mining collide.)*
- **The capital, Ulaanbaatar** — the gravity well. Built for 500,000, now ~**1.7 million (≈half the
  country)**; ~**60% in the unplanned ger districts** — migrant families still in their felt homes but
  crammed in without water, sewage, or heat, their stoves making UB one of the **world's most polluted
  capitals** (winter PM2.5 >20× WHO). **The felt home that breathes on the steppe becomes a smoke trap in
  the city** — the same dwelling, the life gone out of it. (Tuyaa's two worlds; where the lost go.)
- **The ovoo** — the **sacred cairn** at the pass / the high place / by the spring (MYTHOS Rule 1; withhold
  its sacred content). The land is **marked with the sacred**, not blank.

## The in-world "physics" of the wonder (all checkable — earn the object first)

Each is a **real, witnessable thing** the book may show; the wonder is *competence*, never mysticism:

- **The ger (felt home).** Raised in **under ~3 hours**, struck into flat-packs in **about an hour**;
  lattice walls + roof poles + the wooden **crown (toono)**; **felt from the herders' own sheep**; **faces
  south**; engineered for a furnace-to-freezer climate. *(The cold-open image, SEED: a family striking the
  ger at dawn to follow the grass — a thousand-year choreography — while a lease fence cuts the route.)*
- **The migration (otor & seasonal move).** Moves **timed to grass, water, wind** — **2–4 times a year**
  (spring grass → summer high pasture → autumn fattening → sheltered winter camp); an **otor** is a short
  emergency move. The next site is **read** off the land. **This is the wonder the "empty frontier" survey
  cannot see** — the most sophisticated GPS-free land-use the crew has ever stood inside.
- **The sky.** Reading weather, season, and danger off a sky no instrument reads as well — the herder's
  judgement of *when/where/how fast* to move home and animals. (Show it tested against, and beating, the
  consultancy's data.)
- **The dzud (the killing winter — the fear-of-loss engine).** The compound disaster (white/black/iron/cold
  — PLACE_RESEARCH §2). The arithmetic is the dread: a dry summer + a savage winter can take **half to all**
  of a family's herd **in a night**; ~20% of the *national* herd died in 2009–10; six major dzud in a
  decade now. **The dzud is the fast version of the loss; the city-drift is the slow version.** It is *why
  Tuyaa left.*
- **Water.** In the Gobi, **water is the whole game.** A single **year-round spring** can be the only water
  for a district — which is exactly what the mine takes (the real Bor Ovoo / Undai pattern). The wonder and
  the wound share a source.

## The threat (grounded; see PLOT + MYTHOS)

- **The mine** (composited — fictional name, real mechanism): a **copper-and-water Gobi project** whose
  real cost is **water and pasture** — diverting/draining the spring, fencing the commons (the real licenses
  grew **+692% to +3,132%** in two decades), dust that sickens the stock. Its human face frames the steppe
  as **"underused frontier."**
- **The map and the law (the lock the book turns).** Pastureland is **constitutionally state-owned**;
  herders hold **use rights, NOT title**; they are **not recognized as Indigenous** and **cannot refuse a
  mine** via free-prior-informed-consent; the state can designate a site "strategic" and exclude them.
  *(The 2024 herder-association / pasture-use-contract laws are the current backdrop — a use-right, still
  not ownership. The book's tension is intact.)* **The fence is the symptom; the law is the disease.**
- **The flattening story.** The crew's *own* commission — a "timeless Genghis-land / empty steppe"
  travelogue — is the **soft** version of the same erasure: the postcard that makes the fence palatable.

## Texture & props (for grounding, not travelogue — use sparingly, earned)

The **deel** (wrap-coat); **süütei tsai** (salted milk tea — the hospitality cup; butter optional); **airag**
(fermented mare's milk, ~2% — the hospitality drink Jakobus must decline, no alcohol); the **morin khuur**
(horse-head fiddle) at the fire; the **long-song (urtiin duu)** and **khöömei** (the latter western-Altai in
origin — attribute correctly); **bökh** wrestling with its **eagle-dance (devekh)** and **jodag/shuudag/gutal**
costume and its earned titles; the **leather airag-sack by the door** everyone stirs in passing; dried dung
for fuel; the felt, the lattice, the crown-wheel against the sky.

## The keel for this world (binding)

The steppe is **not empty, not timeless, not a relic, and not Genghis's leftover.** It is **alive, modern,
adapting, expert, and under threat** — and the book's whole job is to make the reader *see* that, and to put
the camera, and the win, in the **herders'** hands. (Series §3, §7a; MYTHOS Rule 5.)
