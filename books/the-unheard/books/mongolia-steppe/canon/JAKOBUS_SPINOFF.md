# Jakobus in *The Felt and the Sky* — the road, never the lead (build spec)

> **Per-book spec for how Jakobus lands in the Mongolia book.** He is **the road** (series §5a) — the
> field-protector who gathered the crew, keeps the party alive, and stands between the herders and the
> mine's muscle. Seen **only from outside** (Tuyaa's / a crew member's close third); **never his own POV**
> (SSOT-locked). **The local cast holds the camera and owns the win** (the keel). This is **the strand's
> humility-engine showcase book** — all three SSOT payoffs land here.
>
> ⭐ **Load the SSOT for anything about him:** `../../../history-before-time/canon/characters/
> JAKOBUS_SWART.md`. Do **not** re-derive him.

## 0. Relationship rule for THIS book (the series default holds)

**Brother / uncle with the crew** (male and female alike) — the series default. **No per-book romance
exception** (the Australia/Songlines one-romance rule was a *Songlines* thing; it does not generalise). With
**Tuyaa** he is **kin-respect only** — she is the local lead and her arc is hers; any romantic charge there
would trip the white-savior / "going native" guardrails and undercut her authority (MYTHOS Rule 3). *(If
drafting ever finds a genuine, earned thread, it's an author's call — but the default is **no romance
here**; the heat in this book is the steppe humbling the master, not the heart.)*

## 1. Why he's off home ground — the humility engine is the whole point

Jakobus is the **African desert-and-machine master** dropped onto the **steppe / Gobi edge** — a land that
**does not take his competence.** That dissonance is *why this is his showcase book*: the series' humility
engine, run at full. **POV stays with Tuyaa and the crew member** — they **watch** a legendary-to-those-
who'd-know man meet a country that makes a student of him, and watch the few small true things that still
work. **He never narrates it; it is always *shown*.**

## 2. The three payoffs — made concrete (LOCKED; distributed, never stacked)

> Each is a SHOWN beat, seen from outside, doing double work (humility + warmth + theme). **Never two in
> one scene; never spell out the parallel.** Placement map in PLOT.md.

### ◆ PAYOFF #1 — The wrestling humbling (the strand's sharpest beat)
**SSOT verbatim spine** (*"The wrestling humbling — the one time he forgot the rule"*):
- **How it starts (the flaw):** the herders have **already quietly checked him out** — watched him eat,
  drink what's offered, carry himself, take a joke — and **clocked him as a man who can handle himself**
  (which he *can*; that part's true). The **bökh** bout is offered in that spirit: respect, welcome, a
  little testing. And Jakobus — **off his own ground, a half-step too sure** (maybe a touch of road-
  cockiness) — **accepts it like he's the dangerous one in the ring.** He forgets his own creed (defer; the
  local out-reads you here; you are the **student** of this place). *That forgetting is the sin.*
- **The lesson (the loss):** he gets **comprehensively, almost gently destroyed** by the **regional
  champion** — *"half his fuss and twice his craft,"* a man who has done **this one thing, on this one
  ground, in this one style, his whole life.** No SF combative, no joint-lever, no time-dilation read saves
  him: **this is the champion's grammar, not his.** He **lands on his back in the dust in front of
  everyone,** the breath gone, the legend briefly *nothing.*
- **The grace (why it makes him bigger):** he **laughs** — at himself, flat on his back — gets up and
  **bows to the better man** (the *Boba Fett* lesson lived: you earn the armour, you don't assume it). The
  district **trusts and loves him MORE for the loss.** Locked line: ***"On my ground I'd have given him a
  game. This is his ground. He gave me a lesson."***
- **Drafting guards (SSOT, binding):** (1) the **champion is never a comic foil or a brute** — full
  dignity, real skill, an **expert of his own place** (render the **eagle-dance / devekh** ritual, the open
  contest on bare grass; CHARACTERS); (2) the loss is **clean and a little funny, never humiliating-cruel**
  — the comedy is the legend's human ceiling, not a man broken; (3) **he never makes excuses** (not the
  age, not the long road, not the rules) — the whole point is that he *takes* it.

### ◆ PAYOFF #2 — The tea that beats him (the hospitality cup)
**SSOT verbatim spine** (*"The two that beat him"*): the man who eats *everything* and **drinks anything
that isn't alcohol** is **broken by salted milk tea (süütei tsai)** — *"salt where his body screamed for
sweet, the butter turning on the back of the tongue."* He **tries, genuinely, in front of the people whose
food it is — and his body simply will not.** **The grace is in HOW he fails:** no curled lip, no insult to
the host — **he laughs at himself, names it as *his* limit and not the food's** — and the failing is exactly
what makes the hosts **trust him more.**
- **This book grounds it (PLACE_RESEARCH §5):** süütei tsai is the **standard welcome cup served on
  arrival at a ger** — so the failing is *pointed* (he's faltering on the **welcome**), and the grace
  (owning his limit) is what earns him the fire anyway. **The butter** (optional richness in the real
  recipe) is the detail that turns on his tongue — keep it accurate.
- **Plus the airag (gentle, distinct):** the **fermented mare's milk** (~2% alcohol) is the **hospitality
  drink by the door** — and Jakobus **does not drink alcohol** (the no-drink rule holds, never moralised).
  A small, real beat: he declines the airag with his usual grace (the seeing, not the sermon) — distinct
  from the tea that *beats* him. Don't conflate the two.
- **Placement:** Act I, the ger welcome — the **first crack** in the visiting master.

### ◆ PAYOFF #3 — The horse he won't trust (the machine creed)
**SSOT verbatim spine** (*"The machine creed — and the one thing he won't drive"*): Jakobus can **drive
anything with wheels** — and has a flat, unbragging **distrust of a thing that can't be braked and that
bites: a horse.** *"You control the machine because it obeys the physics you understand; you can stop it"* —
and a horse honours none of it (**no brakes, a mind of its own, teeth**). **Not fear** (he won't perform
it), **not a humbling** (he isn't bested) — he just **won't trust a thing he can't switch off,** and says so
deadpan.
- **The generous flip side (LOCKED — saves it from mere aversion; pure *Sawubona* + the giving doctrine):**
  he won't *ride* it for himself, but is **first to catch it, saddle it, and hand it to whoever's joy it
  is** — *"go on — ride"* (the children; a grace-note of the man who reads a delight that isn't his and
  gives it to them).
- **This book is where it lands hardest:** the **most horse-defined culture on earth** — a herder world
  that lives in the saddle, where his "anything with wheels" is worth nothing and the horse/cattle work is
  the **herders' grammar, not his.** The horsed herders find the wheel-man's distrust **quietly funny and
  like him MORE for owning it** (the same currency as the food and the wrestling).
- **Drafting guards:** **keep it distinct from the wrestling** (not a defeat, just a true seam in a capable
  man, played dry); **use once or twice, lightly**; never slapstick, never real danger (he's competent
  enough to sit a horse to fetch stock — he just resents that he had to); never let it tip into a gag at his
  expense. *(His own lived telling — the open gate, the cattle gone walkabout, "I'll drive anything you've
  got with wheels; I don't trust a thing without brakes, and this one bites" — is **his** story on the
  page, dry, once.)*

### ◆ THE FIRE — the dance grace-note (§7b; the plant this book pays off)
**SSOT spine** (*"He can dance" / "The dance — a thousand fires"*): Jakobus **joins** the people's
fire — the **long-song / celebration** — *with* them, in their circle, on their ground, **never performing
*at* them.** **ONE quiet, earned grace-note** (never re-staging the Egypt ch-13 aria) — by **accumulation,
not repetition**, it retro-proves the man who has "observed people around a thousand fires on every
continent — and actually joined them, and danced." **Joy as resistance** — the dance that empties the fear
out so people can rest, danced one more night against everything taking the steppe away.
- **Placement:** Act II late / the celebration; the grandmother's long-song circle (CHARACTERS).
- **Guard:** the **sacred is withheld** (MYTHOS Rule 1) — a *fictional* long-song, a public celebration;
  never a scripted sacred song or rite.

## 3. The three faces (+ the seam) — keep them straight

The book's humility spine: the master **bested** by the steppe (**wrestling**, **tea**) and **welcomed** by
it (**the fire / dance**) — three faces of the same humility (SSOT) — with the **horse** as an **adjacent
seam** (NOT a fourth face: he isn't bested, he just won't trust it). **Distinct beats, distributed across
the book, never stacked.** Together they make him the perfect figure for this strand: **the man who has
earned his way into a thousand circles, and is still, everywhere he goes, a student of the ground he stands
on.**

## 4. Where he still helps (transferable — the road's real value)

He **reads people** better than anyone (clocks the fixer's "empty frontier" frame as *wrong* before he
reasons it; sorts the sincere from the sold); **keeps the party alive** and runs the vehicles (the **wheels
he trusts** — a night drive to get the herders' testimony / the evidence where it can't be buried is a
*Jakobus* beat in the climax); his survival **discipline** (calm under load, patience, the field kit) ports
cleanly; and **Sawubona / "I see you"** is revealed, again, as the one skill that was always the whole
skill — **deference as his deepest competence, not weakness.** But: **he does not break the case or own the
win.** Tuyaa chooses; Batu and the district lead; the champion and the grandmother carry the future. **He is
the spine that carries them, not the hero who saves them.**

## 5. The kit that travels (the small true things — SSOT, used sparingly)

The **cayenne tin** (the wimpy-eggs quirk; the field coagulant later); the **always-clean hanky**; the
Beast/Cruiser logic where the terrain allows it — the few things that **cross every border**, a quiet
counterpoint to everything that doesn't take here. *(Mongolia's cold is real — and **he owns cold** (SSOT
heat/cold inversion): unlike the Australia furnace that beats him, the steppe winter is *his* element. Let
that be one quiet place his mastery **does** port — the banked-fire body-heat, the calm in the freeze —
even as the horse, the wrestling, and the tea all beat him. A good, true contrast.)*

## 6. Guardrails (HARD — gate-enforceable; see also the SSOT white-saviour firewall)

1. **Locals lead, decide, own the win.** Jakobus witnesses and guards; he never rescues the culture.
2. **He's useless on their ground, and it's shown** — the wrestling, the tea, the horse; his instincts fail
   and he's corrected, dependent, wrong, often. (The cold is the one true exception — §5.)
3. **No deficit/noble-savage framing; the herders are *more* capable than him in their domain.**
4. **He's not here to save anyone.** When a beat *could* be his win or the locals', it is **theirs** (§11).
5. **POV stays Tuyaa-primary / crew-secondary; never Jakobus.** Seen from outside, always.
6. **The sacred is withheld** (MYTHOS Rule 1); the **Khalkha ≠ Kazakh** distinction holds (Rule 2).
7. **Sensitivity read is a FLOOR** (Rule 7) — all names/depictions provisional.
