## The one-line
The crew comes to the steppe for the easy story — *the land of Genghis, empty and timeless* — and finds
instead a **living nomadic civilisation reading the sky for a thousand years**, now being torn between a
mining boom that fences the open land and winters that kill whole herds in a night — and learns, the hard
way (a humbling at the wrestling; a cup of tea that beats a man who eats everything), that **the empty
steppe is the most precisely-known and most-owned land they've ever walked.**

## Premise
The commission is the **"empty land / dead-conqueror"** flattening — a heritage-of-Genghis travelogue, or
worse, a "frontier opportunity" piece for the mining interest. The crew arrives expecting emptiness and a
museum-cliché. The local lead — **provisional: a young herder torn between the ger and the city, or a
herder's daughter who left for Ulaanbaatar and came back** — is nobody's timeless native. What the crew
is let into: the **ger** life (the felt home that *moves*), the migrations timed to grass and water, the
herd as wealth-and-kin, the **long-song** that carries the distance, the reverence for the **sky** and
the land that **cannot be fenced because it is not meant to be owned that way** — which is exactly the lock
the mining leases break. The **fear of loss** is current and brutal: a single *dzud* can erase a family's
herd overnight; the open range is being privatised and dug up; the young are draining to the city; *a
mobile world going still inside a generation.*

The "empty frontier" piece becomes — if the crew defers — a record of a people whose "emptiness" is the
most sophisticated land-use on the continent, and who are losing it. The win: **a migration route kept
open / the herd carried through one more winter** / the telling handed back.

## Jakobus here (the road, never the lead) — ★ the humility-engine book
This is the strand's **humility-engine showcase**, because the steppe is genuinely *not his ground* and
the canon pays it off three ways (SSOT):
- **The wrestling humbling.** The herders **check him out** — watch him eat, carry himself, take a joke —
  and clock him as *a man who can handle himself* (true). Offered a friendly bout of **Mongolian *böx*
  wrestling**, a half-step too cocky and off his own ground, he **accepts like he's the dangerous one** —
  and a wiry regional champion **hands him his ass on a platter.** He laughs, flat on his back, bows to
  the better man, makes **no excuses** — and they trust him *more.* (Render the champion with full
  dignity; the comedy is the legend's human ceiling, never humiliating-cruel.)
- **The tea that beats him.** The man who eats *everything* meets **salted butter tea** (*süütei tsai* /
  the mare-/yak-butter family) and his body simply will not — and he owns it, laughing, *his* limit not
  their fault.
- **The fire.** The **long-song** and the dance/celebration — he knows how to join, in their circle, on
  their ground; the Egypt dance-plant pays off here as one quiet beat.

**Brother/uncle to the crew; defers to the herders; stands guard.** He is the perfect figure for this
book precisely *because* the steppe makes a student of the master.

## The documentary-real hook (the cold open — to be confirmed in research)
A family striking the **ger** at dawn to follow the grass — the whole home folded onto a few animals in
an hour, a thousand-year-old choreography — while on the ridge a **mining-lease fence** cuts the route
their grandfather rode. *A home that moves, meeting a line that doesn't.*

## Guardrails (Bible §4, this book's specifics)
1. **No "empty land / timeless Genghis-land" cliché** — a living, modern, adapting pastoral people.
2. **Distinguish peoples** — Khalkha Mongol ≠ the Kazakh eagle-hunters of the west; name them specifically.
3. **The wrestling champion is an expert, never a brute or comic foil** (the keel: the local out-classes
   the outsider on the local's ground).
4. **No "vanishing nomad" dirge** — the loss is the undertow; the people are the experts fighting to hand
   it on (Bible §7a).
5. **In-culture consultants (veto):** Mongolian herder communities, long-song/wrestling tradition-holders,
   scholars; verify the mining-and-land politics against current reporting.
