## 0. The single most important distinction (guardrail §4.2 — do NOT conflate)

**The Khalkha Mongol steppe herders are NOT the Kazakh eagle-hunters.** This book is about the **former**;
the eagle-hunters are a different people and must be named as such if they appear at all.

- The **eagle-hunters (berkutchi)** of Mongolia are **ethnic Kazakhs** — a **Turkic-language, Sunni
  Muslim** people — concentrated in **Bayan-Ölgii**, the far-western aimag in the **Altai Mountains**
  where Mongolia, Kazakhstan, Russia and China meet. ~**100,000** Kazakhs live in western Mongolia;
  only ~**240** still actively hunt with eagles. **[FACT — multi-source: Al Jazeera, History.com,
  Responsible Travel, UNESCO 2011]**
- The **Khalkha Mongol majority** (the central/eastern steppe herders this book centres) are
  **predominantly Buddhist / folk-religious (Tengri sky-worship, ovoo cairns)**, Mongolic-language, and
  do **not** practise eagle-hunting. **[FACT]**
- **Khöömei (throat-singing) also originates in the *western* Altai**, not the central Khalkha steppe —
  so even within "Mongol" culture, do not assume every famous element belongs to the steppe family the
  book follows. **[FACT — UNESCO 2010]**
- **Rule for the book:** if eagle-hunting / Bayan-Ölgii appears, it is a **named, distinct people**,
  ideally a *separate* thread or an explicit contrast — never "the Mongols and their eagles" blur. The
  steppe-herder *fire* this book joins is the **long-song (urtiin duu) and bökh wrestling**, not falconry.

## 1. Pastoral nomadism today — the ger life (the wonder, grounded)

- **How many still live it. [FACT / FADING]** Estimates cluster around **~190,000–300,000 active
  herders** in **~230,000 herding households**; broader "nomadic or semi-nomadic lifestyle" estimates run
  **~25–30% of the population (~600,000–700,000 people)**. The trend is **down**: Mongolia went from
  ~two-thirds rural in 1960 to **under one-third rural today**; urban share rose from 20% (1950) to **~70%
  (2022)**. *(The numbers disagree at the edges — herding-vs-"semi-nomadic" definitions differ — so the
  book states the **direction**, not a false-precise count: a living world, shrinking within a
  generation.)*
- **The ger (yurt). [FACT]** A circular, portable dwelling: a wooden **lattice-wall frame**, **door
  frame**, **roof poles**, and a **wooden crown (toono)** at the apex for smoke and light — draped in
  thick **felt made from the herders' own sheep's wool** and canvas. **Always oriented to face south.**
  Can be **raised in under ~3 hours and struck into flat-packs in about an hour** for transport. Engineered
  for a brutal climate (Gobi swings from ~**+45°C to −40°C**): warm in winter, cool in summer, sheds the
  spring winds. **The felt ties the dwelling directly to the livestock economy** — the sheep are the house.
- **The "Five Snouts" (tavan khoshuu mal). [FACT]** The herd is **sheep, goats, cattle (incl. yaks),
  horses, and (Bactrian) camels.** **Horses are the cultural backbone.** Mongolia held ~**71 million head**
  (Dec 2022). Livestock is **wealth-and-kin and a survival safety-net** — food, transport, fuel (dried
  dung), materials, status — **not primarily kept for sale.** ~**a third of national employment** depends
  on herding; livestock ≈ **80% of agricultural output** and **~10% of GDP**; **~63% of rural households'
  assets are livestock.**
- **The migration. [FACT]** Moves are **timed to grass, water, and wind** — herders stay while the grass
  lasts and move **2–4 times a year** (spring grass → summer high pasture → autumn fattening → sheltered
  winter camp). A **shorter emergency/temporary move with a makeshift shelter is an *otor*.** The
  next site is chosen on **grass, wind, water** — generations of inherited ecological judgement of *when,
  where, how fast* to move home and animals. **This is the checkable wonder: "empty" land read with a
  precision no outsider instrument matches.**

## 2. Dzud — the catastrophic winter (the fear-of-loss engine, grounded)

> ⚠ **CONTESTED FIGURE — corrected by the verification pass.** Several sources cite "**~3% of the
> national herd**" for the **2009–2010** dzud. The verifiers found this is a **mid-disaster *snapshot*
> from an early-2010 World Bank dispatch, mistaken for the final total.** The **final 2009–10 toll was far
> higher: ~20% of the national herd, ~9.7–10.3 million animals dead** (NSO Mongolia; IFRC benchmark). **The
> book uses the corrected figure.** Localized losses were worse still — **individual herders lost 50–100%**.

- **What it is. [FACT]** A **compound** disaster unique enough to Mongolia to keep its own word: a **dry
  summer** (thin pasture, no hay reserve) followed by a **savage winter** (deep snow, ice, extreme cold,
  wind) that **locks livestock out of grazing** until they starve/freeze *en masse*. **Four named types:**
  **white dzud** (deep snow buries forage), **black dzud** (drought / no snow-water, forage + dehydration),
  **iron dzud** (a brief thaw then hard freeze seals pasture under ice the animals can't break), **cold
  dzud** (extreme cold burns the animals through their fat). *(A "combined" dzud mixes these.)*
- **The toll, grounded. [FACT]** **2009–2010** ≈ **20% of the herd / ~9.7–10.3M animals**, ~**769,000
  people (28% of the population)** affected — the worst on record. **2023–2024** ≈ **~8.1 million animals
  by late spring, ~12.5% of the herd by June 2024**, **~75% of herder households** hit, **76% of the
  country** under white/iron dzud, lows of **−50°C**; IFRC launched a **CHF 4.5M appeal** (Mar 2024) for
  ~10,000 families — by then 2,257 families had already lost **>70%** of their animals. It was Mongolia's
  **sixth major dzud in a decade** (historically ~once a decade). Since 2000, dzud has killed **>20 million
  animals**.
- **Why it's worsening. [FACT / climate]** Mongolia has **warmed ~1.5–2°C+** (some sources ~3× the global
  average since 2005) — drier summers (black dzud) *and* warmer air holding more moisture for heavier winter
  snow. **Overgrazing** (rising herd numbers chasing cashmere income) + mining + urban pressure **degrade
  pasture**, compounding the climatic driver. Tree-ring studies call the recent drought unusual against the
  last **~1,000 years**.
- **The human cascade. [FACT]** Dzud is **the** engine of herder poverty and the **rural→urban drift**:
  herders who lose the herd lose the *life*, and move to the city. UNICEF: **>80,000 children** from
  herding families had schooling disrupted in recent dzud seasons. **This is the book's fear-of-loss made
  literal — a family's whole world can end in a single night, and it's happening more often.**

## 3. Mining vs. herders — the flattening force (the antagonist's real ground)

> The grounded engine for the book's "extractor / empty-frontier" antagonist. **Use composited/fictional
> names** for the specific mine and people of the book (guardrail §4.2) — but the *mechanism* below is real
> and load-bearing, and the back-matter can point at the public record.

- **The mines. [FACT]** **Oyu Tolgoi** (copper-gold, South Gobi / Khanbogd) — majority Rio Tinto via
  **Turquoise Hill Resources (66%)**, **Mongolian state (Erdenes OT) 34%**; becoming one of the **world's
  largest copper-gold mines**, **>80% of its value underground**, 200km+ of planned underground works.
  **Tavan Tolgoi** — vast coal.
- **The land grab, in numbers. [FACT]** Mining license land-take exploded **2003→2022**: **Tavan Tolgoi
  1,391 ha → 44,966 ha (+3,132%)**; **Oyu Tolgoi 1,521 ha → 12,056 ha (+692%)** — open commons converted
  to **exclusionary, fenced, patrolled zones**, restricting the herders' lawful free passage.
- **Water — the real wound. [FACT]** The Gobi is water-scarce and herding *is* water. Oyu Tolgoi **diverted
  the Undai River** and the **sacred Bor Ovoo spring** — *the only natural year-round water source in
  Khanbogd* — which **disappeared during excavation**; a well that watered **1,000 camels dried up**;
  remaining fresh water is **prioritized for the mine**. A **2012 tailings leak** was acknowledged only in
  a **2022 report**. Dust from the mine settles on pasture and **sickens livestock.**
- **The complaint + the partial resolution. [FACT]** Khanbogd herders filed a formal **CAO complaint
  (11 Feb 2013)** (with OT Watch / Gobi Soil) over the river diversion and lost livelihood. **Comprehensive
  agreements signed May 2017** (60 commitments; Undai tributary study); **case closed May 2020** after
  monitoring. **But [CONTESTED/CRITICAL]:** by 2020 ~**two-thirds** of commitments were "complete/in
  progress" — yet the **most important ones (guaranteed access to water, pasture, markets) made no real
  progress**, and **of 250 compensated Khanbogd herding households, fewer than half still herd.** Many
  shifted to **low-wage mine work** — in **May 2024**, OT workers (many former herders) struck for a week
  over a sharp wage cut. *(So the "resolution" is not a clean win — the displacement largely happened. Good,
  honest, un-paintovered story ground.)*
- **The legal keel (the lock the book turns). [FACT]** Under Mongolia's constitution **pastureland must
  stay state-owned — herders hold customary *use* rights, NOT title.** They are **not recognized as
  Indigenous** and **cannot exercise free-prior-informed-consent to refuse a mine.** The state can
  **designate "strategically important" sites and lawfully exclude herders**, creating *de facto* private
  possession for mines on land the law says is open to all. **[UPDATE — 2024]:** Parliament passed a
  **"Law on Herders" / herder-household-association law** formalizing **contract-based pasture *use* by
  carrying capacity (PUA)** + a draft **climate-adaptation-for-herding** law + a **"Wealthy Herder"**
  cooperative-loan program. *(Use-right, still not ownership — the book's tension is intact, with a current
  legislative backdrop.)*

## 4. Urban drift — Ulaanbaatar and the ger districts (where the lost go)

- **[FACT]** **121,753 people** migrated to Ulaanbaatar in the five years before late 2023. UB was **built
  for 500,000** and now holds **~1.7 million — about half the country.** **~60% of residents** live in
  unplanned peripheral **ger districts** (2022) — migrant families **still in their gers**, but crammed in
  **without running water, sewage, electricity, or central heating.**
- **[FACT]** The wood/coal stoves that keep those gers warm make **UB one of the world's most polluted
  capitals** — winter **PM2.5 can exceed 20× the WHO limit.** *(The cruel irony for the book: the felt home
  that breathes on the open steppe becomes a smoke trap in the city — the same dwelling, the life gone out
  of it.)*
- **Why the young leave. [FACT]** Schooling for children, work, and the dzud ratchet — each catastrophic
  winter converts another tranche of herders into city migrants. **The drift is the slow version of the
  loss; the dzud is the fast version.**

## 5. The culture — the "fire," the food, the games (grounded for the payoff beats)

### The fire — long-song & throat-singing
- **Urtiin duu (long-song).** *(The book's primary "fire" — see SEED. UNESCO-listed Mongolian
  intangible heritage; a melismatic, wide-interval song that carries across distance.)* **[FACT — its
  cultural centrality; the specific melody the book uses must be FICTIONAL or used with consultant
  permission, never a scripted real sacred song — §4.1.]**
- **Khöömei (throat-singing). [FACT]** UNESCO 2010. A single singer produces **two sounds at once** — a
  low drone plus a melody of **overtone harmonics** — by modulating mouth/lips/tongue against taut cords.
  **Sung during herding work and inside the ger to lull babies**, at ceremonies and festivities; held to be
  **learned from birds** (shamanic), transmitted master-to-apprentice. **⚠ Western-Altai in origin** — see
  §0; attribute correctly.
- **Morin khuur** (horse-head fiddle) — the iconic two-string steppe instrument *(named in the SEED; pair
  with the long-song at the fire).*

### Naadam & the "three manly games" — where the wrestling humbling lands
- **Naadam. [FACT]** UNESCO 2010. The national festival, **Ulaanbaatar, July 11–13**, built on **wrestling,
  horse-racing, archery** — documented as early as the **13th-century *Secret History of the Mongols*.**
  *(Also held at local/aimag level — the book's bout can be a local Naadam or an informal contest, not
  necessarily the capital's.)*
- **Bökh (Mongolian wrestling) — grounded for the humbling beat. [FACT]**
  - **Object:** make the opponent **touch the ground with anything but feet (or hands)** — back, knee,
    elbow loses. **Throws, trips, lifts.** **No weight classes. No ring. No time limit.** On **open grass
    or bare dirt**, single-elimination. *(This is exactly the SSOT beat: "lands on his back in the dust in
    front of everyone" — and it maps to the rules. A champion "half his fuss and twice his craft" wins by
    grammar Jakobus doesn't have.)*
  - **The ritual. [FACT]** Before and after each bout, wrestlers perform the **Eagle Dance (devekh)** —
    modelled on the flight of the mythical **Garuda**, signalling power/bravery/grace; shamanic origin.
    *(A real, witnessable detail to render the champion with dignity — §4 guard.)*
  - **The costume. [FACT]** Three pieces: **jodag** (tight, collarless, short-sleeved jacket, red or blue,
    **open at the front, chest bared**), **shuudag** (small tight briefs), **gutal** (high leather boots,
    **upturned toes**). *(Open-chest jacket origin myth — that a woman once won in disguise — is folklore;
    flag as legend, don't assert as history.)*
  - **Titles [FACT]:** ranks (Falcon, Hawk, Elephant *zaan*, Garuda, Lion *arslan*, Titan *avarga*) are
    earned **only at Naadam**, by rounds won. *(The book's "regional champion" carries a real, earned
    title — render it as mastery, not menace.)*

### The food — where the tea beats him
- **Süütei tsai (salted milk tea). [FACT — confirmed and corrected].** Tea **brewed with milk, water, and
  SALT** (a basic recipe: ~1 qt water + 1 qt milk + a spoon of green/brick tea + ~1 tsp salt) — **not a
  sweet tea.** Often from a **compressed brick of lower-grade tea**; traditionally **aerated by scooping and
  pouring from a height** (many now skip this). **Butter / tail-fat is an OPTIONAL richness**, and **fried
  millet** a common add — **so "salted yak/mare-butter tea" is a real *variation*, not a fixed dish.** The
  milk can come from **any of the five snouts** (cow now the norm). **It is the standard welcome served to a
  guest on arrival at a ger**, and drunk all day. **[This is the SSOT yak-butter-tea beat, grounded:** the
  thing that beats Jakobus is the **salt where his body wants sweet + the butter turning on the tongue** —
  exactly the canon — and it's the *hospitality* drink, which is why the failing is so pointed: he's
  refusing the welcome cup, and the grace is in *how* he fails.]
- **Airag (fermented mare's milk). [FACT]** The traditional **mildly alcoholic (~up to 2%)**, slightly sour
  drink; mare's milk **filtered into a leather sack (khukhuur)** and **stirred with a wooden buluur** for
  1–2 days — **the sack sits by the ger door so everyone passing stirs it.** Central to **hospitality** and
  heavy at Naadam. Fermentation converts lactose, making it **tolerable for the lactose-intolerant.**
  *(N.B. Jakobus **does not drink alcohol** — airag's ~2% is a real, gentle tension point: a hospitality
  drink he must decline. Handle with his usual no-moralising grace; the salted tea, not the airag, is the
  "food that beats him.")*
- **The deel** — the traditional wrap-coat/robe (named in the SEED) — everyday and ceremonial steppe dress.

## 6. The sacred — what the book must WITHHOLD (guardrail §4.1)

- **Ovoo. [FACT]** Sacred **stone (or wood/branch/soil) cairns** of Mongolian folk religion — sites of
  worship of **Heaven and lesser gods**, led by **shamans and clan elders** (also used in Buddhist
  ceremony). Sited at **mountain tops, passes, near springs/rivers, crossroads.** **Travel protocol:** stop,
  **circle three times clockwise**, add a stone, leave an offering (sweets, money, milk). Worshippers tie a
  **blue khadag (scarf) to Tengri, the sky spirit.** Venerated by Mongols/Buryats — **a Mongol tradition,
  distinct from the Kazakh eagle-hunters.**
- **The withholding rule for the book.** The ovoo, the blue khadag, the sky-worship, any shamanic rite — the
  book may **show the threshold and look away** (a companion is taught to circle the ovoo and add a stone;
  the *content* of a shaman's invocation is **never scripted**). **Withholding is dignity, not a tease**
  (§4.1). Jakobus's own creed models this — he'd circle the cairn correctly and **ask**, not perform.

## 7. Representation — killing the cliché (guardrail §4.5, §3)

- **The "Genghis Khan / empty land / backward nomad" flattening is the painted-over word this book lifts.**
  Genghis is globally reduced to a **colonialist "barbarian horde" stereotype** *and* inflated into a
  **nationalist icon** — both erase the **living, modern, adapting** pastoral civilisation. **[CONTESTED
  terrain — handle as theme:** the book neither demonizes nor lionizes Genghis; it makes the **present**
  people the subject, and the "empty steppe" the lie the antagonist (and the reader) arrives believing.]
- **The land is not empty — it is the most precisely *known* land they've walked.** The whole inversion:
  what reads as emptiness is a **thousand-year-old, sophisticated, GPS-free land-use system** held in
  bodies and memory. The wonder is **competence**, not mysticism.
- **The people are experts fighting to hand it on, not a vanishing relic to mourn (§7a).** Show the **grip,
  not the grave**: the grandchild who learns the song, the migration route kept open, the herd carried
  through one more winter. **No noble-savage, no pity, no dirge.**
- **Distinguish peoples (§4.2):** Khalkha Mongol steppe-herder ≠ Kazakh eagle-hunter ≠ Buryat ≠ the
  western-Altai khöömei tradition. Name specifically; prefer a **clearly-fictional community** over putting
  words in a real, nameable village's mouth.
