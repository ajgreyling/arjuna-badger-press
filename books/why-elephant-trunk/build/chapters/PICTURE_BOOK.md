# How the Elephant Got His Long Nose

*{{DEDICATION}}*

<!-- spread:1 image="spread-01-trunk.png" alt="Long-ago golden bushveld at dawn; a small grey elephant calf with a tiny stubby nose stands among fever-trees, big honest eyes, the herd soft in the background." -->
Long, long ago,
in the wide warm bush,
the elephant had only
a small, stubby nose.

His name was Nkulu.
He was curious about *everything.*

<!-- spread:2 image="spread-02-trunk.png" alt="Hot midday; the elephant herd dozing in fever-tree shade; a brown-skinned village child in a bright wrap-cloth walks barefoot beside little Nkulu toward a glint of river." -->
One hot, humming day,
the big ones dozed in the shade.
But Nkulu wanted to *see.*

So {{CHILD}} took his little nose in hand,
and the two of them tiptoed
down toward the river.

<!-- spread:3 image="spread-03-trunk.png" alt="Reedy riverbank, blue water, weaver nests hanging, red mud; little Nkulu leans down and dips his short stubby nose to drink while the child watches from the bank." -->
The river was cool.
The reeds went *swish.*
The mud smelled of morning.

Nkulu dipped his small stubby nose
to take a little drink.

<!-- spread:4 image="spread-04-trunk.png" alt="A long olive-green crocodile, Ngwenya, lying still as a log in the shallows, one yellow eye sliding open, the unsuspecting calf drinking nearby." -->
But there in the shallows,
still as a sleeping log,
lay Ngwenya the crocodile.

One yellow eye
slid slowly… open.

<!-- spread:5 image="spread-05-trunk.png" alt="The crocodile lunges and clamps the little elephant's stubby nose, pulling him toward the deep water; the child on the bank gasps, eyes wide." -->
**SNAP!**
Ngwenya caught that little nose
and pulled it toward the deep.

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we won't let go!*

<!-- spread:6 image="spread-06-trunk.png" alt="Tug-of-war begins; the calf digs in his heels and leans back, while the brave child grabs the elephant's tail and pulls with all their might." -->
Nkulu leaned back hard.
{{CHILD}} grabbed his tail
and pulled with all their might.

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we won't let go!*

<!-- spread:7 image="spread-07-trunk.png" alt="A yellow weaver bird tugs the child's wrap with its beak and a vervet monkey grips the elephant's leg; the line of pullers grows along the bank." -->
A weaver bird came tugging.
A vervet monkey gripped on too.
The line grew long and loud.

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we won't let go!*

<!-- spread:8 image="spread-08-trunk.png" alt="A warthog with bristly tail and a slow determined tortoise add themselves to the end of the pulling line; everyone heaving together, dust rising." -->
A warthog backed in, snorting.
Even slow old tortoise came —
last in line, but he held *fast.*

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we won't let go!*

<!-- spread:9 image="spread-09-trunk.png" alt="The whole elephant herd thunders down the bank in a cloud of red dust and joins the rope of pullers, every trunk-less elephant lending its weight." -->
Then the herd came thundering —
big feet, red dust, deep rumble —
and added all their weight.

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we won't let go!*

<!-- spread:10 image="spread-10-trunk.png" alt="Comic stretchy spread: the little elephant's nose stretching longer and longer like soft clay between the crocodile and the laughing line of pullers, spanning the whole page." -->
And the little nose went…
s-t-r-e-t-c-h.

Longer… and longer…
and LONGER it grew!

<!-- spread:11 image="spread-11-trunk.png" alt="The crocodile finally lets go and slips back into the water; all the pullers tumble backward into a laughing heap, and Nkulu sits up with a brand-new long trunk." -->
At last Ngwenya let *go* —
*splosh!* — and slid away.

Everyone tumbled back
in a giggling, dusty heap.
And Nkulu… had a LONG nose now!

<!-- spread:12 image="spread-12-trunk.png" alt="Joyful spread: the elephant using his new long trunk to drink water, spray a wash, pick the highest sweet marula fruit, and curl gently around the child in a hug." -->
But oh — what a nose it was!
It could *drink* a whole cool river.
It could *wash* his dusty back.
It could *pick* the highest fruit.

And best of all,
it could curl around {{CHILD}}
and give the softest hug
in the whole wide bush.

<!-- spread:13 image="spread-13-trunk.png" alt="Sunset over the bushveld; a long line of elephants now all with long trunks raised in greeting, the child waving from the bank, golden light over the fever-trees." -->
And that is why,
to this very day,
every elephant you meet
has a long, loving trunk.

The day that felt the worst
became his finest gift.

*Pull… and pull… and PULL!*
*Hold on tight — we never let go.*
