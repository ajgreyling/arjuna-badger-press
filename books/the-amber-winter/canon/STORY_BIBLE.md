# WINTER SONDER EINDE — Story Bible
## Master reference for the saga

---

# CORE PREMISE

WINTER SONDER EINDE is an **adult historical saga** set in the ninth-century Norse world — the high
Viking Age, one fjord, one hall, one woman's whole life across five books. It blends:

- grounded, sourced material-culture realism (the Dan-Brown-accuracy promise)
- the lived texture of the long winter, the great hall, the feast, the faith
- the frank adult sensuality of the saga tradition (André P. Brink / Kleinboer, not pornography)
- a woman's full interior life — appetite, grief, power, and waking
- the ecstatic-sacred undercurrent of seiðr

At its core this is a story about:

> a fire gone to embers under a lukewarm marriage and a lifetime of holding others afloat —
> and the long, unashamed burning of it back to life.

The saga argues:

> a woman's fire is her own, oopoog, to the end of a long life — and the choosing of what to do
> with it is the only genuinely interesting decision a person ever gets to make.

---

# WHO IT IS FOR

Commissioned by an adult reader done with shame and with censorship (she grew up under apartheid's
banned-books regime). She asked for a *Vikings*-like series that **teaches** real history, that
lets her **truly know the people**, and that does not flinch from the warmth of a grown woman's
body. The saga gives her exactly that — **within four absolute gates** (see CANON_CHOICES.md):
no children, ever; consent is the engine; force is only ever a wound; the door closes at the
explicit threshold. The gates are not a compromise with the brief — they are what makes it worth
reading.

---

# GENRE

Adult historical fiction / Norse saga. Tonal kin: the *Vikings* TV saga (the reader's lodestar),
the Icelandic sagas, Bernard Cornwell's *Saxon Stories* for the period-blood, Sigrid Undset's
*Kristin Lavransdatter* for a woman's whole life across a medieval world — and, for the prose
register and the heat-handling, **Arjuna Badger Press's own *The Salt Veil*** (the explicit ceiling).

# PRINCIPAL CAST (full detail in CHARACTERS.md)

- **Þórdís "Tóra" Róðmarsdóttir**, 38 → elder — the húsfreyja; the waking woman; protagonist.
- **Hákon Thorgrimsson**, 46 — her husband, the goði; a good man gone tender-but-cold; never a villain.
- **Eivor the Hound**, 40 — the far-traveller; *the Ragnar* — gentle and reckless both.
- **Solveig the Shield**, 35 — the shield-maiden; *the Lagertha*; one of the true loves of Tóra's life.
- **Heiðr the Far-Seer** — the wandering volva; Tóra's dark teacher in the seeing.
- The household: **Ásta**, **Rognvaldr** (a son, held in absolute Gate-I care), **Gunnhildr**,
  **Ketill**, **Yrsa** (the thrall, the saga's conscience), **Bjorn the skald**.
- **Styrbjorn Greycloak** — the human edge of the brutal age; opposed for what he does, never a cartoon.

---

# SETTING (full detail in WORLD.md)

Ve*strfjorðr, an invented but plausible west-Norwegian fjord-steading, c. 860–880 CE, with the
real sea-roads running out from it: Kaupang, Hedeby, Dublin, the Danelaw, and the long amber-road
east to Miklagarðr (Constantinople). The long winter, the langhús, the langeldr, the loom, the
hnefatafl board, the blót-feast, the seiðr-fire — all grounded in the archaeological and saga record.

---

# THE FIVE-BOOK ARC (full detail in PLOT.md)

I. **Die Vuur in die Donker** (*The Fire in the Dark*) — she wakes. (written now)
II. **Die Seeweg** (*The Sea-Road*) — she goes out into the world.
III. **The High Seat** — she takes up power and pays for it.
IV. **The Long Dark** — she meets the saga's grief and is not unmade.
V. **The Elder by the Fire** — the finale: the circle closes; the fire is handed forward.

No cliffhangers. Each book a closed door. Book V finishes.

---

# WRITING STYLE TARGET (full law in STYLE_GUIDE.md)

Write at the ceiling of *The Salt Veil*:
- the long accreting breath; clause on clause; never hurried
- attention as the erotic instrument — the whole of one person arriving on another like weather
- the threshold close — up to the act in full honesty, then trust the reader's own fire
- choosing as the spine of every intimate scene
- history lived in scene, never lectured
- no machine-tell, no melodrama, no exposition dump, no anachronism

Avoid: clinical or mechanical sex; the explicit; melodrama; modern idiom; horned helmets and the
other genre-myths the reader already laughs at.

---

# FINAL AUTHORIAL THESIS

The saga should leave the reader feeling:

> A grown woman's fire is not a thing to be ashamed of, or to put down when the marriage goes
> lukewarm, or to hide because someone once decided what she may want.
>
> It is hers. It can wake at thirty-eight and burn to the end of a long life.
>
> And the warmest thing one person can give another — across a fire, across a bed, across a
> whole life — is the entire weight of their attention, freely, with open eyes.
