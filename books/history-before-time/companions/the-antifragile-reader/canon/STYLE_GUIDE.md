# STYLE_GUIDE — *The Antifragile Reader*

*A guest-at-the-fire companion to Nassim Nicholas Taleb's* Incerto.
*History Before Time · Companions.*

---

## What this book is

A reverent, faithful **companion** to Taleb's five-volume *Incerto* — *Fooled by Randomness*,
*The Black Swan*, *The Bed of Procrustes*, *Antifragile*, and *Skin in the Game* — told plainly in
the house voice by an outsider who loves the work and claims no authority over it. The same model as
*The Wrath of Achilles*: **a guest at someone else's fire.**

The reader's stated need: *"I got* Antifragile *fully. The rest I can't remember so well."* This book
is the answer to that — a single compendium that carries the whole *Incerto* in one warm read, so a
reader who loved one volume can hold all five and feel the architecture connect.

It is **not** Taleb impersonated, not a substitute for reading him, and not a "Taleb for dummies"
debunk-or-flatten. It is an honest visitor's account of a body of work that is still being written by
a living author who can read this himself.

## The hard rule — a LIVING author, in copyright

This is the one place this book differs sharply from *The Wrath of Achilles* (Homer is three thousand
years dead; Butler's translation is public domain). **Taleb is alive and the *Incerto* is in
copyright.** Therefore:

1. **Paraphrase his ideas; do not reproduce his prose.** Every concept — antifragility, the Black
   Swan, via negativa, the Lindy effect, skin in the game, the ludic fallacy, the Procrustean bed,
   the turkey, Mediocristan/Extremistan, the barbell, *fragilista*, iatrogenics, the agency problem —
   is **his**, credited to him by name and book, but rendered in *our* words.
2. **Quote sparingly, briefly, and only under fair use** — a short aphorism or a phrase, always
   attributed to the specific book, never a passage. The aphorisms of *The Bed of Procrustes* are the
   natural (still brief) candidates. When in doubt, paraphrase and attribute rather than quote.
3. **Never present a paraphrase as a quotation.** If words are in quotation marks, they are his,
   attributed. If they are ours, they carry no quote marks and, where it is *his idea in our phrasing*,
   the credit is explicit ("Taleb's term," "as he argues in *Antifragile*").
4. **Mark the three voices, always** (below). The reader must never mistake our gloss for his claim.

This rule is not legal timidity. It is the house ethic — **the wrapper is not the work; provenance is
disclosed; derivation is faithful** — applied to a living mind. We honour him best by sending readers
*to* him, not by standing in front of him.

## The three voices — keep them visibly separate

Borrowed wholesale from *The Wrath of Achilles*, because it is the load-bearing honesty mechanism.

- **(a) What Taleb argues.** Faithful exposition of his idea, attributed to the book it lives in.
  Neutral, generous, accurate. We state his case at its strongest before we ever reach for our own.
- **(b) Where thoughtful people push back.** The honest scholarship-and-dispute voice: the critiques,
  the places his tone is abrasive, the empirical arguments against, the "reasonable people differ."
  Marked as contested, never smuggled in as settled.
- **(c) Our own plain gloss — marked `plainly:`** Our reach, our modern phrase, our application to the
  reader's own ground (building under uncertainty, Africa, the press, a life). Always owned as ours.

When the reader sees **`plainly:`**, that is our voice, owning the reach — exactly as in the Homer
companion.

## Per-essay structure (the chapter template)

Each essay follows the *Wrath of Achilles* rhythm, adapted from a *book* to an *idea*:

```
# <Essay N> — <The idea, named warmly>

### The fire
   What Taleb actually argues — voice (a). Where it lives in the Incerto. The strongest case.

### Where the smoke goes
   Voice (b). The honest pushback, the limits, the place the idea is overreached or the tone bites.
   (Omit only when there is genuinely little serious dispute — say so rather than fake a balance.)

### Plainly
   Voice (c). Our gloss — one load-bearing paragraph that makes the idea land for a working reader,
   aimed at their real ground. Marked plainly:. This is where the book earns its keep.

### The line
   ONE short attributed aphorism or phrase from the Incerto (fair use), naming the book — OR, if no
   brief quote is safe/needed, a clearly-marked plainly: restatement and a pointer to where to read it.
```

This keeps the same four-beat shape readers of the Homer companion already trust, while respecting the
living-author rule (the "line" is a single short aphorism, not a quoted passage).

## Voice and register

- **Warm, plain, unhurried.** Atkinson-Hyperlegible-of-prose: nothing ornamental that costs clarity.
  (House default typeface is Atkinson Hyperlegible; a11y is foundation, not garnish.)
- **First person, humble, specific.** "I came to this sideways" — the guest, not the professor.
- **No cargo-cult jargon.** Use Taleb's terms because they *disambiguate*, never to sound clever.
  (This is literally the press thesis: correct register helps only when it points into real practice.)
- **South African / builder's ground where it earns the gloss.** The `plainly:` voice may land an idea
  on the reader's own terrain — building a venture under uncertainty, a press grown from a vow, a
  continent written into its own hands — but never forced, never a lecture.
- **Honest about Taleb's edges.** He is combative, sometimes contemptuous, often right *and* abrasive.
  We neither sand that off nor adopt it. We are a guest; we keep our own manners.

## De-LLM / house taboos (inherited)

Per `craft/LLM_TELLS.md` and the de-LLM doctrine: no machine-even tricolons by reflex, no "it's
important to note," no hollow "delve/tapestry/testament," no significance-narration ("this teaches us
that…"). Thin the seams. The reader stays at the fire, not in front of the apparatus.

## The mission fit

This sits on the **History Before Time · Companions** shelf beside the Gita and Iliad retellings: the
house's reverent-companion line. Its job is the companion's job — *send the reader to the source,
warmer and braver than they came.* The proof, as always, is qualitative: a reader who loved
*Antifragile* finishes this and finally **holds the whole Incerto** — and goes back to read the four
they half-remember.

## Coverage — the eleven essays (see PLAN.md)

The compendium is a single read of ~11 essays tracing the ideas across the five books, opening with a
proem (the guest declares himself) and closing with the via-negativa coda. The architecture, not the
book order, is the spine: randomness → the unseen → the bed that cuts → fragility's three states →
skin in the game → the practical creed.
