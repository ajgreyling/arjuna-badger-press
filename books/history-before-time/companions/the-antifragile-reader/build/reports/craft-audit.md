- **quote:** "I came to Taleb the way most people come to him: through one book, hard, and then a long pause. The one book was *Antifragile*. - **problem:** The proem establishes intimacy but immediately over-narrates it. 'The way most people come to him' tells us what to think before we've felt it. 'The thing only a few books ever do' captions an experience that was already landing.

### [major] gravitas_inflation
- **where:** throughout
- **quote:** "This is what a great writer does for you, and it is the most I will claim to do for him here: he hands you the word for the thing you already half-knew, and the having of it changes what you can aim at."
- **problem:** At emotional peaks, the prose gets grander instead of plainer. 'The having of it changes what you can aim at' is straining for profundity where 'changes what you can do' would hit harder. The companion voice goes solemn precisely where Taleb stays sharp.
- **fix:** At peak moments, get plainer. Short, concrete, cold. Let the size of the idea create its own gravity without prose that knows it's being profound.

### [major] voice_homogenization
- **where:** throughout
- **quote:** "I am not an economist. I am not a statistician or a probabilist or a trader who has watched a position go to zero. I have no authority over this material whatsoever."
- **problem:** The repeated disavowal of authority becomes a rhetorical device that distances the reader from the ideas. The humble guest pose prevents the companion from doing what companions must: make the complex feel graspable. Too much 'I am not' prevents 'here's what this means.'
- **fix:** State limitations once, then get out of the way. The reader needs a guide who has internalized the ideas, not one constantly reminding them he's unqualified.

### [minor] abstract_drift
- **where:** throughout
- **quote:** "The interventionist who topples a dictator is not living in the chaos that follows. This, Taleb argues, is **the most dangerous kind of person**: someone with the power to make consequential decisions and no personal exposure to the consequences."
- **problem:** Abstract nouns like 'consequences,' 'exposure,' 'interventionist' float unmoored from physical reality. Where's the specific dictator, the actual chaos, the named interventionist? The prose gestures at categories instead of showing bodies.
- **fix:** Name names or invent specifics. 'The advisor who pushed for Libya sleeps in Georgetown while Tripoli burns.' Concrete beats category.

### [minor] uniform_polish
- **where:** throughout 'Where the smoke goes' sections
- **quote:** "The honest pushback comes from two directions, and it is worth naming both. The first is empirical and friendly: *yes, but skill is real and sometimes detectable.*"
- **problem:** The 'honest pushback' sections are too polite, too balanced, too careful to seem fair. Real criticism has edges. This reads like a diplomatic summary, not the actual fight smart people have with these ideas.
- **fix:** Let critics bite. 'Taleb cherry-picks his turkeys' hits harder than 'The empirical pushback is friendly.' Show the real objections readers will have, not the sanitized academic versions.
