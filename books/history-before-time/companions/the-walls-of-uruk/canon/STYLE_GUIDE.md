# Style — The Walls of Uruk

Borrowed wholesale from *The Wrath of Achilles*, because it is the load-bearing honesty mechanism.

## Rhythm per tablet

1. **The scene** — what happens, plainly and faithfully
2. **Plainly** — one hard human sentence (bold) on what the tablet asks of a life
3. **The line** — named quotation (Thompson) + ``plainly:`` gloss

## Registers

- **(a)** poem / tradition — unmarked teaching voice
- **(b)** scholarship — boxed or prefaced as dispute
- **(c)** author — always ``plainly:`` or **Plainly**

## Names

Prefer familiar English: Gilgamesh, Enkidu, Uruk, Humbaba, Ishtar, Utnapishtim, Siduri, Urshanabi. Note Thompson's *Gilgamish* in quotations. Note Inanna when Sumerian poems matter.

## Do not

- Flatten into Bible-source-code or alien myth
- Skip Tablet XI's flood details or the serpent
- Pretend Tablet XII's belonging is settled
- Rearrange the wood at someone else's fire
