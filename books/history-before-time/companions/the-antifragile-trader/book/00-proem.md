# Proem — The Number You Can Run

The fire is where I left it.

I sat here once before, as a guest, and wrote down what I saw by its light. That account became *The Antifragile Reader*: eleven essays carrying Nassim Nicholas Taleb's five-volume *Incerto* in one warm read, for the reader who loved one book and half-remembered the rest. I kept my manners. I paraphrased and attributed. I reported the family's arguments instead of settling them, and I sent every reader back to the source. Then I went home.

Now I have come back, and I have brought a box.

Any host watching a returning guest set a box down by the woodpile is entitled to the obvious question. *What is that for?*

Let me open the lid.

Instruments. Small machines, one to an essay, live in a small wing of the open web at https://lucid.rodeo/incerto/. Each runs in your browser and does its arithmetic in front of you. One keeps a turkey's ledger, day by day. One shows what an average hides. Another prices the patience a barbell demands. None of them is clever. An instrument has no opinion. It measures.

Here is what changed between the two visits. The *Reader* asked you to trust my account, and the whole book leaned on that honesty. This one asks you to trust nothing — where the *Reader* said *believe me*, this book says *watch*. Every essay ends at a machine, a beat called **Run it**, where the number the essay has been circling runs in front of you, in your own browser, touching nothing of yours. You do not have to believe me about the turkey's last morning. You can go watch the ledger fill. The path runs both ways: the book sends you to run a number, and the wing sends you back to read why it behaves as it does.

The discipline runs through the prose as well. Every number in these pages comes from a paired exhibit, read before its essay was drafted, or from arithmetic simple enough to rebuild with a pencil. A number I merely remember does not appear.

---

Two things the instruments will not do. They are the two promises of this book, and they rank equally.

They will not speak in his voice. Taleb is alive and working, and the *Incerto* is in copyright. In the *Reader* I allowed myself a brief attributed line where fair use permitted; here I allow none. Nothing he wrote appears inside quotation marks anywhere in this book. His ideas are re-derived and named to the book they live in; the phrasing is mine and marked as mine — where you read **`plainly:`**, that is my reach, owned. And where the family still argues — over whether a tail can be priced at all — I report the argument, as before, instead of pretending it is settled. The discipline is tighter here for a reason. This book stands on his professional ground, markets and risk and payoffs, and a borrowed sentence there would read as borrowed authority. His prose stays his.

And they will not tell you what to do. Structure, never advice. I am not a licensed anything, in any jurisdiction, and this book gives no personalised advice of any kind. It describes payoff shapes, estimators, and arithmetic — a shape is all this book is licensed to be. And if a page of mine ever ends in a position, that page has betrayed you. *Run the number* is the only instruction it will ever give, and running it costs nothing and risks nothing. The instruments hold no account of yours and place no order. They replay past prices and roll classroom dice, and each page says so.

---

Who is the second visit for? The reader who trades. And the reader who builds anything that has to stand in market weather — a venture, a press like mine — and who carries the mathematics as slogans. You can say fat tails at a dinner table. You half-remember that the turkey of *The Black Swan* matters and that the barbell of *Antifragile* has two ends and nothing in the middle. What you do not have is the arithmetic in your hands, and a slogan is exactly the kind of knowledge that fails on the day it is needed. The wager of this book is that a number you have run yourself sits deeper than a phrase you have repeated.

One more thing, said here because the exactness could hide it. The instruments are precise and I am not. A machine that replays a past crash replays it correctly, and the correctness belongs to the machine and to the past. A measured tail is the past, not a forecast. You will meet that sentence at the end of every essay, because it is the one honesty a well-built instrument most needs said out loud. And the guest is still a guest. I have read the five books properly now, which is more than I could claim when I first sat down here; it does not make me a householder. There are people who can take the mathematics to him line by line. I am the visitor who built a bench by the woodpile, so you could check the sums yourself.

The fire is lit. The bench is set.

Here is what I saw, and here is what you can run.
