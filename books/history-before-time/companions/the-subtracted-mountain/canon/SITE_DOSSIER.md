# SITE DOSSIER — Kailasa temple (Cave 16, Ellora) — BINDING for real-vs-claimed

> Every load-bearing number and claim in the manuscript must trace to this dossier. Where the
> record is contested, the book says so and stages the contest; it never silently picks a side.
> Same doctrine as the estate's other site books: **official story first, full respect; then the
> real hole; then the maybe. The narrator never over-claims.**

---

## 1. The site — the settled facts (use straight)

- **What:** monolithic rock-cut Hindu temple dedicated to **Shiva as Kailashanatha** ("Lord of
  Mount Kailasa"). Formally **Cave 16** of the Ellora Caves, Charanandri hills, Deccan Plateau,
  Maharashtra, India — ~30 km NNW of Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (formerly Aurangabad).
  Coordinates 20°01′35″N 75°10′45″E.
- **The headline fact:** carved **vertically, from the summit of a basalt cliff downward** — a
  "cut-out," not the usual "cut-in" cave. The artisans excavated a U-shaped trench around the
  future temple and released a complete, freestanding, multi-storey temple from the living rock.
  **No mortar. No added stone. No possibility of revision** — every removal was final.
- **Scale:** roughly **3 million cubic feet (~200,000 tonnes) of basalt removed**. Courtyard
  ~82 m × 46 m; scarp up to ~32 m high; the temple itself ~32 m tall, ~59 m long, ~27 m wide;
  plinth ~8 m, carried on carved elephants and lions. The largest monolithic structure in the
  world.
- **Layout:** two-storey gopuram entrance → Nandi mandapa flanked by two dhvajastambhas
  (monolithic flagstaffs) → 16-pillared sabhamandapa → garbhagriha with the Shiva lingam under a
  Dravidian-style four-tiered sikhara. Five subsidiary shrines, incl. the river goddesses Ganga,
  Yamuna, Saraswati. Circumambulatory galleries cut into the courtyard walls.
- **Geology:** Deccan Traps flood basalt — hard, fine-grained, laid down in vast flows ~66 Ma.
  The stone that makes the top-down method survivable is the same stone that makes it brutal to
  cut with iron hammers, chisels, and rod drills (tool marks are still visible).
- **Context:** Ellora's 34 principal caves span **~2 km of cliff and ~400 years** (6th–10th c. CE):
  Buddhist (caves 1–12), Hindu (13–29), Jain (30–34). Three religions, one mountain, no
  demolition of each other's work. **UNESCO World Heritage 1983**, criteria (i), (iii), (vi).
- **Still alive:** the lingam is worshipped today; this is not only a monument, it is an active
  shrine. Over **1.7 million visitors in FY 2024–25**.

## 2. Patronage and dating (settled core, contested edges — stage the contest)

- **Settled core:** attributed to **Rashtrakuta king Krishna I** (r. ~756–773 CE). Key evidence:
  the **Baroda copper-plate inscription of Karka II, Saka 734 (812–813 CE)**, which credits
  "Krishnaraja" with a Shiva temple at **Elapura** (Ellora) so wondrous that even the gods were
  astonished. Stylistic parallels to the **Virupaksha temple at Pattadakal** (Chalukya, ~734–745
  CE) anchor the 8th-century date.
- **Contested edge 1 — one reign or several?** There is **no dedicatory inscription at the temple
  itself.** Scholars propose multi-phase construction: possible start under **Dantidurga**
  (r. ~735–756), completion under Krishna I, additions under **Dhruva** (r. 780–793). The book
  stages this as a live scholarly question, not a solved one.
- **Contested edge 2 — how long?** Legend says a week (the **Kokasa** story — the architect who
  vowed speed to a queen's fast). Archaeologist **M. K. Dhavalikar** (Deccan College, 1982)
  estimated the *core* could be cut in **~5.5 years** by 250–400 workers removing ~4 cu ft per
  worker-day; the full complex more plausibly **18–20 years**. Do the arithmetic on the page —
  the honest numbers are astonishing *without* being impossible (the Banning move from the
  Göbekli book: resist the "impossible workforce" over-reach).
- **Lineage (use it — it dissolves the "no precedent" myth):** the top-down monolith did not
  appear from nowhere. Chalukya **Virupaksha at Pattadakal** supplied the model; Pallava
  **Kailasanatha at Kanchipuram** the name and the vimana; Rashtrakutas scaled the tradition up
  by an order of magnitude. Masterpieces have parents.

## 3. Iconography (the meaning layer)

- **Ravananugraha** — the south-face, multi-storey high relief: Ravana strains beneath Mount
  Kailasa; Shiva pins the mountain with a toe; Parvati watches. The temple *is* the mountain of
  that story — the whole monument is the myth made walkable.
- Ramayana and Mahabharata **narrative friezes** in sequential bands; Nataraja, Gajasurasamhara,
  Yogishvara; Vishnu avatars in subsidiary shrines; Saptamatrikas; Gajalakshmi at the gopuram;
  yalis and makaras at thresholds; dance poses keyed to the Natya Shastra; traces of **ceiling
  paintings** in the mandapa (the temple was once plastered white and painted — it imitated
  snow-capped Kailasa).
- **The symbol:** vertical axis = Kailasa/Meru, the cosmic mountain; the courtyard descent and
  the lingam at the core make the visit itself a cosmological diagram.

## 4. Damage, weather, conservation (the custodianship layer)

- **Aurangzeb episode:** tradition holds the Mughal emperor ordered the temple destroyed (late
  17th c.); the basalt largely defeated the attempt — treat the details as tradition-with-a-core,
  not verbatim record.
- Natural enemies: monsoon seepage, weathering of exposed sculpture, biological growth; the
  Deccan is low-seismic but not zero. Original builders cut **drainage channels concurrently**
  with the temple — 8th-century hydrology engineering, checkable on site.
- Conservation history: ASI systematic work from the **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 1904**;
  grouting, grills, chemical cleaning; **1992–2002 Japan Bank for International Cooperation**
  drainage and landscaping project; continuous UNESCO monitoring since 1983.

## 5. THE SCANS (the book's news hook — handle exactly as below)

### 5.1 Verified, documented survey work (use straight, cite)

- **2024 — Archaeological Survey of India laser scanning:** high-resolution 3D digital models of
  Cave 16 for documentation, damage analysis, and conservation planning. The first
  millimetre-scale digital twin of the monument.
- **2025 — UNESCO + Indian Ministry of Tourism LiDAR project:** dedicated LiDAR campaign focused
  on Cave 16 to assess and preserve the site; complements the ASI models.
- **Geological Survey of India geophysical surveys:** rock-stability evaluation, seepage-prone
  zones, targeted reinforcement recommendations.
- What the scans actually give the story: exact excavation volumes instead of estimates; tool-mark
  and work-sequence forensics; symmetry tolerances measured, not asserted; the drainage system
  mapped; a baseline against which future weathering is measurable. **The digital twin is the
  new chapter in the temple's 1,200-year custodianship — that is the real wonder; play it
  straight.**

### 5.2 The "Russian team" scans (REAL-VS-CLAIMED — binding)

- **The claim as it circulates:** viral videos and posts assert that a **Russian scientific team**
  scanned Kailasa (georadar / LiDAR variants of the story) and found **hidden chambers, sealed
  passages beneath the courtyard, or "energy machines"** — sometimes garnished with the
  British-era "high radioactivity in the caves below" story.
- **The verified record:** **no published, peer-reviewed, or institutionally confirmed
  Russian-team survey of Kailasa exists** as of mid-2026. The claims are unverified and appear
  to be misreadings (or inventions) layered on top of the *real* survey work in §5.1 — genuine
  resistivity/mapping studies do show voids, galleries, and drainage cavities, which the viral
  versions inflate into secret chambers.
- **How the book uses it (the Why Files move):** do NOT ignore it and do NOT sneer at it. Give
  the claim its own scene — play the tape, trace where the story came from, then set the
  verified scans beside it and let the reader watch the gap. The honest residue is real and
  lands harder than the fantasy: *the mapping does show voids nobody has entered, the sub-court
  drainage network is genuinely under-explored, and the site is ~10% understood the way all
  rock-cut complexes are.* The wonder survives the fact-check. **The narrator never confirms the
  chambers and never mocks the hoping.**

## 6. Open questions (keep them open — the maybes the book ends on)

1. **One reign or four?** No dedicatory inscription; the construction chronology is genuinely
   unresolved.
2. **Where did the spoil go?** ~200,000 tonnes of basalt with no agreed dump site identified in
   the record — a real logistics question the scans may eventually answer (resist the viral
   over-reach; note quarry-fill and valley-spread hypotheses).
3. **The unentered voids** — what the resistivity anomalies and drainage galleries actually are.
4. **The planning question** — what drawings, models, or measure-systems let a crew cut a
   finished multi-storey building *downward* with zero tolerance for error. (The Gita frame the
   fiction sibling uses — *the form is already in the rock; remove what is not it* — is the
   cultural answer; the technical answer is still being assembled from tool marks and setting-out
   lines.)
