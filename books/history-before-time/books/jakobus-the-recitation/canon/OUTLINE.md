## TIMELINE PLACEMENT (LOCKED 2026-06-14 — resolves the prop-continuity audit)

> Binding placement so kit and the Beast stay consistent with the main *History Before Time* arc and
> the Jakobus + Beast SSOTs. The book is Part I's own words: **"the wandering years."**

- **WHEN.** *The Recitation* is set in the **wandering years, AFTER the main arc (post-Egypt).** This is
  the only placement consistent with the kit on the page; treat it as canon.
- **The Litany-of-Fear tattoo** is **present** (it is read by Cheikh Lemine in Part III). Correct: the
  Litany is acquired in **Egypt (Book V, ch-11)**, so a *post-Egypt* placement is required. ✓
- **The firestarter cord** on his wrist (ch-23 "four metres of it, the red strand that would burn") is a
  **fresh commercial re-braid of the same milspec cord**, NOT the original firestarter (the original was
  given away; the *capability ports*, per the Jakobus SSOT firestarter rule). It is bracelet-weaving, not
  the making of a new heirloom. ✓
- **The Beast.** The ch-02 event is a **fuel-starvation stranding in the deep Sahara** (he runs the soft
  sand dry — "the fuel was the problem… not the water"), after which the Cruiser stalls and is left as a
  **landmark/shade**, then **recovered (not stripped, engine covered) by Sidi's people**. The Beast is
  **recovered and survives** — this is a later, separate **wound-and-recovery**, distinct from any main-arc
  engine work; it is **not** a second destruction and does not contradict `characters/THE_BEAST.md`.
- **The hessian water bag** appears **exactly once** (ch-03, among the recovered kit) — satisfies the
  once-per-book rule (`canon/PROP_CONTINUITY.md`). ✓
- **The "Pacifier" is gone.** The retired portable speaker (PROP_CONTINUITY §4; the sound lives in the
  truck) no longer appears; the ch-03 line now names the weeping hessian water bag instead.

## Parts (as drafted)

- **I — The Road In (1–3):** *The Litany* — the wandering years, the Fremen romance, the wood running low.
  *Out of Road* — the breakdown, no water, the real desert vs. the paperback's. *Water Before a Question* —
  taken in by Sidi's people; milk before a single question (*ḍiyāfa*); the recitation first heard.
- **II — The Guest (4–7):** *Useful* — he fixes the pump; the desert takes him at the speed of his hands;
  the five prayers cut the day. *The Call* — the recitation as ballast he knows in his own body; the
  oral-tradition rhyme. *The Fold* — the shemag taught into the correct wrap; the three glasses; the
  one-exception (Mariem withdraws for the visiting men's circle) that costs him. *Ḥassāniyya* — the
  language and the family; the sharing-law; Mariem sends him to the school with a *lawḥ*.
- **III — The Recitation (8–13):** *The Mahadra* — the desert tent-school; learning the Book by heart
  though he cannot read. *The Ink* — the cheikh reads the tattoo, names it Dune-not-Quran, the gentle
  contested-ness owned on the page; the litany's wrong lesson exposed. *The Same Stories* — Ibrāhīm/Mūsā/
  ʿĪsā/Maryam; the "same Bible" of the series dedication paid off; the war's dead man returns. *Sabr* —
  the drought he cannot fix; the freedom-wiring lesson; he stays and does not run. *The Mercy* — the
  clean mercy-kill; *raḥma* as the first name of God (his two-sided tenderness named); "no compulsion"
  (no one will convert him). *The Night the Door Opened* — a rifle-lesson cracks the father-wound; the
  iceberg lowers one true image; Sidi holds it without fixing it.
- **IV — The Shadow & The Leaving (14–17):** *The Men at the Edge* — the militants arrive in the news
  (the faithful as their first victims; never the face of the faith); Jakobus teaches evasion, not war.
  *The Cost* — the gunmen come to the well; he governs the room with tea and bare eyes, banks the
  violence he will not use; realises his presence is the danger and he must go. *The Leaving* — the costly
  gift (the boyhood paperback to Brahim); the partings; "do not forget where there is water." *Where There
  Is Water* — the diptych closes (peace from the bush + patience from the desert = the man of Book I); he
  carries the house, never the faith; sets fear down for good.

## Themes carried (embody, never state)

Patience as the gift that *keeps* the bush's peace · the recitation as the desert's ballast · "the
stories are the same" (the cousin-faith, not the alien one) · the paperback romance (Herbert's Fremen)
corrected by the living Sahel · fear *set down*, not defeated by will (the tattoo vs. the lesson) · the
believers hold the whole, the honest outsider holds a corner and says so · mercy as the first name of God.
