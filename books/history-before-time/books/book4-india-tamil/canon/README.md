## Author's decisions needed (before deep-dive)

1. **The protagonist** — Tamil-native, of this specific world. Options:
   - A Tamil classical scholar (Sangam literature, the oldest continuous literary tradition in the
     world after Sanskrit — often overlooked because it's not North Indian) — carries the text layer
   - A female temple architect from a traditional sthapati family (the hereditary temple-builders of
     Tamil Nadu — the Agama texts as a living manual, architecture as devotion)
   - A marine archaeologist (Mahabalipuram's underwater structures; the 2004 tsunami exposed them
     briefly — real, documented, extraordinary) — carries the drowned-wonder layer
   - Author's call. **Note:** a woman protagonist here would complete a deliberate pattern with Priya
     (Book II, woman) and the Book III lead (TBD).

2. **The Tamil mythology layer** — this is the book the author specifically wanted. The sources:
   - The Murugan tradition (uniquely Tamil; Murugan is not the same as Kartikeya in the north —
     the Tamil version is older, more local, deeply embedded in the landscape)
   - The Shaivite epics (Periyapuranam, the Tevaram — the bhakti tradition, devotion as direct
     relationship with the divine, no priest required, no caste hierarchy)
   - The Ramayana as told in Tamil (Kamban's *Kamba Ramavataram* differs substantially from
     Valmiki's Sanskrit telling — Ravana is more complex; Lanka is not evil; the Tamil version is
     its own epic)
   - The *Silappathikaram* (the Tamil epic of Kannagi — a woman who burns a city with the
     heat of her chastity; one of the great feminist revenge narratives of world literature)
   - **The film tradition** (author: "I saw some awesome movies that weren't subtitled but featured
     gods and demigods and epic battles" — this IS the Tamil visual language; it crosses borders
     without translation because the emotional grammar is universal)

3. **Mahabalipuram as the anchor site:**
   - The Shore Temple (standing in the sea; the oldest stone-built temple on India's east coast)
   - The Five Rathas (five monolithic temples carved from single rocks; each in a different style)
   - The bas-reliefs (Arjuna's Penance / Descent of the Ganges — the largest bas-relief in the world)
   - The drowned city (local legend says seven pagodas once stood here; the 2004 tsunami temporarily
     revealed submerged structures; divers have mapped them; this is REAL)
   - In-world: the drowned structures are the coupling-chamber's far end — water + stone + geometry
     pointing toward Egypt (the relay's final handing-off)

4. **The Islam question** — Tamil Nadu has a significant Muslim population (Nagore, the Moors of
   the coast, the trade-route Islam of the Indian Ocean). The Book IV protagonist should encounter
   this as *natural*, not explanatory. It is part of the world, not a footnote.

5. **How the trilogy closes before Egypt:**
   - The relay handoff to Egypt must be clean enough for a new reader (Book V) but satisfying for
     the trilogy reader. The drowned-city coupling-chamber gives the physical mechanism.
   - What does the protagonist *learn* that only Tamil culture could teach? That is the handoff's gift.
