# EPIGRAPHS — *History Before Time: The Shore That Remembers* (Book IV, Tamil / Meena)

> Revelation-style: book-level epigraph + per-chapter (deferred to polish). Gita movement = **action
> without attachment** (the self that acts is not the self that grasps). Real verse + in-world makers'
> line; Tamil register; never let an invented line pose as a checkable historical quote.

## BOOK-LEVEL EPIGRAPH (front of book — LOCKED)

> **நில்லாத உலகத்து நிலைபெறும் பெருமை.**
>
> *In a world that does not stay, the greatness that stays is the deed let go of.*
>
> — after the Tamil ethical tradition *(Tirukkuṟaḷ register; rendered, not a verse-citation)*

> **कर्मण्यकर्म यः पश्येत्।**
>
> *Who sees inaction in action — that one has understood.*
>
> — Bhagavad Gita 4.18

> *The sea took the city and the city did not mind. It had already done the thing it was for. Do your
> work like a coast: fully, and then let the water have it.*
>
> — attributed to the makers *(in-world)*

**Why:** Gita 4.18 is the trilogy's closing movement (real, attributable) and Meena's exact lesson —
she acts to the utmost, in the most extreme circumstances, *without clinging;* the drowned city is the
image of the deed released. The Tamil line is **rendered in the Tirukkuṟaḷ register, not posed as a
specific couplet** (so it never fakes a citation); the makers' line is marked in-world. *(Devanagari
fallback: karmaṇy akarma yaḥ paśyet.)* **Care:** Tamil + Sanskrit traditions handled with respect; the
diaspora-meets-native payoff is Meena's, not appropriated.
