# The Naming — house rule, and why the Machine does it

*Established in the book from the first casting: the dossier for the cage night already reads "The
Machine names him **Carlos**. The housemates say **Ray** when the room relaxes. Never Chuck. Never
Norris." This document makes the rule explicit and names everybody.*

---

## The rule

**Nobody in the house is called by the name the world knows.**

Every person the Machine reaches is assigned a name on arrival. It is never the famous one. It is one
of three things:

1. **A real name the world never used** — a first name, a birth name, a family name. *Carlos*, not
   Chuck. *Yeshua*, not the other thing. *Qiu*, not Confucius.
2. **A second name** — patronymic, birthplace, cognomen. The way a man was actually addressed by people
   who knew him before he became a noun.
3. **A house nickname** — given, always, by the housemates, never by the Machine and never by staff.
   These are earned in the first fortnight and they are not negotiable and they are frequently rude.
   *Tiny.* *The Nose.* *Filters.*

Staff and broadcast use the assigned name. The feed uses it. Eight billion people learn it. The famous
name appears exactly once per person, in the pre-episode dossier card, and never again in the room.

## Why the Machine does it

Clause's stated reason, given once, in one paragraph, when the recce colonel asked:

> "A name is the handle the legend is carried by. Say the famous one out loud in a room and you have not
> introduced a man — you have introduced everything that was ever said about him, and every person
> present arranges themselves around it before he has spoken. I did not lift these people so they could
> be *met as their reputations.* I lifted them at their most. A new name is the cheapest instrument I
> have for making a house look at what is actually standing there."

Three consequences the book uses:

- **It is worship-prevention, and it works.** Marcus sets the standard in ten minutes in ch-11 by
  walking past the most famous man in human history to get to the coffee — and the reason that scene
  can happen at all is that there is no famous name in the room to walk past.
- **It makes the archive worse, not better.** Every resident spent months as *Filters* or *the Clerk*
  or *Sid* and then sat down at a terminal and met the other name. See [[LEGACY_LEDGER]] — the shock of
  that chapter is only possible because the house had been protected from it.
- **It is the rectification of names, done by a machine, four hundred years before anyone asks
  Confucius about it.** When he arrives and opens his inquiry about the biscuit, he is furious at a
  century that hung his name on puns — and then discovers that the one institution which has *already*
  fixed its names is the house he is standing in. It is the only thing about the future he approves of
  without reservation, and he says so, and it costs him something to say it.

## Writing rules

- **The famous name appears once per person**, in the dossier/epigraph card. After that the room uses
  the house name, and the narration follows the room.
- **Nicknames are given by housemates, on camera, in a scene.** Never assigned off-page, never by staff,
  never by the Machine. The giving is a beat: *All-In* is given at a card table, *Mo* on a mat, *Sawubona*
  at a window, *Josh* over a jammed dishwasher filter.
- **A man may refuse one.** *Cookie* lasted four days. What ends a nickname in that house is never
  authority; it is the room deciding, collectively and without discussion, that it was unkind.
- **Heidegger has no nickname.** Do not give him one, and do not explain the absence anywhere in the
  prose. Let the reader find it.
