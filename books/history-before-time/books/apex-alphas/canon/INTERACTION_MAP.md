# The Interaction Map — who pairs, who fights, what they fight about

*Canon working document for the second half of the book. Every beat in the brains arc is generated
here. If a scene is not on this map, it is decoration and it comes out.*

---

## The four laws of the map

1. **Every pairing is a summit meeting a summit in another grammar.** Nobody in this house is the
   smart one talking to the dumb ones. The fighters are not audience. When Bohr and Bruce recognise
   each other it is *lateral* — the same hill, the far side.
2. **Adversarial is not villainous.** Every adversary in this map is *right about something the other
   one cannot see*, and the book never resolves it by making one of them stupid. Caesar's case against
   G is a good case. Goggins' case against Gautama is a good case.
3. **A debate only earns a scene if neither side can win it alone.** If one of them is simply correct,
   it is exposition. Cut it or find the third person who makes it a real problem.
4. **The muscle is not decoration in the brains arc.** Every intellectual beat has a physical
   counterpart in the same chapter — the school, the wall, the mat, the cage. The book's whole
   argument is that it is *one hill.* Prove it structurally, not in dialogue.

| mark | meaning |
|---|---|
| **◎** | **Bond** — they find each other and it holds. Warmth, alliance, the pair the reader roots for. |

| **◐** | **Mirror** — uncanny recognition across grammars. Often wordless. The book's thesis in miniature. |

## The two spines

Everything else hangs off these. Both run the full length of the back half; neither resolves early.

### Spine I — *the fist and the question*
**Sagan · Jakobus · Jocko · Caesar · the whole muscle roster**

Sagan lands it in chapter six: this is not a predator, it is a test, and the house has spent six weeks
rehearsing a punch. That sentence is a bomb under twenty-nine people whose entire identity is *being
the answer.* The fighters do not take it well and should not — Jocko's case (a team without a mission
rots), Goggins' case (the only thing that has ever worked for me is to out-suffer it), Khabib's case
(then what am I for), Jakobus' case (they were told they were the answer, and I am the one who has to
tell them they are the room around it). **The resolution is not that the muscle was pointless — it is
that its job changed:** the fist becomes the wall around the ones who ask. Land that on the mat, not
in a speech.

### Spine II — *the switch*
**G · Caesar · Alexander · Marcus · Clause · Jakobus**

G holds the consent key to the reach; he says so on camera on his first night. Caesar sees it in four
seconds and is *correct*: one man, one key, no succession, no audit, no vote, and a species depending
on it. That is not a monster's objection. That is the best political mind in recorded history noticing
a single point of failure that happens to be a person. He will move on it — patiently, publicly, by
consensus, the way he actually did it — and every step will be reasonable, and the house will find
itself voting.

Alexander wants the key for the opposite reason (speed; the key is a brake and he has never in his
life met a brake he respected). Marcus is the only one who argues *G's* side, and he argues it from the
one place nobody can dismiss: *I had the whole of it, and the thing that kept me honest was that I
never once believed I was owed it.* Jakobus is caught: he agrees with Caesar's analysis and will defend
G's key with his body, and the book must make him say both.

**Do not resolve this by a vote.** Resolve it by G doing the one thing nobody predicted — see the
Beat Ledger, B-14.

## Triads — the three-body problems

Pairs are stable. These are not, and instability is where beats come from.

1. **Einstein · Bohr · G** — *the clock.* Two dead men and a living one, finishing the derivation nobody read. The only path to a two-way reach.
2. **Caesar · Marcus · Jesus** — *power and what it is for.* Built it / carried it / was killed by it. The last act's moral triangle.
3. **Aristotle · Alexander · Caesar** — *teaching, appetite, empire.* The teacher watching what two students of the world did with the lesson.
4. **Newton · Cayce · Sam Harris** — *the residual.* The believer-who-tests, the tested, and the honest sceptic. Nobody in this triad is a fool.
5. **Jakobus · Jocko · Caesar** — *command.* Invisible / owned / embodied. Three correct answers, one room.
6. **Goggins · Gautama · Jesus** — *suffering.* Engine / to be put down / to be carried. No winner.
7. **Sagan · Rogan · Theo** — *the window.* What eight billion frightened people are owed, and in what tone.
8. **Alexander · McGregor · Jakobus** — *the drift.* The house choosing a leader nobody elected, in real time, on camera.

## Traps — the ways this arc dies

- **Making the fighters stupid.** The single fastest way to break the book. Goggins' objection to Gautama is philosophy. Khabib's silence is an argument. Jakobus out-reads every physicist in the building on the only subject that matters — people.
- **Letting Einstein be a wise old man.** He is *twenty-six*, in a bad suit, and nobody has told him yet. Same for Newton at twenty-three: prickly, secretive, unfamous, and not yet the portrait.
- **A Buddha–Jesus debate.** See above. Two men who wrote nothing, sitting.
- **Proving psi.** The book's honest position is a residual it cannot explain, tested by the harshest protocol in the building, written by the one man who never had contempt for it.
- **Caesar as a villain.** His case against a single man holding a single key is *correct*, and the book must let the reader agree with him for at least one full chapter.
- **Worship scenes.** Every soul in this house knows what being worshipped costs. The test of the house is that it can let a man in a doorway be a man in a doorway.
- **Resolving a spine with a speech.** Both spines resolve in an *action* nobody predicted (B-14, B-22).
