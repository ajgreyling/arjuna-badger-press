## The one-line

Priya Ellis — a South-African-Indian engineer who has never set foot in the land her ancestors were
shipped from — follows the inheritance road off the edge of Africa to India, where the Order's chord
strikes its next note inside temples carved whole from living rock, and where reading an ancient
machine means reading the oldest question her ancestors ever asked: *who is the self that is doing
the looking?*

## How it connects to Book I (the relay handoff)

Book I ends with the bearing pointing **north and east, off the edge of Africa**, up the inheritance
road toward Egypt and beyond. Book II is the **India leg of that same road** — the makers' network
did not stop at one continent (Book I, ch-13/19). The gold-thread and the "knowing travelled hand to
hand" premise carry directly; the Order is the same Order; Vorster (or his reach) can shadow on.
*Standalone for a newcomer, a reward for the Book-I reader* (Series Bible order-independence rule).

## Provisional specifics (author to confirm)

- **Working title:** *History Before Time: The Temple in the Rock* (Book II). *(alt: …The Self That
  Acts · …The Carved Mountain · …What the Fire Remembers)*
- **Anchor site:** the **Kailasa temple at Ellora** — a single temple carved top-down out of one
  piece of living basalt, the largest monolithic rock-cut structure on Earth (real, astonishing,
  bucket-list-native). Plus Ajanta, Mahabalipuram; the "nuclear"/vitrified beat elsewhere.
- **Priya's roots:** South-African-Indian — confirm community/origin (indenture-era Natal Indian;
  likely Tamil or Bhojpuri/North-Indian indenture ancestry → which determines where in India her
  thread leads). The **kala pani** ("black water") history — crossing the ocean as loss of caste/
  origin — is the perfect real wound for a roots story.

## The promise to the reader

The same heart-in-throat wonder + love of place as Book I, now in India; a diaspora woman coming
home to a home she's never seen and finding it stranger and larger than she feared; and a closing urge
to **go and stand in a temple carved whole from a mountain.** (Bucket-list back matter — the mission.)
