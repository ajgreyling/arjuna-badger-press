# STORY_BIBLE — *History Before Time*, Book II (India)

> Under the [Series Bible](../../../canon/SERIES_BIBLE.md) and consistent with
> [Book I](../../book1-africa/canon/STORY_BIBLE.md). Book-II-specific where they differ.

## Premise

**Priya Ellis** — a South-African-Indian engineer with a neurodiverse gift for reading machines (the
lead carried from the *African Gold* trilogy) — is brought into the Order's quest as it follows the
inheritance road off the edge of Africa to **India**. The makers' chord struck across Africa has a
next note here, written into **temples carved whole from living rock** and bound up with the oldest
Indian questions about the self (**Atman/Brahman**, the **Bhagavad Gita**). Priya can read the
machine — but reading it means reading *herself*, and she is a woman who has never been sure where
she's from: too Indian for South Africa, too foreign for India. The roots she came to find turn out
to be older, stranger, and more shared than any flag or caste or ocean-crossing can hold.

## Theme

**Who is the self that acts?** — the Gita's question, made literal by a machine that only answers to a
mind that knows what it is. And beneath the adventure, the same series spine: *the deep past belongs to
everyone* — here, the homecoming of a diaspora woman who learns that "where you're from" is bigger than
a country. (Carry as theme, never preach.)

## Tone & mood

Same as Book I: cinematic, propulsive, sensory; **wonder, not conspiracy**; awe and invitation. India
adds heat, colour, crowd, monsoon, the overwhelming sensory wall of it — and the particular vertigo of
a diaspora homecoming (recognition and alienation at once). The philosophical layer (Atman/Brahman) is
**dramatized, never lectured** — felt in Priya's gut as she reads the machine, not delivered as a
seminar.

## Target reader

Same as Book I (loves history + fiction, wants wonder, not the conspiracy rabbit hole), plus the
*African Gold* reader who will be delighted to find Priya again. Comparable shelf: Dan Brown / James
Rollins / Ashwin Sanghi (Indian mytho-thriller) crossed with *Ancient Aliens* / the rock-cut-temple
wonder and a *National Geographic* eye.

## POV & form

**Single close third, Priya only.** Short propulsive chapters; the relay structure. Her engineer's
pattern-cognition colours the narration (she reads load, tolerance, mechanism — and, increasingly,
herself).

## Content bounds

Adult adventure; nothing gratuitous. **Hindu sacred sites, texts, and living tradition treated with
deep respect** (never set-dressing, never exoticised) — this is a binding sensitivity line, doubly so
because the lead is reclaiming this heritage. The "ancient nuclear" / vimana material is celebrated
in-world but always **earned through a witnessable artifact** (MYTHOS_RULES). Caste, indenture, and
the kala-pani history handled with care, as undercurrent.

## Promise to the reader

A diaspora woman's heart-in-throat homecoming; the wonder of temples carved whole from mountains; the
oldest question about the self made into a key; and the urge to go and stand in the rock. Standalone,
and a reward for the reader of Book I and the *African Gold* trilogy.
