## The shape of this world

The same Earth as Book I: deep antiquity was advanced; the makers left infrastructure; the knowing was
*inherited* up a road that ran out of Africa and across the world. India is one of the great
**inheritor lands** — where the makers' legacy was remembered not just in stone but in **philosophy
and text**, woven so deeply into living religion that it hides in plain sight as scripture and myth.
Nothing on the surface is fantastical; the extraordinary is carved into cliffs, drowned, or encoded in
verses everyone quotes and no one reads as a *manual*.

### Node settings (grounded; real, visitable)

- **Jaipur / Jaigarh Fort (Rajasthan).** The Rajasthan node. Jaigarh Fort sits on the Aravalli
  ridge above Amber Fort — the fort of the Kachhwaha rulers, built 1726. Its anchor is the
  **Jaiwana cannon**: the largest wheeled cannon on its original carriage in the world. Cast in
  1720 from a single pour of bronze, it required **four elephants to traverse** on its platform.
  It was test-fired *once* — across the valley toward the Sambhar Lake salt flats — and the
  shockwave of its single discharge **killed everything living within roughly 100 metres of the
  cannon itself**, emptied the fort's water cisterns from vibration, and the ball is believed to
  have travelled approximately 35km. It has never been fired again. *In-world:* Priya's engineer
  mind recognises what the historians miss — the cannon was never a weapon of war. The tolerances,
  the pour, the single shot: it was a calibration instrument. The Jaiwana was built to measure
  something. The question is what. This is the node's first key. **Canon note (BINDING):** the
  Jaiwana is at **Jaigarh Fort, Jaipur** — NOT the Red Fort in Delhi (a common confusion; the
  Red Fort is Mughal/Delhi; Jaigarh is Rajput/Jaipur). Never write the cannon into the wrong
  fort. The step wells (*baolis*) — Chand Baori at Abhaneri is the most astonishing, 3,500 steps
  descending 30 metres in perfect geometric symmetry — are a day's drive from Jaipur. They are
  infrastructure, not ornament: the geometry creates a microclimate 5-6°C cooler at the base.
  Priya reads them as an engineer reads a machine — which is what they are. This node also
  establishes the **Mughal-Muslim layer** of Rajasthan honestly: the pink city of Jaipur was
  built *in dialogue* with the Mughal empire; the Amber Fort's mirrored halls are Hindu and
  Mughal aesthetics in the same room. Islam is not foreign to Rajasthan. It is one of its layers.

- **Ellora — Kailasa Temple (Maharashtra).** The anchor. A single temple **carved top-down out of one
  monolith of basalt** — they removed ~200,000 tonnes of rock to *reveal* a multi-storey temple
  complete with pillars, sculptures, and chambers, working from the top of the cliff down. Real, and
  one of the most staggering feats of stone-working on Earth. *In-world:* a structure whose geometry
  and acoustics are part of the makers' instrument — and which could only be carved by someone who
  could already "see" the finished form inside the rock (a resonance with Priya's pattern-cognition,
  and with Jakobus's spatial mind from Book I).
- **Ajanta (Maharashtra).** Rock-cut Buddhist caves, the painted older layer; what the rock holds and
  remembers; a quieter node.
- **The vitrified site (Rajasthan / Indus-adjacent, invented-but-plausible).** The "ancient nuclear"
  beat: ground fused to glass, anomalous radiation readings, and the *Mahabharata*'s descriptions
  (the Brahmastra; corpses burned, hair and nails falling out — texts that read, unsettlingly, like
  radiation sickness). *Grounding rule applies hard here:* show the vitrification and the readings;
  let the wonder land; never assert, designate the deepest cause a mystery.
- **Mahabalipuram (Tamil Nadu).** The shore temples; the 2004 tsunami genuinely briefly exposed
  submerged structures offshore; *in-world* the coupling-chamber idea (water + stone + gold geometry)
  that ties forward to Egypt's drowned temples (Book I's bearing).

## The brain-layer — Atman/Brahman & the Gita as the philosophical key (binding)

India's makers-legacy is **bound up with consciousness.** The in-world conceit: the machines here only
respond to an operator who genuinely apprehends the oldest Indian insight — **Atman** (the true self)
is one with **Brahman** (the ground of all being); the self that *thinks it acts* is not the self that
*is*. The **Bhagavad Gita** — Krishna counselling Arjuna on the eve of battle: *act without attachment
to the fruits of action; the Self is never slain* — reads, in-world, as partly a **manual** for the
maker's instrument: a discipline of mind required to operate it safely. **Dramatize this through
Priya's experience** of reading the machine (it answers her pattern-cognition, but demands she let go
of the grasping, anxious self she's built — the engineer who controls) — **never** as a lecture or a
guru's monologue. Ties to the *African Gold* trilogy's consciousness-substrate theme.

## Sensory texture

The wall of India — heat, monsoon, horn and crowd and incense and diesel and marigold; the cool dark
of carved caves; the vertigo of a diaspora homecoming (faces that look like family among strangers;
a mother-tongue she half-remembers; food that tastes like her grandmother's and is somehow not). The
rock as character: living basalt, carved, cool, immense.

## Constraints the plot must respect

- **Real geography, honest distance/travel.** Sites where they really are.
- **Deep respect for living Hindu (and Buddhist/Jain) tradition** — never exoticised, never
  set-dressing; the sacred is sacred in-world. Binding, and personal to the lead.
- **Wonder, not conspiracy.** Resistance is human/fallible; the antagonist is greed or ideology, not
  an omnipotent cabal. Avoid the real-world trap of weaponising "ancient India" for nationalist
  pseudo-history — the book can *feature* that temptation (as an antagonist's sin) while refusing it.
- **The grounding rule.** No marvel without a witnessable artifact (especially the "nuclear" beat).
- **Caste & indenture handled with care** — undercurrent, not spectacle; the kala-pani history is
  Priya's, treated with the same dignity Book I gave the dop-stelsel.
