## Priya Ellis — protagonist, sole POV (carried from *African Gold*)

- **Who:** a South-African-Indian engineer, sharp, dry-witted, fiercely independent; **neurodiverse
  (autistic/ADHD) pattern-cognition** that lets her read ancient machines the way no one else can (her
  defining gift across the trilogy). Appreciates men, relies on none. *(Canonically the *African Gold*
  lead — see that project's bible for her full voice; keep her consistent.)*
- **Why she's here (the roots story — locked):** Priya has spent her whole life as "the Indian one" in
  South Africa — Natal-Indian, descended from indentured labourers shipped across the **kala pani**
  (the "black water") generations ago — and she has **never been to India.** The Order's road leads
  there, and she goes telling herself it's the machine, the work, the mission. It is not only the
  machine. It is the oldest ache she carries: *where am I actually from?*
- **The wound (binding):** **belonging / origin.** Too Indian for South Africa (a century of being
  "not really" South African); braced to be **too foreign for India** (the diaspora's terror —
  returning "home" to be seen as a tourist, an ABCD, a stranger with the right face and the wrong
  everything-else). She fears the answer is *nowhere.* The kala-pani history is the real, specific
  root of it: her ancestors literally **lost their origin by crossing the ocean** — caste, village,
  name, dissolved in the crossing. She is the descendant of erasure-by-water. *(Mirror of Jennefer
  from the other side: Jennefer was "from everybody"; Priya was "from a place that no longer claims
  her.")*
- **The want:** to find where she's *from* — and, beneath it (the arc), to stop needing a *place* to
  tell her who she is. The Gita's lesson lands on exactly her wound: the Self is not the labels, the
  village, the passport, the ocean crossed; *the Self is never slain, never displaced.* She came to
  find her roots and finds something deeper than roots.
- **How her gift meets the brain-layer (the engine of the book):** Priya reads machines by *letting a
  pattern resolve* — she goes quiet, lets the grasping analytical self drop away, and the structure
  reveals itself. That is, almost exactly, the **discipline the Gita describes** (action without the
  anxious ego clutching at outcomes). The makers' instrument here only answers a mind that can do this
  — so Priya's neurodivergence, the thing the world calls a disorder, turns out to be a *native
  fluency* in the oldest Indian insight about the self. The machine sees her clearly. So, eventually,
  does she.
- **Voice/texture:** dry, precise, allergic to mysticism (she's an engineer; she'll resist the
  Atman/Brahman framing exactly as Jennefer resisted the "woo," and be converted the same way — by the
  artifact, by *experience,* not by being told). Funny. Independent to a fault. Carries the trilogy's
  Priya forward; don't soften her.

## The Order — the series throughline (the India members)

The same Order as Book I — keepers and protectors, not a cabal. In India its keepers are rooted in the
guardianship of the temples and the texts. Cast:

- **The Indian keeper** *(provisional — e.g. a temple scholar / a custodian of an oral lineage)* — the
  one who meets Priya, who can read the texts-as-manual, who carries the Gita-as-key knowledge.
  Consider a woman scholar, or an elder priest-historian; handle with deep respect (a real living
  tradition). Possible mentor in the Ma-Tshabalala role.
- **Priya's field companion through India — a LOCAL (binding choice):** the person who travels the
  relay with Priya is **Indian**, the expert of this ground — knows the languages, the back-routes,
  the regions, the temples. *This is a deliberate pillar decision (see below): an Indian reads India.*
  Cast a fresh foil with a different flavour from Jakobus (Jakobus reads land/people as a covert
  operator; this companion might be, e.g., a sharp local fixer-scholar, a guide who grew up at the
  sites, a younger keeper). The complement to Priya is the same engine that worked in Book I — she
  reads *machines*, they read *this place* — but the cultural valence is corrected.
- **Jakobus Swart — CAMEO / BOOKENDS ONLY (LOCKED — see SERIES_BIBLE):** Jakobus is the series'
  recurring fixer, but in the **India trilogy he bookends, he does not guide.** He gets Priya *into*
  India — the relay handoff from Africa, a strong early scene (and his promised road to Egypt runs
  through here) — and he reappears at the **handoff out** (toward Egypt) and possibly the climax. He
  is the **thread, not the navigator.** His tells still apply when he's on the page (the eyes, the
  uniform rule at the border crossing in, *Sawubona*, sibling-love with Priya — she clocks it
  instantly and finds it sweet). **Why bookends, not co-lead (binding rationale):** three books in a
  row of a white South African man as the indispensable competent guide to a woman of colour — *in her
  own ancestral homeland, no less* — would read as exactly the pattern the series' anti-poison pillar
  (§2) exists to refuse, and would re-trigger the "frictionless-saint" note the Book I craft audit
  flagged. So India is carried by Priya + the Indian companion; Jakobus is the connective tissue at
  the seams. Keep him load-bearing for no newcomer.

## The Antagonist — same family as Vorster, India-flavoured (provisional)

- **Who (options to pick from):**
  1. **Vorster's reach extends up the road** — he warned he'd be there "when it's all in one place";
     the inheritance leads to India, so does he (continuity; the patient money).
  2. **A heritage-extractive interest** — someone who'd loot/own the temple artefacts (mirror of
     Vorster, new face).
  3. **An ideologue** — someone who would *weaponise* the ancient past for a nationalist/sectarian
     myth (the real-world danger of "ancient India" pseudo-history), wanting the makers' legacy as
     *proof* of a supremacist story. **This is the richest, most India-specific, most dangerous
     option** — and it lets the book dramatize, and refuse, the exact poison the series exists against
     (the pillar): the past twisted to say *our* people were the only great ones, vs. the truth that
     the deep past belongs to everyone.
- **Tone guardrail (binding):** keep them human and (if the collector) almost-sympathetic, or (if the
  ideologue) frighteningly sincere — never a cartoon. And handle the nationalist-pseudo-history angle
  with care: the target is the *ideology that weaponises the past*, never a faith or a people.
