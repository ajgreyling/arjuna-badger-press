## Coherence flags

1. **PROTAGONIST-NAME DRIFT (canon vs prose) — THE BIG ONE.** Canon CHARACTERS.md names the protagonist/POV **"Dr. Eleanor 'Nell' Kavanagh"** (an Irish-Australian hydrogeologist woman) and the co-lead Ranger **"Daniel Yindjibarndi-Marra."** The PROSE uses neither for those roles: the protagonist is a **male geologist named "Daniel Mercer,"** and the Ranger co-lead is **"Eli."** So canon's co-lead name *"Daniel"* has been reassigned to the white-male protagonist, and the canon protagonist *"Nell Kavanagh"* appears **nowhere in the prose** (0 occurrences across all 18 chapters). This is not a minor rename — it is a wholesale gender + character swap: canon's female lead "Nell" → prose's male lead "Daniel"; canon's Ranger "Daniel" → prose's Ranger "Eli." Anyone using CHARACTERS.md as the binding bible will be working from the wrong protagonist name and gender entirely. **Recommend: reconcile CHARACTERS.md to the prose (or vice versa) before any further drafting.** Note the prose Daniel is explicitly NOT the canon "secret Aboriginal ancestry" character — ch-18 hard-codes "She wasn't secretly one of you... a white woman from Kalgoorlie"; the guardrail is honoured but the POV figure's identity is fully changed.

2. **Antagonist name (canon vs prose).** Canon antagonist is **"Marcus Hale / Carrow Resources."** Prose names him **"Vincent Roe"** (the company "Carrow Resources" is retained). Pure rename; consistent within the prose (ch-02, ch-12, ch-14, ch-15, ch-17).

3. **Two "Vince/Vincent" characters.** Prose has both **Vincent Roe** (Perth executive/antagonist) and **Vince Tolliver** (on-site project manager). They are distinct people and never conflated in the prose, but the shared first name is a latent confusion risk worth noting.

4. **Nyaani's age: "seventy-odd" vs "eighty-year-old."** ch-04/ch-05 call her "the old woman, seventy-odd" and "a woman of seventy-odd." ch-11 has Daniel say "Eighty-year-old woman who knows where the water is." Minor in-prose drift of ~a decade. Likely loose/rounded speech rather than a true contradiction, but flag for tightening.

5. **Nyaani / ch-01 vs ch-03 — who has the marked hand and finds the water.** ch-01 (the framing prologue) gives the marked left hand AND the "dig here" water-finding to **Nyaani** directly. ch-03 first introduces the marked-hand woman as an older woman among the mob and Nyaani is confirmed as her in ch-04–ch-05. This is internally consistent (the marked-hand woman IS Nyaani), but ch-01 compresses the timeline — worth a sanity check that the prologue's Nyaani and the body's Nyaani are unambiguously the same person (they are: same left-hand mark, same "dig here," same role).

6. **Waraki gender presentation drifts within the deep thread (in-prose).** ch-06 keeps Waraki deliberately gender-neutral (no pronouns; "the grandchild"). ch-09 makes Waraki explicitly female ("a girl," "she/her" throughout). ch-13 returns to no-pronoun/neutral handling, and ch-16 reframes Waraki as a *carried name/role* ("They were all called Waraki... handed down the line of throats"), with the viewpoint now an old woman. This is plausibly intentional (the name as a relay across generations), but a reader tracking a single "Waraki" will hit a bump between ch-06's neutrality and ch-09's explicit "she," and again at ch-16's "they were all called Waraki." Flag for an authorial decision on whether ch-09's gendering is meant to stick.

7. **"Milk-pale eyes" belongs to the grandmother, then to old Waraki — not to Nyaani.** Worth noting only to prevent a future mix-up: the milk-pale-at-the-edges eyes are a deep-thread elder trait (ch-13 grandmother; ch-16 old Waraki). Nyaani's eyes are "flat black" (ch-08, ch-14). Do not let the deep-thread eye description bleed onto Nyaani.

8. **Hair is UNCITED for every named character.** No chapter describes hair length, colour, or style for Daniel, Nyaani, Eli, or Waraki. This is a gap, not a contradiction — but it means any future hair detail has no anchor and cannot drift "wrong," yet also has nothing to stay consistent with. Flag as an open description hole if hair is ever introduced.
