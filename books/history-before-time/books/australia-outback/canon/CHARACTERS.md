# CHARACTERS — *The Songlines of Stone*

> Names marked *(provisional)* are the author's call. **POV rule (binding):** *primary* thread is **single
> close third, Nell only** — every present-day character is seen from outside, through her. The *deep
> (Saltwater) thread* is close third on **one ancestral viewpoint** (Waraki, below), a deliberate signalled
> register-shift. We are never in any other head.
> **Care-line note (binding, applies to every Aboriginal character):** these are *fictional, composited*
> people, written **on cultural advice** (see back matter). They are individuals, not symbols or
> spokespeople; the custodians **hold all authority over sacred content** (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 2), and the
> narration **never enters restricted knowledge** — Nell (and the reader) stay outside the threshold.

---

## Dr. Eleanor "Nell" Kavanagh — protagonist, primary POV

> The full character. POV rule: **single close third, Nell only** in the present-day thread. Her
> shame/belonging arc runs as a **strong undercurrent, never a lecture** — felt often, stated rarely.

- **Who:** an **Irish-Australian hydrogeologist**, late 30s, raised in **Kalgoorlie** goldfields country.
  Brilliant, field-hardened, dry as the country she works. The best groundwater reader in the contracting
  game: *she has never once been wrong about water* — and that pride is both her gift and the wall around
  her. Credentialled, then **professionally radioactive** (below). Allergic to anything she can't measure.
- **The name & the inheritance (LOCKED):** **Eleanor**, after a grandmother she never met; she's been
  **Nell** since school, a harder, shorter name she chose herself — *the self she built away from where she
  came from.* The load-bearing inheritance is a **gesture, not a name:** her late mother's habit of laying a
  **flat palm on the ground** and going still before she'd say where the water was — a thing young Nell found
  embarrassing, unscientific, *shaming* — which Nell catches herself **doing** on the Pilbara, hand to red
  rock, before she knows she's doing it. The body remembers before the mind admits. (Pays off late; never
  explained in a speech.)
- **The mother she fled (LOCKED — handle with maximum restraint):** Nell's mother was a **goldfields woman
  who *listened to country*** — read a tree-line for water, talked to the land, kept ways young Nell was
  taught (by a hard, assimilationist father, or by the town's shame) to never mention and to be embarrassed
  by. **Whether there is quiet Aboriginal ancestry in that line is left deliberately unresolved and, if
  touched at all, only on cultural advice** (see guardrail). What matters is **felt, not claimed:** Nell ran
  from her mother's way of being on country toward instruments and proof, and the book is her coming back to
  it — **as a humbled outsider, not as a claim of identity.**
  - **GUARDRAIL (binding — the most important line in this file after the care line):** Nell's arc is **NOT**
    a "white woman discovers she's secretly Aboriginal and is redeemed" story — that is appropriation and is
    **forbidden.** The honourable, default reading is that **she is and remains an outsider who is taught**,
    and whose homecoming is to **humility and right relationship** with country and people, never to a
    claimed Aboriginal identity. Identity belongs to the custodians, who hold it; Nell earns *trust* and
    *belonging-as-a-guest*, not membership. If the ancestry thread is used at all, it is **understated,
    unresolved, and vetted by a sensitivity reader** — and it still does **not** make her a custodian or
    give her any claim to sacred knowledge. (See THEMES; MYTHOS_RULES Rule 2 and Rule 5.)
- **The wound (binding):** she warned — in writing, repeatedly — that a **tailings dam** would fail; she was
  **overruled** by the company and the regulator, the dam failed, country and a creek-system were poisoned,
  and the company made **her** the public face of the failure to protect itself. Her name is now a
  cautionary tale; she's broke, divorced-or-estranged, and **secretly terrified that being right doesn't
  matter — that the people who own the ground will always win, so why keep reading it true.** *Deepened by
  origin:* the disgrace rhymes with the older shame — a lifetime of being told the thing she came from (her
  mother's way, the goldfields, the not-quite-belonging) was something to bury. Being disbelieved and made
  to carry someone else's harm **isn't new to her**; it's the story of her mother, aimed at her career.
- **The want (binding):** at first, **vindication and money** — one clean contract, proof she's still the
  best, a way back. Arc: that want is **replaced**, not satisfied — she discovers a thing worth more than
  being right *for herself* (the country, and the people who belong to it), and the courage to spend her
  hard-won credibility *protecting* it instead of selling it. Beneath that: from *"belong to nothing, prove
  everything"* to *"learn to stand rightly on country as a guest who can be trusted."*
- **The arc in one line:** **from measuring to listening** — the woman who reads water by instrument learns
  to read country by listening to the people who have read it for 65,000 years, and the cost of that
  conversion is that she can no longer help destroy it.
- **Voice/texture:** dry, profane, goldfields-blunt; deflects feeling with technical talk; impatient with
  woo and with herself. The hard empiricist whose **conversion to wonder earns the reader's** — she fights
  every artifact, demands the data, and is *converted by the data* (the sonar return, the solstice line, the
  date). Competent, not flawless; the disgrace left real scars (money, trust, a marriage, a drink she
  watches too closely — a goldfields inheritance she keeps a wary eye on, never a melodrama).
- **Why her:** an outsider is the right primary POV for *this* book precisely so the **custodians can be the
  experts and the source** (anti-poison pillar in structural form) — the wonder comes from Aboriginal people
  *teaching*, witnessed by a woman humble enough, eventually, to learn. And a hydrogeologist makes the
  wonder **checkable**: water is measurable, sea level is measurable, the convergence of her instruments and
  the songline is the book's grounded miracle.

> **To confirm (author's call):** how far (if at all) to take the unresolved-ancestry thread (default:
> barely, and only on cultural advice — she stays a taught outsider); whether the watched-drink beat is used
> or cut (default: a light, controlled undercurrent, never misery-spectacle); her exact sub-discipline.

## The custodians (seen through Nell — the experts, the source, the authorities)

> **Binding (the anti-poison pillar in character form):** these are the **most important people in the book
> after Nell** — never set-dressing, never noble-savage romanticism (the deliberate correction of Chatwin's
> outsider gaze). They are working professionals and law-people with *current* authority. They decide what
> Nell may see, hear, record, or testify to; **the narration honours every threshold and never enters
> restricted knowledge** (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 2). Written **on cultural advice** (back matter).

### Daniel Yindjibarndi-Marra *(provisional name — to be set on cultural advice)* — co-lead

- **Who:** a **Ranger and emerging law-man**, mid-30s, of the country the lease sits on. Tertiary-trained in
  **land management / environmental science** *and* carrying the **law of his grandfather** — the living
  proof that the oldest knowledge and modern science are **one continuous expertise**, not opposites
  (the spine theme, in a person). Quietly funny, watchful, slow to trust an outsider sent by a mining
  company — with good reason. Drives a beaten Ranger ute, fluent in the native-title and heritage
  bureaucracy as a weapon, fluent in country as a birthright.
- **Function:** Nell's guide into right relationship with country; the man who **tests her** (the soakage
  walk) and slowly opens doors as she earns them; the bridge between the elders' authority and the legal
  fight; the one who can say *"that part isn't for you"* and *"this part you need to see, and to testify
  to."* **The friction-then-trust co-lead** — where Nell measures, he *belongs*; where she has instruments,
  he has law and country; each corrects the other. **Not a romance** (see relationship rule).
- **His wound (kept honest, not idealised):** he has watched country be blasted before, watched a previous
  heritage process get **box-ticked and overridden**, watched elders' words ignored in rooms full of
  lawyers — and he carries a cold, controlled fury and a fear that **doing it the right way (patient, lawful,
  by protocol) loses to the people who own the ground.** That fear is the exact mirror of Nell's. His
  temptation (a real dark beat, used carefully): to **stop trusting the slow lawful way** and do something
  that would cost him his standing. The arc lets Nell's stubborn empiricism and his patient law each hold
  the other to the harder, better path.
- **Voice/texture:** understatement, long silences that aren't empty, dry deflecting humour very like Nell's
  (they recognise each other), code-switching between Ranger-report English, family talk, and Language
  (Language **referenced and respected, never transcribed for spectacle**). Authority worn lightly until it
  isn't.

### Marrkura "Nana May" Yindjibarndi *(provisional)* — senior elder & knowledge-holder

- **Who:** Daniel's grandmother; a **senior woman**, a custodian of country and of women's law, in her 70s
  or 80s. The book's deepest authority and its still centre. She has buried more than Nell can imagine and
  fought museums and mining companies and governments and is **not impressed** by a hydrogeologist with a
  drill rig. She is also, when Nell finally stops talking and starts listening, **kind** — and devastatingly
  funny.
- **Function:** the one who **walks Nell to water no instrument found** (the conversion node), reads the
  country aloud (the *sharable* layer — slope, sign, season — **never** sacred content), holds the songline
  in living memory (we hear it **once, at distance, untranscribed**, and that restraint is the beat), and
  whose authority decides the climax. **The keeper/custodian figure** in the house's lineage — but rooted in
  *real* Aboriginal authority, handled with dignity, never a mystical oracle.
- **The care line, embodied:** Nana May is the character who most often **draws the line** — *"wait here,"
  "no photographs," "this is women's country," "that's not for telling"* — and Nell learning to honour those
  lines **without resentment or curiosity-as-entitlement** is one of the truest measures of her arc. The
  withheld knowledge is felt as **weight and presence**, never as a puzzle the reader is invited to crack.
- **Voice/texture:** spare, exact, often silent; says more by what she won't say; a humour that ambushes.
  When she finally trusts Nell with *something* (never sacred — a *story about the country*, a memory of the
  mother-line, a name), it lands as **earned grace.**

### Aunty / Uncle and the wider mob *(supporting custodians)*

- The native-title group, other Rangers, aunties and uncles, young people split between staying on country
  and leaving for work — rendered as a **living, present-tense community** (phones, footy, the native-title
  office, a funeral, a fight about the mining royalties that *divides* the community honestly — not everyone
  agrees; some want the jobs). **Never a monolith.** This honest internal disagreement (jobs-and-royalties
  vs. country-and-law) is one of the book's most important truths and a source of real, non-villainous
  conflict. They are also, not coincidentally, the people the **tourism-dignity** mission directs respect
  and money toward.

## The Saltwater deep thread — the ancestral viewpoint (seen close-third on one person)

> The deep (~12,000–10,000 ya) interludes follow **one viewpoint**, on the **same country** when it was a
> coastal plain by a sea now drowned. **Care line (binding):** depicts survival, weather, water, kinship,
> and **the making of memory into song** — the universal, sharable layer. **Never** ceremony, law, or sacred
> content; the thread shows knowledge being *created and entrusted* and **looks away at the threshold**,
> exactly as the present thread does. These ancestors are **not** "the same people, primitively"; they are
> brilliant, fully modern humans solving the hardest problem there is — a homeland going underwater within a
> lifetime — by **inventing the memory that will outlast it.**

- **Waraki *(provisional)* — the deep-thread viewpoint.** A young adult of the coastal family — a **water-
  and-country reader in training**, the one whose job becomes carrying the names of the lost freshwater
  places forward. We watch, through Waraki, the sea come up over a childhood's worth of known ground; the
  family's grief and decision; the **making of the song** that fixes the drowned spring and the lost plain
  into memory so the children will always know where the freshwater *was.* Waraki's flat-palm-to-the-ground
  gesture is the **deep-time origin of Nell's mother's gesture** — the same motion across 400 generations,
  the braid's quietest, most devastating rhyme (never spelled out).
- **The elder of the family** *(provisional)* — the one who *decides* they must move inland and *insists* the
  knowledge be sung, not just remembered; the deep-time ancestor of Nana May's authority.
- **Function of the thread:** the **emotional proof** of the sea-level fact the present thread *measures.*
  When Nell's sonar pings the drowned spring exactly where the song says, the reader has **already stood on
  that spring with Waraki and watched it go under** — story and science meeting across deep time. That is the
  book's whole thesis, dramatised and never declared.

## The Antagonist — the one who would empty the country

> **The mission's mirror-image:** extraction-and-ownership vs. custodianship-and-listening. Concrete, human,
> clean adventure-thriller villainy — **NOT** a shadowy cabal (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 4), and given a real,
> almost-sympathetic want so he's more than a sneer.

### Marcus Hале / "Carrow Resources" *(provisional name)* — the human face of extraction

- **Who:** a **mining executive** — likely the regional MD or a charismatic projects-VP of **Carrow
  Resources**, the iron-ore company on whose lease the rock-art country sits. Self-made, **persuasive,
  genuinely believes he is creating value, jobs, and a future** from "empty," "idle" country — and that the
  heritage process is sentimental obstruction standing between hard-working people and prosperity. He is not
  a cartoon: he funds the local footy club, employs Aboriginal workers, points to the royalties, and **half-
  believes his own decency.** That half-belief is what makes him dangerous and almost-sympathetic.
- **The want (almost-sympathetic):** not destruction for its own sake — **value creation, legacy, and being
  right.** He wants the ore, the jobs, the share price, the ribbon-cutting; he has convinced himself the
  country is "just rocks" and that protecting it is luxury sentiment. His tragedy is a **failure of
  listening** — the exact inverse of Nell's arc; he is who Nell *was*, scaled up and unrepentant.
- **The function:** the **blasting clock.** He hired Nell to produce a clean survey; her conversion turns his
  asset into his obstacle. He fights with **legal, financial, and PR weapons** (a Section-stripped heritage
  approval, a smear of Nell's already-ruined credibility, an attempt to split the community with royalty
  money), not with thugs — and that *human, legible, balance-sheet* villainy is exactly what keeps the book
  on the wonder side of the line. He may be allowed **one genuine moment of doubt** late (he *sees* the
  drowned-spring proof and, for a breath, understands what he's about to silence) — which he overrides. That
  override, not a gunfight, is his true villainy.
- **Tone guardrail (binding):** keep him a **greedy, self-justifying, fallible human**, never an omnipotent
  conspiracy. The harm that has *already* happened to country (the real, mournable destruction of rock art
  for industry) is shown as the **accumulated choices of people like him**, reversible only going forward —
  not the work of a shadow hand.

### Supporting on the extraction side

- **The compliance/heritage officer** who taught Nell to "tick the box" — a mid-level functionary who is
  *not* evil, just frightened of his mortgage; a small mirror of how erasure actually happens (banality, not
  malice).
- **The regulator** — the same human-and-fallible institution that overruled Nell's tailings-dam warning;
  the system that fails country by procedure, not plot. (Wonder-not-conspiracy, in an institution.)

## Supporting (real-world-rooted)

- **A figure from Nell's old life** — a former colleague or the regulator who witnessed her tailings-dam
  ruin; carries the personal stakes of vindication (someone whose belief she once wanted back — and whose
  approval she learns she no longer needs).
- **An archaeologist / dating specialist** *(optional co-investigator)* — provides the OSL/radiocarbon and
  rock-art-dating artifacts **honestly and on the custodians' terms** (works *with* the mob, not on them);
  a check that the science is rendered correctly, and a foil who, unlike Nell at the start, already knows to
  defer to custodial authority.
- **The country itself** — listed here deliberately: per the register, the **land is the largest supporting
  character** (the "Wilbur Smith of Australia" mission), rendered as living, exact, and an invitation —
  except at the sacred thresholds, where it is a **withheld weight** the reader feels and respects.

## Nell & Daniel — the relationship rule (LOCKED — binding)

**It is respect-and-alliance, edging toward friendship — not a romance** (default), and **never** a "white
woman saved/completed by an Aboriginal man" arc (which would be both cliché and an offence to the anti-poison
pillar). There may be a flicker of the ordinary attraction that arises between two clever people who come to
trust each other under pressure; if so it is **acknowledged, human, and let pass** — because the **point of
their bond is that he teaches her nothing for free and she earns trust, not affection-as-reward.** Their
"I see you" is **earned mutual respect across a hard gap**, not foreplay. The richer, truer ending is **two
professionals who became allies and then friends**, the outsider finally trusted to stand on country.
- **Drafting guard:** if a charged beat starts to read as romance-about-to-be-acted-on, redirect to the
  respect/alliance register (a dry joke, a shared silence over the data, a protective protocol-call). The
  relationship's job is to dramatise *outsider-earns-trust* and the anti-poison pillar — never to resolve
  Nell's arc through a man. **No saviour in either direction.**
