# MYTHOS_RULES — *The Songlines of Stone*

> The binding "physics" of the wonder for this standalone. Adapted from the *History Before Time* mythos
> gate, but **re-pointed for Australia**: here the "wonder" is **not** a fringe lost-civilisation claim — it
> is the **documented, astonishing, true** reality of the oldest continuous living culture on Earth, and the
> sophistication of its knowledge of country. The gate keeps that wonder **grounded, never woo**, and keeps
> the book on the **respect** side of every line. These are the few hard lines; texture is otherwise free.
> **If a scene breaks one of these rules, it is a defect — fix it.**

---

## Rule 1 — The artifact LEADS, the claim FOLLOWS (binding — the grounding lock)

The reader earns the wonder by first earning the **checkable physical thing.** Order is binding: the prose
shows the *physical, measurable* object or reading **first**, and only then lets the awe land. **No hand-
waving, no magic, no telepathy-as-explanation, no "the land just told her."** Nell is a hydrogeologist; her
empiricism is the reader's proxy — she demands the data, and so does the narration. The checkable thing is
one of (all real categories, used honestly):

- **Hydrology that reads as engineering, not accident** — the Budj-Bim-class eel-trap channels and weirs; a
  soakage or rock-hole (gnamma) network sited with a precision no chance could produce; water reached by
  reading cracks, slope, and vegetation that instruments missed.
- **Sea-level memory that *checks out*** — a song/story that names a freshwater spring, an island, a
  hunting plain now *under the sea*; cross-checked against bathymetry and post-glacial sea-level curves and
  found to sit exactly where the feature was when the coast was 20–40 m lower (the Nunn–Reid result,
  dramatised: oral tradition as **datable observation**, 7,000–13,000 years old).
- **Astronomical / solar alignment in arranged stone** — a stone arrangement (Wurdi-Youang-class) whose
  sightlines mark solstice and equinox sunset to within a degree; the seasonal sky-calendar (the Emu in the
  Sky in the dark dust-lanes of the Milky Way) predicting the country's behaviour and proven right.
- **Rock art with anomalous, datable antiquity** — petroglyphs of **extinct megafauna / thylacine**, or a
  human-face engraving, in a gallery whose mineral crust, superimposition sequence, and depicted species
  push the dates into the tens of thousands of years (the Murujuga reality).
- **Lithics and burial of impossible age** — worked stone and ochre-burial / cremation evidence dated by
  real method (OSL, radiocarbon on associated material) to **40,000–65,000 years** (the Mungo / Madjedbebe
  reality).

Show the data. Let the awe do the work. (The Crichton move, pointed at the *true* deep antiquity of
Aboriginal Australia.)

## Rule 2 — The sacred-knowledge care line (binding — NON-NEGOTIABLE; the most important rule in this book)

**Men's business, women's business, and secret-sacred knowledge are OFF-LIMITS in-world. The story does
NOT claim to reveal what is not ours to reveal.** This is not a content-warning; it is the **structural
spine** of how the wonder is handled, and it is what makes this book honourable rather than appropriative.

- The book **honours the existence** of restricted knowledge **without depicting its content.** When the
  narrative reaches a sacred threshold, it **dramatises the threshold and looks away** — Nell is asked to
  wait at the vehicle, to not photograph, to not ask; she is told *"this part isn't for you,"* or *"this is
  women's country,"* or simply nothing, and she learns to honour the silence. The **restraint is the beat.**
  The withheld thing is felt as *weight and presence*, never as a tantalising puzzle to be solved.
- **The artifacts that carry the plot are always the *sharable* layer** — water, weather, sea level,
  astronomy-as-calendar, hydrology, antiquity-of-occupation, the *fact that* a songline encodes navigation.
  The plot is **never** advanced by a character (or the reader) being let into restricted/ceremonial
  content. The mystery the *book* solves is a public-knowledge mystery (where is the water; how old is this;
  what does the blasting threaten); the mystery the *culture* holds is **named, honoured, and left intact.**
- **The deep (Saltwater) thread obeys this too:** it shows ancestors *making* and *entrusting* knowledge
  into song, and looks away at the threshold of anything ceremonial/sacred, exactly as the present thread
  does. We see them survive, name water, grieve a drowning coast, and hand the memory forward — we do not
  see their law.
- **Designated unknowables:** the precise content of the songline's verses, the meaning of specific
  ceremonies, the identity-secrets of particular sites are **permanent designated mysteries** (see Rule 3) —
  not because the book is coy, but because **they are not the book's to tell.** A character (or author's
  note) may say so directly; the reader is trusted to find the restraint *more* powerful, not less.
- **Authority over the line rests with the custodians, in-world and out.** In-world: Daniel and Nana May
  decide what Nell may see, hear, record, or testify to, and Nell defers — including when it costs the plot
  the easy win. Out-of-world: see the **binding back-matter note** (STORY_BIBLE; PLOT §Back matter) — real
  Aboriginal cultural advisors / sensitivity readers must vet the manuscript before publication, under ICIP
  and free-prior-and-informed-consent principles; **no secret or sacred content is to be depicted, period.**

> **The test:** if a scene's wonder or plot-advance *depends on the reader being shown restricted
> knowledge*, it is a violation — re-route it so the **public artifact** carries the beat and the sacred
> stays a felt, respected absence.

## Rule 3 — Designated mysteries stay mysterious

Two kinds, kept distinct:
- **Care-line mysteries (Rule 2):** sacred/ceremonial content — *never* revealed, by principle, forever.
- **Wonder mysteries:** *exactly how* a 12,000-year-old observation was kept word-perfect across 400
  generations; the full extent of the songline network; the deepest age of first arrival. **Named, never
  fully solved.** The book reveals enough to astonish and to drive the relay; it does not "explain away" the
  achievement. Over-explaining kills both wonder and credibility — and, here, risks trespassing.

## Rule 4 — Wonder, not conspiracy (tone is a rule, not a vibe)

Awe and invitation, **never** paranoia or grievance-bait. No omnipotent cabal, no "wake up" register, no
cover-up mythology. The forces against the country are **human, fallible, and legible**: a mining company's
balance sheet, a regulator's box-ticking, a fast-tracked heritage approval, individual ambition and fear.
The harm that has *already* happened to country (real: ~a quarter of one famous petroglyph field lost to
industry within living memory) is shown as a **choice people made**, mournable and reversible-in-future,
not as the work of a shadow hand. Skeptics are respected; the protagonist is one.

## Rule 5 — Aboriginal genius is the SOURCE, never explained away (binding — the anti-poison pillar)

The lost-civilisation genre's poison — *"they couldn't have built/known it"* — is **forbidden here, in
every form.** Aboriginal Australians have the **oldest continuous living culture on Earth (65,000+ years)**;
their knowledge of country, water, fire, astronomy, navigation, and engineering is **brilliant,
sophisticated, and the SOURCE** of every wonder in this book. The descendants standing on the page are the
**experts**; the outsider **learns from them**; the science (hydrology, archaeology, bathymetry) keeps
catching up to what the custodians already knew. **Never** is an outside agency — aliens, a vanished other
race, a "more advanced" prehistoric culture — allowed to author Aboriginal achievement. Oral tradition is
treated as a **primary historical and scientific source** (it is — Rule 1's sea-level result is built on
exactly that). Fire-stick farming, Budj Bim aquaculture, soakage hydrology, and songline navigation are
presented as the **applied science of the world's longest-running civilisation.**

## Rule 6 — Real places, used honestly (and some deliberately *not* mapped)

Real sites are where they really are; geography, distance, heat, and water are real and costed. **But the
honesty cuts both ways:** the *specific* lease, the company, the named characters, and the **exact songline
route and any sacred site** are **fictional / composited and deliberately not mappable** — so the book
cannot function as a treasure map to a real protected place. The closing **gazetteer separates
real-and-visitable (via Aboriginal-owned tourism) from debated from invented** — and explicitly states
which sacred locations are **withheld by design.** That candour is load-bearing for reader trust *and* for
respect. (The tourism mission: send readers to the **Aboriginal-owned tours and Ranger programs**, never to
trespass.)

## Rule 7 — Two ways of knowing water are one truth (the project-specific engine)

The recurring physical clue that binds the book is **water.** Nell's instruments and the elders' songline
**independently locate the same water** — present springs, soakages, and the *drowned* freshwater of the
old coast. The convergence is the proof: not that the science validates the culture (it doesn't need to),
but that **the culture got there first, and was right, for 65,000 years.** Every node turns on a
water-reading; the climax turns on water-memory (a drowned spring named in a song, confirmed on sonar)
becoming the evidence that protects the rock-art country. The "tattoo on the back of the hand to find
water" the author mentioned is **honoured, not literalised**: rendered as the *trained hand and eye reading
country* — the way a custodian reads slope, sheen, ant-lines, fig roots, and birdflight to a soakage —
knowledge carried *in the body and the song*, not a magic mark. (See WORLD.)

---

> **How to use this gate, in order of severity:**
> 1. **Rule 2 (sacred care line)** — the line you never cross. If wonder or plot leans on revealed sacred
>    content, stop and re-route through the public artifact.
> 2. **Rule 5 (anti-poison pillar)** — if any non-Aboriginal agency is credited with the genius, stop.
> 3. **Rule 1 (artifact leads)** — if a claim arrives without a checkable thing in front of it, stop.
> 4. **Rule 4 (wonder not conspiracy)** — if the register tips into paranoia/grievance, stop.
> Everything else is texture, and texture is free.
