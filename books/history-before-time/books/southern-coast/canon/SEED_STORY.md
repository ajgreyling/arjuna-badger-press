# SEED — *Scratching the Surface* (working title)
### History Before Time · the Cape book · a Jakobus book

> **Drafted 2026 in the Calendar of Stone vein** (the author handed it over: "you know Jakobus, you know
> me, make it yours, make it Jakobus"). Inherits the binding STYLE_GUIDE of *The Calendar of Stone*:
> close-third single POV; the artifact LEADS and the claim FOLLOWS; wonder not conspiracy; African
> genius is the source, locals are the experts; the theme is never spoken aloud; machine-tells banned;
> lean and propulsive (~18–22 chapters).

## The one-line

A nomad photographer who has spent his life *scratching the surface* of the world's most beautiful
places photographs a shell midden in the dunes of the southern Cape — and is pulled, by an old friend
who can read what he only captured, into what is actually buried under it: the place where the human
mind first decided to make a mark.

## POV — **Mark Harpur** (single close-third)

**The real Mark Harpur, named openly** (author's call — real friend, real work). Gypsy photographer,
@luckybeanz on Unsplash; lives on a **self-converted electric sailboat**; early-Bitcoin freedom money;
now blogs and shoots for a living; clean-energy / "rEVolution" worldview; the romantic-adventurous
travel-writer voice of his real blog (Happy Campers). Two of his real pieces feed this book: the western
**Overberg** surf-and-photo leg (**Paardevlei/Strand → Gordons Bay → Kogel Bay → Pringle Bay → Betty's Bay
→ Hermanus → Stanford → Struisbaai → Arniston → Cape Agulhas**), and — the one that fixes the site —
**"The Garden Route: A Cultural Journey"** (Happy Campers, Dec 2022), the *eastern* leg that names the real
deep-time spine: **Blombos Cave & Museum (Stilbaai), Pinnacle Point (Mossel Bay), Klasies River Caves
(UNESCO, Tsitsikamma), Nelson Bay Cave (Plettenberg Bay)** — the cradle of the modern symbolic mind. He
photographed both coasts and "only scratched" them.

> **Site LOCKED → Stilbaai (Still Bay), Blombos country.** The opening find is **relocated east** from
> Mark's casual Gordons Bay midden mention to **the dunes east of the Goukou river mouth at Stilbaai** —
> because that is where the science actually is (Blombos opens in the limestone cliffs a few km away; the
> ~73 ka ochre cross-hatch is *of this coast, this red ochre*). The artifact must sit where the real
> cradle-of-symbol evidence sits, not 300 km west of it. Gordons Bay was a Holocene shell heap (~2–5 ka)
> and could never credibly hold a placed ~70–100 ka ochre. This book is the story of the trip where the
> scratch went deep — on the Garden Route coast Mark himself pointed at.

- **Why Mark is the right POV (not Jennefer):** Jennefer reads *stone;* Priya reads *machines;* Ben
  *measures;* Daniel surveys. Mark reads *light.* He is a man trained to find the one frame that holds a
  whole place — composition, the decisive moment, the thing the eye skates past. His gift is the
  photographer's eye, and his wound is that he has spent a freedom-rich life moving on, *scratching the
  surface,* never staying long enough for any place to go deep on him. The deep-time mystery is the one
  thing that won't let him move on. **His arc: the man who skates the surface, made to dig.**
- **The charged engine (LOCKED):** Mark photographs everything. **Jakobus is the man no one has a
  photograph of** — four spellings, no face on record. A friend who shoots the world for a living, and the
  one subject he is never allowed to keep. That tension is a thread, not a gimmick: what does it mean to
  *see* a man you can never *capture?* (Pays the *Sawubona* / being-seen spine from a new angle — the
  camera that can't hold what the eye holds.)

## The find — the artifact that LEADS

- **The midden is real and ordinary** (a San/Khoisan shell heap in the dunes east of the **Goukou river
  mouth at Stilbaai** — Blombos country). Mark shoots it at golden hour because it's beautiful, not
  because it matters. **The artifact that leads:** in one frame, in the raking low light, his camera
  catches what his eye missed — a worked piece of **ochre** turned up in the dune face *below* the shell
  band (washed out from the older layer beneath, which is the tell), scored with a deliberate
  **cross-hatch**, the geometry *too regular to be erosion.* He doesn't know what it is. He just knows,
  looking at the back of the camera, that the marks are *on purpose,* the way a photographer knows a
  composition is intended.
- **The real science it points at (Crichton-credible, the spine):** this coast is the cradle of the modern
  symbolic mind. **Blombos Cave — in the limestone cliffs a few km from the Stilbaai find** — holds the
  **~73,000-year-old ochre cross-hatch, the oldest known *drawing* on Earth** — and engraved ochre blocks.
  **Pinnacle Point (Mossel Bay, ~90 min west)** holds ~164,000-year evidence of the first ochre use, heat-
  treated tools, shellfish diet — the earliest modern human *behaviour.* **Klasies River** and **Nelson
  Bay Cave** carry the spine east along the Garden Route. The mark Mark photographs is of that family and
  that age — but it is **portable, *placed*** (someone carried it to the midden and set it), which is the
  mystery: not *that* such a thing exists here, but that this one was *brought.* **The deep past leads through the lens; the wonder
  lands after the checkable thing** (the geometry, the OSL date, the ochre chemistry).
- **The gold thread (series binding):** the worked ochre is keyed — there is a fleck of **worked gold of
  strange purity** pressed into the deepest scored line, the same impossible gold that runs the whole
  relay (key, not bullion). It points the bearing — *off the edge of Africa, southward,* the way each
  book's bearing runs on. (Ties to the Calendar of Stone relay; Mark's instinct, like Jennefer's, follows
  it without being told.)

## Jakobus in this book

- **Home ground.** This is Jakobus's own coast — Paarl (Mark-of-Paarl the engine-whisperer is nearby; do
  NOT confuse the two Marks), the Atlantis dunes, the Strand, the Cape he was clawing out of as a young
  man. The Beast is in its element; the southern-coast passes (Clarens Drive, the roller-coaster of
  curves Mark's blog names) are a Beast set-piece waiting.
- **How he enters:** Mark, out of his depth on what he's found, calls the one person he knows who "knows
  things about old stone and old people" — an old friend from the road, the soft-bellied African who once
  fixed something for him in a country far from here. Jakobus comes because of what's *in* the frame.
- **The Beast vs the electric sailboat (the counter-melody, LOCKED).** Mark's clean, silent, renewable
  self-converted sailboat against Jakobus's shipped-across-an-ocean diesel Beast — the nomad who went
  *light and forward* vs the man who carries his ancient maxed machine. Two kinds of freedom, two kinds of
  going. Neither wins; they *recognise* each other. (A real friendship beat: the photographer and the
  ghost, the boat and the truck, both built out of love and not money.)
- **Locals lead / the firewall holds.** The Khoisan descendants of this coast are the experts; the find is
  theirs; the Order witnesses; Jakobus is the road, Mark the eye, neither the destination. Sensitivity
  read is a floor (Khoisan heritage).

## The title

***Scratching the Surface*** — from Mark's own blog title ("Scratching the Southern Coast"). The double
meaning is the book: the photographer who skates the surface of the world, on the coast where humanity
first scratched a *mark into a surface* on purpose. The theme — that the deep past belongs to everyone,
and that to truly see a thing (a place, a man) you have to stop skating and let it go deep — is the
book's EFFECT, never spoken.

## What it owes the real Mark

Render him with dignity and joy — the freedom, the light, the boat, the clean-energy faith, the
early-crypto luck held lightly, the gift for the one true frame. Use his real coast and his real eye.
Private specifics stay private; the page gets the best, truest version. He is the man who taught the
author (and the book) that you can photograph a whole world and still need one friend to make you *stay.*
