# EPIGRAPHS — *History Before Time: The Engineer of the Gods* (Book V, Egypt)

> Same system as *Revelation*: a **book-level epigraph** (front of book) + **per-chapter** short
> multilingual quotes (Arabic / Coptic / the makers' invented voice + English translation), each
> rhyming with its chapter beat. Real/invented mix, lightly handled; never let an invented line pose
> as a checkable historical quote. Per-chapter set deferred to polish (draft-now plan).

---

## BOOK-LEVEL EPIGRAPH (front of book — LOCKED)

Two-part, set together (the human proverb + the makers' voice — the book's theme from both ends):

> **اللي ما لوش قديم، ما لوش جديد.**
>
> *He who has no past has no future.*
>
> — Egyptian (Arabic) proverb

> *We did not make the mountain. We were given it. We kept it five thousand years, and wrote our names
> on it as a prayer, and did not sell the camel. Ask of your inheritance only this: that you hand it
> on awake.*
>
> — attributed to the keepers of the Serapeum *(in-world; the makers' inheritors)*

**Why:** the proverb states the book's spine plainly (the past is the ground of the future), and the
keepers' line is the Hagg-Yusuf thesis — *we were the keepers, not the makers; a great house is no
shame to the family that tends it* — and the theme statement (*what we inherit isn't ours to own, only
to keep and hand on*). "Hand it on **awake**" rhymes with Layla's climax (the free choice, awake, not
deployed). Render the Arabic with care; if the script can't be verified clean at typeset, fall back to
transliteration (*illi ma-lush adīm, ma-lush gidīd*) + the English. The second line is clearly marked
in-world (never posed as a real historical quote).
