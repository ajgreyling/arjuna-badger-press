# Dedication

In memory of **Odette Prinsloo.**

She grew up between two impossible things — **lions and flight.** A child of the Free State bush, on a
game farm a stone's throw from my own, she was raised among the big cats. She rode horses and drove a
quad bike at eight, and her father — a fine helicopter and fixed-wing pilot — put her in the air and
taught her to read the land from the sky long before most of us were trusted with a set of car keys.
She could fly, in every sense, before she could legally drive.

She made it her life. By twenty-four she was a flight instructor, doing the thing she'd been doing
since she was small: going up, and bringing others up with her. She was killed in 2013, in the air,
on a training flight — teaching, to the very last, the one thing that was always hers.

This is a book about the few who are brave enough to open the door — who go up, and across, and look
at the world from the one vantage the rest of us are too earthbound to reach. I could think of no one
who belonged at the front of it more than a girl who was fearless on anything that moved, who learned
to fly before she could drive, and who is up there still.

*Blue skies, Odette. Soft landings on the far side.*

\

*And to her mother, **Maryn** — who gave a young man a summer job on that game farm that shaped him
more than she could have known. Thank you. I have never forgotten it, and I never will.*
