## Site

**Petra** — Nabataean capital, Ma'an Governorate, Jordan. UNESCO World Heritage (1985). Primary approach
via the **Siq** (narrow gorge, ~1.2 km; walls up to ~200 m; pinch ~3 m wide). Signature façade:
**Al-Khazneh** ("The Treasury") — Hellenistic-Nabataean tomb façade ~40 m high. **Not a treasury** —
misnomer; interior is a tomb chamber, not a hidden hoard room (UNESCO; standard guide literature).

**Re-entry to the West:** Johann Ludwig Burckhardt, disguised as a Muslim traveller, reached Petra in
1812 — the "rediscovery" narrative (published 1822).

## Engineering (the grounded marvel — load-bearing for the book)

### Hydraulics (primary wonder)

The Nabataeans supported an estimated **30,000–40,000** inhabitants in an arid landscape (~15 cm
annual rainfall) through an integrated system of:

- **Dams** across wadis (flash-flood control + water capture) — including diversion to protect the city
  centre from Wadi Musa floods (ASCE Historic Civil Engineering Landmark; Ortloff 2005).
- **Cisterns** — hundreds of rock-cut and built reservoirs throughout the city (nabataea.net; field
  surveys).
- **Ceramic pipelines** — wheel-thrown pipe segments (~25–35 cm), male-female joints sealed with
  hydraulic cement/lime plaster; open-channel flow design to reduce leakage vs pressurised systems
  (MDPI *Water* 2020; Oleson 1995/2007).
- **Settling basins** for particle removal before potable distribution (Cambridge Archaeological
  Journal 15:1, Ortloff 2005).
- **Spring capture** — e.g. Ain Mousa, Wadi Mataha pipelines feeding royal compounds and the
  colonnaded street / marketplace (MDPI *Water* 2020).

**ASCE** recognises Petra's Nabataean water infrastructure as a Historic Civil Engineering Landmark.

### Rock-cut architecture

- Tomb façades: Royal Tombs, **Ad-Deir** (the Monastery) — ~850 steps climb; ~47 m façade.
- Theatre (Roman-era modifications on Nabataean base), colonnaded street, High Place of Sacrifice.
- Rose-red **sandstone** (iron/manganese oxides) — colour shifts at dawn/dusk (geology: Umm Ishrin
  Formation).

### Trade context

Incense/spice route hub; Nabataean wealth (frankincense, myrrh, bitumen trade) converted into stone
permanence. Roman annexation AD 106; decline after earthquake(s) and trade-route shifts; Byzantine
phase to ~7th century.

## Access / custody today

- **Wadi Musa** — adjacent town; tourism gateway.
- **Bedouin custodians** — B'doul and related communities; many worked as guides for generations;
  resettlement programmes since 1980s UNESCO listing (sensitivity: do not treat as backdrop).
- **Petra Archaeological Park** / Department of Antiquities; UNESCO reporting.
- Tourism: ~1M+ visitors pre-COVID; crowding, site wear, water stress on modern infrastructure are
  live issues.
