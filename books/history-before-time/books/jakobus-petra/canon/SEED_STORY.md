## The one-line

A fixer who reads made things walks the narrow gorge to a city **eaten entirely out of stone** — and
learns the wonder is not mystery but **hydraulics, trade, and the patience to cut a tomb like a promise**
in a desert that should have killed every dream of permanence.

## Premise

The commission looks small: escort a photographer, verify a heritage survey, or settle a friend's debt
with a Jordanian guide — the kind of job Jakobus takes because the road goes through wonder and he
was always going that way anyway. Petra is on every brochure; he expects the postcard. What he gets is
**volume**: the Siq's slot-canyon geometry (200 m walls, 3 m wide at the pinch); the Khazneh façade
(40 m, Hellenistic-Nabataean fusion, **no interior tomb** — the mystery is the face, not a hidden room);
the **water system** (dams, cisterns, ceramic pipes, the flash-flood engineering that made a desert
capital live); the Royal Tombs; the Monastery climb; the **living town** of Wadi Musa and the Bedouin
custodians who never left the ground even when the tourists did.

The book is **not** "who carved it" (the Nabataeans did; the anti-poison pillar forbids the diminishment
move). It is **what they built when they owned the incense road** — and what it costs to keep a made
place alive when the trade moves and the rain gets stranger.

## Jakobus here (lead — the made-thing read)

This is a **Jakobus-lead wonder book**, not a cameo. His question — *what is it for?* — runs the spine:

- The Treasury is **not a treasury** (misnomer; a tomb façade). He catches himself wanting the romance
  and accepts the correction.
- The city's genius is **water, not spectacle** — the cistern he is shown by a local who out-reads his
  camera-ready wonder.
- The tomb façades are **promises cut in stone** — trade wealth turned into permanence; the cost beat is
  what permanence demands in a living landscape (tourism, custody, the people who still live there).

**Iceberg throughout.** He fixes a pump, shares tea, takes the sunglasses off at the checkpoint. He does
not lecture on Nabataean history. The local guide owns the telling; Jakobus stands guard and pays
attention.

## Guardrails (HBT voice + Jakobus sheet)

1. **Anti-poison pillar** — Nabataean genius is real; never "they couldn't have." Living Jordanian
   custodians are experts; Jakobus learns *from* them.
2. **No woo** — no ancient aliens, no hidden library, no secret chamber that rewrites history.
3. **Artifact leads, claim follows** — show the cistern, the pipe, the façade *before* the wonder lands.
4. **Dramatize, don't declare** — theme is the book's effect, never spoken aloud.
5. **Sensitivity read** before publication — Bedouin/Jordanian community protocols; tourism ethics.
