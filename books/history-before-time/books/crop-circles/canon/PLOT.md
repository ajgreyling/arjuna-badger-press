# PLOT — *The Potato* (working title) — the crop-circle book

> Register: **The Why Files** (official story straight → the one hole → the wink; live in the gap).
> POV: close third on **Edie Vane**, Wiltshire chalk archaeologist. Jakobus is the road.
> Author's call: **abandon the other series rules; crank it out; have fun.** Artifact still leads
> where it can, but the *maybe* is the point — we don't resolve to woo OR to "it was all a prank."

## The middle path (never leave it)
- Stonehenge = decoy. The hoax (Bower & Chorley, 1991) = TRUE and celebrated. The **potato** = the
  crack: brightest minds, daylight, laser sights, rope → a wobbling mess. The few flawless overnight
  ones hide in a forest of fakes; the fakes are the cover; the real target is what they sit on — the
  buried chalk landscape, the aquifer, the engineered terrain the world only knows one doorknob of.

## Act I — the potato (ch-01–05)
1. **The Potato** *(written)* — COLD OPEN on the debunk, played straight and funny: a TV crew + two
   famous croppers + Edie (the county's serious skeptic, hired to verify) try to make a clean simple
   circle in daylight with laser sights and rope. It comes out a potato. Everyone laughs. Edie does
   not laugh, because she has just measured, with her own theodolite, exactly how hard the easy
   version is — and she is the one person in the field who knows what landed in Pewsey Vale last June
   in the dark, in one short night, and was *not* a potato. The hook: the debunk she came to deliver
   just debunked itself.
2. **The One That Wasn't** — Edie's own file: the June formation. Bent-not-broken stalks still
   growing, blown nodes, no tramline bruising, no prints in the dew, no light seen, geometry clean to
   the centimetre over 300 feet. She's spent a career explaining these away. She can explain every
   one but this handful. She pulls the drone footage. There's a thing in the *ground* the pattern is
   sitting on that she'd never have looked at — a crop-mark of something buried, that the formation
   is *aligned to.* She emails a photo to a contact who "knows old made things." (Artifact leads.)
3. **The Man Who Read the Frame** — **Jakobus arrives.** Far from home (Beast shipped, or hired —
   he's foreign on the chalk and that's the point again). He doesn't look at the pattern the way the
   believers or the debunkers do. He reads it as a *made thing with a purpose* — a key cut for a
   lock — and the lock isn't in the sky, it's *down.* "Drawing with light" rhyme: here it's drawing
   with the crop, over the buried. Counter-melody to Edie's instruments (theodolite/drone/LiDAR) vs
   his hand-on-the-land. HANDOFF: it's not theirs to dig — Wessex has its own people, its own
   permits, its own ground.
4. **The Forest of Fakes** — they walk the season's formations. Most are obviously, gloriously human
   — Edie reads the plank-drag, the centre stake-hole, the dropped sandwich wrapper; the book LOVES
   the hoaxers, names them, respects the craft. But three in ten years sit on buried features. The
   pattern emerges from the *pattern*: someone has been using the hoax as cover, marking the real
   sites in the one medium nobody believes. The debunk is the disguise. (The fish heckles: "so
   they're hiding the truth inside a prank? …go on.")
5. **The Adversary surfaces** — someone else has noticed the correlation: a clean-handed survey
   company / a collector of provenance / the Cape-Court-in-a-new-English-form, buying up the aerial
   survey rights, the LiDAR, the drought data, to find what's under before the locals do. Edie's
   photographs + Jakobus's read are the thing that can't be un-seen. The familiar deniable race.

## Act II — the lock in the ground (ch-06–13)
- The real Wessex deep-map: Durrington, the Avenue, Silbury, the aquifer; legitimate drought
  crop-mark archaeology as the spine (the land genuinely writes its buried history in the wheat).
  The flawless formations turn out to mark **where the water moves / where the unexcavated thing
  lies / the alignment that Stonehenge is only the visible doorknob of.**
- Edie's arc: the professional skeptic who built a career on *no* has to follow a *maybe* without
  losing her rigour — the *Why Files* discipline made flesh (she never stops checking; that's what
  makes the one she can't explain matter).
- The hoaxers as unlikely allies — the real croppers know which ones aren't theirs and have always
  wondered. A Beast set-piece on the Wiltshire byways / a night chase across the Plain.
- Theme as EFFECT only: the answer is not "aliens" and not "a prank" — it's *look down, and look
  again, and don't let the loud thing tell you where to look.* Never spoken.

## Act III — the wink (ch-14–18)
- The buried thing reached / surveyed / protected; the locals own it; Jakobus is the road; the gold
  bearing runs on, *down* and off the famous map. The one flawless formation's purpose pays off.
- Final register: not proof. The book ends where *The Why Files* ends — with the official story
  intact, the hole still open, and a wink. Edie delivers her report. It is rigorous. It explains
  everything. Except the potato. She leaves the potato in.
- Reverse-payoff / man-no-camera-can-hold: the drone never gets a clean frame of Jakobus; the one
  satellite pass is the day he's under the hedge.
