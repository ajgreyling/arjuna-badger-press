## The frame — the interesting middle path (LOCKED)
The book does **not** say "aliens make crop circles." It does **not** say "it's all two blokes and a
plank." Both of those are the misdirection. The book lives in the **gap between them**:

- **The decoy = Stonehenge.** The most-looked-at ancient site on earth. Every eye, every camera,
  every theory points at the famous stones. *By design.* It's a doorknob the world mistook for the
  door.
- **The official solve = the hoax.** Doug Bower & Dave Chorley confessed in 1991 — plank, rope, a
  loop of wire on a cap for a sight. **TRUE.** Most circles are human-made art, and the book *revels*
  in that — it's gorgeous, it's a craft, some are commissioned (Mitsubishi, Weetabix). The book is
  pro-hoaxer. The hoaxers are heroes of a kind.
- **THE CRACK (AJ's real note — the spine of the book):** the brightest people, in *daylight*, with
  *laser sights and rope* and a *plan*, make a **potato.** A wobbling, off-centre, broken-stalked
  potato. So the "anyone can do it in an afternoon" story has a hole the size of the ones that
  *aren't* potatoes — the few that appear in a single short June night, geometrically flawless,
  hundreds of feet across, the wheat **bent and still growing** not snapped, the stalk-nodes
  swollen/blown from the inside (Levengood's real, hotly-disputed data — *Why Files* catnip), no
  tramline damage, no footprints in the dew, no light seen. Those are not Doug and Dave. **And
  they're not aliens.** They are the **real ones, hiding in a forest of fakes**, and the thousands
  of fakes are the **cover.** Somebody is *grateful* for the hoaxers. The debunk is the best thing
  that ever happened to the thing being hidden.
- **The real target = what the genuine ones sit on.** The buried Wessex landscape — the chalk
  aquifer, the unexcavated henge-scapes (Durrington Walls, the Avenue, the dried-river crop-marks
  that real drought-archaeology reveals from the air). The pattern in the wheat is a **key cut for a
  lock in the ground.** It marks where to look / what's under / when the water moves. The famous
  thing draws the eye up; the real thing is *down,* and the markers that point to it have been
  dismissed as a student prank. Perfect misdirection: make the signpost a punchline.

## Real-world load-bearing facts (the *Why Files* "here's the hole" beats)
- **Bower & Chorley, 1991** — real confession, real plank-and-rope method, real demo. USE IT. The
  book agrees with it. (Then: "…and yet.")
- **The potato** — REAL: experienced croppers and TV crews making clumsy circles in daylight; the
  gap between a planned daylight attempt and a flawless overnight formation is the whole engine.
- **W. C. Levengood / BLT Research** — published (disputed) claims of node elongation, expulsion
  cavities, altered seed germination in "genuine" formations. Treat as *Why Files* would: not proof,
  but the hole the debunk never quite filled. Show the checkable thing; let the wink land.
- **Wiltshire / Wessex chalk** — Stonehenge, Avebury, Silbury Hill, Durrington Walls, the Avenue;
  the chalk aquifer; the real fact that **drought reveals buried features as crop-marks from the
  air** (legitimate aerial archaeology). The land genuinely *writes its buried history in the crop.*
- **The "tramlines," the dew, the single short night, the geometry (diatonic ratios, Gerald
  Hawkins' real theorems on crop-circle geometry)** — all real talking points; all fair game.

## POV (LOCKED)
- **Lead = an England-of-the-chalk local who OWNS this ground the way Auntie Sara owns the Cape
  coast.** Working name **Edith "Edie" Vane** — a Wiltshire field/aerial archaeologist (or a
  farmer's daughter turned drone-survey/crop-mark specialist) whose family has worked this chalk for
  generations; she has spent her career being the serious person who *debunks* the circles for the
  county, and she is the one who notices the potato problem from the inside. The expert is local;
  the dismissed thing is hers to re-open.
- **Jakobus is the road.** He arrives reactively — the man who has carried the gold bearing up out
  of Africa and across the world, who recognises the *key-cut-for-a-lock* the instant he sees the
  geometry, because he's seen the other end of it. The Beast comes too (shipped, or his — TBD; he's
  far from home and that's the point again).
- Close third on Edie. Jakobus is the man no camera — and now no satellite, no drone — can hold.
