## Dr. Jennefer Abrahams — protagonist, sole POV

> The full character. POV rule is binding: **single close third, Jennefer only.** Heritage/identity
> runs as a **strong undercurrent, never a lecture** (Q13b) — felt often, stated rarely.

- **Who:** a young **mixed / "Coloured" South African** archaeologist (with strong geology
  field-craft), early–mid 30s, Cape Town. Credentialled (UCT, provisional), brilliant, field-hardened
  — and **professionally buried.**
- **The name (LOCKED — Q13a):** **"Jennefer"** — *meant* to be Jennifer, but her **semi-literate
  father spelled it phonetically (Afrikaans ear) on her birth certificate**, and the error stuck.
  The misspelling is a small, lifelong identity-marker she carries: visibly *an error someone made*,
  a name that was never quite the right shape — which rhymes with everything else about her sense of
  belonging. She may go by **Jenna / Jen** in the field. **Surname Abrahams** — a real, common
  Cape-Coloured surname *and* a deliberate, subtle nod to the cross-series Brotherhood of Abraham
  (a wink for the series reader; load-bearing for no one).
- **Heritage (LOCKED — Q10), privately uncertain:** she's never been sure exactly what she is —
  **Afrikaans, Khoi, Cape Malay, and a bit of Xhosa**, by family lore, none of it documented. Raised
  inside a community historically told it has *no* single ancestry to claim. Then a **genetic test
  reveals Italian blood** — traced to the **Italian POWs who built Bain's Kloof Pass** and were
  allowed to stay after WWII. *(Real history: the Afrikaners who tossed cigarettes and snacks into
  the train carriages of Italian POWs — a small, strange kindness that, generations later, is
  literally in her DNA.)* The reveal lands as both unsettling and oddly *freeing*: maybe "owed no
  single heritage" was the wrong question all along.
  - **The Huguenot strand (LOCKED — heritage + quiet anti-dogma theme; from the author's own blood).**
    The DNA also shows a smaller **French** sliver — the **Huguenots**, the French Protestant refugees
    who fled a king and a church that decided what a man was allowed to believe and would burn him for
    reading scripture wrong, and who came up the Boland three centuries ago, planting vines and leaving
    their surnames (Du Toit, Joubert, Le Roux, Retief — half of them turned Afrikaans). Real history,
    real in the author's own line. **Its character function:** it gives a name and a root to Jennefer's
    deepest instinct — *the refugee's reflex, three centuries deep, to never take a powerful
    institution's word for what is true.* She has just been destroyed by exactly such an institution
    (academia's orthodoxy) for telling an inconvenient truth; the Huguenot blood is why that injustice
    reads as a *betrayal of type,* not a surprise. **Embodied, never preached** — a vein, not a sermon.
    It quietly rhymes with the series' anti-dogma spine and with REVELATION's Brotherhood-vs-scripture-
    suppressors (faith vs. the power that would own it), but in Book I it stays *hers* — heritage and
    instinct, not a stated crossover. MERGED: book1 ch-02 (the Boland's older promise) + ch-10 (the DNA
    payoff, "ran from a church that wanted to burn them for reading the Bible wrong").
- **The gift — languages (LOCKED):** she has a **knack for languages**, grown up in the mishmash of
  cosmopolitan Cape Town. **Fluent: Afrikaans, English, isiXhosa, Sesotho;** **gets by across the
  Nguni languages; passable Swahili.** She learned the *roots and forms of words* as a kid by reading
  **shampoo-bottle ingredient lists in different languages** *(author's own experience)* — a
  self-taught, scrappy, joyful philology that no one credentialled her for. **Story function:** this
  is her superpower on the relay (reading inscriptions, talking to local custodians in their own
  tongue, the up-Africa leg) *and* the living proof of the theme — a disinherited girl who made the
  whole continent's voices her own. (Ties directly to the studio's Swahili/a13n mission.)
- **The inherited injustice — the dop-stelsel (LOCKED; handle with care):** her family's roots run
  back to **Western Cape wine-farm labour and the *dop-stelsel*** — the historical system of paying
  farmworkers partly in cheap wine, which **deliberately bred generational alcoholism** and bound
  families to the farm (its legacy: among the world's highest FASD rates, entrenched poverty and
  stigma in Coloured farmworker communities; the harm is real and ongoing). For Jennefer this is
  **backstory, felt and rarely stated** (Q13b register): a parent or grandparent marked by the bottle;
  plausibly **why her father was semi-literate** (a farm-labour childhood robbed of schooling — the
  same gap that misspelled her name). It is the engine under her hunger to prove her people's worth.
  **Guardrails (binding):** carry it as *undercurrent + a few earned beats*, **never as
  misery-spectacle, poverty-tourism, or a lecture**; she is a *person*, not a symbol of damage; if a
  human editorial pass touches these beats, prefer a sensitivity-informed reader. (Reinforces the
  apartheid-legacy / social-conscience commitment the studio already holds.)
- **The wound (binding):** she published an anomalous dating on a worked-stone find — a date
  thousands of years too early — and was ridiculed, defunded, and frozen out. Her name is a
  cautionary tale in the field. **She secretly fears she really is the crank they called her.**
  *Deepened by heritage (Q10) and history (the dop-stelsel):* the disgrace rhymes with an older wound —
  a lifetime, and a lineage, of being told she doesn't quite belong / has no real claim (right down to
  the spelling of her name, and the bottle that shadowed her family). Being disbelieved and written off
  isn't new to her; it's the *story of her people* aimed at her career. That's why this particular
  truth — about a deep, *great* African past — cuts so close: vindicating *it* is vindicating *her
  people's worth and her own right to belong to a history at all.*
- **The want (binding):** **vindication** — proof she was right, and the harder thing beneath it,
  the courage to *be right out loud* without flinching. Arc: from self-doubt and hedging to standing
  on the evidence, come what may — and, beneath that, from *"I'm owed no history"* to *claiming one.*
- **Voice/texture:** rigorous, dry, allergic to woo — which is exactly why her conversion to wonder
  *earns* the reader's. Multilingual wit; she code-switches without thinking. The recovering skeptic
  who demands the artifact before she believes (so does the prose). Competent, not flawless; the ruin
  left real scars (money, trust, a bridge burned).
- **Why her:** the expert of her own place (anti-poison pillar in character form) — the wonder comes
  from a South African reclaiming her own past, not a foreigner "discovering" it. And her tangled,
  uncertain, *everyone-and-no-one* ancestry makes her the perfect embodiment of the theme: **the deep
  past belongs to everyone.**

> **Still to confirm (author's call):** her exact discipline emphasis (archaeology vs geology), the
> specific worked-stone site of the ruined date, and whether she goes by Jennefer / Jenna / Jen.

## The Order — the series throughline (seen through Jennefer)

A centuries-old, deliberately quiet society studying the origins of man — *not* a string-pulling
cabal; **keepers and protectors of wonder.** In Book I they are **recruiters**: they watched her
disgrace, know she was right, and offer belief + resources + access. Cast the *individuals* she meets:

- **The Keeper (the order's conscience)** *(provisional)* — the senior figure who approaches her.
  Carries the order's vow (*we do not conquer the past; we remember it and keep it safe*) and the
  revelation-vs-protection burden. The one who says *"you're not crazy."* Likely mentor-coded;
  consider a woman, or an elder rooted in oral tradition (Mutwa-lineage spirit, handled with respect).
- *(Optional 3rd)* **The Skeptic-within-the-order / the doubter** — keeps the order from feeling like
  a cult of believers; argues for caution and proof, mirroring Jennefer's own rigour.

## Jakobus Swart — "the Fixer," Jennefer's field companion (LOCKED — Q14/Q15; sole co-lead beside her)

> ⭐ **Series canonical sheet: [`../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md`](../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md)** — the full, authoritative Jakobus (carries across all books). This section is his Book-I-specific detail; the sheet wins on any conflict.

> The Order's "hands in the field," and the person Jennefer is paired with for the whole book. After
> her, the most important character. Seen only through her POV.

- **Name (LOCKED): Jakobus Swart.** *(Origin, a private character detail — a quiet rhyme with his
  whole nature:* the name was a boy's **pre-loaded cover** — the one he'd give, fast and easy, when an
  adult or a policeman caught him out. A child who always had a contingency, an exit, an alias ready.
  The name *is* the craft and the wound in one word.) He may answer to **Koos**; the Order knows him
  by surname.
- **Who (Q14):** a **charming, infuriating fixer/operator** — the Order's worldly, gets-things-done
  field hand. Multilingual, well-travelled, evasive about his past, quick with a joke; knows the
  back-routes, the borders, and whose palm to grease. **The classic adventure foil:** where Jennefer
  is rigorous, dry, and evidence-first, he is instinct, improvisation, and people. *She reads the
  stones; he reads the people.* Friction → trust → **a fierce sibling-love** (see the relationship
  rule below — *not* a romance).
- **His defining gift — *Sawubona*, "I see you" (LOCKED; the moral key to the whole character):** he
  **subtly mirrors the gestures, rhythm, and manner of whoever he's with** — and reads people through
  body language + accumulated context (his pattern-cognition again). **The crucial thing: this is NOT
  deception or manipulation. It is respect.** It is the lived meaning of the Zulu ***Sawubona*** — not
  "hello" but *"I see you,"* an acknowledgement of another person's full presence and humanity. He
  mirrors people to **meet** them, to **honour** them, to make them feel *seen* — never to work them.
  This single trait **resolves the moral tension** of "charming operator who reads people": his
  tradecraft-shaped skill is, in him, the **opposite** of a con — it's a form of love. (Pairs exactly
  with Jennefer's languages-as-respect and the anti-poison pillar: two people who honour others by
  meeting them where they are.) *Watch the line in drafting:* the narration must keep it **respect,
  not technique** — when he uses it, the beat should read as warmth/attention, not as a chess move. If
  he ever *did* weaponise it, that would be his lowest, most self-betraying moment (a possible dark
  beat — use only deliberately).
- **Blending in — the uncomfortable, honest advantage (LOCKED):** his **white skin lets him move
  through spaces others are afraid to enter, haven't tried, or wouldn't risk** — checkpoints, certain
  rooms, certain authorities. Stated **plainly and without flinching**: it's a real, uneven truth
  about access and safety in this world, and it's a genuine *tool* he has that his companions (Jennefer
  included) do not. Handle it **clear-eyed — neither bragging nor apologising**; it's most powerful
  when the story simply *notices* it (e.g. he walks in where Jennefer can't, and both of them know
  exactly why, and it costs something between them). A real source of friction and of usefulness; a
  quiet, ongoing acknowledgement of South Africa's unequal ground (ties to the social-conscience
  commitment).
- **How autobiographical (Q15 — LOCKED: "inspired-by"):** he carries **AJ's look and spirit** but is
  **his own man** — fictional backstory, real flaws, his own arc; *not* a flattering self-portrait.
  *(Derived from AJ's reference image, rendered as prose — the canon stores words, not a likeness.)*
- **Physical description (from reference — full detail on the series sheet):** mid-40s, **average
  height, normal build** — not tall, not short; **past his prime** with the **soft belly** of a man
  who lives on the road, and **more dangerous now than in his prime** (patience and read, not muscles).
  **Not** a sexy Hollywood ex-operator — a **real** one; shaved and in a suit he'd look uncomfortable
  and still like **Steve from accounting.** Olive, sun-browned skin; salt-and-pepper beard; face seamed
  by sun and squint; calm, hard to rattle. *(Greyling Mediterranean look.)* In Africa he **flows like
  water** — often the only white face for miles, with **bush time, respect, and the old ways** — not
  tourist-white. **Dress:** brown, olive, khaki — practical, same-clothes-for-three-weeks field kit,
  never operator cosplay.
- **The eyes (LOCKED):** **blue / green / grey — they shift with his mood** (cool grey when he's
  closed, greener when he's at ease, pale blue when something's wrong). They are also **light-
  sensitive**, so he wears **wraparound shades almost always**, indoors and out. The quiet irony, and
  a gift to Jennefer's arc: *the most readable thing about this walled, exit-minded man is the one
  thing he keeps covered.* **Payoff beat:** the moment she finally sees his eyes bare — and clocks the
  colour, and therefore his state — should land as real intimacy *earned*, not given. (He takes the
  glasses off for almost no one.)
- **The uniform rule (LOCKED — his single most consistent tell):** he takes the shades **off for
  anyone in a uniform** — a border guard, a cop, a soldier, a parks official — **regardless of how
  unofficial or dodgy the authority is.** A real customs post and a makeshift boom across a dirt road
  manned by a man with a borrowed reflective vest both get the same treatment: glasses off, bare eyes,
  before a word is spoken. **Why (it's tradecraft, not deference):** letting a person with power *see
  your eyes* reads instantly as *I have nothing to hide* — it disarms, it flatters, it's the fastest
  way through a checkpoint. He gives the uniform the exact vulnerability he denies everyone else,
  precisely *because* it's a tool. (It's also a learned submission-display toward authority, not real
  submission — a small window onto his history with men who had power over him.) **Story uses:**
  Jennefer notices the pattern before she understands it; it's a quiet *Sawubona* inversion (he makes
  the gatekeeper feel *seen/safe* to get past them); and it can be subverted once — the time he
  *doesn't* take them off, or takes them off for someone who isn't really official, tells the reader
  something has changed or that he's reading a threat the uniform is hiding.
- **Dress = the character:** faded olive/brown/khaki field layers; **the ubiquitous waistcoat** (military
  webbing × fly-fisherman's jacket — EDC in every pocket, plus smooth stones/crystals he never explains);
  **full-tang bushcraft fixed blade** on the thigh, **Leatherman**, **two+ folding knives** (daily use —
  nails, apple, cord); cargo trousers, worn leather wrist-cuffs, outline-of-Africa forearm tattoo. After
  the **no-gun** beat, Jennefer may call out the knives; see series sheet **blade doctrine**. Desert leg:
  **shemag** (ornamental until it isn't).
- **The credo (from reference — his spine in three lines):** ***"Tech in my mind. Africa in my heart.
  Freedom in my soul." · "I don't seek danger. I respect it." · "This is home."*** The middle line is
  the key to him: **not a thrill-seeker — a man who respects the bush, the wildlife, and the danger,**
  and survives by reading them right. (Dovetails exactly with the series/African-Gold ethos: *respect
  the land; survive, don't conquer.*) The others give him his **tech-mind + bushcraft fusion** —
  *explorer · builder · problem-solver; adapt, improvise, overcome.* He's as fluent in a Linux
  terminal as in tracking; *connect to nature, respect wildlife, leave no trace, protect what you
  love.*
- **Background (LOCKED — pan-African operator):** a child of Southern Africa's crossroads — equally at
  home in **Botswana, Namibia, the Zambezi, the Cape** — **multilingual** (the languages-mind: he and
  Jennefer can banter across three tongues), with a contact in every dorp and at every border post. He
  **IS the up-Africa network** that makes the relay's crossings plausible. *She reads the stones; he
  reads the land and the people.*
- **His flaws (binding — keep him honest, not idealised):** his **evasiveness about his past costs
  them** at least once; his **charm is a shield** he hides behind instead of being known; he **cuts
  corners and bends rules** Jennefer wouldn't (the fixer's pragmatism vs. her rigour is real friction,
  not just banter); his *"this is home / I respect it"* steadiness can curdle into a refusal to risk
  for the *right* reasons. The arc lets her rigour and his instinct each correct the other.
- **How his mind works (binding — the texture that makes him real; drawn from life, kept close):**
  he is **neurodivergent**, undiagnosed and self-managed, and it's woven through everything:
  - **Spatial genius, symbol-blindness.** He *sees* geometry, terrain, structure, mechanism — reads a
    landscape or a machine at a glance — but **abstract symbols slide off him** (think: a brilliant
    field mind who "can't spell to save his life," to whom algebra is a jumble while geometry is
    obvious). His competence is **embodied and spatial**, never bookish-abstract — which is exactly why
    he and Jennefer fit: *she* brings the dates, the symbols, the language; *he* brings the ground, the
    angles, the read of a room.
  - **Numbers and people by *pattern*, not notation.** He remembers a phone number by its **phonetic
    shape/rhythm**, not its digits; he reads people through **body language + accumulated context**,
    catching what's unsaid (a gift that looks like intuition and is really pattern-cognition — a quiet
    rhyme with Jennefer's own way of knowing).
  - **Self-regulation, out loud.** He plays **loud, genre-less music** to regulate himself; he goes
    **dysregulated when two people talk at him at once**, or in **crowds with no exits** (a mall at
    Christmas is his private hell). When dysregulated he **can be a real dick** — short, hard, walled —
    and knows it, and it costs him.
  - **Freedom as a nervous system, not a slogan.** *"Freedom in my soul"* isn't branding — **having his
    movement restricted drops him into a near-primal state.** (Beat to use, lightly: an early,
    out-of-proportion reaction to being *confined* — a checkpoint, a cell, a locked gate — that
    Jennefer doesn't understand until much later. The compulsion to *break out* arrives before the
    reason does.) This is the engine of his whole life on the road.
  - **The two-sided tenderness (do NOT flatten).** He can **cull a sick animal and call it a Tuesday**
    — and **weep for a small wild thing he tried and failed to save.** The mercy and the grief are the
    *same muscle*; the hardness is *because* of the softness, not opposite to it. This contradiction is
    the most important thing about him — never write only one half.
  - **Clever, and quietly so.** He knows he's sharp and **doesn't perform it** — no traveller's-tales
    virtue-signalling, no recounting his own achievements; the self-awareness is real and undramatic.
- **His tradecraft (binding — REAL doctrine, never Hollywood):** Jakobus came up in the world of
  Southern African special operations and the private-security circuit that followed it, and he was
  shaped by its **real** lessons, not the screen version:
  - **Mental strength over physical.** The hardest training he ever did was of the *mind* — composure,
    patience, the will to keep going long past the body's vote. He is not a gym-built action hero; he
    is *unbreakable in a quiet way.*
  - **Economy and silence.** Real operating is **undramatic**: no choreographed fistfights, no
    machine-gun sprays. It's patience, the stragglers, the thing done quietly and once. He avoids
    violence and, when it's unavoidable, is **economical and unshowy** — which reads as far more
    frightening than bravado. *(Use sparingly; the book is adventure, not a war story — his lethality
    is mostly a banked, never-flaunted fact that Jennefer slowly senses.)* **The one sanctioned
    exception (ch-13 *Inheritance*; loosened by author):** in a true set-piece his scale **may be
    confirmed outright**, but only from outside him — by sizing up the *other* hard men's bought
    hardness and letting the most dangerous of *them* be afraid of the stories about him. The violence
    stays economical; the *confirmation* is the new thing. See the **confirmed-legend exception** in
    the series SSOT [`../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md`](../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md)
    for the binding fences.
  - **Survival, evasion, escape.** Urban and wild: reading a town for its exits, crossing borders
    unseen, living off a coastline by night, disappearing. **This is his real value on the relay** —
    he gets them in and out of places they shouldn't be able to reach. The fascination with tradecraft
    is genuine and lifelong.
  - **Weapons: respect, not swagger.** He handles firearms the way a man taught *hard* does —
    correct, economical, never for show, slightly contemptuous of people who treat them as toys (see
    the father-memory below). He'd rather not draw one at all.
- **The wound beneath it all (binding — a FICTIONALISED echo, handled with care):** under the charm and
  the competence is a **stolen childhood**: he was **forced as a boy to make an adult, life-or-death
  decision about a parent he loved and feared** — a damaged father (drink, rage, a profession of
  killing and mercy intertwined) whose threat he understood *in an instant*, and acted on alone, and
  then carried in silence for years. He has **truly forgiven** the father, the way you only can once
  it's far behind you — pressure made him a crystal: *everything in his life shaped him into the man he
  is.* The forgiveness is real; the cost was real too. **GUARDRAILS (binding):** this is a *transformed
  echo* of the author's own history, **deliberately changed in specifics** so it is the *character's*
  story, not a transcript — keep it that way. Reveal it **slowly, never as a trauma-dump**; it explains
  the freedom-streak, the control issues, and the soft centre **without excusing his worst moments**.
  A sensitivity-informed eye on these beats.
  - **A formative father-memory (LOCKED — fictionalised; the wound + the weapons-respect in one
    scene):** as a small boy his father let him fire a handgun far too big for him — stood behind him,
    his body bracing the child's, let him take the shot — and **caught the weapon, and the boy's hands,
    on the recoil, above his head.** It hurt (ringing ears, jarred wrists) and the boy was *furious*.
    Only decades later did he understand the lesson under the brutality: respect — that a weapon is
    never a toy and the world will catch you only if someone loves you enough to stand behind you.
    *(A transformed echo of the author's own memory — keep the specifics changed; it's Jakobus's, not
    a transcript.)* This single memory carries his whole relationship to his father (hard, loving,
    understood-too-late) **and** his economical, unswaggering way with weapons. Use it once, quietly,
    at the right moment — never as exposition.
- **His arc destination — recognition returned (LOCKED; the *Sawubona* payoff):** the man who spends
  his life *seeing* others is, by the end, spontaneously **seen back.** People who owe him nothing
  offer him the respectful elder-form in their own tongue — **Ntate, Pa, Oom, Baba** (Mzee up the
  continent) — and small, unbidden graces: the bakkie that stops not at the zebra crossing but at the
  pedestrian's true desire-line (the shortest path, the way South Africans actually cross) and waves
  him over. *(Drawn from the author's own lived experience of ageing into this grace.)* It is the
  earned **proof that respect-given returns** — the warm counter-melody to his wounds, and the
  completion of the *Sawubona* trait (the practice → the reward). **Shape of his whole arc:** he
  begins mirroring people from behind shades and walls — *seeing others, letting no one see him* — and
  ends **seen**: honoured, named as an elder, the glasses off for the people who matter. (Pair this
  beat with the eyes-bare payoff; same resolution.) Use **a few small, unforced moments**, never a
  speech — the grace lands because it's quiet and undeserved-feeling to him.
- **Spirit-animal motif (from reference — optional, lovely):** **Stoffel the honey badger** ("legend,
  fearless") — the real beloved SA icon. A fitting emblem for him and a neat African-animal echo of the
  studio's pangolin: *small, fearless, never starts the fight but finishes it; impossible to cage.*
  Use lightly (a nickname, a sticker on the Cruiser, a running joke), never heavy-handed.
- **Story function:** Jennefer's guide into the Order's world; the propulsion and the comic relief; the
  network that makes the relay's border-crossings and contacts plausible; and the emotional foil whose
  trust she has to earn (and vice-versa).

## Jennefer & Jakobus — the relationship rule (LOCKED — binding)

**It is sibling-love, never a romance.** There is a real, **healthy, normal current of attraction**
between them — the kind that naturally arises between two people who have shared something
extraordinary and frightening and beautiful (a dawn at the calendar, a blind escape in the dark, a
night of being truly seen). It is acknowledged, human, sometimes funny, occasionally charged. **It is
never acted on.** And the reason it's never acted on is not external obstacle or bad timing — it's that
**they both love each other like siblings and both privately know it would never work.** They are too
alike in the broken places, too necessary to each other as *family,* and each is too clear-eyed to
mistake the heat of shared adventure for the thing you build a life on.

- **Why this is the right choice (don't revert to a romance):** it's more mature and more true; it
  frees both characters from a will-they-won't-they that would cheapen the *Sawubona* spine; and it
  models a kind of love the genre almost never shows — a man and a woman who adore each other,
  completely, platonically, for life. Their "I see you" is **family,** not foreplay.
- **How it reads on the page:** warmth, teasing, fierce protectiveness, the easy shorthand of people
  who've survived things together; the occasional honest flicker of *what if* that they each clock and
  let pass, usually with humour. The night he takes the glasses off (ch-15) is **intimacy, not
  romance** — the intimacy of being known, which is exactly what makes it possible for it *not* to
  become romance. They can say "I love you" and mean it cleanly.
- **Drafting guard:** never tip a charged beat into a romantic one; if a scene starts to read like
  attraction-about-to-be-acted-on, redirect it to the sibling register (a joke, a protective gesture,
  a *jou doos* said with affection). No kiss, no will-they; the tension is texture, the love is real,
  the line is never crossed.

## The Antagonist — the one who would loot or own the wonder

- **Who:** a private collector / extractive antiquities-or-mining interest *(provisional: a name + a
  face — e.g. a charismatic billionaire collector, or a mining-concession fixer)*. Wants the site for
  **profit or prestige** — to strip it, patent it, or lock it away in a private vault.
- **The function:** the mission's mirror-image — **extraction-for-the-few vs. shared wonder and
  tourism-to-locals** (Series Bible §0.5). Concrete, human, clean adventure villainy.
- **The tone guardrail (binding):** keep them a *greedy, dangerous human*, **not** a shadowy
  omnipotent cabal — that's what keeps the book on the wonder side of the line (§1). Give them a real,
  almost-sympathetic want (a collector who genuinely *loves* the objects but cannot share them) so
  they're more than a sneer.
- **Series potential:** can recur or hand off to a larger adversary across volumes; Book I keeps it
  contained and personal.

## Supporting (Book I, local & real-world-rooted)

- **Site custodians / local communities** at each relay node — the *experts of their place*; the
  order learns from them; honoured, never extras. (These are also, not coincidentally, the people the
