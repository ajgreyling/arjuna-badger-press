# MYTHOS_RULES — *History Before Time*, Book I (and series default)

> The binding "physics" of the wonder. Adapted from the African Gold trilogy's mythos gate, but
> **inverted in emphasis**: there, the rule kept the mythos *grounded and never woo*; here the mythos
> is **true and celebrated** — yet still **earned through physical evidence** so it reads astonishing
> rather than silly. This is what lets a wonder-loving, *non-conspiracy* reader stay aboard.

---

## Rule 1 — The fringe is true in-world

A high-knowledge phase of humanity and/or its visitors existed **before recorded time** and left
real infrastructure. This is *fact* in this world, not theory. (Series leans in; African Gold stays
ambiguous — that's the deliberate difference between the siblings.)

## Rule 2 — The artifact LEADS, the claim FOLLOWS (binding — Q7 grounding lock)

The reader earns the wonder by first earning the **impossible object**. The order is not optional:
the prose shows the *physical, checkable* thing **first**, and only then lets the "the ancients /
the visitors did X" land. (Craft lock Q7: *show the artifact, let the data speak* — the Crichton
move. Jennefer's scientific rigour is the reader's proxy; she demands the evidence before she believes,
and so does the narration.) The checkable thing is one of:

- stone worked to tolerances we can barely match (the precision-vase / precision-masonry beat);
- acoustic / resonant / astronomical properties of arranged stone (Adam's Calendar);
- anomalous **dates** on a real lab report; anomalous **metallurgy** / gold purity;
- subsurface **voids** on ground-penetrating radar; vitrified or anomalously-cut rock.

**No hand-waving, no magic, no telepathy-as-explanation.** The *Crichton move*, pointed at fringe
conclusions: show the data, let the awe do the work.

## Rule 3 — Designated mysteries stay mysterious

*Who* the builders were and *exactly how* they did it are **named but never fully explained.** The
book reveals enough to astonish and to drive the relay; it does not "solve" deep antiquity. Mystery
is a feature; over-explaining kills wonder (and credibility).

## Rule 4 — Wonder, not conspiracy (tone is a rule, not a vibe)

The register is awe and invitation, **never** paranoia. No reptilians, no omnipotent cabal, no
"wake up" grievance, no David-Icke turn. Institutional resistance is **human and fallible** (careers,
ego, consensus), not a sinister all-controlling force. The antagonist is **greed** (someone who would
own/loot the wonder), not a global cover-up.

## Rule 5 — African (and Indigenous) genius is the SOURCE, never explained away (binding — the pillar)

The lost-civilisation genre's poison — *"they couldn't have built it, so aliens did"* — is **forbidden
here.** The ancients are brilliant and **ours**; locals are the experts; the Order learns *from* them;
visitors came to **learn and trade for gold**, not to civilise. Credo Mutwa and African oral memory
are treated as **primary historical sources.** Living cultures and sacred sites are honoured, never
appropriated as set-dressing.

## Rule 6 — Real places, used honestly

Sites are where they really are; geography and travel are real and costed. The reader must be able to
**go there** (the tourism mission). The closing gazetteer separates *what you can really see* from
*what the story invented* — that candour is load-bearing for reader trust.

## Rule 7 — The gold thread is the series engine

Worked gold — too pure, formed to *function* — is the recurring physical clue that binds the
continents and ties (softly) to the African Gold trilogy. In Book I it surfaces in the deep-gold
country as the first link in the chain.

---

> **How to use this gate:** if a scene asserts wonder without an artifact behind it (Rule 2), or
> tips into conspiracy/grievance (Rule 4), or lets a non-African agency author African achievement
> (Rule 5) — it's a violation, fix it. These are the few hard lines; texture is otherwise free.
