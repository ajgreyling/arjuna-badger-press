# STORY_BIBLE — *History Before Time: The Calendar of Stone* (Book I, Africa)

> The book's binding overview. Sits under the [Series Bible](../../../canon/SERIES_BIBLE.md);
> where the two conflict, the Series Bible wins on series matters, this file wins on Book-I specifics.
> **Title locked:** *History Before Time: The Calendar of Stone.*

## Premise

**Dr. Jennefer Abrahams** — a young **mixed/"Coloured" South African** archaeologist from Cape Town
(privately unsure what she even is; a gift for languages) whose career was destroyed when she dated a
worked-stone find thousands of years earlier than the textbook allows — is recruited by
**the Order**, a centuries-old society that quietly maps the impossible facts mainstream archaeology
steps around. They prove her ruined find was real, and lead her on a quest-relay up the spine of
Africa — **Adam's Calendar**, the deep-gold country, and beyond — assembling evidence of a
civilisation that existed *before recorded time.* A rival who would loot or own the wonder shadows
them. Her real prize is not the discovery; it is **vindication** — and, beneath it, the right to claim
a history she was taught wasn't hers.

## Theme

Greatness is **inherited and recoverable**, not lost: the ancients were brilliant and *ours*, and
remembering them is an act of belonging, not fantasy. Beneath the adventure: *who gets to decide what
the past is allowed to mean* (anti-dogma), and *wonder over fear.* (Carry as theme, never preach —
see THEMES.md.)

## Tone & mood

Cinematic, propulsive, sensory — a travel-thriller that loves landscape and stone. **Wonder, not
conspiracy** (Series Bible §1): the energy is *"isn't it astonishing that this exists?"*, never
*"wake up, sheeple."* Awe, warmth, and momentum; the dread is adventure-dread (the place, the rival,
the drop), not paranoia. Skeptics are respected; the protagonist is herself a recovering skeptic.

## Target reader

Loves history **and** fiction, wants to be dazzled, and has **never gone down the conspiracy rabbit
hole** (has never heard of David Icke). Reads Dan Brown / James Rollins / Wilbur Smith for the ride;
watches *Ancient Aliens* / Hancock for the wonder. Finishes the book wanting to **travel.**

## Comparable works

Dan Brown (*The Da Vinci Code*), James Rollins (Sigma Force), Clive Cussler, Wilbur Smith's
adventure register — crossed with the *Ancient Aliens* / Graham Hancock subject matter and a
*National Geographic* love of place. The grounded-artifact credibility of early Crichton.

## POV & form

**Single close third person**, the local lead (Jennefer). Short, propulsive chapters with strong
site-to-site cliff-pulls. Each chapter advances, plants, or pays off a **relay node** (a real place).

## Content bounds

Adult adventure; nothing gratuitous. Treats **living cultures, sacred sites, and traditional
knowledge with respect** (never set-dressing). The mythos is celebrated in-world but **always earned
through a witnessable artifact** (MYTHOS_RULES) so it reads astonishing, not silly. Honours the
anti-poison pillar: African genius is the *source* of the wonder, never explained away.

## Promise to the reader

A brilliant African woman vindicated; a heart-in-throat journey through real, astonishing places; and
a closing, irresistible urge to **go and stand where it happened.**
