# PLOT — *History Before Time*, Book III (India: Deccan) — relay skeleton

> The **quest-relay spine** (same as Books I and II). Structure is binding; texture is flexible. Each
> node: **ARRIVE → OBSTACLE → READ → COST → KEY → HANDOFF.** Every chapter advances, plants, or pays
> off a node. This is a *skeleton* from the architect, not a beat sheet — beats expand at outlining.
> Sites are candidates (see [WORLD.md](WORLD.md)); confirm/sequence with the author. **Standalone for
> a newcomer; a reward for the Book I / Book II / *African Gold* reader.** Consistent with
> [Book II PLOT](../../book2-india/canon/PLOT.md) and the [Trilogy bible](../../book2-india/TRILOGY.md).

## ACT I — THE EXPERT WHO CANNOT CUT, AND THE BEARING

**Node 0 — AURANGABAD / ELLORA · the paralysis, and Priya's handoff** *(rank 1)*

- **OPENING (LOCKED to house doctrine: open IN THE WOUND).** Page one is Arjun *failing to decide* —
  a conservation crisis at the rock (a fracture opening, salt-spalling on a painted surface, a
  monsoon-season failure) where the right call is *irreversible* and he cannot make it: he commissions
  one more survey, signs off a reversible half-measure he knows is inadequate, defers. Tense, human,
  low-glamour, *technically real.* We learn — through the texture of his hedging, not a flashback dump
  — that he is the world's leading authority on this rock and that he is **stuck.** Voice and wound
  first; marvel later. *(Plant the failed-restoration backstory as a shadow, not yet a scene.)*
- **Arrive:** Arjun in his element and his trap — the man heritage bodies call, who can read anything
  and decide nothing.
- **Obstacle:** his own paralysis + a heritage bureaucracy that mistakes his caution for wisdom (so no
  one *makes* him choose).
- **Read (Arjun):** he sees the form the rock needs perfectly — and cannot bring himself to cut toward
  it.
- **The handoff (BINDING — Priya, cameo, non-POV):** **Priya Ellis** arrives with the Order's
  instrument reading. She's carried the makers' chord this far and **cannot read it further** — *you
  grew up inside this rock, Arjun; you can hear what I can't.* Warm, dry, brief. In one oblique aside
  she names his wound (she's just come *through* the Gita's lesson from the other side — Book II — and
  clocks a man waiting for a certainty that isn't coming). She gives him the **bearing** and goes.
  *(Jakobus / the Indian keeper may flicker here — author's call; load-bearing for no newcomer.)*
- **Cost:** to follow the bearing, Arjun must do the thing he's organised his whole life to avoid —
  *act on incomplete information.* He almost refuses.
- **Key-out:** the Order's reading points him at **Kailasa as an instrument, not a monument** — and at
  the antagonist, whose "heritage foundation" has just been granted authority over Ellora's
  interpretation and restoration.
- **Handoff:** he goes — telling himself it's only to *assess.* **Establish Imtiaz** (old friend, the
  Deccani Muslim craftsman/conservator who *commits*) as his partner into the Order's world.
  **(End Act I.)**

## ACT II — THE RELAY ACROSS THE DECCAN (the cliff, the layers, the deepening threat)

**Node 1 — ELLORA / KAILASA & THE THREE-FAITH CLIFF · the anchor** *(rank 2)*

- **Arrive:** the Kailasa courtyard. The reader looks *up* at a temple carved *downward* from the sky.
- **Obstacle (physical + the wound):** it only reads as the makers' instrument if you grasp that it was
  carved by a mind that could **see the finished form inside the rock and commit, with no revision
  possible** — the exact discipline Arjun lacks. The geometry/acoustics won't fully resolve for a man
  who won't commit to a reading.
- **Read (Arjun):** his conservator's eye delivers the **artifact-leads** beat — the tolerances, the
  top-down logic, the no-mortar/no-scaffold impossibility, on his own instruments (MYTHOS_RULES Rule
  2). His first earned *wonder* — the insider recovering first sight. And the dawning recognition that
  the makers were everything he is not: **certain enough to cut.**
- **Cost:** the antagonist's hand shows — his foundation's "master interpretation" is already
  rewriting the cliff (signage recasting the Buddhist caves as "proto-Hindu," the Jain as "a sect,"
  the Muslim layer as desecration). Arjun sees the erasure beginning, and freezes at exactly the
  moment a *signature* (his) could block it.
- **Key-out:** the **acoustic / proportion fingerprint** (Rule 7) points to the **older layer** —
  Ajanta — where the instrument's *use* is remembered.
- **Handoff:** the reading forces Ajanta.

**Node 2 — AJANTA · the older layer; the rock that sings** *(rank 3, quieter)*

- **Arrive:** the horseshoe gorge above the Waghora; the painted caves; the chaitya halls.
- **Obstacle:** the marvel here is *sound* — the chaitya resonance — and it only reveals itself to
  someone willing to *commit to a single pitch* and hold it (a small, physical rehearsal of the larger
  lesson).
- **Read:** the chaitya hall resonates; chant pitched to it fills the chamber bodily (artifact first —
  the readings — then the awe). The **texts-as-manual hint** deepens (the Gita's discipline as the
  *operating instruction* for the instrument; dramatized through experience, never lectured — Rule 9).
  Imtiaz's craft-mind and Arjun's stone-mind read it *together* (neither alone).
- **Cost:** the antagonist's reach extends — pressure on custodians, a "re-survey" that's really a
  pretext, a first real danger; and a personal cost (the failed-restoration backstory gets its scene
  here, or its first real wound, via the figure-from-Arjun's-old-life).
- **Key-out:** the fingerprint runs *beyond the carved Hindu/Buddhist rock* — into the **Sultanate
  layer** (the same competence in cast metal and domed stone).
- **Handoff:** the reading forces south, into Imtiaz's territory.

**Node 3 — THE DECCAN SULTANATE LAYER · Bidar / Golconda / Gol Gumbaz · the fourth stratum** *(rank 4)*

- **Arrive:** Imtiaz's world — the Persianate-and-Indian Deccan (pick two of: **Bidar's** bidri
  workshops + half-ruined **Mahmud Gawan madrasa**; **Golconda's** clap-relay acoustics; **Gol
  Gumbaz's** whispering dome).
- **Obstacle:** reading *this* layer needs **Imtiaz** — the inscriptions, the *mujawar* who'll open a
  closed tomb, the craftsman's eye for what a Persianate proportion *does.* Arjun, the stone-reader, is
  out of his medium and must *trust his partner's read* (he can't verify everything himself — the
  paralysis directly challenged).
- **Read:** the **acoustic fingerprint surfaces in the Muslim layer** — the whispering dome / the
  clap-relay carry the *same* deep competence as Ajanta and (ahead) Hampi. **The artifact proves the
  theme:** the genius runs through *every* stratum (Rule 7). Often *heard* rather than measured (the
  returning whisper under Gol Gumbaz is the most spectacular set-piece location — sound betrays
  everyone in it).
- **Cost (BINDING — the theme made personal):** the antagonist's project lands as a **gut-punch
  through Imtiaz** — his family's thousand-year belonging called a footnote; the half-ruined library as
  the image of *knowledge that can be lost*; a closed door, a scrubbed plaque, a real threat. **Not a
  speech** — what it does to Arjun's friend (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 8). Imtiaz is at risk, here or later, of
  meeting hatred with hatred; plant his refusal-to-become-an-erasing-man.
- **Key-out:** the fingerprint runs *on, in a third rock* — **granite**, south, at **Hampi** — and the
  antagonist is already moving there too (selective "restoration," looting, neglect).
- **Handoff:** the reading forces Hampi.

**Node 4 — HAMPI / VIJAYANAGARA · the granite echo; the wound of erasure** *(rank 5)*

- **Arrive:** the boulder-strewn Tungabhadra ruins; the Vittala temple; the Stone Chariot; the
  **SaReGaMa musical pillars.**
- **Obstacle:** the tuned-granite pillars are the **southern proof** of the fingerprint — and Hampi is
  where the book **refuses the easy version of its own theme.** Vijayanagara was a Hindu empire
  **sacked in 1565 by the allied Deccan Sultanates** — the same sultanates whose syncretic culture the
  book honours. Plurality is real; so is the fact these peoples sometimes made war. Arjun (and the
  reader) must hold the harder truth.
- **Read:** the pillars ring to svaras — the same competence, a third rock, a fourth century (Rule 7
  pays off in full). And the **honest answer to the antagonist arrives, dramatized**: not "everyone
  always got along" (Hampi disproves that), but ***the layers are all real, and erasing any of them is
  still a lie.*** Arjun feels it standing in the burned Vittala — the complexity that the antagonist's
  purity-myth cannot survive contact with.
- **Cost (the hardest yet):** the antagonist forces a reckoning — the figure from Arjun's old life is
  revealed as his ally/instrument; something or someone is lost; Arjun's paralysis directly *fails*
  the people relying on him at least once (the nadir — his caution becomes the greater destruction).
- **Key-out:** the bearing turns decisively — the chord runs **south, to the Tamil shore** (Book IV);
  and the antagonist's endgame becomes clear: an **irreversible physical alteration to the Ellora
  cliff**, framed as restoration, that would destroy the three-faith reading forever. Only Arjun's
  signature/expertise can authorise or block it.
- **Handoff:** back to Ellora, to the climax.

### Threaded through Act II (subplots)

- **The friendship thread (Arjun ↔ Imtiaz) — the emotional engine.** Old friends, tested: Imtiaz the
  one who *commits* (the living counter-argument to Arjun's paralysis); the asymmetry of temperament
  driving warmth, needling, and real friction (Arjun's caution endangers them; Imtiaz's haste has its
  own cost). The antagonist's abstraction made unbearable through what it does to *this* friendship
  (never a tolerance speech). **Settled brotherhood, never a romance** (CHARACTERS).
- **The failed-restoration / personal-irreversibility thread.** Revealed slowly across the nodes (never
  a dump): the intervention that froze Arjun; the figure-from-his-old-life who carries it (and turns
  out connected to the antagonist); and — if used — the personal loss he couldn't take back. Builds to
  the climax, where the *same* fear must finally be overcome.
- **The Order / keeper thread.** The Deccan keeper hands Arjun the *moral* key (the stone is one rock;
  the three faiths are one inheritance) as Priya handed the *physical* bearing; the devout-Hindu
  opponent appears, making unmistakable that the antagonist is a *misuse* of a revered tradition
  (MYTHOS_RULES Rule 8). Possible Priya touchpoint (non-POV) at a low moment.

## ACT III — THE RECKONING & THE CUT

**Climax — the irreversible call (personal arc + plot resolve in ONE beat) (title payoff: *The Temple in the Rock*)**

- **The antagonist's move:** with quasi-official authority over Ellora's "narrative and restoration,"
  he begins the **irreversible physical erasure** of the cliff — an alteration framed as restoration
  that would destroy the three-faith reading forever (the bomb under the table is *permanence*: once
  cut, basalt cannot be uncut — the book's whole physics weaponised). He is **frighteningly sincere,
  almost sympathetic** — answering a real historical wound with a monstrous cure (CHARACTERS); there
  is a beat where Arjun (and the reader) *almost* understand him.
- **Arjun's choice (the want, resolved — and the arc, in the same stroke):** the one man whose
  signature/expertise can **block** the work is the man who cannot make an irreversible call without
  certainty — and certainty is not coming, and *waiting has become the greater destruction.* So Arjun
  does the thing the whole book has been carving toward: **he makes the irreversible decision on
  incomplete proof** — he *commits* — trusting the form he has spent his life learning to see. (The
  literalisation: he stops the erasure by an irrevocable act of his own — a signed authorisation that
  ends his career, the physical interruption of a cut, the public stand that can't be unsaid — and/or,
  in the cleanest version, he must himself make a *real, irreversible cut in the rock* to save the
  greater whole, becoming, finally, the kind of maker Kailasa was made by.) **The Gita's teaching
  lands not in a sermon but in his hand** (Rule 9): act, fully, release the fruit.
- **How the antagonist is beaten (BINDING — the theme's hardest edge):** **not** by denying his wound
  (the book grants it — real injury to Hindu heritage occurred) but by **refusing the erasure** —
  Arjun proves, *in the rock and in front of witnesses*, that you can honour your ancestors' genius
  *without* cutting anyone else out of the mountain. The artifact does the final arguing: the cliff
  itself — three faiths, four layers, one rock, the acoustic fingerprint running through all of them —
  is the evidence the purity-myth cannot survive. **Imtiaz** is central to the win (the friend, the
  fourth layer, the man who refused to become an erasing man).
- **Cost & grounding:** a real price (career, the figure from his old life, a true loss); the deepest
  mechanism stays a **designated mystery** (Rule 3) — Arjun wins enough to *protect the cliff and free
  himself*, not to "solve" deep antiquity. **Wonder, not conspiracy** held throughout (Rule 4): the
  antagonist is a fallible, defeated *human*, not an omnipotent cabal.

**Resolution — the maker, and the door south**

- Arjun changed — the decider unfrozen; the insider who looks *up* again; a man who can cut. The
  failed-restoration ghost laid to rest (he learns the difference between *false* certainty and
  *committed* action — he was never going to be sure, and that was never the point).
- The **bearing points south** — the Tamil shore (Book IV). **The forward handoff (non-POV, warm,
  brief):** Arjun passes the bearing to the **Book IV protagonist** (the Tamil sthapati / marine
  archaeologist) — *what he couldn't read from the Deccan rock, she can read from the shore.* He hands
  it on **without** knowing what waits at the other end — *which is exactly the Gita's act: pass the
  bearing, not the outcome.* (The relay's deepest payoff: he's learned to release the fruit.)
- **Standalone close + open door** (the series' order-independent-but-rewarding rule).

**Back matter (BINDING) — *The Real Places in This Book* (the tourism mission's payoff + the care lines)**

- **Real / what you can really see:** Ellora (Kailasa = Cave 16; the three-faith cliff, Buddhist 1–12,
  Hindu 13–29, Jain 30–34; ~200,000 tonnes removed top-down; Krishna I / Rashtrakuta); Ajanta (the
  horseshoe gorge, the chaitya halls, the paintings, the 1819 re-encounter — noting the locals always
  knew); the Deccan Sultanate monuments (Bidar's bidri + Mahmud Gawan madrasa, 1472; Golconda's
  acoustics; Gol Gumbaz's ~44 m dome and whispering gallery, 1656); Hampi/Vijayanagara (Vittala, the
  Stone Chariot, the SaReGaMa pillars; the 1565 sack after Talikota). **Go and stand in the Kailasa
  courtyard and look up.**
- **Debated / honestly flagged:** the *chronology* of the Ellora caves (which faith carved first — a
  genuine scholarly debate; the book keeps it in); the *precise* acoustic readings dramatized at
  Ajanta (real resonance behaviour; specific numbers are plausible-extrapolation); the exact "200,000
  tonnes" figure (estimates vary — the conservative number is used).
- **Invented:** the makers'-instrument reading of the sites and the acoustic "fingerprint" thread; the
  Order; the antagonist and his foundation; Arjun, Imtiaz, and the failed restoration; any specific
  modern erasure plot (a *fictional* dramatization of a *real category* of danger).
- **The care lines, stated plainly (BINDING — MYTHOS_RULES Rules 5 & 8):** the genius of these
  monuments belongs to the living descendant cultures who made and keep them; the book is
  **pro-heritage and anti-erasure**, and its antagonist is a *misuse* of a revered tradition, never a
  faith or a people; plurality is real and so was historical conflict — and **erasing any layer of the
  rock is a lie.** Honour every living tradition — Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Muslim — that shares this
  ground.
