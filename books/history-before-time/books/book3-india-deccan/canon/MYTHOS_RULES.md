# MYTHOS_RULES — *History Before Time*, Book III (India: Deccan)

> The binding "physics" of the wonder. Inherits the series mythos gate from
> [Book I](../../book1-africa/canon/MYTHOS_RULES.md) (Rules 1–6 below are the series default, restated
> for the Deccan) and [Book II](../../book2-india/canon/MYTHOS_RULES.md), and adds the project-specific
> rules for Book III (Rules 7–9). The mythos is **true and celebrated in-world** — yet still **earned
> through physical, checkable evidence** so it reads astonishing rather than silly. This gate is what
> lets a wonder-loving, *non-conspiracy* reader stay aboard.

---

## Rule 1 — The fringe is true in-world

A high-knowledge phase of humanity and/or its visitors existed **before recorded time** and left real
infrastructure, and the knowing was *inherited* up a road that ran out of Africa and across the world.
The Deccan's rock-cut and Sultanate monuments are a late, glorious chapter of that inheritance — built
by the descendant cultures, who carried the makers' competence (proportion, acoustics, the discipline
of irreversible making) forward in their own faiths and their own stone. This is *fact* in this world,
not theory.

## Rule 2 — The artifact LEADS, the claim FOLLOWS (binding — the grounding lock)

The reader earns the wonder by first earning the **impossible object.** The order is not optional: the
prose shows the **physical, checkable thing first**, and only then lets the "the makers / the
inheritors did X" land. **No hand-waving, no magic, no telepathy-as-explanation.** The Crichton move,
pointed at fringe conclusions: show the data, let the awe do the work. **Arjun is the reader's proxy**
— a conservator who, by training and by wound, *demands the reading before he will believe* — and so
does the narration. Book III's checkable artifacts are:

- **Subtractive impossibility / tolerances of the irreversible cut** — Kailasa's geometry,
  joint-spacing, and finish, carved top-down with no revision possible, to tolerances that imply a
  finished form held wholly in mind. (Arjun *knows what the chisel can and cannot do*; the numbers
  stop him cold.)
- **Acoustic / resonant architecture** — the Ajanta chaitya resonance; the Gol Gumbaz whispering dome
  (~10–11 echoes); the Hampi SaReGaMa pillars (granite tuned to svaras); the Golconda clap-relay.
  Sound engineered into four rocks, four faiths, a thousand years. (Book III's binding artifact-thread
  — see Rule 7.)
- **Conservation data as evidence** — GPR voids, anomalous joint-spacing, salt-creep / weathering that
  contradicts the assumed sequence, a survey that reads *wrong* on Arjun's own instruments. (The most
  honest grounding available, because checking the rock is literally his job.)
- **Proportion / alignment** that *does something* (acoustic, structural, astronomical) rather than
  merely *looks like* something.

> If a scene asserts wonder without a witnessable artifact behind it — it's a violation. Fix it.

## Rule 3 — Designated mysteries stay mysterious

*Who* the makers were and *exactly how* the deepest feats were achieved are **named but never fully
explained.** The book reveals enough to astonish and to drive the relay; it does **not** "solve" deep
antiquity, and it does **not** over-explain the acoustic/subtractive marvels after showing them. In
Book III the load-bearing designated mystery is **the source of the irreversible-making discipline** —
*how* a mind could hold and execute a form of that scale with no error correction. Name it; let
Kailasa embody it; never reduce it to a trick. Mystery is a feature; over-explaining kills both wonder
and credibility.

## Rule 4 — Wonder, not conspiracy (tone is a rule, not a vibe)

The register is **awe and invitation, never paranoia.** No reptilians, no omnipotent cabal, no "wake
up" grievance, no David-Icke turn. Institutional resistance is **human and fallible** (heritage
bureaucracy, academic consensus, ego, funding, sincere conviction), not a sinister all-controlling
force. The antagonist is an **ideologue** (and the money behind him), **not** a global cover-up — a
greedy/zealous *human*, fallible and almost-sympathetic (see CHARACTERS, and Rule 8). Skeptics are
respected; Arjun is himself a man of evidence.

## Rule 5 — Indigenous / descendant genius is the SOURCE, never explained away (binding — THE PILLAR)

The lost-civilisation genre's poison — *"they couldn't have built it, so someone else (aliens,
outsiders) did"* — is **forbidden here.** The makers' legacy is brilliant and **belongs to the living
descendant cultures**: the carvers of Ellora, Ajanta, Hampi; the builders of Golconda, Bidar, Bijapur.
**Locals are the experts; the Order learns *from* them.** Marathi conservators, Deccani craftspeople,
temple priests, *mujawars*, Buddhist and Jain caretakers, Hindu and Muslim scholars are the
authorities on the page (anti-poison pillar in character form — Arjun and Imtiaz embody it). Oral and
craft memory are treated as **primary historical sources.** Living cultures and sacred sites are
honoured, never appropriated as set-dressing. **Never write "ancient Indians couldn't have done this."
They did. That is the point.**

## Rule 6 — Real places, used honestly

Sites are where they really are; geography and travel are real and costed (the Deccan is large — see
WORLD constraints). The reader must be able to **go there** (the tourism mission). The closing
gazetteer (PLOT back matter) separates **what you can really see** from **what is debated** from
**what the story invented** — that candour is load-bearing for reader trust.

## Rule 7 — The acoustic / proportion thread is the Book III engine (the series-binding "signature")

Just as Book I's recurring physical clue was **worked gold** (too pure, formed to function) and Book
II's was the **machine that reads to pattern-cognition**, Book III's binding artifact-signature is
**sound and proportion engineered into stone** — the same deep competence surfacing across faiths,
dynasties, centuries, and *different rocks*: Ajanta's resonant chaitya (Buddhist basalt), Gol Gumbaz's
whispering dome (Muslim masonry), Hampi's tuned pillars (Hindu granite), Golconda's clap-relay
(Sultanate fort). **This is the physical proof of the theme:** the genius is not the property of one
layer — it runs through *all* of them. The relay advances when Arjun (with Imtiaz) recognises the same
acoustic/proportional fingerprint in a new layer. Designate the *origin* of the fingerprint a mystery
(Rule 3); show the *readings* on the page (Rule 2).

## Rule 8 — The erasure-of-history poison is FEATURED and REFUSED — and the target is the ideology, never a faith or a people (BINDING — the care line)

Book III dramatizes, and utterly refuses, the real-world danger of **weaponising the past to purify
it** — the rewriting of a plural history into a single supremacist story, and the erasure of minority
strata (Buddhist, Jain, Muslim) from the record. **Binding guardrails:**

- The **target is always the *ideology of purity-and-erasure*** — never Hinduism, never Hindus, never
  any contemporary movement, party, or real figure by name. The book is **pro-Hindu-heritage and
  anti-erasure**; never let the two blur. (Arjun is of Hindu heritage and loves the Hindu caves most;
  devout Hindu custodians and scholars are among those most appalled by the antagonist.)
- The antagonist is a **misuse** of a tradition the book reveres, given a **real, wounded, almost-
  sympathetic** core (genuine historical injury to Hindu heritage *did* occur — the book grants the
  wound and refuses the cure). Never a cartoon, never a strawman.
- **Keep the complexity honest.** Plurality is real; so is historical conflict (Talikota, Aurangzeb,
  iconoclasm on more than one side). The book does **not** claim "everyone always got along." Its claim
  is narrower and truer: **the layers are all real, and erasing any of them is a lie carved in stone.**
- **No misery-spectacle, no communal caricature.** A sensitivity-informed editorial eye on every beat
  involving communal politics. Render the danger with care; refuse it without preaching (the theme is
  never stated aloud — THEMES.md).

## Rule 9 — The Gita is a KEY, never a sermon (binding — the brain-layer, Book-III note)

The Bhagavad Gita's *karma yoga* — *act without attachment to the fruit; the form is already there;
remove what is not it* — is, in-world, partly the **discipline required to make the makers'
irreversible works** (above all Kailasa), and it is **Arjun's personal answer.** It is **dramatized
through Arjun's experience** (the conservator who cannot commit, made to read a thing made by total
commitment, and finally to cut) — **NEVER summarised aloud by any character, never delivered as a
guru's monologue, never quoted at the reader as a lesson.** The reader should *feel* the teaching land
in Arjun's hand on the chisel, not hear it explained. (Consistent with Book II's brain-layer rule and
forward-compatible with Book IV's *action-without-attachment-as-craft*.)

> **How to use this gate:** a scene violates it if it (a) asserts wonder without a witnessable artifact
> (Rule 2/7), (b) tips into conspiracy/grievance/woo (Rule 4), (c) lets a non-descendant agency author
> the descendants' achievement or treats living tradition as set-dressing (Rule 5), (d) blurs the line
> between *anti-erasure* and *anti-a-faith*, strawmans the antagonist, or tips into communal caricature
> or misery-spectacle (Rule 8), or (e) states the Gita / the theme aloud as a lesson (Rule 9 /
> THEMES.md). These are the hard lines; texture is otherwise free.
