# EPIGRAPHS — *History Before Time: The Temple in the Rock* (Book III, Deccan)

> Same system as *Revelation*: book-level epigraph (front of book) + per-chapter quotes (Sanskrit /
> Marathi / Dakhni / the makers' voice + English), each rhyming with its beat. Real/invented mix,
> lightly handled; never let an invented line pose as a checkable historical quote. Per-chapter set
> deferred to polish. **Gita note:** the book is the Gita's *Krishna's teaching* movement (act without
> grasping the fruit) — so the epigraph should echo *action*, never lecture it.

---

## BOOK-LEVEL EPIGRAPH (front of book — LOCKED)

Two-part (the deed + the rock):

> **कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन।**
>
> *Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits.*
>
> — Bhagavad Gita 2.47

> *The form is already inside the rock. Carving is only the courage to remove what was never the
> statue. He who waits to be certain before he cuts will die holding a whole and useless mountain.*
>
> — attributed to the carvers of Kailasa *(in-world)*

**Why:** the Gita line is the book's spine in nine words — Arjun's whole arc is learning to *act*
without the guarantee of the outcome — and it is a genuine, attributable verse (BG 2.47), so it can
stand as real. The carvers' line is the theme made physical (Kailasa was carved *top-down from one
rock* — the ultimate no-take-backs act of faith) and the title's source; it's marked in-world and
never posed as a real historical quote. (If the Devanagari can't be verified clean at typeset, fall
back to transliteration: *karmaṇy-evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana* + the English.)
