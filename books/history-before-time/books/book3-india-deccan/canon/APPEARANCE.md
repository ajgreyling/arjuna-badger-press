## Arjun Desai (Arjun-saheb, "bhau"/"bhai" to Imtiaz)

- **Hair:** Colour given indirectly as grey/greying — Kale: "They told me you'd gone grey and miserable and here you are, grey and miserable" (ch-07); Apte notes his "silver temples" belong to *Kale*, not Arjun — but Arjun is repeatedly coded as aged/grey. Length and style: never specified.

## Coherence flags

1. **ANTAGONIST NAME DRIFT — Sushant Kale vs Devdatt Apte (MAJOR).** The villain is **Sushant Kale** throughout ch-07, 11, 12, 13 (warm hands out, "silver at the temples," good kurta + lanyard). In ch-14 the antagonist is named **Devdatt Apte** — "the thin man" from the dome (ch-09) — "a plain dark kurta and no lanyard at all... he was past lanyards now," and explicitly frames *Kale* as a subordinate: "Sushant overplays his hand... a wounded man" (ch-14). Then ch-15 reverts to an unnamed "thin man," and **ch-16 returns to Sushant Kale** as the on-site antagonist at the Dashavatara. So the books present TWO antagonists (Kale = the warm Ajanta/Hampi face; Apte = the thin dome/Kailasa principal) but ch-16 collapses the Kailasa-wall confrontation back onto *Kale*, contradicting ch-14 which staged that exact confrontation with *Apte* and the saw. Physical descriptions also diverge: Kale is "lean... silver at the temples" with a lanyard; Apte is "thin," "smaller in daylight," **no lanyard**. This needs an authorial decision: one antagonist or two, and which one is at the cliff in the finale.

2. **SURNAME COLLISION — "Apte" used for two unrelated characters.** Ch-05 has **Apte**, the beloved 80-something epigraphist (thick lenses, wet kind eyes, takes rubbings) who is a *mentor-conscience* ally. Ch-14 names the **antagonist** Devdatt **Apte**. Same surname, opposite moral roles — confusing on the page. Recommend renaming one.

3. **SURNAME COLLISION — "Kale."** Ch-02 names **Rohan Kale**, Priya's good-man contact who reads the rock. The antagonist is **Sushant Kale** (ch-07+). Shared surname with no stated relation; either intend the connection or rename.

4. **HAMPI CUSTODIAN DRIFT (likely duplicate-draft chapters).** The Hampi sequence appears in FOUR overlapping variants with DIFFERENT custodians, all introduced as "the keeper who shows the singing pillars":
   - ch-10: **Vasanthi Rao**, ~60, steel-grey plait, river-coloured sari, felt mallets.
   - ch-11: **Venkataraman**, spare upright old man, white veshti + grey shawl + vibhuti, 51 yrs of service.
   - ch-12: **Raghu / Raghavendra**, ASI assistant in his 50s, clerk's shirt, worn shoes, drafted objection-letter.
   - ch-13: **Hampanna**, 30 yrs at Vittala, "colour and texture of the granite."
   These read as competing drafts of the same scene rather than four distinct people (each is "the Vittala/SaReGaMa custodian who demonstrates the pillars and delivers the burned-temple 'harder truth'"). Also the same beat (pillar destroyed by Foundation drill / objection unfiled) recurs across ch-12 and ch-13. Consolidate to one Hampi keeper and remove the duplicate chapter(s).

5. **OLD-LIFE WITNESS — gender/identity inconsistency.** The "figure from Arjun's old life, witness to the failed restoration" is rendered inconsistently:
   - ch-10 (Hampi): the Sittannavasal-era colleague reaching for the pillars is **female** — "She had not gone quiet. She had gone *here*"; "I know her" (Arjun). Unnamed.
   - ch-15 (Ellora): an unnamed man "who says he worked with you. At Dharanikota," male, "the man who had pushed that timeline."
   - ch-17 (Ellora): named **Vivek Deshmukh**, **male**, Arjun's old teacher who handed him the consolidant.
   So the same structural figure is a woman in ch-10 and a man (Deshmukh) in ch-15/17, and the failed-restoration SITE drifts too (see flag 6). Pick one person and one gender.

6. **FAILED-RESTORATION SITE NAME DRIFT — Sittannavasal vs Dharanikota.** Arjun's career-defining failure is named **Sittannavasal** in ch-07 and ch-11 ("the man who killed the Sittannavasal wall"; ch-12 references it). In ch-15 and ch-17 the same backstory failure is set at **Dharanikota** ("worked with you. At Dharanikota"). These are different real sites; unify the name.

7. **ARJUN's HAIR — internal consistency OK but thin.** Prose only ever codes Arjun as grey/greying (ch-07) and never gives length, style, eye colour, or skin. No contradiction, but note canon (late 30s–early 40s) sits oddly against the repeated "grey and miserable" / aged presentation; not a drift, just a tonal flag for the author.

8. **"Bhosale" pallor vs hair.** "grey to the lips" (ch-13) and "grey to the lips" (ch-15) describe stress-pallor, not hair colour — recorded here only to prevent a future reader mis-reading it as a hair cue. No drift.
