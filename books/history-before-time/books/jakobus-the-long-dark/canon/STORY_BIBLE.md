# *THE LONG DARK* — Story Bible

> **The fourth Jakobus-Book — and the one that carries his death.** Where *The Silver Thread* is the
> Kalahari and the gift of **peace**, and *The Recitation* is the Sahara and the gift of **patience**,
> *The Long Dark* is **home — South Africa — and the gift he spent a whole life learning how to give:
> making sure that when the lights go out for good, nobody's night dies.** It is the **present-tense**
> Jakobus (not a backstory volume): the fixer at the end of the road, in the year the grid does not come
> back. It braids directly into the locked death/wake canon — **Jakobus dies in 2030, aged 60** — and it
> **claims the canonical send-off** ([`../../../canon/JAKOBUS_WAKE.md`](../../../canon/JAKOBUS_WAKE.md)),
> staged as a **bookend frame** (§7): the prologue opens on his death and the Wake's first beats, the body
> is the grid-down arc under that known shadow, and the finale completes the Wake.

> **Single source of truth for Jakobus is binding.** Read him from
> [`../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md`](../../../canon/characters/JAKOBUS_SWART.md). Where this
> bible and that sheet disagree, **that sheet wins.** This book inherits — verbatim, not reinvented — his
> field-kit (the cayenne tin, the clean hanky, the Beast's weeping hessian water-bag, the zeer-pot
> exposure thesis), the Beast and its one-shot nitrous, the no-gun rule, the giving doctrine ("it only
> counts if it hurts"), the *Sawubona* spine, and the languages-mind. The collapse is the world that
> finally makes every one of those small true things **load-bearing for everyone, not just him.**

> **Tone (binding — inherited from *The Sheltering Desert*, the tonal cousin):** *great real bushcraft;
> not always a fun read; real story; real respect.* Unsentimental. The romance of "prepper fantasy" is
> **earned down to truth** — what's left is the water problem that never ends, fuel that goes bad on a
> schedule, the slow attrition of a body at sixty, and a country discovering — against the story it was
> told about itself — that **most people, most of the time, help.** We honour it by telling it **straight.**

## 0. The two bedrocks (one documented, one fictional-but-locked)

**A. The real engine — infrastructure collapse, told accurately (Lewis Dartnell, *The Knowledge*).**
This book's "wonder of place" is **competence**: the real, researched, correct physics and chemistry of a
world losing its grid. The technical layer is **Dartnell-grade — heavy and accurate** (author's call).
The non-negotiable facts that drive the plot (full sourcing in [`PLACE_RESEARCH.md`](PLACE_RESEARCH.md)):

- **Fuel degrades on a clock, and the clock is the plot.** **Petrol goes "gunky" first** — modern pump
  petrol oxidises and gums in **months** (ethanol-blend pulls water; gum and varnish clog carbs and
  injectors; octane drops). **Diesel lasts far longer** — **a year-plus** stored well, longer still if
  kept dry, cool, and biocide-treated — but it grows **"diesel bug"** (microbial sludge at the
  fuel/water interface) and **waxes/gels in cold.** This asymmetry is **why the Beast's old 4.2
  straight-six diesel is a lifeline and every petrol car becomes a brick** — and why the window to move
  is measured in *weeks of good fuel left*, not "whenever."
- **The grid is not a switch, it's a cascade.** Load-shedding (the SA reader knows it cold — Stage 1
  through Stage 8, the schedule, the dark) escalates past any stage anyone planned for; then a **failure
  that doesn't self-heal** — a black start that won't take, substations cannibalised for copper, no diesel
  to run the open-cycle turbines, no grid to refine the diesel: the **doom loop** is real and gets it
  right. Water follows power (pumps stop); sewage follows water; comms follow both (cell towers run
  ~hours on battery, days at most on their own generators, then go silent).
- **The survival science is real and is the texture, not an aside** (Jakobus's whole field-kit, now
  everyone's problem): **water** (the litres-per-day arithmetic, boiling vs filtering vs the hanky-and-
  charcoal field filter, the zeer/evaporation cooling, the weeping canvas bag); **food** (what stores,
  what's already rotting in dead freezers, the protein-without-fat trap that nearly killed the Sheltering
  Desert men — *the same crisis, deliberately echoed*); **fire and heat without the grid**; **improvised
  power** (a salvaged solar panel + a car battery + an inverter is a different civilisation from nothing;
  the limits of it, honestly); **medicine** (the insulin that has no cold chain left — a quiet, real,
  un-survivable problem for someone in the group; antibiotics; the cayenne coagulant). **Get all of it
  right** (the gate enforces it — §9). The pleasure is the accuracy.

**B. The fictional-but-locked engine — Jakobus, and the year 2030.**
- **Jakobus is 60 and this is his last year** (canon-locked: born 1970, dies 2030). The collapse is not a
  thriller backdrop; it is **the final exam of the only curriculum he ever taught** — that the way through
  fear is other people, the heartbeat, the road, the shared cup. He has spent a life being the man who
  drinks from the village cup while the careful go thirsty. Now the whole country is at the dry communal
  zeer, and he is the one person who was *always* ready for this — and who is also, quietly, **running
  down.** A man whose every instinct ports to a world without machines, in a body that is finally failing
  on its own schedule, the way the grid is failing on its.
- **POV: close-third on Jakobus throughout** (matches *The Silver Thread* and *The Recitation*). The
  iceberg rule holds — *the power is in what he won't say.* But this book lets us **inside the cost** more
  than the bookend cameos ever did: this is his story, in his country, at his end.

## 1. The one-line

When the load-shedding that South Africa learned to live with finally stops coming back on — petrol going
to gum, diesel on a one-year clock, the taps drying behind dead pumps — a sixty-year-old fixer who has
spent his whole life being ready for exactly this leads a handful of strangers and the people he loves out
of a dying city and into the high country, to prove, in the year he will die, the one thing he always
believed and the world keeps forgetting: that when the lights go out, **most people reach for each other,
not each other's throats** — and that the hardest, last skill is not lighting the fire, but making sure
that when your own battery finally dies, somebody you taught is there to bring the heartbeat back.

## 2. Premise & shape

**2030. South Africa.** Load-shedding is back, and heavier than it has ever been — Stage 8 and past it,
schedules nobody honours, the dark a fact of every night. The country grumbles and copes, the way it
always has. Then comes the week it **doesn't come back on** — not a stage, not a schedule: a cascade that
won't black-start, and the slow, dawning, un-cinematic understanding that *this is not getting fixed.*

Jakobus Swart is in **Johannesburg** when it goes (LOCKED 2026-06-16). The highveld megacity is the
sharpest collapse-engine in the country: the biggest crowd, the fastest to eat itself, and the most
water-vulnerable — Joburg's water is **pumped uphill from far away** (the Vaal / Lesotho Highlands
system), so when the pumps die the taps die, and the city is built on no river that can save it. He is
sixty, he is tired in a way he doesn't name, and he is the only person on his street who watches the
lights die and feels something almost like **recognition**: he has lived in this world before, on three
continents, in every place the grid never reached. He does the arithmetic everyone else is too frightened
to do — **how many weeks of good fuel, how many litres of water a day, how long before the city eats
itself not from malice but from thirst** — and he knows the answer is *not long*, and the move is *out,
and soon, while the diesel's still good and the roads are still roads.* The refuge is the **Drakensberg
edge** (LOCKED 2026-06-16) — KwaZulu-Natal's high country, streams and rain and cover and defensible
terrain, San rock-art in the overhangs (the quiet hand of HBT's ancient thread). The road is the **N3
corridor, Joburg → the 'Berg** — roughly a single tank of good diesel, long enough that the road becomes
its own act (the first proof of Bregman: the verges shared, the way pointed), short enough to be real.
*(Note: the refuge is refuge-as-discovered-place, not his childhood ground — Jakobus grew up Free State,
§SSOT; the 'Berg is where the road finally stops, not where he began. The going-home is to the country,
not the farm.)*

The novel is **the getting-out and the building of a place to last** — close-third on Jakobus (§3): the
triage of who comes and what's carried; the convoy out of a city tipping from order into fear (and the
discovery, again and again, that the fear is mostly *manufactured* — by rumour, by the few, by the state's
own panic — and that the actual people on the actual roads mostly *share the verge and point the way*); the
high-country refuge that runs on no grid at all; the grinding, real, *un-fun* work of water and food and
heat and medicine; the one death the kit can't prevent (the insulin); the human predators who are real but
**rare, and beaten by numbers and decency, not by a lone hard man with a gun** (the anti-*Lord of the
Flies* spine, §4); and — through all of it — the man at the centre teaching, almost without words, the
thing he came to teach: *you are not as alone, or as savage, as they told you.*

**The book is built as a bookend frame** (author's call, 2026-06-15; §7). It **opens** on the end: the
**bush assassination** (rusk half-chewed, coffee in hand, he never heard or saw a thing) and the **Wake
beginning** — doors thrown open, the system up, the battery dying mid-peak — and cuts to black on that held
breath of silence and dark. Then *"earlier"* — and the whole grid-down arc runs forward under the shadow of
a death the reader already knows is coming. At the close the book **returns** to the Wake and **completes**
it: **someone he taught bringing the heartbeat — and the dawn — back**, *Sawubona* to the empty seat. We
know he dies; the suspense becomes **how he spent his last months, and who among these people becomes able
to restore the dance.** *The dance is the eulogy. Nobody speaks. They spin.* (He **dies in 2030, aged 60**,
killed by the Forger — §7, [`THE_FORGER.md`](THE_FORGER.md).)

**Structural shape (bookend frame):** **PROLOGUE — the bush death + the Wake's first beats, battery dying,
cut to black (told from OUTSIDE Jakobus — the one-time POV break, §3)** → *"earlier"* → **the cascade (the
lights don't come back) → the arithmetic and the triage → the convoy out (the road as the first proof of
Bregman) → the building of the refuge (the Knowledge layer at full strength) → the diet/attrition midpoint
(the Sheltering-Desert echo — the high is staying alive another month) → the real threat and how it's
actually beaten (decency + numbers, not heroics) → the death the kit can't stop (the insulin) → the
gathering / the road family converging → the Forger and Jakobus's own end (now caught up to the prologue)**
→ **FINALE — the Wake resumes from the held breath and completes (the heartbeat brought back, the dawn,
*Sawubona*).** The everything-he-builds is, in frame, everything he builds **to outlive himself.**

## 3. POV & voice (LOCKED)

- **Close-third on Jakobus Swart**, throughout the body. The reader lives the collapse through the one man
  who was always ready for it — which makes both the wonder (he is *calm*, he is *useful*, he is *home* in
  the catastrophe) and the cost (he is sixty; he tires; he cannot save everyone; he is dying) land off the
  same eye. **Iceberg, never exposition** — but this is *his* book and his country and his end, so we are
  permitted closer to the ache than the cameo books ever allowed.
- **The ONE deliberate exception — the frame prologue (and its completion in the finale).** The opening
  death + Wake-beginning is told from **outside Jakobus** — the natural eye is **the Forger's cold one**
  (the anti-Jakobus watching the man he reveres take his last rusk; it plants the killer in the first
  pages without yet spoiling the who/why), and the fire/Wake glimpsed over the road family. This is a
  **one-time, intentional POV break that signals "this is the frame"**; the instant the book cuts to
  *"earlier,"* it **snaps to the locked close-third on Jakobus** and stays there for the entire arc. The
  finale (the Wake completing) may briefly hold that same external/over-the-fire vantage again to close the
  bracket — Jakobus is gone by then, so the lens is the people he taught. **Do not let the external frame
  bleed into the body; the body is his, undiluted.** *(This is the only POV licence in the book; everything
  between the brackets is Jakobus's close-third, per the locked house rule.)*
- **The author's voice (binding — the project house style).** Grounded, sensory, unshowy; reverence for
  real competence and for the land; the *Sawubona* attention turned, here, on **a society under stress** —
  reading people the way *The Sheltering Desert* reads rock and water. Where that book's literacy is
  geology, this book's literacy is **two things at once: the engineer's reading of a failing machine-
  civilisation (the fuel, the water, the grid) AND the fixer's reading of a frightened crowd** (who will
  panic, who will share, who is dangerous, who only looks it). That double literacy — *systems-and-souls* —
  is the book's signature, the exact analogue of Martin's scientist-and-survivor double act.
- **Strip the fantasy; keep the awe.** No bunker-cosplay, no lone-wolf power-trip, no zombie-movie crowd.
  The awe is for **how much a competent, generous person can actually do with a clean square of cotton, a
  diesel that still runs, a tin of cayenne, and a refusal to be afraid of other people.**

## 4. Themes (carry as theme, never preach — §4 is the moral spine)

- **THE SPINE — Rutger Bregman against William Golding.** *Lord of the Flies* is a **lie about human
  nature** dressed as a parable; Bregman's *Humankind* marshals the real evidence (the actual marooned
  boys of ʻAta who **cooperated and survived**; the disaster sociology showing crowds in catastrophe
  default to **mutual aid, not the war of all against all**; "elite panic" — that the people who expect
  savagery and pre-emptively arm against it are usually the *authorities and the privileged few*, not the
  ordinary crowd). **This book dramatizes that thesis and never states it.** The collapse keeps *promising*
  the reader the Golding story — and keeps delivering the Bregman one: the roadblock that turns out to be
  neighbours sharing water, not bandits; the township and the suburb that **pool** rather than fight; the
  looting that is mostly frightened people feeding their kids; and — the honest other half — the **real
  predators, who exist, and are few, and are beaten by a community that holds together**, not by one hard
  man. Jakobus is the **proof-of-concept of the thesis**: the most dangerous man in any room, who has spent
  his life *disarming* rooms; the soldier who won't carry a gun; the evidence that strength's truest use is
  to make other people brave. *(Drafting law: the thesis must be EARNED by events, never argued in
  dialogue. If a character ever says "people are basically good," cut it. Let the roadblock-that-was-
  neighbours do the arguing.)*
- **The Knowledge as humility, not power.** The same humility engine the whole project runs on: *knowing-
  about is not knowing-how.* The book is full of people who knew *facts* (the prepper with the gear and no
  community, the engineer who can model a grid but can't find water, the suburbanite with a generator and a
  month of diesel and no plan for month two) — and the thing that actually keeps people alive is **lived
  competence + other people.** Jakobus's mastery is real and earned and *shared*; the lone hoarder's is
  brittle and dies alone. (The prepper-with-everything-and-no-one is a deliberate foil — gear is not a
  plan; the network is the plan, exactly as it was for the Beast.)
- **Endurance without glory** (the Sheltering-Desert inheritance). Survival as attrition, not heroics; the
  un-fun truth of it; the water that is never enough; dignity in simply *lasting* another month. The high
  is staying alive; the cost is watching a body — the country's, and Jakobus's own — come apart by degrees.
- **The cost of knowing** (the trilogy's theme substrate, here at its most literal). Jakobus *saw this
  coming* the way he sees everything coming, and being the one who is ready is its own loneliness — and the
  one death the kit cannot prevent (the insulin) is the limit of all competence: there are things no skill
  saves you from, and the wisdom is in how you stand with someone through the thing you can't fix.
- **Home, last.** Three books took him to the Kalahari and the Sahara and the world; this one is **South
  Africa**, his actual country, and it is where he dies. The book is, quietly, a **love letter to the place
  and its people** — to the specific South African genius for **coping, sharing, and making a plan** ("'n
  boer maak 'n plan," the township stokvel and the suburb WhatsApp group both becoming, under stress,
  exactly the mutual-aid networks Bregman describes). The country that the world (and its own fear)
  underestimates turns out to be **full of the thing the whole series believes in.**

## 5. The South African substrate — load-shedding as the door (author's note)

Load-shedding is **the most natural possible door into this story** because every South African reader has
already lived the first act. The book opens *inside the shared national experience* — the schedule, the
EskomSePush app, the inverters and the gas bottles and the "Stage 6 again" sigh, the candlelit supper that
became normal — and then asks the question the country has half-feared for a decade: *what if it just…
didn't come back?* This is not dystopia imported from elsewhere; it is **the SA present taken one honest
step further**, which is what makes it frightening and what makes it *credible*. And the answer the book
gives is **a South African answer**: not the American prepper's bunker-and-rifle, but **the plan, the
network, the shared cup** — the stokvel, the spaza, the boer-maak-'n-plan, the many-peoples-one-place
fabric the whole project honours, revealed under pressure as the survival technology it always was. *(This
is the same conviction that runs the German-rainbow-nation thread, the Songlines ensemble, the whole HBT
mission: this country and its peoples are underestimated, and they are full of exactly what saves them.)*

## 6. Cast

> **Naming (LOCKED 2026-06-16): role-labels only until the human sensitivity/accuracy read (§9).** The
> outline and the prose draft against *the nurse, the teen, the diabetic child, the engineer, the son,
> the older couple, the prepper, the captain* — **no invented names land until a real sensitivity pass is
> done.** This is deliberate: a name dropping wrong is the kind of thing the read exists to catch, so we
> hold all naming rather than risk it. The cast is the **rainbow-nation fabric** — the point of the
> Bregman thesis is that the *mix* holds.

- **Jakobus Swart** — lead, close-third, 60, dying (he may or may not fully know; the body knows). SSOT-
  bound. The fixer at the end of the road, home at last, useful at last, in the catastrophe he was always
  ready for. **No-gun rule holds** (it is *load-bearing* here — the man who keeps a frightened group from
  arming up against phantoms is the man who proves the thesis). The field-kit is now everyone's salvation.
- **The found group (the ʻAta boys, in effect)** — the handful who leave the city with him; the living
  proof that a random, mixed bunch of frightened strangers **cooperate** when given half a leader and half
  a chance. Drawn from the rainbow fabric; each carries one real competence and one real fear. The group:
  - a **nurse/paramedic** (the medicine literacy; the one who must watch the insulin run out, and who
    teaches Jakobus that standing-with is a skill too);
  - a **young woman engineer or electrician** (the salvaged-solar/inverter competence — *knowing-about*
    meeting Jakobus's *knowing-how*);
  - **THE TEEN — the heir (LOCKED 2026-06-16).** The Golding-bait: the reader braces for the feral child
    and gets the brave one. **This is the person Jakobus's teaching outlives him through** — *the hands on
    the battery at the Wake.* The inheritance comes **directly from Jakobus** (not routed through the
    child's death, §below): he teaches the teen the work, almost without words, and at the end it is the
    teen who restores the failed system — brings the heartbeat, and the dawn, back. The youngest hands;
    maximum thesis payoff (the child is brave, not savage) and the book's whole generational hope. *(The
    teen and the dying diabetic child are **unrelated but bonded** — the teen befriends and shields the
    younger child, fights for the insulin, and loses anyway; the loss does not *make* the heir, but the
    heir carries it. Keep them in **different households** so no one family carries every heaviest beat.)*
  - an **older couple** (the ones the lone-wolf survival fantasy would leave behind, who turn out to be the
    social glue);
  - a **prepper** who joins late, gear-rich and people-poor — the foil, fate LOCKED below.
- **The one who dies (the insulin) — a Type-1 diabetic CHILD (LOCKED 2026-06-16).** Their Type-1 diabetes
  is a **death sentence the moment the cold chain and the supply chain end** — no skill, no kit, no decency
  manufactures a biologic. This is the cruellest, most thesis-*testing* version on purpose: the whole
  "*boer maak 'n plan,* we can solve anything with competence + network" engine runs full-tilt at a child
  who will die. The network sources insulin **once** (a thesis win — strangers help); the Knowledge
  jury-rigs a **cold-chain** from the field-kit (the zeer pot, the weeping hessian water bag, real
  evaporative cooling — [[jakobus-field-kit-canon]]) and buys **weeks** (a Knowledge win); and then the
  supply is simply *gone,* because there is no factory anymore. **Jakobus — the man who fixes anything —
  fixes nothing, and that is the most important thing he does in the book**: he sits with the parent and
  the child through the thing no skill saves you from. (This **pre-echoes his own death** — the battery
  that cannot be recharged, the limit of all competence.) The brave teen (the heir, §above) is bonded to
  this child and loses them. **Melodrama is the danger here**; handle with the restraint the project
  demands and the Sheltering-Desert register — *the dignity is in how they are stood-with, never in a
  miracle, and never milked.* No deathbed speech; the grief is in the work that goes quiet.
- **The refuge community** — the people already in the high country (a Free-State or Drakensberg-edge
  farming district; Jakobus grew up Free State, §SSOT) who take the group in or are joined by them: the
  **boer-maak-'n-plan** competence, the boreholes and the diesel and the cattle, the suspicion-then-welcome
  arc done **without the easy beats** (real friction, real overcoming). The proof that the rural fabric
  holds.
- **The real threat — a corrupt remnant of AUTHORITY (LOCKED 2026-06-16).** **Not** a horde, **not** a
  Golding-tribe. The genuine danger is **the few**: a cop or soldier or official (the "captain") who keeps
  the uniform and the gun after the state that issued them is dead, and turns "order" into a **protection
  racket** — the controlled road, the requisitioned fuel, the checkpoint that is really a toll. This is
  Bregman's **"elite panic" made flesh**: the danger comes not from the frightened crowd but from the one
  who *expected* savagery and **became it pre-emptively, while wearing its legitimacy.** (Sharp thematic
  point: the threat is the man who looks most like safety.) He costs something real — a death, a loss — and
  he is **beaten by the community holding together and by Jakobus's refusal to meet him on his terms**
  (no-gun; out-thought, out-organised, out-*peopled* — mostly by **denying him the fearful, fragmented
  crowd he needs to rule**: a community that shares water and points the way cannot be checkpointed). Never
  a heroic shoot-out. *(Drafting firewall: the moment this becomes one hard man with a rifle saving the
  day, the book has betrayed its own thesis. The win is collective.)* **Kept distinct from the prepper**
  (§below) on purpose — the prepper is pitiable, not menacing; the captain is the menace. Do not fuse them.
- **The prepper — survives hollow, then joins (LOCKED 2026-06-16).** Gear-rich and people-poor: the bunker,
  the rifle, the year of MREs, the generator and the month-two-he-never-planned-for. The **inversion of the
  lone-wolf fantasy** — his preparation *works*, physically: he stays alive. And it is a **tomb.** He is the
  most hollowed-out, frightened, joyless creature in the book — he *won* the Golding game and it cost him
  the only thing that makes survival worth it: other people. Jakobus's lot are hungrier and **happier.**
  Arc: the group's warmth eventually pulls him in; he learns, on the hardest possible case, that **gear is
  not a plan — the network is the plan** (the same lesson the Beast taught, [[jakobus-machine-creed-horse]]
  / the favour-economy). Once he shares it, his gear genuinely helps. **No comeuppance** — he is redeemed
  by community, not punished (consistent with the book's whole no-revenge stance, §The Forger). He is the
  thesis proven on its most resistant subject: even the man who built a life *against* other people is
  saved by them. *(Keep him a foil, never a villain — the captain is the menace; the prepper is the
  cautionary soul who gets a second chance. Do not let his early menace-coding curdle into the threat
  role.)*
- **The Forger — Jakobus's killer** (full bible: [`THE_FORGER.md`](THE_FORGER.md)). A **master forger** of
  the adversary world's *control-of-provenance* creed — the man whose art is the lie made to wear the true
  thing's name; the **thematic anti-Jakobus** (true things & debts-in-person vs. convincing lies & bought
  hands). **NOT of the Brotherhood of Abraham and NOT of the Order** (those are keepers, not killers, and
  they *owe* Jakobus a bloodless debt — the faith-dignity firewall is absolute; see THE_FORGER §firewall).
  He **reveres** Jakobus as the one true thing he could never counterfeit, and when his work finally
  requires Jakobus dead he pays the highest courtesy his trade can: a **clean, kind, unseen death** (§7),
  and a **calling card stating it plainly** — the single un-forged document of his life. He is the rare
  true predator the Bregman thesis always admitted exists; his killing **proves** the thesis by contrast
  (even the assassin is not Golding's savage; he kills one man and changes nothing). **He is the one
  shadow at the edge of a humane story — never the book's engine** (the collapse + the community are the
  engine). *(Binding: no one gets a revenge-kill of the forger; he leaves unpunished and matters not at
  all to what survives — that IS the answer. See THE_FORGER §5.)*
- **Ulfberht "Wolf" Swart — the son: reaches him, and they EARN each other's respect (LOCKED 2026-06-16,
  refined from the partial-thaw)** (SSOT/Wake canon). **The name is canon and load-bearing.** Jakobus —
  the man who deals only in true things — named his son **Ulfberht**, after the genuine crucible-steel
  blade (the [[north-forge-standalone]] sword): *the true thing that gets counterfeited.* The boy grew up
  to "**burn money**," to live in the bought-and-faked world — and goes by "**Wolf**," the lone-wolf name,
  *the very thing his father's whole Bregman thesis stands against.* So the estrangement is **encoded in
  what everyone calls him**: the true-blade's son, living as the lone wolf, in the counterfeit economy. (He
  thus sits inside the Forger's thematic universe — true-name vs. counterfeit — by birth, before the story
  starts.)
  - **The arc closes — no hanging thread; the reader is ultimately satisfied (author's directive).** In a
    world where money is paper and the favour-economy is the only currency left, **Jakobus's whole life is
    brutally vindicated**, and Wolf comes to the refuge. It is **still hard-won, not a tearful
    all-is-forgiven scene** — but it **lands on real, mutual respect.** *(The §7 "do not resolve it cheaply"
    guardrail holds — the respect is **paid for in a crisis, not granted in a hug.**)*
  - **What earns it (LOCKED): they save each other ONCE.** A single concrete crisis where **Wolf's
    world-skill and Jakobus's bush-skill are *both* required** — neither man gets through it alone. Wolf
    turns out to have a real competence the collapse needs (from his money-world: reading people-as-
