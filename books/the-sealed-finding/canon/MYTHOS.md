# MYTHOS — *The Sealed Finding* (binding rules)

> The house rule is **engineering, never woo**. In this book that rule has teeth, because the source ran
> on a supernatural mechanism and we have deliberately removed it. Everything the Directorate can do must
> be buildable with today's technology plus ten years, defensible to an engineer, and boring to a
> statistician. The dread comes from it being *real*, not from it being strange.

## The hard line: there is no precognition

**The Directorate does not see the future. It computes a person.** Nothing in the manuscript may be
ambiguous about this — not a dream, not a Reader's hunch, not a coincidence staged to imply more. If a
scene works only because the reader suspects something uncanny, it is the wrong scene.

What the Directorate actually has:

- **Coverage.** Thirty years of municipal camera estate, vehicle recognition, mobile telemetry, payment
  rails, medical and court and employment record, and — after 2041 — the consolidated *Safety Data*
  mandate that made all of it one queryable substrate. Nobody voted for this. Everybody was frightened.
- **Base rates.** Homicide is not random. It has antecedents: proximity, grievance, escalation, means,
  and a small number of very reliable precursor behaviours. At population scale these produce accurate
  cohort forecasts. Everyone agrees on this. The argument is what you are permitted to do with it.
- **The individual leap.** From cohort to person is the leap the Act made lawful. The stack produces a
  behavioural forecast; a **Reader** — a human being, because the statute requires a human signature on
  a deprivation of liberty — carries the **intent finding**. That signature is the whole legal edifice,
  and it rests on a person in a sealed room who is not told when they were wrong.

## The law of the book (the thing that replaces the Dark Forest / the Substance)

Three clauses. They must be **demonstrated in scene** — discovered, argued, paid for — and never
lectured.

1. **The intervention destroys the evidence.** A prevented murder leaves no body, no confession, no
   counterfactual. The Directorate's accuracy is not merely unmeasured; it is *unmeasurable in principle*.
   This is not a secret. It is paragraph four of the founding white paper, and it is taught at induction.
2. **The machine does not see you. It sees people like you.** The stack's power comes from the cohort;
   the warrant lands on the individual. A man is detained for the future of his statistical neighbours.
   Modernity's addition to Dick: the precogs at least looked at *him*.
3. **Telling a man his future hands him the means to break it — and that break is itself a future.**
   A prediction disclosed becomes an input. This is not paradox; it is feedback, and it is documented in
   a change-control log nobody read (CANON_CHOICES: the recursion).

The three clauses assembled are the book's argument, and **no character ever states all three.** The
reader assembles them (negative-space contract).

## Both truths (mandate #3 — the hardest binding rule in the book)

The book must hold, on every page, without flinching either way:

- **The Directorate is a horror.** It imprisons people who have done nothing, on sealed evidence, mostly
  poor, for as long as the order is renewed, forever.
- **The Directorate works.** Murder in Gauteng has fallen further than any intervention in the country's
  history. Eleven thousand people a year are alive who statistically should not be. The people who built
  it were not cynics; they were mourners.

Any scene that lets the reader settle on one side is a failed scene. Any character who *only* embodies one
side is a failed character — including Grobler, whose case is correct and whose motive is money, and
including Motaung, whose grief is real and whose machine eats the poor.

## The all-sides law (house rule, load-bearing here)

**Indict the machine, never the people.** This book is set in a country with a living memory of preventive
detention, and the named are disproportionately poor and Black. That fact is in the book, in the data, and
in the mouth of a character who has counted it — and the book does not use it to convict any *person* in
the story. The architects are Black, the victims are Black, the Federation's board is mixed, the machine is
nobody's revenge. It is a *system*, built by frightened good people, doing exactly what it was built to do.
That is the accusation, and it is aimed correctly.

## The hard lines (capped reveals)

- **No conspiracy behind the conspiracy.** One level of reveal per device. The Federation is exactly what
  it appears to be by Part Two: an industry with a legally sound grievance and a commercial motive. There
  is no secret cabal above it.
- **No villain with a speech.** Grobler's case is delivered as argument, not as gloating; Lombard's
  betrayal is a payroll entry, not a monologue.
- **No technological miracle and no technological rescue.** Nothing in the stack can be hacked, purified,
  or turned against itself to save anyone. The machine is not the problem. The statute is not the problem.
  *The arithmetic is the problem*, and arithmetic cannot be exposed, only obeyed or refused.
- **The Readers are not oracles and must never be shot as though they were.** No scenes of them in
  reverie, no mystic staging, no lit-from-within. They are shift workers with a headache.

## The one sanctioned scale

Everything is procedural, grounded and human-sized, so that **the recursion** (Part Two) and **the
assembly** (Part Three) can break the reader's footing completely. The book earns those two moments by
being rigorously, boringly real everywhere else.
