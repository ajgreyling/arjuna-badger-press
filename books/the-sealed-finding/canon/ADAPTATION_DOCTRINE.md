# Adaptation Doctrine — *The Sealed Finding*

> How this book is faithful to *The Minority Report* without being a copy of it. The governing rule for
> every canon and manuscript decision in this project.

## The line we hold

Dick's novella is under copyright. We are not licensed. Therefore the fidelity is to **arcs, craft and
engine**, and the text is **ours**. Two failures to avoid, equally:

1. **The legal/ethical failure** — reproducing his plot, scenes, characters, or sentences; or doing the
   same thing with names swapped and details nudged. A "find-and-replace adaptation" is a derivative work
   we have no right to make, and the house has never made one.
2. **The craft failure** — which is the *same* failure wearing a different coat. Beat-for-beat copying
   with substitutions produces airless, second-hand prose. A borrowed skeleton with our flesh stretched
   over it reads as taxidermy. The book has to be **alive on its own ground**.

## The particular tension in this book (read this before drafting)

The author's brief was **"all the original craft elements and arcs adhered to."** That is a fidelity
instruction, and it pulls directly against the anti-transcription rule. Both are binding. They are
reconciled exactly one way, and it is not a compromise:

> **An arc is a shape. A scene is an expression.** [`SOURCE_NOTES.md`](SOURCE_NOTES.md#the-arc-ledger-binding)
> lists twenty-two shapes and every one of them is honoured. **Not one of them is honoured by staging
> Dick's version of it.** "He is taken by the victim's faction" is an arc; a man bundled into a car in
> 1956 New York by a general's aides is a scene, and it is Dick's. Ours happens in a parkade off Steve
> Biko Road, with a signed indemnity form, because the men doing it are a compliance department.

If a chapter can only be described as "the part where the Minority Report thing happens," it has failed —
even though the arc it covers is on the ledger. Cover the ledger; invent the scene.

## What we keep (the engine — ideas, and Dick's gift to the form)

- **Punishment before the act, administered with a clean conscience.** The state is not sadistic. It is
  *proud*, and it has the numbers to be proud with.
- **The unfalsifiable machine.** The intervention destroys the counterfactual. Nobody involved is lying
  about this; it is in the founding white paper, paragraph four.
- **The founder caught by his own instrument** — and the awful competence of a man fleeing a net whose
  mesh size he personally specified.
- **The dissent that is real, correct, and filed.**
- **The recursion** — that the reports were never a vote, and that "the majority" was a category error.
- **The deliberate fulfilment.** He does it knowingly. This is the whole book and it is not negotiable.
- **The trap in the chair,** handed to the successor in the last scene.

## What we invent (everything that is *expression*)

- **Every name, character, relationship, and backstory.** Josiah Motaung is not John Anderton. Ayesha
  Cassim is not Ed Witwer. Miriam Motaung is not Lisa. Cornelius Grobler is not Leopold Kaplan. Dries
  Lombard is not Fleming. Grace Sithole, Wynand Loots and Karabo Ntuli are not Donna, Jerry and Mike, and
  are not disabled, not silent, not in tanks, and not owned. They share a *function* in the engine; they
  do not share a life, a scene, or a line.
- **Every scene and beat.** The PLOT rhymes with the novella's shape (naming → flight → abduction →
  revelation → recursion → deliberate fulfilment → exile → warning) the way any tragedy rhymes with any
  other. The actual events are ours, set in places we built out of a place that exists.
- **The mechanism.** **Inference, not precognition** — a legally-constituted stack of behavioural models,
  three of them, air-gapped, with human Readers who carry the intent finding because the statute requires
  a person's signature on a deprivation of liberty. This is not the precogs with a coat of paint; it is a
  different machine that produces the same moral catastrophe, which is the point.
- **The substrate.** Near-now South Africa: real streets, real weather, real bureaucracy, real history.
  Dick-faithful in *ordinariness and dread*, not in gadgetry. There are no flying cars. There is a
  photocopier that jams.

## The test, applied to every page

Before any scene ships, ask: *if Dick's novella did not exist, would this scene still be a real scene —
its own people, its own logic, its own surprise?* If it only makes sense as a reference, it is taxidermy.
Cut it and build the real one.

Second test, specific to this book: *would a reader who has never heard of the source be able to describe
what just happened without using the words "minority report"?* If not, we have written a footnote.

## Provenance, disclosed

Per the house thesis (*immersion is the craft obligation; provenance is disclosed, never hidden*): the
foreword names the debt to Dick plainly, and the house attribution notice names the work, the author, and
the estate, states that this is published **in honor of the original**, that it is **not endorsed by,
authorized by, or affiliated with** them, and that it is **not for commercial release**. We do not pretend
the engine is ours. We pretend nothing.
