# STYLE_GUIDE — *The Sealed Finding*

> The voice laws. This book has **three craft mandates unique to it** (PROJECT.md) — inference not
> prophecy; the named are people; both truths always — and two registers it must hold apart with a razor.

## The two registers

**1. The prose register — close-third, procedural, dry.** Motaung's POV is the spine: a competent man's
attention, moving over surfaces, noticing the operational detail before the emotional one. Short
declaratives under pressure. Interiority earned by what he *notices*, not by what he explains. No purple,
no thriller-adrenaline prose, no adjectival dread. The book is frightening because it is calm.

**2. The artifact register — flat, institutional, exact.** Findings, custody logs, session transcripts,
the Act's own language. Never editorialised, never winking, never "chillingly worded" — genuinely written
the way a Republic writes. The horror is that it is correct English, properly formatted, and it ends a
life. Use monospace blocks. Keep them short. Three part-head findings + three "THE NAMED" case cards, and
that is the budget (CANON_CHOICES).

**The razor:** the two registers never blend. No character narrates in officialese; no artifact carries a
metaphor. When the registers touch — a man reading his own name on a form — the collision does the work.

> **Anti-self-plagiarism (binding):** [`../the-scramble/`](../the-scramble/) already owns the
> two-column LIVED/WATCHED device. The artifacts here are **not a second narration** of scenes we have
> seen; they are documents about people we mostly never meet. If an artifact starts recapping a chapter,
> cut it.

## Mandate #1 — Inference, not prophecy (binding on prose, not just plot)

- Never write a sentence that would be at home in a story about oracles. No "she saw," no "it came to
  her," no "the future arrived." Readers **assess**. Stacks **compute**. Findings are **filed**.
- Technical language is used correctly and sparingly, the way professionals use it: without explanation,
  in the middle of doing something else. If a paragraph explains the machine, cut it and write the scene
  where someone uses it badly.
- **The one exception, deliberate:** the *public* talks about the Directorate mystically — talk-radio,
  church, a taxi driver — because that is what people actually do with statistics. Let the folk register
  exist in other mouths and never in the narration.

## Mandate #2 — The named are people (binding)

- Every named person who appears gets a body, a Tuesday, and one specific wrong-sized detail. No composite
  victims, no montage of arrests, no "thousands like him."
- **The three case cards** (one per part) each name a real person and do not return. Half a page. The book
  never comments on them.
- The uptake is always shown as courteous, and the courtesy is never undercut by narration. Let the reader
  do it.

## Mandate #3 — Both truths, always (binding)

- Any chapter that lets the reader settle — *the machine is monstrous* or *the machine is necessary* —
  gets rebuilt. The tool is proximity: put the eleven thousand alive and the eleven thousand held on the
  same page and refuse to arbitrate.
- **Nobody says the theme.** No character delivers the thesis; the reader assembles the three clauses of
  the law (MYTHOS) across the book. The single allowed near-statement is in **Karabo Ntuli's Reader's Note**
  — and it is a *question*, not a statement, which is why it is allowed.

## The clock (velocity)

The six days are the engine. **Every chapter carries the clock** — a dated head, and at least one concrete
sign of the day burning. No chapter exists to explain something. If a beat can only be summarised, it is
sag and gets rebuilt as a scene with stakes.

## Idea-as-detonation

Dick's gift is that the twist collapses a *concept*, not a mystery. Two moments in this book are staged at
that calibre and no others:

- **The recursion** (Part Two): the reader realises there was never a majority, and that "right" was never
  a coherent thing to ask. Deliver it as a person working something out under pressure, in a room, with
  something at stake — never as a briefing.
- **The assembly** (Part Three): the shot. **The horror is the reader understanding a half-beat before the
  room that the shot is the only remaining argument.** Trust the image; never caption the awe. No speech.

## House non-negotiables (inherited from Craft Doctrine)

1. **Embody, don't state.** Cut the sentence that explains the sentence before it (§16).
2. **Body before thought** under pressure (§8); under peak stress syntax **breaks**, not refines (§18, §22).
3. **One quotable closer per scene, in one mouth** (§7). Not every paragraph ends on a mic-drop.
4. **No LLM tells:** ration the "not X, but Y" reframe; kill em-dash addiction; cut half the "the way…"
   similes; specific noun over smooth abstraction. Smooth abstraction is also how a procedural book goes
   grey — double-duty rule.
5. **Language braids, never performs.** Afrikaans, isiZulu and Sesotho appear as they actually appear in a
   Gauteng office: unglossed, unitalicised, meaning carried by the scene. Never a character explaining
   their own language to the reader.
6. **Engineering, never woo** (MYTHOS). No precognition, ever, not even as atmosphere.

## The ending guard (§20, §26, L-08)

The machine wins, and the last weight is **human and smaller than the idea**: a man on a decent lawn behind
a fence he specified, handing a thirty-year-old docket to the woman who has his chair, saying the plain
thing. No paragraph explaining what it means. The dedication carries the final note, and it is **original**.
