# WORLD — *The Sealed Finding*

> The substrate is **real near-now South Africa**, November 2054 — Gauteng, mostly: Pretoria for the
> paperwork, Johannesburg and the East Rand for the work, the Highveld for the Estate. Dick-faithful in
> *ordinariness and dread*, not in gadgetry. The future here is thirty years of drift, not a redesign:
> the same potholes, better cameras.

## How it happened (the Brownout years, 2027–2031)

The grid did not fail all at once; it failed the way a tooth goes. Four years of rolling blackout became a
year of no schedule at all, and then the municipalities stopped answering the phone. Water followed
electricity. Policing followed water. The murder rate — already the worst outside a war — went vertical,
and what filled the gap was the **private security industry**: armed response, tactical, convoy, and the
suburbs that could pay paid, and the ones that could not were counted afterwards.

The Preventive Enforcement Act was passed in 2035 by a coalition that agreed on nothing else, with a
three-year sunset clause. The clause has been renewed four times. Nobody campaigns on repealing it. In the
2051 election both major parties promised to *expand* it.

**Murder in Gauteng is now at its lowest recorded level since 1908.** The book never disputes this figure
and never lets the reader forget it.

## The Directorate of Preventive Enforcement

Head office is on **Struben Street, Pretoria** — a 1970s government block with brown tile, a security desk
staffed by a man named Piet who has worked there longer than the Directorate has existed, and a canteen
that does a good bunny chow on Thursdays. There is a two per cent vacancy freeze. There is an audit
committee. There is a communications office that runs a campaign called *Nobody Has To Die Tonight*, and
the campaign is, on the numbers, honest.

### The instrument
- **s.14(2)(a) — the finding of anticipated homicide.** Two of three Readers must concur.
- **The anticipatory warrant.** A person against whom two Readers concur is **named**. The ordinary phrase
  is "he's been named," said the way people used to say "he's been diagnosed." Nobody says *precriminal*;
  the word tested badly.
- **s.14(6) — sealing.** Where one Reader dissents, the dissenting finding is sealed and retained but not
  disclosed to the named person or their representative. Written as a temporary measure. Nineteen years
  old.
- **Preventive custody.** An indefinite order, reviewed annually by a magistrate who has never once
  declined to renew, because declining would require her to be the person who let one out.

### The Uptake
The arrest is called an **uptake** and it is designed to be gentle: two officers, no weapon drawn, a
printed card read aloud, twenty minutes to pack, a call permitted. The Directorate is proud of this and
should be. **The gentleness is the most frightening thing in the book.** A state that does this badly is a
scandal; a state that does it *well* is a settlement, and settlements last.

### The Estate
**Doringkop Preventive Estate**, out past Delmas where the Highveld goes flat and the thunderstorms can be
watched arriving for an hour. Eleven thousand-odd residents. Not a prison: rooms not cells, work programs,
a clinic, a soccer league, family visits every second weekend, a decent library. The word *inmate* is not
used. The word used is **resident**.

Nobody has ever been released except by dying, because release requires someone to sign that the finding
was wrong, and nobody can know that, because of paragraph four.

## The Readers

Three sealed suites in the Struben Street basement, lettered **A**, **B** and **C**. Each holds one person
and one air-gapped stack. The suites share a wall and no door. In nineteen years no two Readers have met.

- They are **never told outcomes**. Knowing would contaminate the next finding. A Reader can serve twenty
  years and never learn whether one single name was right.
- They are recruited from people with no other options, and their consent is documented, witnessed, and a
  fiction (CHARACTERS).
- The stacks are **air-gapped from each other and not from the Directorate** — they ingest the
  Directorate's own operational record as features, because an institution's actions change the world it
  predicts. This is the plumbing that produces the recursion (CANON_CHOICES).

## The Federation

The **Federation of Security Providers** is what is left of the industry the Directorate replaced: 240,000
members at its peak, 31,000 now, a head office in Sandton, a legal fund, and an annual congress every
November. Its chairman is a retired general. Its grievance is commercial and its argument is
constitutional, and the book must never let the reader use the first to dismiss the second.

## Texture rules (so the world reads lived, not researched)

- **Real places, real names, real weather.** Struben Street, Steve Biko Road, the N12, Delmas, Sandton
  convention floors, the smell of a Highveld storm forty minutes before it lands, jacarandas in the wrong
  month because the seasons moved.
- **Real language.** Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho and English braid the way they actually braid in a Gauteng
  office. Untranslated where the scene carries the meaning; never italicised into exotica; never a glossary
  in the mouth of a character.
- **Bureaucracy is the horror's delivery mechanism.** Forms, retention schedules, change-control logs,
  vacancy freezes, a magistrate's annual signature. The worst thing in the book happens because of a
  two-line entry in a log signed by a man who has since emigrated.
- **No spectacle.** There is one set-piece in this book (the assembly) and it is a man standing up in a
  conference centre. Everything else is offices, cars, kitchens, and a very long drive.
