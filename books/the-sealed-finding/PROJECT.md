## The three craft mandates (why this book exists)

The novella is seventy years old and its central machine is no longer a metaphor — we built it. This
retelling exists to do three things the source could not or did not:

1. **Inference, not prophecy.** Dick needed mutants in a tank because in 1956 there was no other way to
   know the future. There is now, and it is worse: a state that does not *see* your future but *computes*
   it, and is right often enough to be unarguable. Every capability in this book must be buildable with
   today's technology plus ten years. **No precognition. Ever.** (MYTHOS hard line.)
2. **The named are people.** The source's precriminals are a statistic — a number in Anderton's proud
   speech about the murder rate. Here the ones the machine takes are **on the page, with names and
   Tuesdays**, and the reader is never once allowed to hold them as arithmetic. (House all-sides law:
   indict the machine, never the people — including the people who run it.)
3. **Both truths, always.** The Directorate is a horror. The Directorate also saves eleven thousand lives
   a year, and the men and women who built it did it for reasons the reader must be unable to dismiss.
   The book never resolves this and never lets the reader off. Dick held both; we hold both harder.

Kept at full calibre from the source: the **paranoid procedural velocity**; the **flat institutional
register** of the paperwork; and above all the **twist as an idea rather than a reveal** — the recursion,
which lands not as "the butler did it" but as the floor going out from under the concept of a verdict.

## Premise (renamed, modernized, original)

Thirty years after the grid failed and the murder rate went vertical, South Africa built the
**Directorate of Preventive Enforcement**: three sealed Readers, three air-gapped inference stacks, and a
legal instrument called the **anticipatory warrant**. Two of three Readers must concur. When one dissents,
the dissent is sealed by law and never disclosed — because a state that admits its machine disagrees with
itself has handed every defence advocate in the country the same question.

**Director Josiah Motaung**, sixty-one, who built the thing after a woman named Beauty Ngobese was murdered
exactly the way he had told three magistrates she would be, receives a finding with his own name on it. In
six days he will shoot a man he has never met: **Cornelius Grobler**, retired general, chairman of the
private-security federation the Directorate put out of business — and Grobler is about to stand up in front
of the country and prove the Directorate imprisons innocent men.

There is a finding that says Motaung will not do it. It is sealed, in his wife's registry, where she has
been quietly keeping every one of them for eleven years.

Both findings are true. That is the problem.

## The structural engine (what makes it *this* book)

**The recursion made structural.** Three parts. Each opens with the verbatim artifact of one finding, and
each part is the same six days walked by a *differently-informed man*: the Motaung who does not know, the
Motaung who has read the first finding, and the Motaung who has read both. The reader is handed all three
findings; **no character ever holds more than one until the end.** That gap is the book's dramatic-irony
engine, and it is the source's twist converted from a reveal into a shape.

**The unfalsifiable machine.** The Directorate's accuracy cannot be measured, because the arrest destroys
the evidence. This is not a flaw the book exposes in Act Three; it is stated in the prologue, admitted by
everyone, and lived with — which is far more frightening. The horror is procedural and consensual.
