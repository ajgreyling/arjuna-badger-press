## 16. The Contact Itself


The material is not only a teaching. It is also, unavoidably, the record of what the teaching cost the three people who took it down, and reading that layer changes how the rest lands.

**The mechanism.** Carla Rueckert was not channelling in the ordinary sense. Ra describes a trance state in which she vacated the body entirely and Ra used it directly, which is why she had no memory of any session and why the physical toll was cumulative and severe. Ra states repeatedly that the information passes through the instrument's own mind, her vocabulary, her concepts, her frameworks, and that this constitutes a permanent limit on accuracy. Where her mind had no available concept, the transmission degraded.

**The cost.** Her health declined across the three years and never fully recovered. Ra's own warnings about this grow more insistent as the sessions proceed, and a substantial fraction of the later transcripts is taken up with the arithmetic of whether she could bear another session. Don Elkins, who asked the questions, deteriorated psychologically during the same period and died in 1984. The contact ended.

**The interference.** From roughly the halfway point, the group reports sustained pressure from a fifth-density negative entity, and the sessions become partly occupied with defence. Whatever one makes of the metaphysics, the group's own account is that doing this work drew something hostile, and that it was persistent and intelligent rather than crude.

**The transient and the lasting.** There is a distinction running through the sessions between questions about timeless principle and what the group called transient questions, meaning specifics about people, history, technology and current events. I should be careful about who it belongs to. It is Don Elkins who draws it most sharply, saying he wants to focus on things that are not transient and apologising when he falls back on the other kind (Session 27.2). Fatigue is separately and repeatedly real: sessions are cut short when the instrument tires, from the first session onward.

What I have not found is Ra stating that transient questions return less accurate answers. That inference is mine, assembled from Don's distinction and the fatigue record, and an earlier draft of this book attributed it to Ra directly. It is still, I think, the most useful key to reading the original — **the philosophical material is where the contact is strongest, and the specific factual claims are where it is weakest** — but you should hold it as one reader's rule of thumb rather than the source's own verdict on itself.
