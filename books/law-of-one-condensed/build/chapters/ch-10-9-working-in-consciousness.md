## 9. Working in Consciousness


The material is thin on technique and says so. Ra declines, again and again, to hand over methods, partly because method without understanding is useless, mostly because instruction that removes the seeker's own work removes the point of the seeking. What it does give is a handful of principles that recur.

### Meditation

The recommendation is close to universal in the sessions and remarkably unadorned: sit, and let the noise settle, so that something below the noise can be heard. No technique is prescribed. The mechanism, in the material's own terms, is that the conscious mind's chatter is the veil's *active* component, and quieting it lets a little traffic through from below.

Two forms are distinguished. **Passive** meditation: emptying, listening, receiving. And **contemplation** or visualisation: holding a single thing steadily in the mind, which trains the will. The material rates the second as harder and, for most, more productive, since the capacity to hold one image without wavering is the foundation of everything the adept later does.

### The disciplines of the personality

Ra's answer, whenever asked how one progresses, comes back to a single unglamorous phrase: the disciplines of the personality. Meaning the long unrewarding labour of knowing yourself, accepting yourself, and then becoming the master of yourself, in that order and no other.

That order is load-bearing. **Knowing** comes first: seeing without flinching what you are, including the parts that are petty, frightened, and cruel. **Acceptance** comes second, and it is not resignation but the refusal to be at war with what you found, on the grounds that self-hatred is a blockage rather than a purification. And only then **control**, which is possible only over something you have first seen and stopped fighting.

Nearly every spiritual failure the material describes comes from attempting these in the wrong order, from trying to control a self one has not yet honestly looked at.

### Balancing

The one specific practice Ra describes, and the most useful thing in the material for daily life.

At the end of a day, find the moments that carried charge: the flash of anger, the stab of envy, the surge of desire. Take one and look at it honestly, without deciding in advance that you should not have felt it.

Then find its antithesis in yourself. Ra's instruction is that every thought has an opposite already present in you, and the discipline is to identify both the things you approve of in yourself and the things you don't, and balance each charge against its equal (Session 5.2). What follows is acceptance of the whole, which Ra names as the second discipline and treats as the harder one.

On anger specifically, Ra's language is worth keeping precise, because a great deal of bad advice has been built on paraphrases of it. The instruction is not to vent the anger and not to suppress it, but to have it understood, accepted, and integrated, so that the considerable energy anger began with is reclaimed rather than discharged (Session 46). The aim is reintegration, not catharsis.

The aim is not to become a person who does not feel anger. It is to become a person for whom anger is no longer a *hole*, an unbalanced place where energy leaks. The balanced entity still feels everything. It is simply no longer pulled off course by what it feels, and the material is explicit that suppression achieves the exact opposite: what is pushed down stays charged, and keeps steering.

### Faith and will

The two wings, in Ra's account, of everything that works.

**Will** is the capacity to want one thing consistently over time, the engine, and the thing that separates the adept from the enthusiast. **Faith** is action taken without the support of evidence, which is the only kind available under a veil. Will without faith becomes brittle and eventually negative in flavour; faith without will stays a pleasant feeling that never does anything. Together they are, more or less, the whole of the practical teaching.
