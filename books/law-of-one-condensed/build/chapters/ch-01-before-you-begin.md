## Before You Begin


Between 1981 and 1984, three people in Kentucky sat down a hundred and six times to ask questions of something that called itself Ra. Don Elkins asked. Carla Rueckert lay unconscious on a bed and spoke. Jim McCarty wrote it down. The transcripts run to something over three hundred thousand words, and they are, by common agreement of nearly everyone who has tried, unreadable straight through.

Not because they are bad. Because they are a transcript. The same ground gets walked four times. A question in session 12 is answered again, better, in session 60, and neither answer refers to the other. Whole exchanges are consumed by logistics: the instrument's fatigue, the alignment of the candles, whether the session may continue. And Ra's own register is strange, formal, sideways, fond of the word *distortion* where an ordinary speaker would say *thing*.

What follows is an attempt to lift the argument out of the transcript.

**What this is.** A paraphrase and a rebuild. I have taken the ideas, ordered them as they would be ordered if someone were teaching rather than answering, and written them in plain English. Ra's technical vocabulary is kept where it carries real weight (*density*, *catalyst*, *harvest*, *distortion*, *service to others*) because those words do work that ordinary English words don't, and because they let you find your way back into the original.

**What this is not.** It is not the Ra material. It is a reading of it. Every condensation is an argument about what matters, and mine will have left out something that matters to you. Where I have compressed four sessions into a paragraph, I have made a judgment call, and you did not get to vote on it. Nothing here should be treated as the source. The source is free and easy to find, and the last chapter tells you how to enter it.

**On the contradictions.** You said you found some, and you were right to. The material contradicts itself in places, drifts in others, and is at its shakiest exactly where it is most specific about history. I have not smoothed this over. There is a chapter near the end that lays the problems out on the table, because a condensation that hides the seams is worse than no condensation at all. It hands you a false confidence you didn't earn and can't check.

**On copyright.** The Ra material is copyright L/L Research. They permit free non-commercial reproduction of the original, which is unusually generous and worth honouring. This document is a derivative work, my words carrying their ideas, and it sits fine for private reading. It should not be sold or published under any imprint without asking them first. They are reachable, and they are reasonable.

**On verification.** Every substantive claim in this book has been checked against the full corpus of all 106 sessions, and where a claim is specific or surprising I cite the session so you can go and look. That process found eight errors in my first draft, which I have corrected rather than quietly patched:

The corpus is about 306,000 words, not the 700,000 I had asserted. Ra explicitly denies the asymmetry of difficulty I had invented between the two paths, and offers a much better idea in its place, the sinkhole of indifference. I had made the material's account of illness considerably more defensible than it is. I misattributed the relistening project. I put the negative path's reversal in the middle of sixth density when Ra puts it early. I embellished the balancing practice with an instruction to intensify the emotion that Ra does not give. I attributed to Ra a hierarchy of reliability that is really Don Elkins's distinction plus my own inference. And I claimed a failed prediction that, on inspection, has not failed and cannot fail for another six centuries.

Two of those, the illness passage and the balancing instruction, were cases of me making the material either safer or more vivid than it is. Those are the failure modes to watch for in any condensation, including the parts of this one I did not manage to catch. Assume some remain.

One more thing. The material asks to be tested, not believed. Ra says so repeatedly, and it is the single most trustworthy thing about the whole strange document: take what is useful, leave the rest without a backward glance, and do not accept a word of it on the authority of its source. Read it in that spirit and it can't hurt you.
