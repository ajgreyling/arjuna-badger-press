# PLOT — *The Belly Hill* (working title) — the Göbekli Tepe book (Why Files, Book 2)

> Register: **The Why Files** (official story straight → the one hole → the wink; live in the gap).
> POV: **the crew owns it** (rotating close third) — the line's new model; this is the book where the crew
> *assembles*. The **local out-reads them** (Bible §3) and the answer is hers to give. Jakobus is the road.
> Author's call: **abandon the other series rules; crank it out; have fun.** The grounded-procedural grain
> (Blomkamp/§8) under the warmth law. The *maybe* is the point — never resolve to woo OR to "mystery solved."

## The middle path (never leave it)
- The famous monuments (Stonehenge, the Pyramids) = the decoy the eye is trained on. Göbekli Tepe is
  **~6,000 years older** and most people have never heard of it. The official solve — **"hunter-gatherer
  ritual centre, ~9,500 BCE"** (Klaus Schmidt, secure radiocarbon) = **TRUE and revered.** The **crack** is
  *mainstream*, which is what makes it lethal: it **inverts the order of civilisation** — monuments came
  *before* farming, pottery, the wheel, writing — and the archaeologists themselves ask whether *gathering
  to build this drove the invention of farming.* No aliens. The truth is weirder. The real target = the
  **~90% still in the ground** (~20 enclosures, ~200 T-pillars; only ~10% dug) and the buried sister-sites
  (Karahan / the Taş Tepeler). The thing the world calls a finished mystery is **mostly unopened**, and
  someone wants in before the people whose ground it is.

## Act I — the potato (ch-01–05)
1. **The Belly Hill** — COLD OPEN on the official story, played straight and a little funny: a slick
   foreign **"heritage foundation"** films a glossy promo on the Urfa plain — *"the world's first temple,
   solved, catalogued, safe in our hands"* — the tidy version, the doorknob. The crew is there at the edge
   of the cordon (Jakobus brought them; see ch-03). The **potato** lands when the foundation's own hired
   "experimental archaeology" stunt — modern crew, modern kit, a day to raise a replica T-pillar "the easy
   way" — **can't do it clean**: the block fights them, the copy comes out crude and wrong, and the
   foundation quietly cuts the camera. **Naila** has been timing it. She has the numbers for exactly how
   hard the "easy" version is. The hook: the promo meant to *close* the site just showed everyone the hole.
2. **Seven to Fourteen** — Naila's file: the **labour arithmetic** (E. B. Banning — *7–14 people* could
   move a pillar with ropes + a lubricated track; the "impossible workforce" is an over-reach, and she kills
   it cold). But the thing she *can't* flatten is the **order**: foragers, pre-pottery, pre-farming, raising
   8–10-tonne *people* in stone. She reads the **shared template** across Karahan/Taş Tepeler — the same
   measure, the same grammar, repeated across sites that shouldn't share one. (First whisper of the season
   spine — Bible §6. Plant it; do not pay it off.) She's spent a career proving things *aren't* mysteries.
   She can flatten every claim here but one.
3. **The Man Who Read the People** — **Jakobus, and how the crew got here.** He *gathered* them between
   Book 1 and now — Edie Vane's chalk showed him the holes are a **pattern, not a one-off**, the thing Naila
   exists to prove — and he sent for the ones he's been watching: **Frik** (the LIE), the **LENS** (the
   camera he keeps asking to lower). Far from home (the Beast shipped; his desert field-kit — the cayenne
   tin, the clean hanky, the weeping water bag — works on the Anatolian plain when little else of his
   African mastery ports; and that's the point again). He doesn't read the pillars the way believers or
   debunkers do. He reads them as **made things with a purpose** — *the T-pillars are people* (the relief
   arms, the fingered hands meeting over a belt; Pillar 18's fox-pelt loincloth) — and asks the master
   question: *not who, not is-it-real, but **what is it for.*** HANDOFF: it's not theirs to dig or to
   answer — Şanlıurfa has its own people, its own ground, its own permits.
4. **The One Who Owns the Ground** — the **local lead arrives and out-reads them all.** A Şanlıurfa-region
   **Kurdish/Turkish field archaeologist** on the Taş Tepeler project — she reads **what the carvings *mean*
   to the people whose land this is**, the thing no outsider's instruments can reach. The crew reads the
   *pattern*; she reads the *place.* The found-family clicks into being around her, deferring. (The **LENS**
   walks straight into the consent-to-photograph beat — wants the gorgeous shot of the dig, the local woman
   against the pillars; learns the local rules of the image; lowers the camera. Recurring, not one-off.) The
   book LOVES the archaeology — names Schmidt, reveres the radiocarbon, treats the science as the wonder it
   is. (The fish heckles: "so the temple came *before* the farm? …go on.")
5. **The Foundation surfaces** — the clean-handed adversary's real shape: the "heritage foundation" is
   buying excavation access, geophysics data, and rights to the **unopened 90%**, weaponising the *official
   story* (the Book-1 rhyme — *the debunk is the cover*): **"it's a solved site, just a gathering-place"**
   is exactly the line that lets them quietly strip the unexcavated enclosures of provenance. Give them a
   **mortal want and clean hands** (§6) — never a moustache. Naila's arithmetic + Jakobus's read + the
   local's standing are the thing that can't be un-seen. The familiar deniable race — but the prize is
   *provenance itself*: the oldest made things on Earth.

## Act II — the buried tradition (ch-06–13)
- The real deep-map: **Enclosure D**, the T-pillars as **people**, the **Taş Tepeler** sister-sites
  (Karahan, Nevalı Çori) as a *whole regional tradition ~90% still in the ground* — the spine is legitimate,
  current archaeology (the **monuments-before-agriculture** inversion; the **shared template/measure** across
  sites). The grounded-procedural grain runs here (§8): permits and provincial bureaucracy, dust and diesel
  and rust, the gap between the foundation's glossy promo and the real, mud-on-the-boots dig — the *texture*
  that makes the wonder land harder, never replacing the warmth.
- **Naila's arc:** the professional flattener who built a career on *no* has to follow a *maybe* without
  losing her rigour — the *Why Files* discipline made flesh (she never stops checking; that's what makes the
  one thing she can't flatten matter). Her pattern-cognition *is* the read of the buried tradition. (Rendered
  from the inside — the cost (the crowds at the Urfa bazaar, no exits) and the sideways warmth both real;
  **humanised, never caricatured**, lived-experience veto.)
- **The contested burial** — the *real* scholarly fight, staged honestly: Schmidt's **intentional-backfill**
  ("they buried their own temple") vs the post-2014 **landslide/slope-wash** reading. The crew (and the
  foundation, who'd love a tidy answer) split on it. The narrator never settles it. Frik, at the worst/best
  moment, **blurts the lie** the foundation is leaning on — the smooth "it's all decommissioned and done,
  nothing left to protect" — names it out loud in front of everyone, where it can't be taken back. (⚠ the
  Tourette's rendered with full dignity, consultant veto, never swearing-for-laughs.)
- A **Beast set-piece** on the plain / a night run between Göbekli and a Taş Tepeler site the foundation is
  quietly stripping; the local's community as the ones who actually hold the ground.
- Theme as EFFECT only: the answer is not "aliens" and not "just a gathering-place" — it's *what we became
  may have started with the thing we built together, not the thing we grew.* Never spoken.

## Act III — the wink (ch-14–18)
- The **"what is it for"** turn (the grounded payoff that *re-opens* wonder): the T-pillars are **people** —
  the enclosures a place the living gathered to **stand with the raised dead / the watching ancestors** — and
  the act of *gathering to build* is, plausibly, **the thing that made them settle, and farm, and become
  us.** The monument didn't follow civilisation; it may have *caused* it. **The local hands the crew that
  reading — it's hers** — and the narrator never declares it proven. The wonder gets *larger,* not smaller.
- The unopened enclosures protected / the foundation's deniable grab broken — **the locals own the win**;
  the crew helped, then stepped back; Jakobus is the road. The buried-or-not question **stays a maybe** to
  the last page.
- Final register: not proof. The book ends where *The Why Files* ends — official story intact, hole still
  open, a wink. The foundation's glossy promo gets quietly shelved (its tidy ending walked out the back
  door); the real, messier, *bigger* story stays in the ground, mostly unopened, waiting for the people
  whose ground it is. **The shared template** is noted and *not* explained (season spine, kept a maybe).
- Reverse-payoff / man-no-camera-can-hold: the foundation's drones and the LENS's best frame never get a
  clean shot of Jakobus; the one survey flyover is the hour he's under the awning with the local's uncle,
  drinking tea, off every lens.
