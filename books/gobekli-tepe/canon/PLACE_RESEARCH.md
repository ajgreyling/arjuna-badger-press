## 1. The headline facts (official story, straight — REVERE these) [FACT]

*All load-bearing; cross-checked against §D.12.*

- **Date.** Pre-Pottery Neolithic; the oldest enclosures ≈ **11,600 years old (~9,500 BCE)** — ~6,000 years
  **before** Stonehenge and the Pyramids. Secure, peer-reviewed radiocarbon from **multiple labs**.
  Calibrated dates for **Enclosure D ≈ 9,650–9,000 BCE** (late PPNA). Built and used **before** farming,
  pottery, the wheel, writing, or metal.
- **Excavation.** From **1995** by **Klaus Schmidt** (DAI), with stratigraphic control + radiocarbon
  cross-checking, until his death in **2014**; continued by his successors (Kinzel, Clare, Dietrich) and the
  Taş Tepeler project. UNESCO World Heritage. *(See §7 correction on the 1994/1995 start year — use 1995.)*
- **The form.** Paired freestanding **T-pillars** of local limestone, set in roughly circular **enclosures**.
- **The flagship pair — Enclosure D.** Its two central T-pillars (**Pillars 18 and 31**) stand
  **≈ 5.5 m tall and weigh ≈ 8–10 tonnes each.** The **crossbar = the head, the shaft = the body.**
- **The pillars are PEOPLE.** Pillars 18 & 31 carry low-relief **arms bending at the elbow**, and **hands
  with individual fingers** meeting over a **belt / loincloth** — this is the book's uncanny, checkable
  spine (hang scenes on it). Pillar 18 is associated with a **fox-pelt loincloth** and a **carved fox**.
- **The bestiary.** Densely carved with **dangerous animals**: foxes, lions/leopards, boars, aurochs,
  snakes, scorpions, vultures, spiders, cranes.
- **The scale of the buried whole.** Tell **≈ 15 m high, ≈ 9 ha, ≈ 300 m across.** Geophysics + excavation
  indicate **≈ 20 enclosures and ≈ 200 T-pillars** in the mound — and **only ~5–10% has been excavated**
  (about four enclosures exposed). **The famous site is mostly still in the ground.**
- **A whole tradition, not one hill.** **Karahan Tepe**, **Nevalı Çori**, and the **Taş Tepeler ("Stone
  Hills")** project share the **T-pillar DNA.** Göbekli is the **flagship of a regional tradition, ~90%
  still buried.** *(Detail in §4.)*

## 2. THE REAL HOLE (the mainstream textbook-breaker — lean here hardest) [FACT / live debate]

> This is the residue the book actually stands on. It is **accepted archaeology**, which is exactly why it
> is lethal: no skeptic can wave it away.

- **Monuments before farming.** Göbekli was raised by **pre-agricultural hunter-gatherers** — no domestic
  plants, no pottery, no permanent towns — yet the enclosures required **sustained labour, ritual
  coordination, and (probably) gathering-based feasting.** This **inverts** the textbook order
  (farming → surplus → towns → *then* monuments). Here the **monuments come first.**
- **The open question the archaeologists ask themselves:** did the need to **assemble crowds to build and
  feast** *drive* the shift to agriculture (reverse causation), or did the two develop **in parallel**?
  **Genuinely unresolved.** The narrator never settles it; the crew can establish the *facts* around it and
  still be left with the **WHY** (§6).
- **Honour the accuracy caveat (do not romanticise).** Newer work found **domestic structures**, **heavy
  cereal processing** (standardised grindstones/mortars ≈ **40% of the stone-tool assemblage**; phytoliths
  confirm wild wheat/barley ancestors processed at scale on the terraces by Enclosure D), and a **spring-fed
  water system** on the hilltop. This **softens Schmidt's "temple of nomads, nobody lived here."** **The
  monuments-or-farming astonishment SURVIVES; the "purely a sanctuary" claim is being REVISED.** The book
  uses the **honest current picture**, not the romantic old one. *(See §5 fringe note on over-romanticising
  "nobody lived here.")*

## 3. THE SECOND HOLE — the burial (genuinely disputed; PERFECT for the maybe) [CONTESTED]

> The book's staged **scholarly fight** — real, current, and unsettled. **The narrator must resist settling
> it** (tone law). Both the crew and the foundation (who would love a tidy answer) split on it.

- **Schmidt's reading (until 2014): intentional backfill.** He argued the enclosures were **ceremonially
  "decommissioned"** — rapidly filled with anthropogenic debris as a closure rite.
- **The post-Schmidt reading (now in favour): natural burial.** Kinzel, Clare & Dietrich now lean toward
  **landslide / slope-wash** from adjacent higher knolls burying **Enclosure D** — evidence includes
  **pillars leaning in the same compass direction** (suggesting a single event) and the composition of the
  fill.
- **Why it stays open (don't collapse it):** the **anthropomorphic limestone heads** found near **Pillar 31**
  (at least two confirmed, intentionally deposited) point to **deliberate human placement.** **Both
  mechanisms may have acted.** So *"they buried their own temple on purpose"* vs *"the hill buried it"* is
  **genuinely live**, not settled. **[DO-NOT-STATE]** either position as fact.
- **Story use (from PLOT Act II):** Frik **blurts** the foundation's lie that leans on the tidy version —
  *"it's all decommissioned and done, nothing left to protect"* — and names it where it can't be taken back.
  The disputed burial is the lever; the foundation needs it *settled*, the truth keeps it *open*.

## 4. The regional tradition — the REAL target (the ~90% still in the ground) [FACT]

> The thing the world calls a "finished mystery" is **mostly unopened**, and that is the chase's stakes:
> someone wants in before the people whose ground it is. The **shared template across these sites** is the
> book's **season-spine whisper** (§8 below; plant, never pay off).

- **Karahan Tepe** (≈ 11,000 years old, ~15 km south of Göbekli). **[FACT]** Preserves **250+ T-pillars
  *in situ*** with **realistic carved heads and facial features**, and **rock-cut underground ritual
  chambers** centred on anthropomorphic figures. Overlaps Göbekli's symbolic repertoire but uses a **distinct
  design** (underground chambers vs. open enclosures).
- **Nevalı Çori** (≈ 8,400–7,500 BCE). **[FACT]** T-pillars and the same anthropomorphic tradition; a
  tower-like structure; its **later dating** shows the **T-pillar tradition spanned ~3,000 years** across
  multiple sites.
- **Taş Tepeler ("Stone Hills").** **[FACT]** A geospatial project mapping **~20 contemporaneous or
  near-contemporary PPN sites** across the Urfa region (Hamzan Tepe, Harbetsuvan Tepesi, Sefer Tepe, Taşlı
  Tepe, and more). **~90% of the tradition remains unexcavated.** **This is what the foundation is buying
  access to.**
- **The genuinely-open question of transmission. [CONTESTED]** How is a shared symbolic + architectural
  template carried across **~90 centuries** and **200+ T-pillar sites** (per geophysics), when each site
  makes **distinct architectural choices** (Göbekli: open enclosures; Karahan: underground chambers; Nevalı
  Çori: a tower)? One continuous ritual practice? Periodic revival? Independent reinvention? **The data do
  not yet answer this.** *(This is the honest, in-world seed of the "who keeps leaving the same measure"
  season-spine whisper — keep it a question, never an answer.)*

## 5. The grounded HOW — who, how, is-it-real (so the book can KILL the over-reaches) [FACT / CONTESTED]

> The crew's job (PLOT Act I–II) is to flatten the easy mysteries with rigour, so that the **one thing they
> can't flatten — the WHY — lands clean.** Naila does **arithmetic and pattern, never miracles** (load-bearing
> humanity law). The book **resists the "impossible" over-reaches** — it is astonishing **without** being
> impossible.

- **WHO — foragers. [FACT]** Hunter-gatherers, pre-pottery, pre-farming. Not a lost race, not "advanced
  ancients." Seamlessly integrated into the **PPNA chronology** and local Neolithic sequences (which is
  exactly why lost-civilisation claims fail — §9).
- **HOW (moving the pillars) — the Banning arithmetic. [FACT/CONTESTED]** Against the popular "hundreds of
  workers," **E. B. Banning's** friction analysis is mechanically sound:
  - **~7 adult men** to move a pillar **on flat ground**; **~14** to start one **up an ~8° slope.**
  - Erection via **ramp, ropes, and levers** (as at Stonehenge / Easter Island) was **within the capability
    of extended families or clans.**
  - **The Banning Track Method:** **lubricated wooden tracks** (animal fat or water) cut friction by **~50%**,
    letting a single strong team move stones that *look* impossible. *(So: astonishing, NOT impossible. Naila
    kills the "impossible workforce" cold — PLOT ch-02 "Seven to Fourteen.")*
  - **[CONTESTED]** "Hundreds of workers" is the over-reach the book rejects; Banning's low estimate is the
    grounded counter. Present the labour as **clan-scale and clever**, not mythic.
- **HOW (the carving) — the open skill-timeline. [CONTESTED]** The pillars are **anatomically precise**:
  low-relief **arms, hands, separated fingers**, facial features and animal motifs, cut with **stone tools
  (likely flint chisels)** on the **narrow sides** of the Ts. But **who trained the carvers**, and how many
  **generations of apprenticeship** the stylistic evolution represents, has **no Early-Neolithic
  art-historical framework** to answer it. **The pillars appear fully formed — implying a prior tradition.**
  *(A real, honest open seam; not woo — craft-history we simply don't have yet.)*
- **IS-IT-REAL — yes. [FACT]** **Secure radiocarbon** anchors it (§1). The carved pillars and the
  stratigraphy are physical and present. *(One honest wrinkle, §7: early radiocarbon ran ~400–800 years
  *later* than Schmidt's stratigraphic expectation; now explained by methodology — old-wood/sampling — not
  by any anomaly in the site. Modern dating is secure.)*
- **THE SKULL CULT — confirmed practice, contested meaning. [FACT / CONTESTED]**
  - **[FACT]** **Modified human crania** (~11,000 years old): evidence of **secondary burial, defleshing,
    decapitation, ritual display**; **deep grooves carved into skulls with flint tools**; a **limestone
    kneeling "gift-bearer" statue holds a human head in both hands.**
  - **[CONTESTED]** Whether this is **ancestral veneration**, **ritual trophy-taking**, or
    **sacrifice/punishment** — and whether displayed skulls are **enemies, kin, or revered figures** — is
    **ambiguous and genuinely open.** The "skull cult as proto-religion" reading is one interpretation;
    a violent-conflict reading is possible and under-examined. **Do not let the narrator decide.** *(This
    feeds the §6 "what is it for" without supplying a verdict — the dead were clearly central; what the
    living *did* with them is the maybe.)*

## 7. Corrections / DO-NOT-STATE-AS-FACT (so the book doesn't inherit a source's error) [DO-NOT-STATE]

> Honour these; they keep the residue clean and the prose honest. Prefer **"approximately / roughly"** over
> false precision throughout.

- **Excavation start year:** sources vary (1994 survey vs 1995 formal). **Use 1995.**
- **Pillar height/weight:** most sources say **~5.5 m** (some older say 5 m); weights **~8–10 t** are
  **orders of magnitude, not exact.** **Use "approximately."**
- **The radiocarbon offset:** early dates ran **~400–800 years *later*** than Schmidt's stratigraphic
  estimate — now explained by **methodology (old-wood / reservoir / sampling)**, **not** any anomaly in the
  site. **Modern dating is secure**; the older literature reflects the past uncertainty. Do **not** dramatise
  the offset as a "mystery."
- **Tell size:** cite **~9 ha** (consensus), diameter **~300 m**; "8 ha" reflects earlier surveys. Note the
  figure is subject to survey precision.
- **Percent excavated:** **"approximately 5–10%"** (most recent work tends lower, ~5%). **Avoid false
  precision.**
- **The burial:** Schmidt's "rapidly / intentionally backfilled" is **his interpretation, not fact**; the
  post-2014 natural-burial reading is **also not settled.** **State neither as settled** (§3).
- **"World's oldest temple":** archaeologically conventional but **interpretive** — "temple" implies a
  religious function some scholars won't assert. If being precise, prefer **"monumental enclosure" / "ritual
  centre" / "sanctuary."** (Schmidt used "temple"; a character may, the narrator should be careful.)
- **"11,600 years old":** a **shorthand** for ~9,600 BCE; calibrated Enclosure D ≈ **9,650–9,000 BCE
  (~11,600–11,000 years ago).** **Do not treat as absolute.**
- **Pillar 18's fox:** described variously as "cradled," "held," or "beside the arm." **Do not over-specify
  the pose** — use **"carved alongside / associated with a carved fox."**
- **The "fox-pelt loincloth":** an **interpretation** of the carving's appearance; **no biological evidence**
  (tanned leather, fur impressions) survives from PPNA contexts. Archaeologists hedge: **"a fox pelt or
  similar garment."** Write it as a reading, not a proven fact.
- **"Nobody lived here":** Schmidt's older "temple of nomads" framing is **undermined** by domestic
  structures, cereal processing, and water management (§2). A character or narrator implying it should cite
  the **post-2014 evidence**, not the old model.

## 9. FRINGE — clearly labelled (a character may over-reach; the narrator NEVER confirms) [FRINGE]

> Kept on purpose. Useful as **texture** and as a **flaw to dramatise** (the LENS chasing the sensational
> frame; the foundation's hired believers; a credulous tourist beat). **Never load-bearing. The narrator's
> verdict is always the real residue + the maybe.**

- **Astronomical-alignment claims.** Some sources argue the **7 birds on Pillar 18's pedestal align with the
  Pleiades' heliacal rise** (or midday shadow patterns). Göbekli's location and solar orientation are real,
  but the **Pleiades reading is one researcher's conjecture**, not mainstream-endorsed. A character may treat
  it as established (an over-reach); **label it as speculation.**
- **Ancient-astronaut / advanced-technology narratives.** Claims that the carving precision or the "sudden"
  appearance of monumental architecture implies **non-human / supernatural intervention.** This **contradicts
  the record** (gradual refinement of stone-carving; stylistic evolution). "Göbekli is impossible for humans
  to build" rests on **underestimating hunter-gatherer capability** (§5 Banning), not evidence. A character
  voicing it is **unreliable** on this point — dramatise the over-reach; **never confirm.**
- **"Atlantis" / lost-civilisation speculation.** Framing Göbekli as a **pre-flood advanced civilisation** or
  a **lost people unrelated to modern humans.** **No archaeological, genetic, or stratigraphic evidence**;
  the site sits seamlessly in the PPNA sequence. **Do not lend credence.**
- **Over-romanticising "nobody lived here."** Schmidt's early **"temple of nomads"** line is now a media
  shorthand undermined by the domestic/cereal/water evidence (§2). Treat the stark "purely a sanctuary,
  uninhabited" version as a **revised claim**, not current fact.
- **The "giant workforce" over-reach.** "Hundreds / thousands of workers" / "impossible without modern
  equipment" — **factually false** against Banning's **7–14 people** with ropes, tracks, leverage (§5). A
  character impressed by the labour should cite **Banning**, not mythologised numbers.
- **"Pottery-absence = non-human builders."** Some treat the **absence of pottery** as anomalous/supernatural.
  **Pottery is a technology choice, not a civilisation-level marker** — foragers often lacked it while
  building sophisticated architecture and art. This is **standard Neolithic archaeology**, *not* an anomaly;
  do not let it be misrepresented as one.

## 10. Şanlıurfa / the Urfa plain — present-day setting (grounded, respectful) [FACT]

> The **place is not a puzzle-box for the crew's growth** (tone law). The texture below is for the
> grounded-procedural grain (Blomkamp/§8: mud, rust, bureaucracy, fluorescent light, heat) **and** for the
> warmth + the wonder that **sends curiosity toward Şanlıurfa** (the line's tourism echo). The
> **consent-to-photograph** beat with the local community is **live and recurring** (the LENS). The local
> lead **out-reads the crew** on what this ground means. ⚠ Community customs are **PROVISIONAL** pending the
> in-culture read.

### The land
- **Şanlıurfa** sits on a **limestone plateau** in **SE Turkey**, ~15 km from Göbekli Tepe, on a **ridge
  overlooking the Euphrates valley.** The **Urfa plain** runs east and south — **semi-arid grassland** giving
  way to **scrub oak and pistachio woodland**, **treeless for long stretches**, exposed to **wind and
  seasonal flash flooding.** Summer heat reaches **~45°C** on open streets.
- **Limestone is the visual constant:** the city's buildings, minarets, market stalls, and the terraced
  slopes are the **same tan-and-pale-grey stone** that formed the **T-pillars 11,600 years ago.** *(A real,
  load-bearing rhyme — the city is built of the temple's stone.)*
- **The sky.** From the excavation trenches, **sunset throws the T-pillars and enclosures into amber light**;
  at night the stars over the Urfa plain are **vivid and unobstructed — the same sky the PPNA builders saw.**

### The city texture (use as scene fuel)
- **Balıklı Göl** (the **sacred carp pool**, "Pool of Abraham") in the city centre, in the courtyard of the
  **Halil-ur-Rahman mosque** (Ayyubid, **1211 CE**). **Spring-fed, cool even in high summer**, framed by
  Ottoman gardens and stone seating. The **carp are a protected species** — mostly dark grey/black, the rare
  **white one considered blessed.** **Vendors sell small bags of fish food** from weathered stalls; feeding
  the fish is **both tourist ritual and spiritual devotion** (handle with respect; ⚠ in-culture read).
- **The Gölbaşı gardens** (rebuilt Ottoman) frame the pool with **cypresses, pomegranate and fig trees** —
  the heat and dust cut by **running water and bird calls.**
- **The Gumruk Han**, a **1563 Ottoman caravanserai** (Behram Pasha, under Süleyman; one of ten surviving),
  with **water piped from Balıklı Göl** through its courtyards — **stone arches, narrow shaded passageways**,
  merchants' stalls along the interior bazaar.
- **The central bazaar:** **wool merchants, copperworkers, sellers of dried fruit and pistachios**; narrow
  **covered lanes (bedestens)** that hold a steady cool despite the 45°C streets above. *(This is Naila's
  cost-beat ground: PLOT — the crowds at the Urfa bazaar, no exits; render the dysregulation from the inside,
  humanised, never caricatured, distinct from Jakobus's.)*

### Food (where the warmth lives)
- **Turkish çiğ köfte** — a **raw bulgur-and-spice mixture** (with herbs; the meat version is the older
  form), **hand-mixed, never cooked**, sold by street vendors and at family meals. Its preparation echoes the
  **Neolithic grinding of grain and mixing of foraged plants** — a quiet, real through-line to the dig. *(A
  good Jakobus/crew table beat; ⚠ regional food customs, in-culture read.)*
- **The dig camp:** local labour from surrounding villages (**Çevirme, Orencik**) and archaeologists **share
  meals** — **bread, cheese, olives, lentil soup, tea served all day.** The **heat and dust are relentless**
  on a summer dig. *(Jakobus's field kit — the cayenne tin, the always-clean hanky, the Beast's weeping
  water bag — ports to arid Anatolia when little else of his African mastery does; cross-ref the Jakobus
  SSOT, do not re-derive.)*

### Language (Bashir's ground — light touch unless a beat needs him)
- **Turkish dominant; Arabic widely understood; Kurdish (Kurmanji) in the surrounding villages.** The city
  sits on old **Silk Road junctions** and carries **Persian, Armenian, Arab, and Kurdish** cultural layers.
  *(Bashir reaches the local in their own tongue and the room half-disarms; the **Sawubona courtesy** is
  Jakobus's, not Bashir's — cross-ref the Jakobus SSOT.)*

### The dig logistics (the procedural grain)
- Teams working **Göbekli Tepe + the Taş Tepeler project** are **based in Şanlıurfa and commute daily.** The
  **DAI maintains a research house + storage/curation facility** in the city. **Permits and provincial
  bureaucracy, diesel and rust**, the gap between the foundation's **glossy promo** and the real **mud-on-the-
  boots dig** — the texture that makes the wonder land harder, never replacing the warmth.
