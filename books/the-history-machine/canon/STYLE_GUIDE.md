# Style Guide — *The History Machine*

> Ensemble close third across five rooms. Procedural surface, epistemic dread underneath.
> House lineage: Jamie-Uys restraint on pain — the flat line is the loaded one.

---

## Form law

- Four parts: **SYMMETRY · BREAK · RECORD · CONSTRAINT**. The part title is a promise about the
  chapter's physics *and* its human content; earn both, name neither.
- One consciousness per section. Section break when the skull changes. Chapter tag names the centre:
  **K** Karoo · **T** Technopark · **A** Accra · **N** Nairobi · **I** Ice.
- Rooms, not montage. The consortium is never seen from above; a reader should have to assemble the
  project out of five partial views, the way the machine assembles an event out of five layers.

## POV law

- Close third only. No omniscient narrator explaining the consortium, the ethics or the physics.
- **I (Ice)** sections are the loneliest voice in the book: present, physical, bounded by a data
  budget and a satellite window. Never let Yusuf narrate the plot he cannot see.
- Nobody gets a chapter to be right in. If a position is correct, someone with standing argues the
  other side in the same chapter.

## Writing the machine

- **Never cinema.** No hologram, no ghost walking through a lab, no gasp at a beautiful render. A
  reconstruction is a sparse solved configuration with marked gaps, delivered slowly, and it is
  usually **ugly**. The horror and the beauty both come from its honesty.
- **Gaps on the page.** When the residue does not force an answer, the output says so. Write the gap.
  A team learning to live with gap markers is more frightening than any full picture.
- **Show the cost every time** (C-21): grid load, cryogen, queue position, an anchor that had to be
  borrowed from a museum, a night nobody slept. A free read is a continuity error.
- **The vocabulary is technical and consistent:** residue, break, anchor, inversion, separation,
  dating, gap, seal, read-trace. Characters do not say "recording," "footage," "playback," "time
  travel," or "memory of the universe" unless they are journalists, politicians, or being
  sarcastic — and someone in the room should wince.
- Physics is earned inside scenes — a drift figure, a borehole depth, a separation that took eleven
  weeks — never a lecture. **No infodumps of the 420 Code.** If a principle must be named, name it
  the way working people name their tools: fast, casually, mid-task.

## Writing the anomaly

- The dread is arithmetic. A number appears twice; the second one is wrong by an amount that cannot
  be a mistake. Play it cold and let the cold do the work.
- Elimination in order (C-16). Each step is real work by a real specialist, and each step *narrows*.
  Nobody guesses. Nobody has a dream about it.
- Never let a character say "unless the universe was already being watched" as a thrilling line. Let
  it arrive as the last thing not yet eliminated, in a meeting, and let the room go quiet about it.

## Dialogue

- Everyone in this book is good at their job and speaks like it: short, specific, unfinished
  sentences among peers; careful complete ones for the record.
- Ethicists sound like philosophers, not like disclaimers. Advocates sound like advocates. Officials
  are courteous and immovable. Intelligence officers are the most reasonable people in the book.
- Community representatives are never given the "spiritual objection" voice. They argue custody,
  consent and standing, and they are frequently the sharpest people in the room.
- No speeches about truth. If a character wants to say *this changes everything*, cut the line and
  give them a task instead.

## Texture

- **Karoo:** engineered silence, dust, the physical discipline of no-transmission, cold nights, the
  bakkie that must be pushed rather than started, the sky, the towns that host a famous machine and
  are not rich.
- **Technopark:** tolerances, thermal drift, a workshop that smells of solvent, procurement, the
  crane at the docks, torque values as moral positions.
- **Accra:** heat and racks, power discipline, the human music of a room unmixing a choir, the
  fastest arguments in the book.
- **Nairobi:** paper, seals, court corridors, the specific politeness of people who will not be
  hurried, the weight of a signature.
- **Ice:** dark, dry, deafening quiet, the smell of the station, the borehole cable, the eight-month
  arithmetic of a person and a machine and no witnesses.

## What not to write

- The 420 Code as textbook. The consortium as conspiracy. The Illuminatie in a room with a table.
- A scientist explaining ethics to a community representative and winning.
- Time travel, paradox, warning-from-the-future, or a message addressed to the characters (C-19).
- Aliens as the answer; a secret earlier government machine as the *final* answer; a benevolent guide.
- The Reader as a voice, a character, an oracle, or a threat. It is a sealed tool that refuses to
  invent, and that is all it ever is.
- G on the page (C-06). No mentor cameo, no ribbon-cutting, no phone call in act three.
- AFRIKA 2100 foreshadow winks.
- A shutdown ending. A "we destroyed it" ending. A "humanity wasn't ready" ending.
- Grief pornography around the missing-child case. Under-dramatise. The flat line.
- Any scene where the machine is impressive and free.
