# Characters — *The History Machine*

> Ensemble close third (C-02). Five threads, five rooms. Everyone in this book is competent; the
> conflict comes from incompatible correctness, not from anyone being stupid.

---

## Principals

### Dr. Refilwe Mangope — **K** (Karoo · detection) · primary spine
Radio astronomer turned residual-signal physicist. Grew up in Carnarvon, two hours from the array
that ate the district's silence and gave back a bursary she took. She has spent her career defending
quiet: no ignition, no microwave, no transmitter, the long war against a single badly-shielded
bakkie. Now the same quiet is being used to hear people die.

She is the one who hears things first and says them last. Dry, procedural, allergic to awe; the
closest she comes to wonder is a longer silence than usual before she answers. Her private position,
which she will not publish: the machine is not a telescope pointed at the past, it is a **microphone
in every room that ever existed**, and she has spent twenty years learning that a microphone is a
promise you make to the people who did not know it was on.

Wants: the instrument to stay honest.
Fears: that honest and harmless were never the same thing.

### Dr. Yusuf Salie — **I** (Antarctica · ARIADNE) · second spine
Quantum metrologist from Salt River, Cape Town. Optical clocks, entangled photon links, a talent for
being alone that he thought was a strength when he volunteered to winter over. He keeps Bara. When
the second timestamp comes up, he is 4,000 km from anyone who can check his work, in the dark, for
eight more months, with a satellite window and a data budget.

The book's dread runs through him because he is the only person who cannot be visited, cannot be
verified in person, and cannot walk away from the instrument. The first thing the consortium
suspects is not the universe. It is him.

Wants: to be believed.
Fears: that he will be believed.

### Dr. Ama Boateng — **A** (Accra · ingestion & separation)
Runs the hardest unglamorous problem in the project: billions of overlapping breaks at every busy
coordinate, and one of them is the one you asked for. Separation is her invention; she describes it
as "unmixing a choir back into people." Fast, funny, ruthless about method, and the only person who
routinely tells the consortium's directors that an answer is not ready.

She is the one who proves the pre-echo is present through the entire ice column — the finding that
kills contamination and makes the anomaly physics. She is not happy about being right.

Wants: her method to be the standard.
Fears: her method being good enough to be used without her.

### Advocate-Dr. Wanjiru Kamau — **N** (Nairobi · authentication)
Advocate first, provenance scientist second, and the only principal who was in courtrooms before she
was in laboratories. She built the custody chain — anchor to signature — because she understood
before the physicists did that the machine's output would arrive in a courtroom long before it
arrived in a journal. She is the person who says *inadmissible* and means it as a technical term.

Her arc is the book's legal spine: the first case she authenticates frees an innocent man, and every
case after that teaches her what she has actually built — a mechanism by which the state can be
right about anyone.

Wants: a standard of evidence that survives its own power.
Fears: that she has already handed it over.

### Nadia Abrahams — **T** (Technopark, Stellenbosch · sensors & engine)
Instrument builder. Not a professor and does not want to be. Hands, tolerances, thermal drift, a
workshop temper, the one who has to make the physics survive contact with a bakkie's suspension, a
ship's crane and a borehole. She named the array in the proposal, submitted BARA with a straight
face, and has never been allowed to forget it (C-22).

She is the practical conscience: she knows exactly what the machine costs to run because she signs
for the parts, and she is the first to notice that the cost is being paid by people who never
authorised it.

Wants: the thing to work.
Fears: the second one, built by someone who did not have to lift it.

### Prof. Nomvula Dhlamini — ethical architecture (joint African chair)
Holds the seat that governs how recovered history may be used — the fifth of the Code's five faces
(seed: an ethical system, not only a physics). Philosopher, not a bioethics-committee decoration:
her argument is that the Code itself forbids what the consortium is drifting toward, because
**Constraint** is a principle and not a footnote, and a civilisation that recovers everything has
removed the constraint that made memory survivable.

She is the character most likely to be right and least likely to be obeyed. Her veto is real in Act
I and is the thing Act II goes around rather than defeats.

## Pressure (not villains)

### Dr. Marguerite Lenoir — CERN · consortium co-chair
Superb measurement physicist. Genuinely, unglamorously wants the centre of gravity to move north —
not out of contempt but because she has spent thirty years watching what happens to instruments that
depend on a seasonal ship. Her arguments are good. Her budget is better. She is the polite face of
O-08.

### The states
Not one government — a weather system of them. Access requests escalate from courteous to statutory
to classified. The best of them have real cases: mass graves, disappeared persons, a truth
commission with no witnesses left alive.

### The services
Intelligence agencies arrive last and understand fastest. They do not want history. They want rooms,
then conversations, then — once experiential reading is demonstrated — the inside of a specific
skull on a specific afternoon. C-14 rail: their reasoning is sound and that is the problem.

### The claimants
Families who want to see their dead. Faith institutions requesting verification and dreading it.
Corporations filing for ownership of recovered performances, inventions and lost works. Communities
holding that the dead consented to nothing and that an anchor in a museum is now a stolen
instrument, not a stolen object (C-15).

## Offstage (binding — C-06)

### Gerhard "G" van Niekerk
Author of the 420 Code. Alive, 58, working, uninterested. Appears as a letter, a refusal, a
citation, third-party report. His published position on record inversion is four words long and the
consortium has been arguing about it for two years: *structure is not permission.* He does not
endorse the machine and he does not try to stop it, and both facts infuriate different quarters.

### AJ / arjunabadger.press / CongoNet
Press and network texture only. The coverage that reaches the Karoo comes over CongoNet; the book
that will eventually be written about all this is not this one.

### The Reader
The sealed inversion engine (MACHINE §9). Never a character. Listed here only so nobody drafts it as
one.
