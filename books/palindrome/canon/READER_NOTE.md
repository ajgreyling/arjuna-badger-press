# A Note to the Reader

This story was written backwards.

It began as a film — four actors, one room, an idea I could not shake: a dying man who claims to
have lived his life many times, told to the only people who could have stopped him, with no effects,
no leaving the room, nothing on the screen but faces and a thing said quietly. The kind of film that
was once made without apology and is hardly made any more. So before I wrote a single line of
screenplay, I wrote the book — because the book is where you find out whether the idea can actually
hold a person's weight for an hour, or only looks as though it can.

The lineage is honest and I'll name it. The engine here is the one Jerome Bixby built in *The Man
from Earth* — a premise interrogated by people who have something to lose by believing it — and the
discipline I set myself is his: every proof must have a door. There is nothing in these pages you
cannot explain away if you want to. That is not a weakness in the story; it is the story. The man
who can no longer tell the true thing from the frightened thing is not a device. He is most of us, on
the nights we are honest.

A palindrome reads the same forward and backward. I leave it to you to decide which way this one
runs — and, if you are very honest, whether you would rather know.

— *the author*
