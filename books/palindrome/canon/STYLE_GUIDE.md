# PALINDROME — Style Guide (working; flesh out later)

The contract for the prose. Where a chapter and this differ, this wins.

- **POV:** close third on **Voss** (the skeptic). We know only what Voss perceives and reasons; we
  never cut to objective proof of August's claim. Voss's clinical vocabulary is our lens — and it is
  the thing that slowly fails.
- **Register:** adult literary-thriller. Sentences that *reason*. Cool, precise, humane; the dread
  arrives through accumulation, not adjectives. Earn every chill.
- **Dialogue carries the film.** This is a chamber piece and a future four-actor audiobook/serial —
  keep speaker turns clean and distinguishable by *voice*, not tags. Each man speaks unmistakably:
  August (precise, unhurried, kind), Voss (urbane, parenthetical, testing), Earl (short, flat,
  Southern-cold, says the worst thing plainly), Danny (warm, reluctant, deflecting the role he's
  given).
- **The uncanny rules (from STORY_BIBLE):** no effect ever; every proof deniable; the premise must
  wound someone in the room; August never mad, never smug; one room, one night.
- **No exposition dumps.** Backstory arrives only as it's pried out of the room. If the reader learns
  a thing, a character in the room learns it at the same moment and pays for it.
- **Serial/audio shape:** the disclosure staircase (STORY_BIBLE) doubles as natural episode breaks —
  each step ends on a worse question. Write chapters to land on a turn, not a lull.
- **Length:** novella, single tight POV (~25–35k). Propulsive. The candle burns down in real-ish
  time; resist time-skips.
- **The ending is a line, not a twist.** Hold ambiguity to the last sentence (Prestige-shelf).
