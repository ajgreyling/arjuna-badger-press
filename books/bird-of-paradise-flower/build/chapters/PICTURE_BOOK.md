# The Flower That Watches the Sky

*{{DEDICATION}}*

<!-- spread:1 image="spread-01-garden.png" alt="Early morning in Gogo's garden at the edge of the veld — red earth, aloes, a low stone wall, open grassland beyond. A small barefoot child stands very still, having just noticed one strange flower among the others." -->

In Gogo's garden,
at the edge of the wide veld,
the morning came up soft and gold.

{{CHILD}} was walking barefoot in the dew
when something made {{CHILD}} stop.

There, among all the ordinary flowers,
stood one that was not ordinary at all.

<!-- spread:2 image="spread-02-bloom.png" alt="Close on the strelitzia bloom — an orange crest of sepals, a blue petal-tongue, rising from a grey-green beak-shaped sheath, beaded with nectar. It looks exactly like a small bright bird with its head tipped back, looking up. The child leans in, wondering." -->

It had an orange crest,
like feathers.
It had a blue heart,
like a throat.
It rose from a long green beak
and tipped its head back —

and it looked, for all the world,
like a small bright bird
looking up at the sky.

"Gogo," whispered {{CHILD}},
"what kind of flower is *that*?"

<!-- spread:3 image="spread-03-gogo-tells.png" alt="Gogo, an older grey-haired woman, lowers herself down onto the low stone wall beside the flower, smiling, gathering the child in close to tell a story. Warm morning light." -->

Gogo set down her watering can.
She sat on the low stone wall
and pulled {{CHILD}} in close.

"Ah," she said.
"That one is not only a flower.
Long ago, little one,
that flower was a *bird* —

and the bird could fly."

<!-- spread:4 image="spread-04-the-bird.png" alt="Memory spread. A bright orange-crested, blue-throated bird high over the golden veld, wings wide, the whole blue sky around it. Joyful, free, the same bird that becomes the bloom." -->

"Once it flew so high
the hills looked small.

The whole blue sky was its own —
the warm wind, the high gold light,
the long, long road of the air.

No bird was ever so glad of its wings."

<!-- spread:5 image="spread-05-cannot-rise.png" alt="Memory spread, gentle and wistful. The same bird on the ground at dusk, wings half-open but heavy, looking up at the darkening sky it can no longer reach. Soft, sad, not frightening — no violence. The veld quiet around it." -->

"But there came a day, my child,"
said Gogo softly,
"when the bird could not rise.

It opened its wings —
and the wings would not lift.
It looked up at the road of the air
and could not find the way back.

*It cannot fly. It cannot rise.
Still it lifts its face to the sky.*"

<!-- spread:6 image="spread-06-rooting.png" alt="The rooting — the bird's feet becoming gentle roots, the bird sinking warm and soft into the red earth, the veld grass closing around it like a hand holding, not trapping. Warm gold light, tender." -->

"Its feet found the warm red earth.
And the earth — being kind —
held on.

Slowly, slowly,
its feet became roots,
and the roots went down,
and the bird grew still.

The veld closed round it
like a hand that would not let it fall."

<!-- spread:7 image="spread-07-lifts-HERO.png" alt="HERO SPREAD. First morning rooted. The bird-now-flower — the full glowing strelitzia bloom — lifts its bright orange-and-blue face to a vast dawn sky it can never reach again. The whole spread is the small lifted face against the enormous sky. Beautiful, longing, luminous." -->

"And on the very first morning
that it could not fly,
do you know what it did?

It lifted its face.

Up, and up,
to the sky it could not reach —
not to weep, not yet,
but only to *look*.

*It cannot fly. It cannot rise.
Still it lifts its face to the sky.*"

<!-- spread:8 image="spread-08-tears.png" alt="Close on the lifted bloom — small bright drops of sweet nectar gathered at its tipped-back face, like tears that catch the morning light. Beautiful and shining, not grim. The blue and orange glowing." -->

"And then, {{CHILD}},
the small bright drops came.

Right there, at its lifted face —
sweet, and shining,
catching the light.

Those are its tears, little one.
Not sad-sour tears.
The tears of looking up,
and remembering the sky."

<!-- spread:9 image="spread-09-sunbird.png" alt="A tiny iridescent sunbird hovers and sips the nectar from the lifted bloom; bees drift nearby; the morning wind moves the grass. The rooted flower kept company by the living veld. Warm, full of small life." -->

"But it was never left alone.

A sunbird came, quick and bright,
and drank the sweet, shining tears —
and flew off into the high gold air

carrying a little of the longing
up where the flower could not go.

The bees came. The wind came.
The whole living veld
kept it company."

<!-- spread:10 image="spread-10-faithful.png" alt="A turn of seasons in one image — the same crane-flower standing tall and bright through changing light, faithfully lifting its face every dawn. It is plainly the most beautiful thing in the whole garden. Other flowers around it, none so striking." -->

"Season after season it stayed.

It never once
turned its face down to the ground.
Every single morning,
it lifted its head to the light.

And do you know —
of every flower in the garden,
it grew the most beautiful of all.

Not in spite of its longing.
*Because* of it."

<!-- spread:11 image="spread-11-tends.png" alt="The child kneels at the flower — not pitying, simply tending: cupping the lifted bloom in two small hands, tipping a little water to its roots, sitting with it in the morning light. Quiet, loving, present." -->

{{CHILD}} knelt down in the dew.

{{CHILD}} did not feel sorry for it.
You don't feel sorry
for a thing so brave.

{{CHILD}} cupped the bright bird-face
in two small hands,
and gave its roots a little water,
and sat with it
in the light, a while.

<!-- spread:12 image="spread-12-gogos-truth.png" alt="Gogo and the child together by the flower, foreheads almost touching, the child's face lit with understanding. The bloom between them lifting its face. The veld wide and gold behind." -->

"Gogo," said {{CHILD}},
"it can't fly anymore.
But it's not sad. Not really."

Gogo smiled.

"We cannot all fly, my child.
Some of us are rooted —
the old, the still,
the ones who stay.

But every one of us
can lift our face to the light.

*It cannot fly. It cannot rise.
Still it lifts its face to the sky.*"

<!-- spread:13 image="spread-13-evening.png" alt="Closing image. Evening. The child and Gogo walk away hand in hand along the garden path, looking back. The crane-flower stands in the last gold light, still lifting its bright face to the fading sky. Peaceful, complete, wistful and warm." -->

That evening, when the gold ran low,
{{CHILD}} and Gogo walked home
hand in hand.

And {{CHILD}} looked back.

The little flower
that had once been a bird
stood faithful in the last of the light,
still lifting its bright face
to the going-down sky —

the way the brave ones do.

*It cannot fly. It cannot rise.
Still it lifts its face to the sky.*

— THE END —
