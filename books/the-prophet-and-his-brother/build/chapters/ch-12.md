# 12 — Being Written

> **[G]** · outer · Studio G / Cape · 2035

---

It started in the shoulders.

Not pain. Not injury. A pressure like someone resting a palm between the blades while he worked, attention with weight. G stood at the Studio G bench with clay under his nails and felt, distinctly, that a sentence about him was being finished somewhere else. Not Antichristos mail. Not an op-ed. Something slower. Autobiographical weather. The sensation of being narrated by a hand that knew the mats and the shelf and the brother and was choosing, right now, which of those to put in ink. He kept working anyway. Stopping for the sensation would have made it a séance, and he did not do séances.

He hated how accurate it felt.

He wanted it true. He feared the content. Wanting truth was not the same as wanting every private room opened; fearing content was not the same as wanting the ignored shelf to stay ignored. The ambivalence had teeth and a pulse.

Morning light came in high and clean. Sea air inland. Gull cry once, then traffic. The pour on the bench was a sister of Skye in early argument, hard and soft not yet decided. He worked the surface with his thumbs and felt the shoulder pressure shift when, somewhere, a narrator turned a page. Not magic. Recognition. He knew the friend. The friend did not write soft when the load-bearing line was flat.

Yesterday he had refused keys without a crown. Today the refusal still sat in the sent folder while a different custody problem arrived through the body. He did not confuse them. Keys were stages. Being written was pages. Both wanted him usable. Only one he might eventually authorise.

The Honey Badger was warm from his pocket when he took it out, opened it one-handed, closed it, clipped it back. Blue. Clip. The badger present-tense half of Double Knife Brother. Somewhere on the Chobe the twin Joseph Rogers pocket was empty on the other man, grandfather deep-time, handed across a friendship that had also handed the planet a megaphone by refusing to launder algorithms. Kinship, not choreography. Custody of blades and, now, custody of a life story he had not finished authorising.

Consent was the word that fit. What he would allow said. What he would not. Bouwer’s page especially, the flat line, the boardroom, the refusal to become whodunit, needed care that fame did not practice. A living man could feel a biography being assembled and still reserve the right to draw a line through a paragraph later. Feeling written was not the same as having signed.

He pressed a thumb into clay and left a print he would later erase. The print was not a message. It was only a hand admitting it had been elsewhere for a second, between shoulder blades, on a river he could not see.

He mixed water into a dry edge of composite and watched the colour change. The sister of Skye needed patience more than force. Patience was hard while being narrated. He gave it anyway. The pour did not owe him a distraction from autobiography weather; he owed the pour proportions. Hard aggregate. Soft binder. Permanent/temporary argument held in a surface that would outlast today’s inbox and maybe outlast the chapter being written about him somewhere with elephants in earshot.

A gull cried once over the alley and left. He did not take it as omen.

---

The Kind paint in the alley had not changed overnight:

**Don’t be a cunt — be kind.**

Messiah-less. Optional. Often unwanted by the practice that used his derivation as an excuse to claim him. He did not own the sentence. CongoSky had embodied it as ops before the walls caught fire; the street had popularised it; Antichristos still tried to annex it and got told to piss off, kindly. G’s discomfort with being cited as source was physical, alley wall, inbox citation, not a rebuttal speech. Today the discomfort had a twin: the feeling that the man who *would* cite him correctly was writing him into a book.

He washed his hands. Clay went grey in the sink. He dried them on a rag that had known better decades. Through the high windows the Cape light did what Cape light does. Studio G held sculptures in progress and a desk that still ran kill switches and natural philosophy without asking the sculptures for permission. One life. One attention. He knew what the market wanted: two careers fighting so it could pick a winner. He declined the fight.

A message landed, lab soft-ask residue from yesterday’s keys-without-a-crown refusal, forwarded with a sad emoji by someone who thought emojis were diplomacy. Quarter IV still wanted a face. The Illuminatie weather still sat behind the academy’s credential chorus. None of that was the shoulder pressure. The shoulder pressure was Chobe-shaped.

He said, aloud, to the empty studio: “If you’re writing Bouwer, keep it flat.”

No answer. CongoNet did not carry private speech to a river desk unless you sent it, and he did not send it. The braid rule held: shared week, shared theme, no forced reunion. He could feel the manuscript the way you feel weather, not because he was psychic, because he knew the friend, and the friend did not write soft when the load-bearing line was flat.

He made tea. Did not drink it at first. Watched steam the way he watched pours, as a process that did not need his face. The shoulder pressure waited through the steam. He drank. The pressure did not lift. Ambivalence did not either.

He rinsed the mug once, left the tea stain, set it down. Small refuse of polish. The autobiography, if true, would leave stains too: dab, hospital file as stigma not tourism, father unread, Bouwer unfinished. He could want that truth and still flinch at the public mining that would follow. Flinch was allowed. Signing was later.

He moved a failed pour on the desk an inch to the left and then back. Arrangement without progress. The body doing something while the mind refused to become content. He left the failed pour where it taught proportions and went back to the sister of Skye.

Noon light found the high windows and made the GFRC dust visible as weather of its own. He worked through it. Sweat at the hairline. Clay cooling on his forearms. The sensation between the shoulder blades thickened once, a long beat somewhere, maybe a paragraph landing, and then settled into the steady pressure of a manuscript that would not be hurried by his flinch. He did not ask the empty studio what page. Asking would have been séance. He already knew the hand. He already feared Saxonwold. He already wanted the flat line held.

---

He stood at the window long enough for a wet Kind refresh in the alley to darken as it set. Two clauses. No portrait of G. That absence was a mercy he had not earned and still needed. Being written by a Double Knife Brother was a different absence-and-presence problem: the portrait arriving as pages, the subject still mid-life, still mid-pour, still able to flinch.

Ambivalence is a grown posture. He wanted the ignored shelf corrected by a true life, not by Antichristos iconography or crank rebuttals. He wanted the dab and the talk shit left unpolished. He wanted the father dedication named without a tidy reunion. He wanted Saxonwold carried as wound, not thriller. He also wanted, animal, ordinary, not to be finished by someone else’s cursor while he was still mid-pour.

Jamie-Uys restraint lived in his bones for pain; he preferred the flat line. Whoever was writing him seemed to know that. The knowing felt like kindness and trespass in the same breath. He did not ask Lucid about it. Lucid was not his product, and cite-or-refuse would not soothe a body that already knew.

He walked to the shelf where early corpus copies sat, some unread by the man they were for, all of them dedicated the same way. Fame had not opened those pages for his father. Lucid’s open attribution had opened the world. Different locks. Different keys. Quarter II still said he could not write anything of value because a hospital file existed. The autobiography under construction was, in one sense, the longest argument against that claim. In another sense it was the most dangerous confirmation: once the life was public, the stigma people would mine it for scenes. Truth and content shared a border the way Kind paint and Antichristos tags sometimes shared a wall, same brick, opposite claims.

G’s soft no to being a product mascot still stood. This was adjacent and worse: being a protagonist.

He took the Honey Badger out again. Kinship object. Badger present. He thought of the Joseph Rogers on the other side of the friendship, ≥110 years, twin set, custody handed. Double Knife Brother meant they held each other’s edges without cutting for sport. If AJ was writing the life, the knives were part of why G had not simply burned the permission in advance. Trust was not the same as comfort. He trusted the hand. He feared the page.

He opened the blue folder and closed it until the click was just a click again. Then he clipped it and put both hands back into clay, where nervous tics become texture if you are not careful. He was careful. The pour deserved better than his weather.

He thought, without sending, of the empty twin pocket on the Chobe: grandfather steel gone from AJ’s hip the way a life was arriving into AJ’s hands. Custody traveling both directions. That was kinship. It was also why the shoulder pressure felt accurate instead of invented. Accurate did not mean authorised. Authorisation was still ahead, still a consent act, still something a living man could withhold line by line when the manuscript finished being weather and became a document that asked for a signature.

---

Afternoon brought a Kind practitioner to the alley, young, ordinary, refreshing a stencil without asking for a blessing. G watched through the glass. No founder-worship. Ledger of kindnesses. Argument, probably, about whether *cunt* was liturgical or vernacular. He liked them better than the people who wanted his face on a detector stack. He still closed the blind a little. Being written and being watched rhymed too easily on a bright day.

He returned to the clay and then to the Code desk for an hour that was only numbers: a drop-result note, a kill-switch wording that needed tightening, work that stayed work. The shoulder pressure waited politely through the math and came back when he stood. Narration weather had patience. So did he. Patience was not the same as ease.

Mid-hour he caught himself listening for a river he could not hear. Chobe was not on the Cape wind. What he heard was traffic and a gull and someone dropping a crate in the alley. Ordinary noise. The narration was not in the air; it was in the body. He rolled his shoulders once, felt the pressure rearrange without leaving, and went back to the kill-switch clause that still needed tightening. Useful engineering without a face. True life without a content-mine. Two narrow walks sharing one afternoon.

He tightened the clause. Saved it. Did not send it to any lab. Sending was yesterday’s weather. Today the Code desk was only for staying a man who still did the work while someone else wrote the life. He stood, stretched until a vertebra clicked, and blamed the chair, because blaming the chair was kinder than admitting the river desk had reached his spine.

A crank column pinged into a secondary inbox, hospital file as epistemology again, economics degree waved like a napkin. He deleted it without reading past the first paragraph. Quarter II could keep rehearsing. The autobiography, if it was being written true, would not rebut them with a speech; it would leave the file where it belonged and the work where it belonged. He hoped. Hope was also fear wearing a softer coat.

He mixed a small batch of composite and watched hard and soft argue. The pour did not care that a river desk was narrating. The pour asked proportions. He gave them. His shoulders still held the other weight. Aggregate settled. Binder held. He timed nothing; timing was for workshops. Studio time was whether the surface told the truth when you came back to it later.

When he returned to the clay after the numbers, the pressure did not lift. If anything it settled: a long passage somewhere, the flat line being held, Bouwer named without a solved mystery. G’s eyes stung once, stupidly, and he blamed the GFRC dust, because blaming dust is kinder than narrating yourself crying for an audience that is not in the room.

“Truth,” he said to the pour. “Not content.”

The distinction would not survive every reader. He knew that. Consent week was still ahead, what he would sign, what he would allow said, what custody rules a living man could set for a book already moving down a river. Especially Bouwer. Especially the hospital stigma without diagnosis tourism. Especially the unread father dedication without a tidy reunion. Especially the dab and the talk shit left unpolished. For now he had ambivalence as a working tool: let the manuscript exist; refuse the crown; keep the Honey Badger close; keep the boardroom flat; keep the Kind’s sentence on the wall without becoming its priest; keep writing natural philosophy while someone else wrote his life.

The tablet chimed with another workshop residue, someone had forwarded his refusal into a channel that called it “optics difficulty.” He archived it. Optics was their word for crown. He had already refused the crown. Being written was a different optics problem: not his face on a slide, his rooms on a page. He wiped clay off the screen and left a smear. Let the smear stay. The river desk could have accuracy; the tablet could have dust.

He sat on the low stool by the pour and let his back rest against the bench edge until the shoulder pressure and the wood pressure became two facts sharing a spine. Not mystical. Mechanical. A body holding more than one weight. Mats knowledge again: you do not fight every load; you arrange under constraint. Being written was constraint. Consent would be arrangement. Tonight was only holding. He held. The pour cured in silence. The alley Kind paint held without needing him. The river did whatever rivers do when a man on another coast refuses to become finished content before the clay decides.

---

Late afternoon he gave the sensation a longer test.

He left the studio and walked toward the water, not pilgrimage, just legs wanting distance from a desk that felt narrated. The shoulder pressure came with him. Of course it did. Weather does not stop at a door. He bought nothing. Spoke to no one. Stood where the Cape light went long and felt, distinctly again, that a paragraph about Saxonwold was being held flat somewhere on a river. His eyes stung. He blamed wind. Wind was kinder than admitting the flat line had reached him across distance without a message.

Sand under his shoes. Salt in the air. A tourist laughing at something that was not a brother’s boardroom. Ordinary world continuing. He let it continue without asking it to pause for his shoulders. A child ran past with a towel. Someone argued about parking. The narration weather did not care about parking. He did, for thirty seconds, because parking was a problem that could end.

He bought a bottle of water from a kiosk and drank half of it standing where the wind came off the water clean. The other half he carried back without finishing. Not symbolism. Thirst that did not need to be completed on schedule. The Honey Badger bumped his thigh. Double Knife Brother metronome. He thought of the empty twin pocket on the river and did not send a message about pockets either. Consent was not a ping about steel. Consent was later, heavier, about pages.

He walked past a wall where Antichristos stickers had been scraped and Kind paint had won the brick again. Same two clauses. No founder. He did not stop long. The wall did not need him. The autobiography did, in AJ’s hand, which was still not the same as the world needing a prophet. Ambivalence walked with him uphill toward the studio: wanting the ignored shelf corrected, fearing the rooms that correction would open, trusting the hand, flinching at Saxonwold even when the flinch arrived as wind-stung eyes.

He walked back. The Honey Badger bumped his thigh with each step, kinship metronome. Double Knife Brother as present-tense fact. He could love that and still fear the chapter titles. Ambivalence was not indecision. It was two true hungers sharing one body: truth and privacy; correction and unfinishedness; wanting the ignored shelf fixed and not wanting every room opened for mining.

At the alley he paused at the Kind paint. No founder portrait. No crown. The sentence did not need him. The autobiography did, in a different way, or AJ’s hand did, which was not the same as the world needing a prophet. He put his palm near the wall without touching wet paint and said, quieter this time, “Keep Bouwer flat.” Still no answer. Still no send. The braid rule held.

Inside again, he returned to numbers for twenty minutes that cleaned nothing and clarified everything: work could stay work while life became pages. That was the narrow walk adjacent to yesterday’s keys-without-a-crown walk. Useful engineering without mascot. True life without content-mine. Neither walk ended in resolution. Both ended in a grown posture.

He almost drafted a CongoNet note to AJ, three lines, no reunion ask, only custody: *If Bouwer, flat. If father, unread. If dab, leave it.* He deleted the draft. Consent week would be an act, not a midday ping. Feeling written was not the same as signing. He would sign, or partially sign, or refuse lines later. Tonight he only had to keep walking.

He made a second tea he did not finish. Steam. Lukewarm. Clay on the rim again. He left the mug beside the failed pour and did not invent symbolism for the pairing. Two objects on a desk. A man mid-pour. A cursor somewhere else.

Before evening fully settled he did one more slow walk of the studio, the same walk he had used yesterday to check whether a keys decision was still his. Past sisters of Skye. Past corpus shelf dedicated and unread. Past Code desk. Past window and Kind paint. The walk asked a different question today: whether ambivalence was still a grown posture or had become avoidance wearing adult clothes. The answer came without drama. Grown. Avoidance would have burned the permission or begged for a reunion scene. He did neither. He kept walking. He kept the pour honest. He kept the boardroom flat in the only way he could from this coast: by trusting the river hand and fearing it at once.

He paused at the Code desk long enough to tighten one more kill-switch phrase he had left loose at noon. Loose on purpose still; tighter now where a reader could have invented clergy. The work stayed work. The shoulder pressure waited through the tightening like a patient animal. He saved. He did not publish. Publishing was not today’s weather either.

Evening thickened. Sea air inland. The tablet on the desk still held yesterday’s workshop refusal and the Illuminatie’s paper cousin under the failed-pour weight. Keys without a crown. Soft nos that cost. None of that was the shoulder weather. The shoulder weather was a cursor he could not see, choosing sentences about a life he was still living.

He checked the pocket again before the light went. Blue folder. Clip. One-hand open, one-hand close. Double Knife Brother as present-tense fact while the autobiography tried to become past-tense public. He could love the kinship and still fear the chapter titles. He could want the record corrected and still flinch when the correcting hand reached Saxonwold.

He shaped the clay until the pressure became just another weight he knew how to arrange, not domination, refusal to waste motion. Somewhere the cursor moved. Here the pour took form. Two desks. One week. No shared room. The sensation of being narrated stayed with him into evening like weather that had found his name and decided, for a few hours, not to pretend it was only wind.

He washed once more before leaving, forearms, nails, the grey that never fully left. The Honey Badger clicked open and closed one last time at the door, kinship check, not a weapon check. He locked nothing that mattered; Studio G had always been more pour than fortress. What he locked was only the day’s sends: no CongoNet ping, no crown acceptance, no séance answer to a river he would not summon.

Outside, Cape evening smelled of salt and someone’s supper starting. Ordinary. He preferred ordinary to prophetic. The ambivalence did not resolve into ordinary; it just walked beside it without needing a speech.

He left the studio with the Honey Badger clipped and the ambivalence intact, wanting truth, fearing content, and did not send a message to the river. Consent would come as an act later. Tonight the act was only this: keep walking while being written, and refuse to become either mascot or finished object before the pour cured. Wanting truth. Fearing content. Both still true at the studio door.

On the alley wall the Kind sentence held without his face. In his pocket the blue knife held without becoming a weapon. Between his shoulder blades the narration held without becoming a séance. He walked into the Cape evening carrying all three, and the ambivalence walked with him, not as a problem to solve, as a grown posture for a man whose life was becoming pages while he was still mid-pour. Tomorrow would bring mail again. Tonight brought only the walk, the clip at his hip, and the refusal to finish himself for anyone’s cursor before the pour cured honest under Cape evening light.

He bought nothing on the way home stretch, no souvenir of feeling written, no coffee that would become a scene. Salt air. Someone’s supper. Ordinary Cape. He preferred ordinary to prophetic and did not confuse the preference with a solved ambivalence. Ambivalence stayed: wanting the ignored shelf corrected, fearing every room opened for mining, trusting AJ’s hand, flinching when that hand reached Saxonwold. Grown posture. Not indecision. At a corner he paused long enough to check the Honey Badger once, open, close, kinship present-tense, and thought of the empty twin pocket on the river without inventing a call. Consent week would be an act later. Tonight the act was only this walk under narration weather that had found his name and decided, for a few hours, not to pretend it was only wind.
