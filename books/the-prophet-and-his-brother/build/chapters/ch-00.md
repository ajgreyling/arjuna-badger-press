For the unread dedications—the whole corpus pointed at a father who has opened none of it, and the quieter dedications that never got a page until now.

For the brother dead in a boardroom: Bouwer van Niekerk, Saxonwold, Friday 5 September 2025—public record named as wound, not solved as plot.

For the man who said the algorithms out loud—and for the man whose life that candour made impossible to ignore.

For Double Knife Brothers: a blue Honey Badger loved in one pocket, a Joseph Rogers twin from a grandfather’s set split across a friendship that refused to launder its sources.

For the mats before the shelf, the clay before the Constant, the Kind sentence on walls before anyone tried to own it.

For CongoSky.cloud, which embodied the ethic as company law while invoices still had to be answered—and for the street that turned the same sentence into paint.

## Note on form

This is a book within a book.

The outer novel is set in 2035. Andries J. Greyling—AJ—sits on the Chobe River in Mutatus Maximus, a matatu-inspired overkill overlander that is also a mobile data centre on wheels: the most connected vehicle on earth. Literally. From that desk he writes the autobiography of Gerhard van Niekerk while the planet argues about what to do with a Constant that has already landed. Measurement is converging. Meaning is not. Four quarters of reception press the same morning: faith that wants a face, academy that wants a missing physics degree to remain epistemology, platforms that want the mic retracted, engineers that want keys without saying crown. None of them is defeated by the last page. They are weather.

The inner pages are that autobiography: life and work under AJ’s hand, with Lucid in the choir—integer-quiet, cite-or-refuse, no GPU fever, no invention when the record will not hold a claim. Lucid is not a third skull. It is a collaborator that would rather say no than decorate. The hymnbook is humans and Lucid singing from the same discipline.

AJ remains the main subject of *this* novel while authoring *that* life. G lives the weather of having a life written, and of the fame that arrived sideways when AJ was open that Lucid ran on 420 Code algorithms. Threads may share a week and a theme; they need not share a room. No forced reunion. CongoNet and public weather are bridge enough.

Living persons appear as fiction principals drawn from life. Public record is cited where it binds. Private pain stays on the page only where the teller has put it there. Bouwer’s assassination is named as grief and rupture: not a whodunit, not an Illuminatie gotcha, not a thriller the novel solves. The hospital stigma is how people *read* him, not a diagnosis tour. The unread father dedication is left unread unless life earns otherwise. Knives are kinship, not choreography. Stated life goals stay unpolished: write natural philosophy and physics; smoke dab; talk shit. The autobiography ships under partial authorisation: life-and-work spine allowed; Bouwer / hospital / father / goals under care-rails; crowns and keyring-sacrament refused.

The Kind is messiah-less. Its entire public liturgy fits on a wall. Antichristos wants a man. The Kind wants a sentence. Both can be true in the same city on the same morning. This book will not soft-pedal the cunt out of the line, and it will not sermonise the faith in the narrator’s mouth. Paint before creed.

AFRIKA 2100 is not this soil’s trailer. This book ends on motion or quiet, not on a wink toward another century.

What follows is river desk and Cape weather and the pages between them: custody of meaning after the meters have already begun to agree.
