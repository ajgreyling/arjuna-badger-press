# 20 — Last Pages

> **[I]** · inner autobiography · manuscript under AJ’s hand

---

These are the pages I will stand behind.

Not a finale. Not a textbook. Not the tidy arc where the ignored shelf apologises and the father opens a dedication and the brother walks out of the boardroom alive. Life does not offer those edits. A book that pretends otherwise is a different kind of hallucination, the kind Lucid refuses when the record will not hold a claim, the kind I refuse when I am honest enough to prefer a flat line to a speech.

I am writing this as handoff, not as closing theorem. The ladder is public. The life is not a lemma. If you came for a last chapter that defeats the weather, you came to the wrong book. Measurement defeats silence slowly. It does not defeat appetite. Appetite wants a face, a degree to mock, a throat to blame, a keyring to hold. Appetite will still be there after the colophon. These pages are not a spell against it. They are a record of what I will carry and what I will not.

I stand behind the mats.

Before the axioms there was the floor. Judo first, then Jiu-Jitsu, the long apprenticeship of putting a body where physics already intended it to go. You do not argue with gravity in a gi. You set yourself down so the fall lands soft, weight transferred without waste. Mastery, if that word means anything, was not domination. It was refusal to waste motion. People who met me later as a man with a corpus and no physics degree liked to begin at the ignored shelf. Clean morality play. Wrong beginning. Weight first. How a body learns not to waste the fall. The same attention later poured clay and derived a constant. Body knowledge before corpus knowledge. Do not sand that order into a prettier origin.

I stand behind Studio G — born 1980, Stellenbosch; clay to GFRC composite mixed with my own hands; Village N Life to mid-2005; mentored rooms under Maree Brink’s weather; more than a year at Camps Bay Retreat when it was still Earl’s Dyke rooms and Hannetjie’s long hospitality; Skye on a lawn at sunset; Studio G curated at The Bay Hotel, official launch 27 July 2018; Saatchi; hard/soft, smooth/rough, growth/decay, permanent/temporary in one surface. Tourists bought half of what sold because light on a body in a hotel path is still a body. The Full Send warehouse/gallery room and the Code desk are one attention. Artist and axiom-installer are one man. Quarter II can keep pretending the sculptor cannot be the physicist. Antichristos can try to turn sculptures into relics. Neither gets custody of the workroom. Friend James van Niekerk’s sudden death is a mark on the life; it is not Bouwer; do not conflate the dead to thicken a chapter. Living-person care includes that refusal.

I stand behind Bouwer.

Bouwer van Niekerk. Brother. Johannesburg attorney. Respected insolvency practitioner. Assassinated at his Saxonwold practice on Friday, 5 September 2025. Public record: four suspects under the pretext of a booked consultation; gunshots in the boardroom; died at the scene; attackers fled. Contentious commercial and insolvency matters sit in the fog of reporting, including business-rescue litigation weather the Law Society noted without this book adopting allegation as fact. LSSA condemnation 8 September 2025: targeted attack on a legal practitioner and on the rule of law; investigation ongoing as of that statement. Grief flat. Not a whodunit. Not an Illuminatie gotcha. No invented assassin CV. No solving the murder on a page for plot. Wound carried. If the autobiography ships, this page ships careful, named, weighted, unsolved. Curiosity that wants a thriller can go elsewhere. Jamie-Uys restraint: the flat line is the loaded one. I will not decorate his boardroom for strangers’ appetite. I will not let Quarter I make him a martyr brand or Quarter III make him a reason to stop a book. He was my brother. That is enough weight.

There is a morning texture under that paragraph that is not cinema: tea gone cold, a studio that still had work in it, the particular silence after a public statement that cannot bring a person back. I will not write the boardroom as set dressing. I will not invent the attackers’ CVs. I will not hang the killing on a continuity network for thriller payoff. Living-person care is the refusal to turn a brother’s death into the novel’s engine. The wound stays a wound. The fog of reporting stays fog. The name stays a name.

I stand behind the hospital stigma without giving the voyeurs a tour. I was institutionalised. Because of that, people close to me, and the wider dismissive weather, believed I could not write anything of value. That belief is the ignored shelf’s emotional engine. Render as how they read me. Not diagnosis tourism. Not DSM spectacle. Not a redemption arc that uses a ward as set dressing. The wound is the reading, not a scene for atmosphere.

I stand behind the economics degree in the quiet and the missing physics credential that Quarter II waves like a flag. No degree means no physics degree. Correct the frame. Do not join their fight as entertainment. Do not sand the dab and the talk shit to make me respectable for Quarter IV’s grant photographs. Credentials are facts. Stigma is weather. Neither is a TED arc.

I stand behind the corpus dedicated to my father and the fact that he has read none of it. Outreach’s true goal stays unread. I will not invent a reunion to close a chapter. Unread is the load. Leave it. The fame that arrived sideways does not open that book for him. Measurement does not either. Some dedications stay one-way. A last page that forces him to open a book he has not opened would be a lie with soft lighting.

I stand behind my stated life goals: write natural philosophy and physics; smoke dab; talk shit. Unpolished on purpose. Not a joke to undercut the work — the work and the dab and the talking shit are the same man’s declared arrangement. The believers who want a clean prophet can polish someone else. The academy that hears dab and reaches for the file can keep reaching. The cannabis bar and the ignored shelf were one room before Lucid made the room audible. CongoSky’s Kind practice and the street’s spray can hold the sentence without requiring me to become photographable.

What I will not stand behind is a polished martyr brand, a solved assassination, a father scene that never happened, a textbook dump of the 420 Code, a forced reunion with the man on the Chobe for someone else’s emotional payoff, or a last chapter that pretends the four quarters have gone quiet because a Constant landed.

There will be readers who want the last pages to defeat the weather. I will not give them that. There will be readers who want me grateful for fame as if fame were vindication. I will not give them that either. Measurement was vindication. Fame was weather from a beam. Soft no was cruelty that felt like professionalism. Open attribution was kindness that did not feel kind while the quarters shouted. I have lived all of it. These pages carry what I can bear to have said in public under living-person care.

These pages hand to custody.

What I allow said about this life is a consent act, not a surrender. Partial is allowed. Careful is required. Bouwer careful. Hospital without spectacle. Father unread without tidy fix. Dab and talk shit left in. Crowns refused. Keyrings refused as sacrament. Stop-the-book is not my ask, that fight is platform weather on a river desk. My fight is rails around a life, not ownership of a mic.

If you need a moral, do not take one from me. Morals get polished into brands. Take the structure: a man wrote what he lived; a friend named the rails; a planet argued; two desks chose custody of meaning without requiring a shared room; the weather kept blowing.

The last thing I will say in this voice, before the outer weather takes the next turn, is simple:

I wrote what I lived.
I hold to what my conscience allows.
I will not become someone’s trophy or someone’s ghost.

The rest is custody rules and a planet that keeps shouting.
