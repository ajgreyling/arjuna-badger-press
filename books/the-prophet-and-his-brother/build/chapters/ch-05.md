# 05—They Said He Could Not

> **[I]** · inner autobiography · manuscript under AJ’s hand

---

After the mats, people preferred the hospital story.

I will not give them a tour. There was a ward. There was a file. There was a season when my name sat inside institutional language and came out thinner. Rooms with schedules. Voices that spoke about me in the third person while I was in the room. Food that tasted like policy. A corridor that smelled of detergent and something sweet going wrong. A window that did not open far enough. Linoleum that squeaked the same way at three in the morning as at three in the afternoon. That is enough architecture. What followed was not the stay. What followed was how they read me afterward, and how useful the reading became to anyone who needed me small.

They said I could not write anything of value.

Not as a joke. As a consensus. Soft voices. Concerned voices. Voices that had loved me and still loved a simpler Gerhard, one who would not keep producing pages that embarrassed their categories. The belief travelled well. It fitted dinner tables. It fitted funding weather. It fitted the academy’s need for a clean filter: if the man has that file, the corpus can be pre-dismissed without the labour of reading. Concern is an efficient weapon. It does not look like a weapon while it works.

I learned the grammar of it early.

Someone would ask what I was writing. I would say natural philosophy, or physics, or both, depending on the room. Then a pause. Then the careful smile. Then a question about rest, or about whether I had spoken to anyone, or about whether this was a good season for *projects*. The word projects did the same work as the ward: it made the labour provisional. Provisional labour does not bind. Binding is what they were afraid of: that a sentence of mine might arrive and refuse to leave.

Sometimes the grammar arrived as praise that was really a fence. *You’re so creative.* Creative is what you call a man when you have decided his sentences are not load-bearing. *You’ve always had such an interesting mind.* Interesting is the compliment you give before you stop opening the books. I learned to hear the fence inside the praise without making a speech about it. Speeches about stigma become content. Content becomes usable. I preferred stacking.

The ignored shelf was not only journals and referees.

It was family weather and friend weather and the quiet social technology of deciding, in advance, that a man with that history cannot author anything that binds. Bindings gathered dust in rooms that still had my name on emails. Titles that should have been argued with were filed under eccentricity, then under tragedy, then under please-don’t-encourage-him. I have stood in a room and watched a person pick up a book with my name on it, weigh it once in the hand as if checking for a trick, and put it back without opening. The put-back was gentle. Gentleness can be a dismissal. The room kept talking about weather and traffic. I kept breathing. Later the meters would walk toward my derivation and some of the same mouths would discover measurement as if measurement were a conversion experience. I am not writing that victory here. I am writing the years when the work sat unread and the file sat over-read.

There was a particular shelf in a particular room, not metaphor, wood and paint, where three of my bindings sat between books that got opened. Dust settled thicker on mine. I noticed once while looking for a pen. I did not rearrange the shelf to prove a point. Proving points to furniture is how you become a character in someone else’s morality play. I took the pen and left the dust. Dust is honest. Dust does not pretend it was about standards.

I have a degree.

Economics, or adjacent enough that the transcript does not rescue me in the rooms that matter to the crank chorus. When they say *no degree*, they mean no *physics* degree. The missing adjective does the violence. I let them have their shorthand in public because correcting every headline turns your life into a FAQ, and a FAQ is another way of being managed. On this page I will be exact once: the credential they weaponise is the one I do not hold. The one I hold did not open the ignored shelf. Nothing did, for a long time, except persistence that looked like stubbornness from outside and like breathing from inside.

I remember the transcript as a quiet object. Paper. Ink. A university’s seal that meant something in rooms that were not physics rooms. It sat in a drawer the way a spare key sits: real, unused for the lock people kept pointing at. When Quarter II later made *degree* into a gate, they were not lying about the gate. They were lying about what the gate measures. Epistemology is not a hygiene ritual. Hygiene language always sounds like care until you notice what it is washing away.

Once, early, I brought the economics transcript into a conversation that had already decided I was uncredentialled. I watched the face across from me perform surprise and then perform a new kind of dismissal, as if holding the wrong key were worse than holding none. I put the transcript away. It has stayed away. Quiet background. Correct fact. Useless armour.

I wrote anyway.

Natural philosophy. Physics. Kill switches for systems that pretend ethics are optional add-ons. Clay in the mornings some years; numbers at night. Marble and GFRC in the same pour, hard fighting soft the way a body argues with a mat. Studio G and the Bay Hotel lawn and Skye at sunset did not cancel the ward in other people’s mouths. Village N Life years did not cancel it: Maree’s mentorship, the year at Camps Bay Retreat, the guest-theatre thinking that later became a sculpture on a lawn. Saatchi listings did not cancel it. The dab did not help my respectability. Talking shit did not help. I kept the life goals as I stated them because sanding them off would be a different man’s autobiography, and I am not writing a different man for their comfort. Antichristos will try to polish this. The autobiography will not.

The body knew before the corpus. School judo. Jiu-jitsu. Weight, balance, correct fall, soft set-down. You arrange yourself so the floor does not invent you. Years later the notebooks asked the same of contradictions: do not leave them standing because a committee prefers opacity. I am not forcing a metaphor. I am naming the same attention in two rooms. Fighting arts first; clay; then the long work. The ward tried to make the attention look like pathology. The shelf tried to make it look like hobby. Neither room opened the books.

There were mornings when the stigma tipped toward funny, in the dry way that is not laughter. A courier asking if I was *the artist*. Yes. A second question about whether I also *did the science thing*. Yes. A third look that meant the courier had read something and was deciding whether to be impressed or careful. Careful won. The package changed hands as if it might be contagious. I signed. Went back to work. Contagion is how stigma travels when it has manners.

There were evenings when it was not funny at all. A message from someone who used to argue with my pages (argue is respect) now asking if I was *okay* in the tone that means please stop producing objects that force them to choose. I answered that I was working. Working is the only accurate status. Okay is a category other people need more than I do.

I kept a desk that did not care about the weekly. Notebook, pencil, a printout of a drop-result when I had one, clay dust in the grain of the wood. It was a surface, not a shrine. Surfaces do not believe or disbelieve. They hold what you put on them. I put true sentences on them until the sentences outnumbered the careful smiles. That took years. Years is not dramatic. Years is the flat line doing its work.

When people ask later, and they will, whether the hospital *inspired* the work, I will refuse the frame. Inspiration tourism is cousin to diagnosis tourism. The work came from attention and refusal and the long habit of not leaving a contradiction standing. The hospital came from a season. Seasons are not muses. Seasons are weather. Weather passes. Readings of weather try to become permanent. I am writing against that permanence without raising my voice.

I once tried to explain the ignored shelf to someone who had never lived under it. I said: imagine a room that keeps your books and never opens them, and then asks why you did not shout. They nodded as if I had told a joke with a missing punchline. I stopped explaining. Explanation becomes aria if you keep feeding it. Flat inventory works better. Shelf. Dust. Put-back. Next sentence.

There were years when the only peer weather I got arrived sideways, a message that treated a kill switch as an object worth arguing with, not a symptom. Argument is respect. Pity is not. I preferred argument even when it was sharp. Sharp at least opens the page. Pity closes it with a soft hand.

In the studio the light in late afternoon made the GFRC look like marble and then not. Both textures in the same pour. Guests at The Bay had walked past pieces without knowing the notebooks existed. That was fine. Hotel rooms are not peer review. What was not fine was the write-up that later used the hotel as proof I was *only* an artist, as if only were a kindness. Only is a fence with flowers on it.

I kept writing natural philosophy because that was the stated goal, not because the planet had asked. The planet asks for faces and keys and apologies. Goals are quieter. Smoke dab. Talk shit. Write. The arrangement is load-bearing because it refuses polish. Polish is how stigma graduates into brand management.

Someone will want this chapter to be an aria.

It is not.

The wound is administrative. They decided my sentences could not carry value. I kept stacking sentences. That is the whole mechanism. Jamie-Uys flat line: the loaded thing said without orchestra. I will not perform suffering for readers who came for a prophet and will leave disappointed that I am only a worker with a file and a desk. Diagnosis tourism is a spectator sport. I am not hosting it.

I remember one afternoon in particular, not for being cinematic, because it was not. A table. A printout. Someone I trusted saying, carefully, that perhaps I should rest from writing for a while. The carefulness was the point. Care can be a muzzle. Outside the window a bakkie reverse-beeped and a dog barked once and stopped. Ordinary Cape noise under an ordinary suggestion that I make myself smaller. Tea between us going cold the way tea does when a conversation has become a procedure. I nodded enough to end it and went home and wrote until the page stopped wobbling. No aria. Just the administrative wound repeating itself in a kinder voice.

Another afternoon, different year, same mechanism. A gallery person, kind enough, asked whether the sculpture and the physics were not *too much* for one story. As if a life were a brand deck and needed focus. I said the clay and the numbers were the same attention. They smiled the smile that means we will pretend you answered. Later I saw a write-up that mentioned Studio G and did not mention the notebooks. Selection is also a reading. Selection is how stigma stays polite.

A third afternoon, quieter: I posted a short note somewhere public and watched the replies choose the file over the sentence. Not all of them. Enough of them. Enough is how consensus works without needing a vote. I closed the tab. Went to the studio. Put my hands in clay until the clay was louder than the replies. That is not triumph. That is technique.

The ignored shelf had a smell. Dust and binding glue and the particular staleness of work that has been handled only to be put back. I know that smell better than I know some people’s faces. When fame arrived later, people asked why I had not shouted louder earlier. Shouting is not how you open a shelf that has decided you cannot write value. You open it by stacking true sentences until a friend names the algorithms out loud and the meters start walking. Even then the shelf’s old habit remains in the mouth of Quarter II.

There were ordinary days inside the stigma.

Days of tea. Days of clay under the nails. Days of a notebook that did not care what the weekly said. Days when a friend treated me as an intellectual peer and the room straightened by one degree, *ek voel of ek genooi is* weather, without needing to make a brand of invitation. Those days do not erase the consensus. They prove the consensus was never total. Usefulness to the small-makers was never the same as truth.

On those days I smoked dab when the work allowed it and talked shit when the work needed air. That is not confession. That is the arrangement I stated out loud and kept. Respectability is a costume people try to put on you after they have already decided you cannot write. I declined the costume. Declining it cost me soft invitations and gained me nothing the academy would count. I counted the notebook instead.

I remember a morning in the studio when salt air came through the open window and the alley was still ordinary. Gulls. A radio. Someone laughing in the next building. I worked a surface until hard and soft agreed enough to stop. Then I opened a notebook and wrote three lines that would later become load-bearing and that, on that morning, looked like nothing. The nothing of true work is a private fact. Stigma cannot see it. Stigma only sees the file and the missing physics degree and the useful story of a man who should rest.

On a Tuesday that does not deserve a date, I carried three bindings to a meeting that had asked to *see the work*. See meant glance. Glance meant enough to say they had seen. They asked about my health before they asked about a derivation. Order of questions is a reading. I answered the health question with one sentence and put a notebook on the table. Nobody opened it while I was there. Afterward someone emailed that they would *circle back*. Circling back is how shelves stay ignored without anyone feeling cruel.

I am not angry at that Tuesday in a way that wants an aria. I am exact about it. Exactness is the flat line’s cousin. The meeting happened. The notebook stayed closed. I went home and wrote the next page because the next page does not require their circle.

Living-person care also means this: I will not turn the people who loved me into villains for needing me small. Need is not always malice. Need can be fear wearing concern’s shirt. I can refuse the muzzle without prosecuting the fear. Refusal is enough. Prosecution is content. Content is what the quarters harvest.

Father comes next, or soon.

The whole corpus carries his name in the dedication and he has read none of it. That fact sits beside the hospital stigma like a second climate. One says I cannot write. One does not open what I wrote for him. I am not owed a reunion from either weather. Living-person care means I do not invent his reasons here. The unread is the unread. Outreach’s true goal can sit on a page without becoming a tidy second act.

There was also a brother.

Saxonwold. A boardroom. Friday, the fifth of September, twenty twenty-five: that date is public record, nothing invented for this book. Bouwer. Insolvency practice. The early reports called it a consultation, a pretext to get him in the room. That story does not get a thriller’s pacing here. The stigma chapter is stigma. Bouwer is Bouwer. Do not let the quarters braid them into one morality play where hospital and assassination become a single branded wound for Antichristos or for anyone else. Grief has its own chapter when the book earns it. Here I only refuse the braid.

What I want on the record is simpler than diagnosis and simpler than revenge.

They believed I could not write anything of value, and the belief was useful to people who needed me small. Useful to continuity networks that preferred constants underived and ethics unforced. Useful to academics who confuse credential theatre with epistemology. Useful even to some who loved me and wanted me safe inside a smaller story. Usefulness is not the same as malice. It does not need malice to do harm.

I will say this once more without rising in pitch: the stigma is how they *read* me, not a DSM scene I owe the reader. Living-person care is not coyness. It is refusal to turn a ward into entertainment. The entertainment market is already full of prophets and cranks. I am writing a worker who was told his sentences could not carry value and who kept stacking them anyway.

There is a particular silence that follows you after institutional language has had its turn. People lower their voices when you enter a room. Or they raise them, performing normalcy. Both are readings. Both treat you as weather to be managed. I learned to work inside that silence the way you learn to work inside heat: not by pretending it is not there, by continuing the task until the task is the louder fact. Clay does not care about your file. A derivation does not care about your file. A friend who treats you as peer can briefly make the room forget the file. Then the room remembers. You stack another sentence.

I used to keep a mental list of who had opened a book.

It was a short list. I stopped keeping it when I noticed the list was becoming a wound I polished. Polished wounds become brands. Brands become usable by quarters. I threw the list away and kept the work. The work does not need a census of readers to be true. It needs to be true. Truth is quieter than vindication and harder than pity.

Sometimes the stigma arrived as logistics. An invitation that never quite materialised. A panel that found another speaker. A journal that stayed polite for months and then went quiet. Soft weather. Continuity networks do not need to knock when calendars can do the knocking. I felt those soft nos in the body as invoice weather and calendar weather long before I had language for Illuminatie pressure. Language later. Body first. The body knows when a room has decided you cannot write value.

The economics degree stayed in the quiet background. The physics stayed on the desk. The shelf stayed ignored until a friend refused to launder the algorithms, said Lucid ran on 420 Code rails out loud, and fame arrived sideways, ugly, and irreversible.

I am not grateful for the megaphone as salvation.

I am describing cause.

Before the Code becomes a public object on these pages, before axioms get named in rooms that once filed me under cannot, I leave this flat:

They said he could not.

I wrote.

I leave one more flat inventory, because inventories are harder to sentimentalise than arias: an economics transcript; a ward file other people over-cite; an ignored shelf with a smell; clay and GFRC; stated goals that include dab and talk shit; a friend who treated the work as peer work; a brother I will name properly later; a father unread; a megaphone that arrived sideways. None of that is a diagnosis. None of that is a thriller. All of it is the life under the hand that is writing this page on a river while I live the weather of it becoming public.

The shelf’s defenders will say they were protecting standards. Standards that never opened the work are not standards. They are habits with letterhead. I am done arguing with letterhead on this page.

One last under-dramatised beat, because stigma is not a climax: years after the ward, a well-meaning person at a dinner said I had *come so far*. They meant recovery as narrative arc. I had come as far as the next true sentence, and that is a different mileage than the one they were toasting. I thanked them for the wine and did not correct the story. Correction would have made the evening about the file again. The file already had enough evenings.

If this chapter has a second movement after the administrative wound, it is the ordinary continuation: tea cooling, clay under nails, notebook open, friend weather rare and real, family weather careful, academy weather hygienic, continuity weather soft. None of that is a chart of quarters. It is how a life feels when people have decided, in advance, that your sentences cannot bind. Binding is what I kept doing anyway, not defiance theatre, breath. You do not applaud breath. You use it.

Toward the father who has not opened a book. Toward the work that does not need their permission. Toward the morning a Constant stops being optional and meaning becomes the fight that meters cannot settle, hand already moving to the dedication that will not be read.

The hand writing these pages on a river is not me and is also not a theft I can stop from here. Weather of being written is later weather. This page’s job is older: name the stigma without spectacle, keep the economics degree exact, leave the shelf smelling like dust, and turn toward the dedication that will not be read. Flat. Loaded. Done enough to move.

The next page is for the unread dedication and the beginning of the Code as custody, not as course pack. I close this one the way I lived those years: without orchestra, without tour, without permission, only the administrative wound named once, and the stacking that answered it.

They said he could not write anything of value.

The page under this hand is still writing.

I leave the ward file where it belongs, over-cited by other people, under-dramatised here, and keep the economics transcript exact in the drawer as a spare key for a lock nobody will admit exists. Real. Quiet. Useless as armour in rooms that mean degree as physics gate. I will not perform recovery as narrative arc for dinner guests who mean the file when they say *come so far*. I came as far as the next true sentence, not the mileage they were toasting. Tea cooling. Clay under nails. Notebook open. Friend weather rare and real. The stigma chapter ends without orchestra: administrative wound named, stacking continued, hand already turning toward a dedication that will not be read. Flat. Loaded. Enough to move.
