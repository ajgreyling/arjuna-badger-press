# 14—Clay and Constants

> **[I]** · inner autobiography · manuscript under AJ’s hand

---

Before the Constant had a public argument, I had clay under my nails.

Stellenbosch, 1980—that is the birth year the bios keep. The body arrived first; the corpus arrived later; the academy’s preferred story prefers to begin at the ignored shelf because a shelf is tidy. Clay is not tidy. Clay remembers every thumb. Marble dust remembers the mix. GFRC remembers the day you got the pour wrong and the day you got it right enough to cast again.

I learned the body on mats, Judo first, then Jiu-Jitsu, the long apprenticeship of correct fall, and then I learned it again by modelling it: hard against soft, smooth against rough, growth against decay, permanent against temporary, realistic against abstract, classic against contemporary. Those juxtapositions are not a brand deck. They are what a hand discovers when it refuses to choose only one truth for a surface. On the mats, posture wrong meant someone smaller could end you. In the clay, posture wrong meant the figure lied about weight. Later, in the axioms, posture wrong meant a structure that looked clever and fell when measured. Same attention. Same refusal to waste motion.

The dojo smell is still under the studio smell if you know where to look, sweat-salt in the plaster, the particular quiet after a correct throw when both bodies agree the fall was fair. I did not become a sculptor to escape fighting arts. I became a sculptor because the same question kept arriving: where does weight go, and what happens when you lie about it. A hip joint that flatters the eye will betray the hand that has to hold the cast. A premise that flatters a room will betray the measurement that arrives later without applause.

I modelled in clay. I cast in a marble-and-GFRC composite I mixed myself: glass fibre, cement, marble dust arguing inside the same pour. Hard and soft in one object. People who later met me as a man with axioms liked to call the sculpture a hobby. It was never a hobby. It was the same attention that would later refuse to invent what the record did not support. You arrange weight so the fall is correct. You arrange mass so the figure holds. You arrange premises so the structure does not collapse. Same man.

The studio smells of dust and wet earth even on clean days. That smell is older than any inbox. It gets into the creases of the knuckles and stays. When people later asked how an economics graduate without a physics degree could derive what they preferred underived, I could have said: I already knew what happens when a surface pretends to be one thing. I did not say that in rooms that wanted a TED answer. I kept pouring.

Early clay taught me patience the mats had already started. You cannot rush a figure into honesty. You can rush a figure into looking finished, smooth where it should argue, soft where it should hold, and then the light arrives and exposes the lie. Hotel light is merciless that way. So is measurement. So is a friend who writes your life without laundering the awkward rooms. I prefer the merciless teachers. They keep you from becoming a costume.

There is an afternoon light in Stellenbosch I still use as a private standard, not nostalgia, a calibration. When a figure looks honest in that light, it has a chance elsewhere. When it only looks honest under gallery spots, it is already lying. The Constant later asked for the same honesty under instruments instead of windows. I brought the old virtue into a colder room.

---

Village N Life came before Studio G had a name people could search.

I worked there until about mid-2005. Maree Brink mentored. Mentorship in that register is not a LinkedIn word; it is someone correcting your eye when the guest experience is lying about itself. I lived more than a year at Camps Bay Retreat, Earl’s Dyke then, with Hannetjie Brink, Maree’s late mother. A hotel is a theatre whether the guests admit it. Guests move through light and lawn and the promise of being briefly elsewhere. I began to think of sculpture as part of that theatre: not décor stuck in a corner, not a card with a shadow that does the wrong job, an anchor in the guest’s first breath of place. The Retreat taught me that atmosphere is not a slogan. It is where a body stands when it arrives.

Camps Bay light is a specific teacher. It does not flatter forever. It shows edges. Morning softens; afternoon argues; sunset tells the truth about mass. I learned to place work where the light would finish the sentence the clay had started. That is craft. The same craft later refused metaphor when a derivation needed a drop-result instead of a speech.

There were mornings at Earl’s Dyke when I walked the lawn before guests woke and practised seeing the theatre empty, no performance, only place. Empty theatre teaches you what an object must do without an audience applauding it into meaning. Skye would later need that lesson. Studio G would later need that lesson. The desk would later need that lesson when fame arrived sideways and tried to applaud the work into a costume. Empty lawn first. Weight second. Applause never.

Maree’s mentorship corrected the eye when a placement was décor pretending to be anchor. Hannetjie’s house corrected the life when a younger man mistook intensity for exactness. Village N Life until mid-2005 was the long apprenticeship in guest flow and atmosphere, a set of rooms where I learned that bodies arrive carrying hope for elsewhere and will forgive many things except a false first breath of place. It was work before it was a story anyone could date.

I remember a Tuesday when a guest path had been routed past a figure that caught light wrong, the shadow made the belly look soft in a way the clay had not earned. Maree did not lecture. She moved the path three degrees. The figure became honest again. Three degrees. That is how most lies die if you are willing to move your feet instead of your mouth. Years later, when soft institutional weather arrived dressed as process, I recognised the opposite skill: people moving the path so the figure stays flattering and the truth never gets a clean angle.

Skye went onto a lawn at Camps Bay Retreat, feminine sculpture, sunset work, a presence that does not need a label to do its job. People photographed her. People stood near her without knowing my life goals. That was fine. The work does not require a biography to hold weight. Antichristos, years later, would try to turn figures like her into relics. Quarter II would pretend the man who made her could not also derive. Both mistakes need me simpler than a pour.

I remember the install days more clearly than the praise. Scaffold. Level. The argument between lawn and plinth. A guest asking whether she was for sale while the light was still deciding her edges. Maree’s eye. Hannetjie’s house holding a younger man who had not yet been told, by institutions and by people who loved him badly, that he could not write anything of value. The sculpture held. The holding was the point.

Skye taught the guest experience what a brochure could not: that arrival can include weight. Camps Bay Retreat’s theatre was lawn and light and the sea’s argument beyond; she became part of that first breath. I did not stand beside her explaining juxtaposition. It was already in the surface, hard/soft, permanent/temporary, for anyone willing to look without a guide. Years later, when fame arrived sideways and people hunted relics, some would photograph her as if prophecy had always lived on that lawn. Prophecy is what you invent when craft embarrasses your categories. She was craft. She still is, wherever the lawn keeps her.

The Retreat years were apprenticeship in atmosphere. Earl’s Dyke then; Camps Bay Retreat in the telling that stuck. Mentorship from Maree; household gravity from Hannetjie. Village N Life until mid-2005 was work: guest flow, place-making, the slow understanding that sculpture could be load-bearing in a hotel’s first impression without becoming lobby candy. When I left that chapter, the attention did not leave. It waited for Studio G to have walls.

---

Later I conceptualised Studio G at The Bay Hotel in Bloubergstrand, curated sculpture and painting for guests, a must-visit for collectors if the longer vision held, a sculpture garden at The Retreat if time and money and courage aligned. Short-term: unique contemporary art experiences at the hotels. Longer: the garden. Official launch of Studio G at The Bay: 27 July 2018. Village N Life partnership weather around that summer. Tourists about half the sales in those years, if the interview numbers still mean anything. Saatchi Art from 2017. studiog.co.za. My own site with the 2018 interview still sitting where interviews sit, Julia Strydom / Village N Life blog copy in the corporate archive, public record of a man who was already both gallery and desk.

Launch days are ordinary when you are inside them. Paint still drying somewhere it should not be. A figure catching the wrong reflection. Collectors arriving with the particular hunger of people who want to own weight. Hotel staff moving guests past curated work the way people move past anything that asks them to feel without a brochure lecture. I talked shit in the corners when the frame was wrong. I smoked when the day earned it. I did not sand those facts for the opening photographs. The photographs preferred cleaner surfaces. The life did not.

The night before the 27 July opening, humidity argued with a pour I had scheduled too confidently. Marble dust clumped. Fibre won a patch it should have shared. I stood in the studio with the failed section cooling and considered sanding the whole surface into the lie of smoothness so the morning guests would not see the quarrel. I did not. Hard edge stayed where hard belonged. Soft belly stayed soft. By noon the next day, people walked past a truth that did not need my speech, and I was tired the way only exactness tires you, not drama-tired, craft-tired.

Village N Life’s corporate archive still holds the interview weather and the grand-opening report, public, citeable, useful to anyone who needs a date. Useful is not complete. Complete would include the humidity fight. Complete would include the friend who stood in the doorway without needing a tour. Complete would include the quiet after guests left when the room smelled of wine and dust and I knew the longer vision, the garden at The Retreat, might never fully arrive, and that the short vision was already enough if I kept the route honest.

Bloubergstrand light differs from Camps Bay light the way two honest teachers differ: same subject, different angle. The Bay Hotel asked for curated sculpture and painting as guest experience, Studio G as room and route, not a closet of afterthoughts. Short-term vision: unique contemporary art experiences at the hotels. Longer: sculpture garden at The Retreat; Studio G as must-visit for collectors. Tourists about half the sales in those years. Collectors the other weather. Saatchi Art from 2017 sitting in parallel as another public shelf. None of that made me respectable enough for the academy’s preferred story. Respectability was never the goal. Exactness was.

There was a collector once who wanted the parable. I gave him the mix ratio instead. He left irritated and later bought a smaller piece through Saatchi without asking for myth. That is the transaction I trust: weight for money, not prophecy for applause. Antichristos would later reverse the trade and call it devotion. Quarter II would reverse it differently and call the clay proof I was unserious. Both trades prefer a simpler man than the pour allows.

---

The warehouse nights and the hotel days were one life. Cannabis bar and kill-switch dark. Talking shit and CODATA weather. A T-shirt that carried a sentence the walls would later spray everywhere: **Don’t be a cunt — be kind.** Guests at The Bay moved past curated work. Collectors came when they came. Tourists bought what the light made undeniable. I was not practising to become respectable. I was practising to stay exact, in clay, in conversation, in the long work that would later be called natural philosophy by people who needed a category and physics by people who needed a fight.

Studio G’s longer vision, the garden at The Retreat, collectors treating the hotel rooms as a route, was never separate from the desk where axioms got installed. People who only saw one room missed the pour. The Bay’s official opening date sits in Village N Life’s corporate archive the way public dates sit: available, citeable, insufficient. A date does not tell you about the mix going wrong at midnight. A date does not tell you about the friend whose sudden death sits in the remembering posts on my site, James van Niekerk, friend not brother, grief with a different address than Bouwer’s boardroom. Living-person care: I name the mark and do not make a thriller of either. The studio kept working. The desk kept working. That is the arrangement after the fall is already correct enough to stand up again.

GFRC is an argument you win with patience. Marble dust wants one thing; fibre wants another; cement holds the quarrel. I developed the composite because the figures needed hard and soft in one cast, not a surface apologising for itself. When engineers later asked for keys to stacks built on Code rails, I recognised the hunger: they wanted a clean interface. Interfaces are useful. Interfaces are also how a life gets flattened into a product face. Sculpture had already taught me that a clean interface can be a lie if the weight underneath is wrong.

I keep a scrap of failed pour somewhere the guests never see, not as relic, as teacher. The failure shows where fibre won too hard and marble dust lost the argument. Looking at it steadies the hand before a new mix. Looking at a bad frame in conversation steadies the mouth before talk shit. Looking at a premise that wants to be clever steadies the derivation before it falls in public. Same scrap logic. Same man.

On good studio days the body forgets the inbox. Hands in clay. Dust on the floor. A figure’s belly soft where the world expects armour; a shoulder hard where the world expects comfort. Juxtaposition as method, not brand. The academy that preferred me silent liked to point at the studio as proof of unseriousness, as if a man who understands mass in one room cannot understand mass in another. Antichristos liked to point at the studio as proof of prophecy, as if a feminine sculpture on a Camps Bay lawn were a relic waiting for liturgy. Both hungers need a single surface.

I kept two surfaces. I kept them as one attention.

The warehouse had its own light, less flattering than Camps Bay, more honest about dust. Cannabis bar weather and kill-switch dark could share a night without becoming a brand deck. Someone would ask for the elevator version of the work; I would talk shit until the elevator gave up. Someone would ask whether kindness was a personality trait; the T-shirt already carried the structural claim: **Don’t be a cunt — be kind.** CongoSky would later embody that as company law; walls would catch fire; I would refuse ownership of the religion that wanted a face. The warehouse night did not know it was rehearsing 2035 weather. It knew exactness and smoke and the refusal to sand a life into respectability.

There were nights when the axioms sat open on a laptop with clay dust on the keys, and the figure on the stand waited, and neither room won. That is the arrangement people keep trying to split for me: gallery or desk, artist or crank, soft man or hard theorem. Split is how you make a usable lie. Unsplit is how you keep a life from collapsing under its own false categories.

---

My stated life goals are not a slogan I test-drive for a crowd.

Write natural philosophy and physics.

Smoke dab.

Talk shit.

I keep them unpolished because polishing is how a quarter of the planet turns a man into a usable lie. The academy hears dab and reaches for the hospital file. The faith that wants a face hears talk shit and tries to sand it into parable. The engineers who want keys hear natural philosophy and ask for a signature on a lab door. I am still the man who said those three goals out loud because they are true. The mats are under them. The clay is under them. The GFRC pour is under them. The ignored shelf is under them. The unread father dedication is under them, entire corpus pointed at a man who has read none of it. Bouwer is under them, Saxonwold, Friday 5 September 2025, insolvency practice, public record wound, consultation pretext, boardroom, and that page is not this page’s thriller. This page is clay and constants in the same life. The wound travels forward without being solved.

Talk shit is not a slogan for cruelty. It is the refusal to let a false frame stand because the room prefers manners. Dab is not a footnote. It is part of the arrangement that keeps the work from becoming a costume. Write natural philosophy and physics is the long labour that does not care whether Quarter II has finished singing about credentials. Economics sits in the quiet background where it belongs, training, not redemption arc. The missing physics degree is a weapon other people hold. I do not hold it for them.

I have said the three goals in rooms that wanted me to flinch. Warehouse rooms. Hotel corners after guests left. Desk rooms where axioms were being installed and someone asked for a cleaner personal brand. Cleaner personal brand meant: hide the dab, baptise the talk shit into wit, turn natural philosophy into a soft TED shape that would not frighten funding weather. I declined. Declining is also a pour, you leave the hard edge where the hard edge belongs.

On warehouse nights the goals were visible without being performed. Someone would say something false about the work; I would talk shit until the falsehood had to sit down. Someone would try to turn kindness into a brand deck; the T-shirt already carried the load-bearing line without a founder portrait. Someone would ask whether the sculpture and the axioms were a phase. Phases are what people invent when they need you to graduate into respectability. I did not graduate. I poured.

There is a particular quiet after a good dab and a true sentence, not enlightenment, not brand wellness, just the nervous system settling enough to see where a premise lies. People who need me housebroken call that unserious. People who need a messiah call it sacrament. It is the arrangement. Write. Smoke. Talk shit. Repeat until the shelf that ignored the work has to pretend it always knew, or until measurement does the knowing without their permission.

Studio G and the axiom desk were never two careers fighting for custody of a résumé. They were one attention learning where weight goes.

At the Bay, guests walked past figures that held hard/soft without apology. At the desk, I derived kindness the way I derived structures that do not fall, not as a sermon, as coherence. CongoSky would later embody a sentence on company law; walls would catch fire with spray paint; Antichristos would want a man; the Kind would want a line. I was already living the juxtaposition. The believers who arrived after the megaphone tried to polish the gallery into a relic hall. The academy that preferred me silent tried to pretend an artist cannot be a physicist. Both need a single surface.

I once watched a tourist photograph Skye as if the lawn were a shrine and then ask staff where the gift shop was. I once watched a physicist’s inbox treat a correct derivation as noise because the sender lacked the preferred letters after his name. Same hunger for a simpler story. Same refusal, from my side, to become that story’s prop.

---

When AJ writes these pages from a river truck I cannot see, the weather of being written arrives as pressure on the present body. Honey Badger in the pocket, blue, clip, one-hand open and close. Double Knife Brother kinship across distance. I do not need a reunion scene to know the twin Joseph Rogers went one way and the empty pocket stayed the other. Knives are custody, not choreography. Fame arrived sideways because a friend refused to launder the algorithms. Lucid popular. Open attribution. 420 Code named. The ignored shelf did not become a discovery story. It became a megaphone story. Quarter II still weaponises the missing physics degree. Measurement converging on my derivation of G does not stop that song. Vindication as measurement is not vindication as manners.

I feel the manuscript choosing clay before it chooses the soft nos. The choosing is a kind of mercy. Soft nos will get their page. Degree choruses will get their page. This page keeps the hand in the mix: Skye on a lawn, Studio G at The Bay, the guest-flow years at the Retreat that ran long before anyone thought to date a launch, Maree’s mentorship, Hannetjie’s house, the mix that let cement, glass fibre and marble dust hold their quarrel in one surface, the mats under everything, the small blue knife carried later not as souvenir but as a brother’s weight in the hand.

If you begin only at the ignored shelf, you get a morality play. If you begin only at the Constant, you get a clean lecture shape. If you begin at the mats and the clay, you get a man who already knew what soft set-down costs. Kindness derived, not preached, the same ladder later popularised as wall paint, started as structure, not brand. I will not pretend the dab is a footnote. I will not pretend talking shit is accidental. Those goals are the arrangement that keeps the work from becoming a costume.

There were afternoons at The Bay when a collector asked for the story of a figure and I gave them weight instead of myth. Hard edge. Soft belly. Growth and decay sharing a surface. They wanted a parable; I gave them craft. There were nights at the desk when a premise wanted to be clever and I made it fall on purpose so I could see where the structure lied. Same refusal. Same man. The public record can cite Saatchi and studiog.co.za and the 27 July 2018 launch and the Village N Life tag and the interview that still sits on gerhardvanniekerk.com. It cannot cite the smell of the mix at midnight or the exact moment Skye’s edges told the truth at sunset. That is what autobiography is for, not invention, custody of the un-citeable ordinary.

CODATA weather and gallery weather can share a calendar week without sharing a press release. A drop-result converging on a derivation does not cancel clay under the nails. An axiom installed cleanly does not apologise for a feminine sculpture on a Camps Bay lawn. The quarters need those cancellations. I do not. Artist and axiom-installer is not a paradox I perform for sympathy. It is a fact I live on purpose.

James’s sudden death and Bouwer’s assassination are not the same wound and not this page’s centre. They sit under the goals the way mats sit under clay, present, load-bearing, not available for thriller pacing. Father’s unread dedication sits under them too. Hospital stigma sits under them, the belief, in people close and far, that a man who has been institutionalised cannot write anything of value. The knife’s later click sits under them too, a brother’s weight carried in the palm across a distance that needs no shared room. AJ’s river truck can hold the manuscript; I can hold the life. Consent about what ships remains a hinge for another chapter. This chapter keeps the pour.

Artist and axiom-installer: same man.

Clay under the nails. Constants on the page. Dab when the day earns it. Talk shit when the frame is wrong. Write natural philosophy and physics until the shelf that ignored the work has to pretend it always knew. Skye on a lawn. Studio G at The Bay. The Retreat years that were work long before any launch date got quoted at me. Maree’s mentorship. Hannetjie’s house. Cement, glass fibre, marble dust holding their quarrel in one pour. The mats under everything. The small blue knife, later, carried as a brother’s weight, not a prop.

I did not become simpler for any of them.

Next page can carry the soft nos and the degree chorus. This page carries the hand in the clay and the same hand at the desk, unpolished goals intact, gallery and derivation unsplit, hard and soft in one surface because that is how a life that does not collapse is made. The manuscript can move on. The clay stays under the next axiom whether the quarters like it or not.
