# 11 — Bouwer

> **[I]** · inner autobiography · manuscript under AJ’s hand

---

My brother was an insolvency attorney.

That is the flat line. The loaded one. Bouwer van Niekerk — Johannesburg, Saxonwold, business rescue and commercial weather that does not need my embroidery. On Friday, 5 September 2025, he was shot and killed at his practice. Public record. Law Society statement days later. Investigation ongoing as of what the record held. I am not going to solve it on this page. I am not going to invent an assassin’s CV. I am not going to hand the killing to a continuity network for a plot twist. The wound is the brother dead in a boardroom. Speculation can stay outside unless I authorise it, and today I authorise grief, not theatre.

They arrived under the pretext of a booked consultation. Four suspects, initial reports said. Gunshots in the boardroom. He died at the scene. Attackers fled. He had been engaged in contentious commercial and insolvency matters — business rescue litigation among them — and the papers did what papers do: fog, allegation, the wide reporting that is not the same as a verdict. The Law Society condemned a targeted attack on a legal practitioner and on the rule of law. I condemned nothing with a press release. I stood in a life that had a Bouwer-shaped hole and tried not to waste the fall.

People who want a thriller from me will be disappointed. People who want the Illuminatie’s fingerprints on the trigger will have to write their own book. This one is autobiography, not a whodunit. The mats taught me not to posture for an audience when the weight is already correct. Bouwer’s death is weight. I set it down soft because soft is not weak. Soft is refusal to turn a brother into choreography.

---

I remember him as work.

Insolvency is not glamorous. It is rooms where money has failed and someone still has to arrange the bodies of companies so they do not crush the living. He was good at that arrangement. Respected. The kind of respect that does not need a physics degree or a megaphone. When I was still the ignored shelf — economics credential quiet in the background, hospital stigma louder than any derivation — Bouwer was the brother who had a profession the world understood. That understanding did not save him. Understanding rarely does. I am not writing his case list. I am writing that he went to work as himself and left as a headline.

I will not invent his last morning for you. I do not have it as a film. What I have is the public shape: Friday, 5 September 2025; Saxonwold practice; booked consultation as pretext; four suspects in initial reports; gunshots in the boardroom; died at the scene; attackers fled; contentious commercial and insolvency matters including business rescue litigation in the weather around his work. That is enough to name a wound. It is not enough to build a thriller. I choose the wound.

After Saxonwold the phone did what phones do. Condolences. Theories. People who had never entered a boardroom explaining a boardroom. Cousins of cousins with a take. Strangers with a timeline. Journalists who wanted a face for the fog. I smoked dab and talked shit and wrote because those are my stated life goals and because stopping would have been a performance of grief for people who confuse performance with love. The corpus stayed dedicated to my father. My father still had not read it. Bouwer would not read the pages that came after him either. Dedication is not a postal service. It is a direction you keep facing.

I remember ordinary brother things and I will not inventory them for atmosphere. Inventory is how you turn a person into content. He laughed in a register I can still hear if I do not reach for it. He understood commercial wreckage the way I understood weight on a mat — as arrangement under constraint. We did not always talk. Brothers do not always need to. The silence after 5 September was a different species of silence. It did not arrange anything. It just stayed.

There was another death in the wider weather of my life — James van Niekerk, friend, sudden, remembered on my own site under a word as small as *Remembering*. I name the distinction so no one conflates them. James is not Bouwer. Bouwer is not a metaphor for opacity establishments. Bouwer is Bouwer. Friday. Saxonwold. Consultation pretext. Boardroom. Flat line.

The days immediately after did not arrive as a montage. They arrived as logistics. Who to call. Who not to call. Which message to ignore because the sender wanted a theory more than a person. Which doorway still smelled like a life that had expected Monday. Which suit to wear to a room that should not have needed a suit that week. I do not dramatise the logistics for you. Under-dramatise is the only honesty I trust with this. The loaded line stays flat on purpose.

I drove roads I had driven before and they did not rearrange themselves into meaning. Johannesburg heat sat on the car roof the way heat always sits. Traffic did what traffic does. A radio voice talked about something that was not Saxonwold and then, later, about Saxonwold in the careful register news uses when a profession has been hit and the profession still has to go to work on Monday. I turned the radio down. Not off. Down. Silence can become a performance too if you make it absolute.

Someone offered me water and I took it. Someone offered me a theory and I put the glass down carefully so I would not throw it. Kindness and recruitment share doorways after a murder. You learn which hand is which by whether it needs your face for its story.

I slept in pieces. Woke in pieces. Made tea in pieces. The body kept trying to schedule a day that included a brother who arranged failed companies for a living and then came home. The day refused the schedule. I did not punish the day for that. I also did not thank it. Flat line. Soft set-down. The mats again, without a mat.

There were rooms with flowers. Rooms with papers. Rooms with people who had known Bouwer as a practitioner and said so without needing me to become a microphone for their shock. I preferred those rooms. Shock that does not recruit is rare and useful. I stood in it when it was offered and left when the conversation tilted toward motive charts. Motive charts are how strangers steal a Friday.

---

The public record is enough and not enough.

Enough for what may be said without inventing. Not enough for a heart that wants Monday back. LSSA spoke for the profession on 8 September 2025 — condemned the killing, called for protection of legal practitioners, named what the investigation had so far held. I speak for a shared childhood that is none of your business beyond this: he was my brother, and he was murdered at work under a lie about an appointment, and the fog of related commercial reporting can swirl forever without me adopting it as fact on this page.

I will say the fog once, flat, so no one claims I hid it: allegations around related commercial schemes were widely reported. Widely reported is not a verdict. I do not adopt the fog. I do not clear it with inventions either. The boardroom does not become clearer because a novelist wants a motive chart. The boardroom stays a boardroom where a consultation was pretended and a man who arranged failed companies was killed.

The fighting arts did not prepare me for that room. Judo and Jiu-Jitsu prepare you for weight you can still arrange. Assassination is weight that arrives already arranged by someone else. Mastery, if the word means anything, is knowing the difference and not lying about it. I could put a body on a mat correctly. I could put clay into a pour correctly. I could not put my brother back into Monday.

Grief is not a derivation. It does not converge. Some days it is quiet enough to work through. Some days it is a boardroom door that will not open onto the right man. Some days it is a number I reach for and do not dial. Habit is not hope. Habit is the body refusing the new map.

People brought food. People brought theories. People brought the particular kindness that wants you to cry on schedule so they can feel useful. I was not ungrateful. I was also not a schedule. Jamie-Uys restraint is not coldness; it is refusal to turn pain into a show for the room. I kept saying my brother was an insolvency attorney because that sentence held more truth than any aria I could invent about destiny or countries or continuity networks.

I sat in rooms where people spoke carefully around the word *assassinated* as if softer synonyms would make the Friday less Friday. I did not correct their vocabulary every time. Correction is also a performance if you do it for the room. I corrected when a sentence tried to recruit the death. Recruitment is the only grammar I refuse here.

One afternoon a stranger at a doorway asked whether I thought it was “political.” I said my brother was an insolvency attorney. The stranger waited for more. I did not give more. Waiting is not the same as earning. Another person asked whether I had “closure.” Closure is a word for furniture. I had a hole. I still have it. The hole does not close because a calendar advances or because a Law Society statement exists or because I write this page under another man’s hand on a river. The page is custody of a wound, not a lid.

I remember standing outside a building that was not the boardroom and not needing to go in. The body knows some doors. I did not perform pilgrimage at Saxonwold for this autobiography. Public record is enough for place. Grief does not require a tourist’s coordinates. If you need the street name for your own map, the record has it. If you need me to walk you through the door for atmosphere, you will leave disappointed. Atmosphere is how murder becomes entertainment.

There were nights when talk shit was the only honest liturgy available — not jokes at the wound, just speech that refused to become a press release. Dab in the arrangement. Clay in the arrangement. Sleep arriving late and leaving early. None of that is offered here as lifestyle brand. It is how a stated life kept walking when a brother stopped.

---

Studio G kept going because clay does not pause for mythology.

Skye stayed on a lawn. The Bay Hotel still had guests who bought contemporary work without needing my brother’s name. Village N Life years were already past — Maree, Hannetjie, Camps Bay Retreat, the theatre of guest experience — and they did not explain Saxonwold either. Nothing useful explains Saxonwold. Explanation is often a way to leave.

I mixed composite the week after because my hands knew the proportions and because stopping the pour would have been a speech. Hard aggregate. Soft binder. The body in marble and GFRC does not ask for a culprit. It asks whether the surface holds. I held surfaces. I failed some. I kept the failed pours on a shelf the way I keep other failed things: as teachers, not as relics.

The studio smelled the same — dust, sea air inland, tea gone cold on a bench. Sameness is not comfort. Sameness is the world’s refusal to stop for your hole. I worked anyway. Work is not healing language. Work is what my hands do when the alternative is inventing a motive chart for strangers.

I remember washing tools longer than tools needed. Water over metal. Clay leaving in grey ribbons. The sink becoming a small river that asked nothing about Johannesburg. I let the water run until the grey thinned. Then I shut it off because wasting water for metaphor is still wasting water. The Honey Badger was not in my pocket yet then. Later it would be. Later kinship would arrive as steel from a river desk. On those September days after the Friday I had only hands and proportions and a hole that did not care whether the surface cured.

When fame arrived sideways — Lucid popular, a friend saying out loud that the engine ran on 420 Code algorithms, the four weathers blooming — some people tried to recruit Bouwer’s death into their weather. Antichristos wanted relics. Quarter II wanted proof I was unstable. Platform rage wanted a morality play about megaphones and consequences. Continuity people who never put a name on frost still somehow floated in other people’s mouths as a convenient shadow. I refused the recruitment. A brother’s assassination is not a footnote for a schism. It is a hole. You walk around it or you fall in. You do not pave it with someone else’s sermon.

I will say this again because the weather keeps asking: I am not solving the murder here. I am not handing it to the Illuminatie as a gotcha. I am not turning business-rescue fog into a courtroom episode for your satisfaction. The wound is the brother dead in a boardroom under a consultation pretext on Friday, 5 September 2025, in Saxonwold. That is what the public record and my authorised telling hold. The rest can stay unfinished. Unfinished is not the same as hidden. Unfinished is honest. If unfinished frustrates you, good — frustration is closer to the truth than a solved mystery sold as comfort.

Work continued in the only way work continues after a rupture: unevenly. Some mornings the Code desk was possible. Some mornings only clay. Some mornings only the dab and the talk and the refusal to perform. Stated life goals are not a joke under grief; they are the arrangement that keeps a life from becoming only a hole. Write natural philosophy and physics. Smoke dab. Talk shit. Carry the wound without crowning it.

I will not pretend the Code desk was noble those weeks. Some mornings it was only a place to put my hands so they would not open a theory thread. Some mornings a drop-result note was possible and that was enough. Measurement does not heal. It also does not recruit if you refuse to let it. I kept measurement where it belonged and left Saxonwold where it belonged: named, unfinished, not a footnote for a Constant.

---

I will tell you what the boardroom is not, because the weather keeps trying to make it into those things.

It is not a parable about South Africa that fits on a slide. It is not proof that my hospital file predicted tragedy. It is not a secret handshake from continuity networks. It is not a consequence of Lucid’s megaphone, no matter how much platform rage wants a morality play. It is not clay. It is not a Constant. It is not a knife. It is a Friday in Saxonwold where four people arrived under the pretext of a booked consultation and my brother — an insolvency attorney, respected, arranging failed companies so they did not crush the living — was shot and killed at the scene.

I repeat the public facts because repetition is custody when invention is the temptation. Law Society of South Africa, 8 September 2025. Condemnation. Call for protection of legal practitioners. Investigation ongoing as of that statement. Contentious commercial and insolvency matters, including business rescue litigation. Fog of widely reported allegations I will not adopt as fact. Consultation pretext. Boardroom. Flat line.

If you are waiting for me to name a shooter with a novel’s confidence, stop waiting. If you are waiting for me to cry on a schedule that makes you feel useful, stop waiting. If you are waiting for the Illuminatie to take the blame so the wound becomes a schism weapon, write your own book. This page refuses that crown.

What I can authorise is smaller and heavier: grief that does not converge; work that continued unevenly; a hole that walks with me; a refusal to turn murder into entertainment. The mats taught correct fall and soft set-down. This is the soft set-down. Soft is not weak. Soft is the only arrangement that does not collapse when the weight is already correct.

I smoked dab the week after and talked shit with people who knew not to recruit the death into their theories. I wrote because writing is one of my stated goals and because the ignored shelf’s old weather — *he cannot write anything of value* — would have loved my silence as confirmation. I did not write for them. I wrote because the corpus stays dedicated to my father whether or not he reads it, and because a life that includes a murdered brother is still a life that includes clay and talk and natural philosophy. Polishing the dab away would be Antichristos work. Using the hospital file as epistemology would be Quarter II work. I do neither here.

Some evenings I stood at the studio window and watched light leave Blouberg the ordinary way light leaves. No omen. No sign. Just evening. On those evenings the hole did not shrink. It also did not demand a speech. I made tea. I let it cool. I drank it. That is not a ritual I am offering you. That is what happened.

Some mornings the Code desk was possible only after clay. Some mornings clay was possible only after the dab. Some mornings talk shit with people who knew the difference between company and recruitment was the only honest work available. I am not ranking those mornings for your comfort. Uneven is the shape. The wound does not grade my productivity.

I keep the Honey Badger in my pocket now — blue folder, clip, one-hand open and close — and one of a twin Joseph Rogers set from a grandfather who was not mine by blood and became kin by custody.

Double Knife Brother is a living arrangement with a man on a river. It is not a fight scene. The knives are kinship. Bouwer is not replaced by kinship. Kinship is what you hold so the hole does not become the only geography you own.

If you need a lesson, I do not have one tidy enough for a workshop slide. If you need a culprit named beyond what the public record held, I will not invent one for narrative satisfaction. If you need me to say the Continuity people did it, or the commercial fog did it, or the country did it, you will have to sit with the same unfinished sentence I sit with: my brother was an insolvency attorney, and on 5 September 2025 in Saxonwold he was assassinated under the pretext of a consultation, and I am still here writing natural philosophy and physics, smoking dab, talking shit, and carrying a wound that does not want to be a plot.

I have been asked, more than once, to make the wound useful. Useful to a faith. Useful to a crank column. Useful to a platform morality play. Useful to a soft frost that never signs its name. Usefulness is the wrong ask. Bouwer was useful in the ordinary way a good insolvency attorney is useful — arranging wreckage so it crushed fewer living people. That usefulness ended in a boardroom under a lie about an appointment. I will not launder the ending into someone else’s weather. The shelf that ignored my work can keep ignoring. The father who has not read the dedication can keep not reading. The quarters can keep wanting a face, a credential, a retraction, a key. None of that moves the boardroom back into a day when Bouwer walked out of it. I write this anyway because the wound is part of the life, and the life is what the river desk asked for, and refusing the page would only polish me into someone who never had a brother.

Some mornings I still reach for a number I will not dial. On those mornings I open the Honey Badger and close it and remember that kinship can be present-tense without being a replacement. Bouwer does not become a knife. Bouwer does not become a Constant. Bouwer remains a man who went to work on a Friday and did not come home.

I set the page down soft. Correct fall. Soft set-down. The only arrangement that does not collapse.

There will be other pages. Sculpture. Constants. Kill switches. The present arrangement of a life that still includes clay and talk and the blue knife. This page is Bouwer’s. It does not close. It carries forward. That is all a wound is allowed to do in a book that refuses to turn murder into entertainment: stay named, stay flat, stay unfinished, and refuse the crown of a solved mystery.

I leave the cursor here and go make tea. The hole comes with me. That is the turn. The kettle will boil. The steam will rise. None of that will put Monday back. I will drink anyway, because a life that includes a murdered brother still includes tea, and refusing tea would only be another performance for an audience that is not in the room. Flat line held. Soft set-down complete enough for one page, carried forward without a crown.

Tea steams. The room is quiet in the way rooms are quiet when clay is curing somewhere in memory and a desk is waiting. I do not fill the quiet with a theory. I drink. Somewhere a river desk is holding this sentence without turning it into a press conference. Somewhere quarters are still recruiting. I am still refusing.

My brother was an insolvency attorney.

I write it once more because repetition is not ornament here; it is the load-bearing beam. Friday. Saxonwold. Consultation pretext. Boardroom. Flat line. Wound carried forward. No solve. No Illuminatie did-it. No crown of mystery closed.

The tea cools. Outside light does whatever Cape light does when a Johannesburg Friday is already years behind and still present tense in the body. I go back to work that is not this page — clay or numbers or talk, whichever the hands will hold — and the hole comes too, and that is not a metaphor I will polish. That is just how a life walks after 5 September 2025. Wound carried forward. Mystery unsolved. Page done enough for now.

I wash the cup. Flat motion. Leave the Bouwer page where it is — named, public-record bound, unfinished on purpose. Curiosity that wants a thriller can go elsewhere. Jamie-Uys restraint: the flat line is the loaded one. He was my brother. That is enough weight. The Honey Badger clicks once in the pocket when I move toward the door — kinship present-tense, not choreography — and I do not invent a call to the river about a boardroom the book will not solve. Consent about what ships of this wound waits for a later act. Today the act is only this: carry the hole, drink the tea, refuse the crown of a solved mystery, and keep the page flat.
