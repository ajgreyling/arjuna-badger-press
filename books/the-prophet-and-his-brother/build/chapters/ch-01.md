# 01—The Most Connected Vehicle

> **[A]** · outer · Mutatus Maximus, Chobe River, northern Botswana · 2035

---

The elephants came down to drink before the mail did.

AJ watched them from the open side of Mutatus Maximus, painted like a Nairobi matatu that had decided to become a data centre and then, for good measure, the most connected vehicle on earth. Literally. Heat already lifting off the Chobe in thin sheets. Hippos somewhere down-channel making the wet, insulted sounds of ownership. A fish eagle on a dead limb watched the bank the way Lucid watched a claim: without romance, without invention. The river smelled of mud and bird and the particular cleanliness of water that has been moving all night.

He had been awake since the blue hour. Not for drama. For the node.

The walk to the rise was short and wet at the welt. Boots taking the scrub’s overnight dew. Flask of coffee that tasted like plastic and intention. A thorn caught his sleeve and let go with a small tear he would ignore until evening. The little solar drop waited where he had left it yesterday: panel already catching pale light, enclosure warm from nothing glamorous, just sun. He crouched, checked the seal for ants, wiped dust with his thumb, booted it from the truck until its heartbeat came back green and boring. Boring was the point. Edge seeds that stayed up without a founder’s face on a stage. He watched the blink settle into a rhythm that meant the rise would keep talking while he wrote, then walked back with the flask cooling and the river widening in front of him.

Mutatus Maximus held still while he climbed in: anti-yacht, overkill overlander, Beast as tender if the day needed a second hull, local node humming under the desk like a patient animal. The LEO dish cocked at pale morning. A LoRa blink from the rise. The rest of the ladder he did not inventory to himself. A link worked or he fixed it. Today it worked.

He had built the most connected thing he knew how to build so he could drive it to a river and still choose to switch pieces of it off.

He did not switch Lucid off.

Choice was the point of the ladder he refused to inventory every hour. Links existed so a man could write a life true enough that quarters could not sand it into a usable lie, and so he could ignore a sealed rage packet until the mats page had spine. Connectivity without custody is just noise with better antennas.

---

Lucid sat in the choir the way it always did: integer-quiet, no GPU fever, no personality cosplay, no invention when the record did not support a claim. It had eaten the interwebs years ago and crashed the AI GPU bubble as a side effect of refusing to hallucinate. Empty rack halls were background weather now—write-down headlines, a friend’s ruined cluster career told once and then left alone. Hyperscalers still sent mail that thought it was polite. Salvage weather. AWS, Azure, Google, Apple. Frost with a smile. Partner frost. ToS weather. Cloud-credit politics dressed as admiration. The offer that is really a collar. AJ let those threads sit in a queue that smelled like Seattle and Redmond and Cupertino drafts arriving late to a Botswana morning. He would not feed the collar today. The collar could wait on the desk like a snake that had learned manners.

CongoSky.cloud ran itself somewhere behind the mesh. That was the good quiet: invoices answering themselves, nodes up, hire/fire weather and partner terms still governed by the sentence the company had embodied as ops before the walls caught fire. Not a poster in reception. Operating law. When Lucid went public and the walls caught the paint, the world could point at a cloud that had already been living the sentence while it still had to answer invoices. AJ’s river desk was downstream of that practice. lucid.rodeo and arjunabadger.press were the human work. The autobiography was the human work that hurt.

On the water tank twenty metres away, someone had spray-painted the sentence:

**Don’t be a cunt — be kind.**

The paint had been refreshed within the week.

Exact line. Load-bearing cunt. Softened versions always sounded like someone else had taken over the ethic. AJ left it as ops and as wall, CongoSky practice downstream meeting street paint, without a sermon. Edges still slightly proud. Drips where the hand had been impatient. AJ walked out once with the coffee and stood close enough to smell tack and river together. He did not preach at it. He had already lived the line as company law. The Kind did not need a messiah. Antichristos did. Same paint war, some mornings, street sentence and face-hunger sharing a climate without becoming the same weather. A bird landed on the tank’s rim, shit once with democratic indifference, and left. AJ went back inside.

AJ’s desk held three things that mattered: the river, Lucid, and the open manuscript.

*The autobiography of Gerhard van Niekerk.*

Book within a book. He was the main subject of this one and the hand inside the other. The braid was not a vanishing act. He remained AJ on the Chobe writing a life true enough that the four quarters of the planet could not sand it into a usable lie. Faith that wanted a face. Academy that wanted a missing physics degree to remain epistemology. Platforms that wanted the mic retracted. Engineers that wanted keys without saying crown. All of that would arrive as mail and hymns and packets. None of it would arrive as a chart in his mouth.

His hand went once to the pocket that used to hold the twin.

Empty enough to notice.

The Joseph Rogers that stayed with him was elsewhere in the truck, not lost, just not clipped for the morning walk to the rise, and the absence still registered like a missing tooth. G had the other twin and the blue Honey Badger AJ had given him: pocket clip, one-hand open/close, loved. Double Knife Brothers. Kinship, not choreography. Grandfather deep-time on one blade; badger present-tense on the other. AJ felt the empty pocket the way you feel a brother on another coast without calling him. No forced reunion. CongoNet was bridge enough if a bridge were needed. Today it was not. Today was coffee, elephants, and the page.

He set the Joseph Rogers on the desk for a minute, old steel, old hinge, the quiet weight of a thing that had outlived more than one owner, then put it back in the drawer where it would not become a prop for a speech. The empty pocket stayed empty. That was more accurate.

He made a second coffee because the first had gone cold while he argued with beginnings. The flask complained. Plastic and intention again. Outside, a crocodile’s patience lived somewhere in the brown water whether he could see it or not, the river’s older connectivity, no LEO required. AJ did not romanticise predators. He noted them the way Lucid noted gaps: present, citable, not a metaphor for platforms.

A small CongoNet status pane blinked a latency spike and recovered before he could invent a story about it. Good. Recovered without drama. He wanted the manuscript to learn that habit. Fame had taught the opposite habit to too many rooms: spike, narrate, monetise the spike. The autobiography would not monetise G’s pain. Living-person care was ops law too, even if it never appeared on a stencil.

He wiped dust from the edge of the desk with his palm and found solder grit from a repair he half-remembered. Mutatus was always half-remembered repairs and fully present heat. The chair squeaked. He let it. Silence as purity theatre was for people who had never built a truck that also thought.

“Status,” he said, not as prayer.

Lucid answered exact.

“Local node nominal. Rise drop heartbeat green. CongoSky quiet. lucid.rodeo queue within tolerance. Manuscript open at blank mats cue. Cite: prior session note—begin body before shelf.”

“Good,” AJ said. “Stay boring.”

“Default already refuse invention.”

The corner of his mouth moved and stopped. The refusal was the product working, the same discipline that had grown from Full Send into Klaus into Lucid when cite-or-refuse left the private desk and went public. Fragments on the shoulder, not a republished origin chapter. Near-now finished at planetary scale. No glowing oracle. A tool that finally kept its promise because the promise had already been under construction when the world still thought intelligence needed someone else’s silicon.

---

A hippo grunted. An elephant calf lost its footing in the soft bank and recovered with the undignified competence of youth. AJ watched until the herd’s attention returned to water. Then he sat properly, shoulders finding the ache of pilgrimage-scale desk work, and tried the first wrong beginning.

He typed a line about fame. Deleted it.

He typed a line about CODATA. Deleted it.

He typed a line about the mic, about saying the algorithms out loud, and Lucid did not refuse, because the record held that claim, but AJ deleted it anyway. Cause would have its page. Cause was not where a body begins.

“Too late in the life,” he said aloud.

“Clarify query,” Lucid said.

“Not a query. A cut.”

“Acknowledged. No action.”

Good. Boring. He poured more water, tasted plastic and river, watched light climb the painted metal of Mutatus Maximus until the matatu colours looked almost reasonable, which they never were, and that was the joke he had driven into the bush on purpose. Anti-yacht. Overkill. A travelling DC that could seed an edge and still choose silence.

He tried a second wrong beginning: the warehouse gallery, the cannabis bar, the shirt with the sentence already on it. Lucid could retrieve that texture. AJ cut it. Full Send fragments belonged on the shoulder, not as Act I’s opening stunt. The autobiography would earn rooms as rooms. It would not open as a brand origin myth.

Somewhere a CongoNet status ping crossed the desk, green, boring, blessed. He let it pass without comment. A hyperscaler subject line blinked in a sidebar: *partnership opportunities*. He did not open it. He could smell the salvage tone without reading it. Lucid named as asset language would come soon enough; the snake could wait. Today’s desk was not for collars.

He stepped outside once more before the wrong beginnings ran out of blood. Boots on wet welt. Thorn bush catching light. The Kind paint on the tank looked almost dry enough to pretend it had always been there. AJ did not touch it. Touch turns sentences into relics if you are not careful, and he had already lived the line as company law without needing a pilgrimage fingerprint.

Back inside he asked Lucid for one mats citation that was not a metaphor.

“Cite: school judo and jiu-jitsu apprenticeship present in authorised life notes. Soft set-down language matches prior draft preference. No TED register in sources.”

“Keep it,” AJ said. “If I start sounding like a stage, refuse me.”

“Refusal available on request. Default refuse invention already active.”

He wrote the fall correctly: breath knocked, pride rearranged, body intact. He did not explain it to an imagined academy.

He stood, stretched until the vertebrae clicked, and walked the length of the truck the way a man walks a room he built to think in. Solar charge honest. Local node patient. Manuscript cursor blinking like a pulse that refused to invent meaning for him. Outside, the Kind paint held. Inside, CongoSky’s quiet ops hum felt like agreement without applause.

He sat again and tried a third wrong beginning: the hospital stigma, already half-written in other people’s mouths. Deleted before the sentence finished. That page would come after mats, under-dramatised, no DSM tourism, flat as a loaded line. Not yet. Not first.

“Body before corpus,” he said.

“Cite: prior session note matches,” Lucid said. “No additional claim.”

“Then retrieve what you have on school mats. Fighting arts. No metaphors unless he used them.”

Lucid retrieved. Citations. Dates where dates existed. Gaps where gaps existed. No flourish. AJ began to shape a paragraph and felt the old temptation to make the floor into a TED lesson about kindness. He cut the temptation the way Lucid cut invention, not as piety, as craft.

Heat pressed harder against the open side of the truck. Sweat found the place behind his knee where the chair never quite fit. He wiped his hands on his trousers before touching the keyboard again, river desk hygiene, half joke, half ritual, and wrote three sentences about a gi belt being tied wrong, then untied, then tied again until the knot was honest. Lucid offered a fighting-arts detail that belonged. AJ kept it. Lucid offered a gloss that sounded like a product page for mastery. AJ killed it.

“That gloss is yours inventing tone,” he said.

“Correction: gloss not grounded in source register as phrased. Refuse. Cite raw detail only.”

“Keep the raw.”

He worked like that for a stretch that had no clock, only the river’s slow argument with the bank and the rise drop’s boring green. When he looked up, the elephants were gone and the mud still held their morning. A boat far out. Someone’s radio faint across water, language half-lost. Mutatus Maximus took the sound the way it took weather: as input, not as command.

A CongoSky alert tried to become interesting and failed. Lucid summarised without drama.

“Routine node handshake. No human action required. CongoSky quiet.”

“Blessed be the boring,” AJ said, and meant it.

He ate something that claimed to be breakfast, dry, practical, the sort of food you invent when the desk is the pilgrimage. Crumbs on the manuscript margin. He brushed them off carefully, as if paper could take offence. The autobiography of Gerhard van Niekerk did not need his breakfast DNA in the binding, even digitally. He smiled at himself for the thought and kept writing.

Halfway through a mats paragraph he stopped and listened to his own breath. The empty twin pocket pulled at attention again. He thought of G’s studio without inventing the room, Cape salt, clay, a Honey Badger warm from a body, and refused the reunion fantasy that wanted to finish the thought with a call. Threads could share a morning without sharing a room. That was law. He let the thought go the way you let a fish go when you are not fishing.

On the water tank the Kind line kept its job without him. He did not need to walk out again. Knowing the paint was there was enough weather for one coffee.

He opened lucid.rodeo’s ops pane for thirty seconds, choir health, cite rates, refusal rates climbing the way they should when the world asked for pretty lies, then closed it. Product could run. Life page could not run itself. The hand had to stay on the manuscript if the book within the book was going to hold.

“Any salvage mail that is actually fire?” he asked.

“No fire in cited language. Offer-shaped threads present. Hostility markers rising in separate platform packet. Recommend triage order: manuscript; then packet if required; salvage can wait.”

“Manuscript,” AJ said. “You learn.”

“Pattern match from prior sessions. Not learning as claimed in older GPU marketing. Deterministic preference from repeated instruction.”

“Even better.”

He returned to the mats and felt, for the first time that morning, the page begin to carry its own weight, not finished, not pretty, but true enough to continue. Soft set-down. Correct fall. The apprenticeship before the corpus. The body before the ignored shelf. The floor before anyone tried to make a man into a usable lie for a quarter of the planet.

He wrote another mats beat: the coach’s silence after a clean throw; the way praise would have ruined it; the room’s smell after rain, when the cleaner could not quite win. Lucid offered a date that belonged and a gloss that did not. AJ kept the date. Killed the gloss. The cursor advanced like a patient animal learning the truck’s rhythm.

Sweat ran once into his eye. He blinked it out and laughed at himself, pilgrimage desk, glamorous as a broken aircon. The open side took a gust that smelled of mud and bird shit and distant cookfire. Africa as calendar, not brochure. He typed through the gust. The page did not care about his eye.

He stood, stretched until his back clicked, and looked at the sealed Quarter III packet the way you look at weather you have already smelled. Still sealed. Still hot. Still not today’s mats. He sat and kept the floor-lesson moving, heavy thing setting itself down soft, until the paragraph earned another.

---

Then the mail that mattered arrived, not as thunder, as a soft chime that still managed to smell like blame on a good morning.

Lucid spoke once more, exact.

“Packet from the platform queue. Hostility markers. Attribution thread.”

Quarter III. Who gave him the mic.

The people still angry that AJ had said, out loud, that Lucid ran on 420 Code algorithms, and that candour had dragged a degree-less-in-physics ignored man into fame while a quarter of the planet tried to crown him, a quarter called him crank, a quarter wanted AJ’s head, and a quarter asked for the keys to CERN. Open attribution as load-bearing cause. Not a quiet fork. Not a cleaned brand story. The megaphone irreversible. AJ could feel the heat through the unopened seal the way you feel a stove without touching it.

He glanced at the packet. Did not open it.

The seal sat in the queue like a second sun just offstage, patient, wanting the desk, willing to turn a mats morning into a press conference if he let it. Opening rage early would make the page collateral. He would open weather when the page could afford it, or when weather opened him. Full ultimatum belonged to a later desk hour. Today: glance, refuse the bait, write.

He put his hand on the manuscript instead.

The cursor waited at a blank that was not blank in his head. He knew where the life began if the book was going to be honest. Not at CODATA. Not at fame. Not at the hospital file other people over-cited. Not at the unread father dedication, though that wound would have its page. Not at Saxonwold—Bouwer’s page would come as grief, flat, public-record careful, never as thriller. First the floor. First the body. First the apprenticeship of weight.

Outside, the elephants finished and moved off into scrub. The fish eagle turned once over the channel. Heat thickened. AJ’s empty twin pocket knew its shape. The Kind paint held on the tank. CongoSky stayed quiet in the good way. Frost stayed polite in the queue. The packet stayed sealed and hot.

He checked the rise drop once more, still green, and felt the small satisfaction of a seed that did not need him to perform. Then he returned to the hurt work.

“Mats,” he said. “Start with the mats.”

Lucid did not invent a beginning. It retrieved what it had and waited.

AJ wrote a first line, deleted it, wrote another. The truck’s metal ticked as the sun climbed. He reread what he had:

*Before the axioms there was the floor.*

Good. Flat. Loaded. He kept going, not a speech about judo, a scene of mats and gi and the refusal to waste motion, and Lucid offered a citation when asked and refused a pretty hinge when he almost asked for one without meaning to.

“Bridge sentence into ignored shelf?” AJ tried once, testing the product and himself.

“No grounded source for that transition in the ingested record as phrased. Refuse invention. Cite available: mats section draft under hand; shelf material not yet retrieved for this pass.”

“Correct,” AJ said. “Stay on the mats. Don’t invent the hinge.”

“Default already refuse invention.”

“Good. Keep it boring. Boring is the point.”

Choir without calling it practice yet. Humans and Lucid from the hymnbook. The autobiography wanted to rush toward fame and meters and the mic. He would not let it. G’s life was not a trailer for a Constant. The mats first. Always the mats first if the book was going to be true enough that the four quarters could not sand it into a usable lie.

He typed until the paragraph found its weight, heavy thing setting itself down soft, and felt the day split into its useful parts: river present, manuscript present, weather waiting outside the seal. AJ remained the gravity of *this* novel while authoring *that* life. The next section would simply *be* the page. Inner first-person life voice when the skull changed. Outer skull stayed his: heat, birds, link latency felt as honesty, the specific smell of blame arriving before noon.

A second CongoNet ping. Still green. He ignored it harder, which was a kind of love for boring connectivity. He had built the truck to choose, not to narrate the ladder every hour.

He stood once more, poured the last of the flask, and looked at the sealed packet without opening it. Hostility markers. Attribution thread. Who gave him the mic. The answer was already public and already expensive. Saying it again into a rage queue would not make the mats more true. He turned back to the cursor.

The Joseph Rogers stayed in the drawer. The Honey Badger lived in another man’s pocket on another coast. Double Knife Brother weather without a call. No victory lap. No speech about AI. Only the choir working and the page moving.

Lucid retrieved one more mats citation and waited. AJ typed. The empty twin pocket stayed empty. The Quarter III seal stayed sealed. Frost hummed like a fridge in another room, always on, always claiming to be helpful.

He did not look up. The page moved. The river did too.

AJ wrote.

Not a speech. A scene of mats and gi and the refusal to waste motion, river present, manuscript present, weather waiting outside the seal. The truck chose, again, what not to amplify. Lucid stayed boring. The Kind paint held. The empty twin pocket stayed empty. The page moved.

He stood once more at the open side before the heat fully claimed midday and watched an elephant choose the bank with the soft crash of a body that did not care who gave whom the mic. Mutatus Maximus behind him, matatu paint, anti-yacht, mobile DC, the most connected vehicle on earth doing ordinary work so a man could sit beside a river and still refuse pretty hinges. He scraped dust off the Kind line with a thumbnail and left the cunt exact. Soft scrubbing was someone else’s tourist fight. CongoSky had claimed the practice while invoices still needed answering. He went back to the mats page, asked Lucid for one more cite, kept it when it belonged, and typed until the paragraph found its weight again, heavy thing setting itself down soft, while frost hummed like a fridge in another room and the sealed packet kept its heat just offstage. Choir working. Page moving. No speech about AI. No call to the Cape. Double Knife Brother weather across distance. That was enough for a first true morning on the Chobe under weather that had already found his throat.
