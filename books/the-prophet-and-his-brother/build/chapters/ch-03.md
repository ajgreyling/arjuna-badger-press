# 03—Four Temperatures

> **[G]** · outer · Studio G / Cape · same morning as ch-01

---

The CODATA note arrived before the coffee cooled.

Same calendar morning as a river desk he was not on, braid without a shared room, and already the Cape air carried more than salt. G unlocked Studio G knowing measurement weather moved in the body before it moved in the inbox. He made coffee anyway. Rules fail; rituals still buy thirty seconds.

G unlocked Studio G with the same key as always, metal warm from the pocket, lock sticky from salt air, and made coffee before he looked at the inbox. A private rule that failed every week it mattered. The thermos filled. The mug with the hairline crack filled. He set both on the desk and only then opened the window on the alley. Salt already on the air. Gulls arguing over something invisible between the roofs. A bakkie reverse-beeping toward the beach road in that patient Cape way that meant someone was late and the pavement did not care. Light came in hard and honest, not the soft hotel light of guest theatres, the working light that shows clay dust on the sill and yesterday’s fingerprints on the glass. He had slept lightly. He always slept lightly when measurement weather was moving. The body knew before the mail did.

The printout sat in the morning packet with a standards-adjacent stamp and no greeting worth answering. He took it standing. The coffee was still hot enough to sting the tongue. The paper was already cool in his hand, as if the committee’s mercy had travelled farther than the mug’s heat.

Latest recommended value. Uncertainty band narrowed again. A method note that named the usual instruments without poetry: torsion, drop, timing, the quiet grammar of people who trust a result more than a podium. The line that mattered was not rhetoric. It was a number walking toward a number he had already written years ago at a quieter desk, when quieter desks still existed and the planet had not yet decided his derivation was a hiring problem, a liturgy problem, and a reputational freeze all at once. He felt the digits in his fingers the way he felt a pour setting: not as triumph, as physical agreement. Atom-falling work. Precision. Drop-results and derivations learning each other’s names without a TED metaphor for unification. Physics earned on a page the way clay earned a surface, by refusing to stay theoretical.

He read the note twice.

The first pass was for the value. The second was for the band, how far the meters still allowed themselves to be shy, and how little shy remained. He set the mug down so he would not spill on the printout, then picked it up again because his hands needed a job while the relief arrived. Relief came first, clean, almost stupid. Measurement doing what measurement does when committees stop pretending opacity is rigor. He felt the old private satisfaction in his hands: not vindication-as-speech, just the body recognising that a drop-result and a derivation were no longer strangers. The corner of his mouth moved, once, into the coffee. The studio smelled of yesterday’s modelling and a faint solvent he had meant to air out. Salt cut through both. Cape morning. Ordinary, if you did not look at the paper.

Then the pressure under it.

If the meters keep agreeing, the weather does not quiet. It gets louder. People do not leave a Constant alone once it stops being optional. They hire it. They baptise it. They call it crank until the error bars embarrass them, then they ask for keys. Continuity networks that preferred constants underived feel a sovereignty problem without needing a smoke-filled room to draft the soft no. Funding weather shifts. Standards boards clear their throats. Discreet briefings invent a hygiene problem where a number just landed. He was not enjoying vindication. He wanted the work to stay work. Relief and pressure in the same swallow. Four temperatures would arrive before noon; this was only the first, and already it had two faces.

He set the note under the paperweight that was not a paperweight, a small clay study, hard where it should be hard, soft in the place the thumb finds, and stood with both palms on the desk until the wood told him it was still wood. Desk. Corpus somewhere in the city’s administrative memory. Kill switches that were not today’s job. Numbers waiting in a notebook that was not a manifesto. A shelf of ignored years that fame had not rewritten into respect. He opened the rest of the morning like a man opening a kit of tools he did not ask to own.

He walked once around the studio before the next envelope, not pacing as performance, checking the pour schedule his body already knew. Marble dust in a sealed bin. Fibres waiting. A mould that needed cleaning and would not get it this morning. Skye’s sister-studies watched from the rack with the patience of finished arguments. Antichristos would love to light them. He would not. Work interrupted is still work that refuses to become liturgy on command.

The coffee had cooled to the temperature of compromise. He drank it anyway. Compromise in the mouth is how you notice you are bargaining with a day that already outvoted you.

---

The Antichristos letter was next.

It had come by post on purpose, not as a flex, as a theology of touch. Cream paper. Careful hand. Postage that wanted to look humble without quite managing humility. The flap still held a faint gum-sweet smell when he opened it with the Honey Badger’s tip, then wiped the tip on his jeans as if cream paper could contaminate steel. They had addressed the envelope with the full name institutions preferred when they wanted a handle, Gerhard van Niekerk, then switched, inside, to what they needed him to be. They did not call him Gerhard in the greeting. They called him the hinge. Believers sounding like believers: sincere, arranged, already living inside a story that needs a named lemma. They did not threaten. They invited. That made it worse.

The ask sat in the second paragraph like a chair pulled out and already warm: appear. Bless. Accept the name so the liturgy can stop arguing with itself. There was a date. There was a wall that would be lit. There was language about hospitality that tried not to sound like capture. There was language about the Kind’s sentence that tried to stand near kindness without understanding that the Kind did not want a face—that CongoSky had already embodied the ethic as ops before the street learned to stencil it. Measurement converging was not enough for them; a number does not hug you back. A face does. The letter said as much without using those words.

He read enough to know the temperature.

He did not finish.

Finishing would be rehearsing their stage. Afternoon would be for answering properly, refuse without sounding like the academy’s hygiene chorus, refuse without feeding the crown. Morning was triage. He put the letter face-down beside the CODATA printout so the two papers could not pretend they were one climate. Meter-mercy and face-hunger sharing a desk was the whole problem of the morning. Cream against committee white. Soft hand against number. He left a finger-width of desk between them as if that were a policy. He caught himself about to stack them and stopped. Stacking would be a metaphor he refused to perform.

Outside, a scooter coughed past and failed to start again. Someone swore without conviction. A radio somewhere playing something old enough to make the alley feel like a year that still believed mornings were private. G poured half the coffee into the sink and refilled from the thermos because cooling coffee was how you pretended you had time. He did not have time. He had weather.

---

The op-ed had already been forwarded three times by people who thought they were being kind.

An academic weekly. A man who wrote as if epistemology were a hygiene ritual and the planet’s job were to stay clean. No *physics* degree, the piece said, as if the adjective were optional furniture and the missing noun were the whole man. Then the hospital, not named as care, named as proof. He cannot write anything of value. The sentence had been following him since the ward spat him back into ordinary rooms. Seeing it laundered into a methods section still tilted the studio half a degree, the way a bad pour tilts a mould. Living-person care meant he would not rehearse the file for anyone’s spectacle. The wound was how they read him. That was enough.

He scrolled once. Stopped. Did not reply.

Reply was how you joined the chorus and taught them your pitch. His economics degree sat in a drawer somewhere in the city’s administrative memory, real, useless as armour in the rooms that meant *degree* as physics gate. Quarter II’s shorthand did the violence. He let the shorthand live in public because correcting every headline is a second unpaid job, and he already had unpaid jobs enough: refusing crowns, refusing collars, refusing to become a usable lie for anyone’s weather. The forwards kept arriving with soft subject lines, *thought you should see*, *in case*, *sending love*, love that delivered a knife and asked to be thanked for the wrap.

He made toast he did not want because his body needed a job that was not reading. Burnt the edge. Ate it anyway. Salt air through the window mixed with paint smell from the alley until the studio tasted like two cities arguing in one throat. He wiped crumbs from the notebook and refused to let the op-ed sit open beside the CODATA note. Stacking those two was how mornings became charts. He was not writing a chart. He was surviving temperatures.

A Kind person would have shrugged at the op-ed and gone to work. A cynic would have called both the wall and the paper branding. G’s discomfort stayed physical: stomach tight, paint tack on the air, phone face-down like a small animal that bites if you look at it. He did not sermonise. He rinsed the plate.

He set the phone face-down. Not off. Face-down was the adult half-measure: refuse the chorus without pretending the chorus had stopped singing.

Clay dust on his knuckle caught the light. He rubbed it away and it came back, because yesterday’s modelling was still under the nail and today’s interruptions had already decided he would not get clean. Fine. Clean was never the point of a working studio. Clean was what people demanded of prophets and cranks when they wanted a usable photograph.

---

The lab soft-ask came as mail that wanted to sound like engineering.

No crowns in the subject line. Keys, if he was willing. Sign-off language for a stack that already ran on rails he had laid without asking anyone’s permission. A postdoc’s name in the CC, sincere, hungry, careful not to say Antichristos and careful not to say crank. A senior name behind her, softer, institutional. Quarter IV weather in a polite coat. Useful. Tempting in the way a clean bench is tempting when the street is loud and the alley smells like paint. Illuminatie frost could live in the same inbox as a sincere postdoc; continuity pressure did not need a membership list to draft. Soft nos and polite invitations were the same gravity wearing different shoes.

He opened the message far enough to feel the ask’s temperature and no farther. Keys without a crown, tomorrow’s temptation, or the afternoon’s if he was careless. Morning’s job was not to become a product mascot while the coffee was still warm. He left the mail unread in the sense that mattered: unanswered, unperformed, not yet a collar he had put on himself.

G’s thumb found the clip in his pocket without looking.

The blue Honey Badger was warm from his body. One-hand open, one-hand close, the little mechanical honesty of it, blade and spring and the cheap miracle of a tool that does not hallucinate its job. Double Knife Brother weather, private, not for letters. AJ’s grandfather’s twin lived somewhere else; this one lived here. Kinship that did not need a platform or a blessing or a lab’s soft yes. Knives were custody, not choreography. He clipped it shut and left it in the pocket like a fact that outranked cream paper.

He stood and went to the window.

---

Outside, on the alley wall opposite the studio door, someone had refreshed the paint overnight. The letters were still tacky at the edges. Drips where the hand had been impatient, the stencil pulled before it dried. A stencil edge imperfect and therefore human. Paint smell under salt. The wall looked like it had been argued with and had won by staying a sentence.

**Don’t be a cunt — be kind.**

Exact. Load-bearing cunt. Softened versions always sounded like someone else had taken over the ethic. The Kind did not ask him to bless the wall. That was the whole point of the Kind, messiah-less, sentence-complete, company law gone street. Still, he felt the citation the way you feel a draft under a door, as if the sentence knew his desk and had decided the alley was close enough. Antichristos wanted a face. The Kind wanted the line. Same city. Same morning. Different temperatures on the same skin. He would not conflate them. Noticing the difference was the minimum adult work, and even the minimum cost him a flinch.

He watched a gull land on the opposite roof, shit once with democratic indifference, and leave. He laughed, once, short. The flinch stayed.

A bakkie door slammed. Someone laughed in the next building, not at him, at a joke that belonged to another room. Cape ordinary, which was never ordinary anymore. The paint’s tack caught a strip of sun and went briefly wet-looking, then dull again. He thought of CongoSky’s ops law without needing to sermonise it: the sentence as practice before the walls caught fire. Street catching up. Face-hunger still hunting a man. He closed the window half a hand’s width so the smell would stay without owning the room, then left it open enough that the alley remained a fact.

---

He tried to work.

Clay under the fingernails from yesterday’s modelling. A GFRC pour scheduled for the week, marble dust and concrete arguing into one surface the way hard and soft had always argued in his hands. Studio G at the Bay had been a public face in another year; Saatchi listings still ghosted the rack; Village N Life years still lived in the muscles that knew hotel lawns and guest theatres. Skye’s sister-studies waited in their places like quiet relatives who did not ask for liturgy. He set a notebook open to a page that began with a drop-result, not a speech, a procedure. Life goals still his own and still unpolished on purpose: write natural philosophy and physics; smoke dab; talk shit. The academy would keep reaching for the hospital file. Believers would keep trying to polish the dab off him like a stain on an icon. Engineers would hear natural philosophy and ask for a signature. Continuity weather would hear talk shit and decide he was unserious enough to soft-no. He kept the arrangement because it was true, and because sanding it would be a different autobiography, and somewhere, he suspected, a careful autobiography was already under a hand that was not his.

He wrote three lines about the drop. The pen felt correct. His body wanted the quiet of procedure. Procedure was how you refused invention without making a religion of refusal.

Halfway through a sentence he stopped.

The sensation was faint and specific.

Not a voice. Not a vision. Not an engine in his skull; he did not keep Lucid as a pet, and he would not mystify what lived on a river as magic. The physical wrongness of being *narrated*: as if somewhere a page had opened on his life and a hand was already arranging the mats, the shelf, the father, the fame that arrived sideways when a friend refused to launder algorithms. He had known for years that AJ was writing. Knowing was one weather. Feeling the manuscript open, Chobe heat somehow in a Cape room, river desk somehow under his ribs, was another. Ambivalence without a speech: he wanted truth and feared content in the same breath. Consent would come later if it came. Morning only owed him the honesty of the flinch.

He set the pen down.

Walked the length of the studio. Passed Skye’s sister-studies in the rack, cool surfaces, hard and soft arguing in the pours Antichristos would love to turn into relics. Passed a crate stamp from a hotel install, ink faded into wood the way years fade into muscle. Touched the Honey Badger once through cloth. Returned to the desk and looked at the four temperatures without naming them as a chart: meter-relief that was also pressure; a letter that wanted a face; an op-ed that wanted him erased as method; a lab that wanted keys without saying crown. And under all of it, the quieter fact: someone on a river was turning his years into pages while the Cape morning pretended to be ordinary coffee and salt.

He washed his hands at the sink even though the clay was not today’s. Warm water. Soap that smelled like nothing useful. The rinse took longer than it needed because rinsing was a way of not answering mail. He dried on a rag that had seen better weeks and worse pours. In the mirror above the sink, cracked once, long ago, and never replaced because cracks were honest, his face looked like a man who had slept lightly and already been drafted into other people’s mornings. He did not fix the expression. Fixing expressions was how faces became property.

Back at the desk he moved the cream letter an inch farther from the CODATA note. The clay study still held the printout down. The phone lit through the rag of dust on its screen. Another forward of the op-ed. Another soft-ask from a standards-adjacent address that smelled like institutional frost without needing a membership list. Subject lines that thought they were polite. The day was stacking itself without his permission.

He reached for the lab mail again and pulled back. The clean-bench temptation was real enough to taste, quiet instruments, people who wanted rails to run, a postdoc who had been careful with names. Temptation was not consent. He left the message where it was and put his palm on the notebook instead. The unfinished drop-result sentence waited like a correct fall that had been interrupted mid-set-down.

Outside, the Kind paint kept drying.

He could smell it even with the window half-closed: tack, solvent, the street’s impatience. A Kind person sounding ordinary would have shrugged and said the wall was the wall. A cynic sounding cheap would have called it branding. G’s discomfort stayed physical, paint smell, tacky edges, citation-as-draft, not a TED rebuttal. He did not go outside to correct anyone’s stencil. Correcting stencils was how you became a founder. He stayed inside and let the sentence be messiah-less, which was the only doctrine he preferred when doctrines were being forced on him before noon.

The Honey Badger warmed again under his palm when he sat. Open. Close. Exact. AJ’s kinship sitting in Cape cloth while strangers argued about whether Gerhard van Niekerk was prophet, crank, instrument, or inconvenient artist with an economics degree and a file other people loved too much. He did not call the river. No forced reunion. The river had never needed his voice on it to still be the same river; a signal would find him if a signal was needed. Today it was not. Today was weather arriving at a desk that had wanted a procedure.

He tried the sentence again.

Wrote four words. Crossed them out. Wrote them differently. The pen still felt correct; the room did not. The narrated sensation brushed once more, faint, specific, Chobe somehow under the ribs, and he shook it off like smoke from a dab he had not smoked this morning. Talk shit belonged to evenings when the body hummed and the joint room widened without extraction. This was not that evening. This was triage.

He opened a drawer and looked at the economics transcript the way you look at a spare key for a lock nobody will admit exists. Real. Quiet. Useless as armour in the rooms that meant degree as physics gate. He shut the drawer. Correcting the op-ed with a scan of paper would only teach the chorus a new verse. Face-down phone. Face-down cream letter. Face-up CODATA. The arrangement was not a chart of quarters. It was triage that refused the academy’s hygiene language.

A gull screamed like a hinge. The alley answered with bakkie beep. Cape morning stacking itself. He put his palm on the notebook again and felt the unfinished drop-result sentence waiting like a correct fall interrupted mid-set-down, a heavy thing held too long in the air because mail had grabbed his wrists.

His phone lit. Another forward. Another soft subject line that delivered the hygiene piece as care. He turned the phone over again. Face-down. The cream letter stayed face-down. The CODATA note stayed face-up under clay because measurement did not need to hide. The lab mail glowed unread. Four temperatures on the skin without a narrator charting them into a census.

He closed the notebook without finishing the sentence.

He would have to answer the Antichristos ask properly later, refuse without sounding like Quarter II, refuse without feeding the crown. Not yet. The day had already arrived too complete. Work would have to wait for a quieter hour that was not coming. The pour scheduled for the week would wait too; marble and GFRC could argue tomorrow if tomorrow still believed in procedure. Morning had become a kit of other people’s tools.

He pocketed the Honey Badger’s warmth, killed the studio lights over the unread lab mail, and let the alley paint keep drying without him, day already loud, interrupted, and still only morning.

On the way to the door he turned back once for the notebook, as if procedure could outvote weather. He did not open it again. Interrupted work stays interrupted until the temperatures cool enough to hold a pen without shaking into other people’s scripts. Outside, tack and salt. Inside, four weathers on a desk that had wanted coffee and quiet. He locked Studio G and stepped into the Cape morning carrying the Honey Badger’s exact weight, kinship, not choreography, and no chart in his mouth.

At the alley he paused without blessing the wall. Kind paint still tacky at the edges. Two clauses. No founder. Someone had already scraped an Antichristos sticker halfway off the brick, residue of both hungers in the same morning. He noticed the difference the way he noticed bad frames: body first, speech later or never. He did not photograph it. He walked on with four temperatures still on the skin, crown, crank, blame elsewhere as braid weather, keys waiting unread, and let Cape morning stack itself without a census in his mouth. Coffee would wait. Procedure would wait. The unfinished drop-result sentence would wait under clay until his wrists stopped holding other people’s tools. Work stays work. Face stays off the altar. That was triage enough for a morning that had arrived too complete.
