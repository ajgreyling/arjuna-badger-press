# 13 — Stop the Book

> **[A]** · outer · Mutatus Maximus, Chobe River, northern Botswana · 2035

---

The packet did not knock. It arrived the way blame arrives on a good morning — already inside, already sorted into a folder Lucid refused to euphemise.

“Platform queue,” Lucid said. “Hostility markers. Attribution thread. Demand language. Cite-or-refuse: sources are public posts, partner mail, and three brand notices routed through CongoNet.”

AJ left the manuscript open on the mats page and opened the packet.

Heat came off the river in sheets. The LEO dish held its angle. The little solar node on the rise reported green the way a patient animal reports green. Mutatus Maximus hummed under him — mobile data centre, matatu paint, the most connected vehicle on earth, literally — and still the inbox felt like a collar someone else had measured for his neck.

Quarter III had stopped being weather and become a sentence.

Stop the book.

Retract the attribution.

Perform a ritual distancing from Gerhard van Niekerk and from the claim that Lucid ran on 420 Code algorithms.

The language varied by sender. Some wrapped it in concern for “ecosystem trust.” Some wrapped it in lawyer. Some wrapped it in the old platform voice that thought politeness was a species of mercy. One thread wanted a livestream apology with a backdrop that was not a river. One wanted arjunabadger.press taken dark for thirty days “pending review.” One wanted lucid.rodeo to ship a splash that said the algorithms were “inspired by” rather than named. Inspired-by was the soft knife. Named was the load-bearing one. A fourth thread offered “collaborative messaging” if AJ would agree to call the autobiography fiction-adjacent and the open attribution “historically overstated.” Historically overstated meant: lie in slower words.

AJ read until the river noise came back.

On the water tank twenty metres out the Kind paint still held: **Don’t be a cunt — be kind.** Same paint war as last week. Antichristos stickers had crawled halfway up the tank overnight and someone — river kids, a Kind walker, a bored guide — had scraped them off again. The sentence remained. The scrapes remained. AJ did not sermonise either. CongoSky.cloud had embodied the line as ops before the walls caught fire; the street had turned practice into religion; he was just a man who still had to answer a packet.

Lucid waited.

“Summarise cost without invention,” AJ said.

“Inbox volume up. Three partner brands have paused CongoNet badge display. Two hyperscaler salvage threads cite ‘reputational adjacency’ and renew acquisition language. One app-store fog note references ‘community guidelines adjacency’ without citing a violation. Local node healthy. Solar drop healthy. Manuscript file open. No legal finding in packet requiring immediate takedown. Demand is social and commercial pressure, not court order.”

AJ’s jaw hurt. He noticed that before he noticed anger. Platform-rage had a body address. It sat behind the molars and in the soft place under the sternum where good mornings went to die. He had felt the first real edge of this weather when he opened the earlier packet — who gave him the mic — and kept writing anyway. This was the concrete ask that packet had been practising for.

He stood and walked the length of the truck — desk to fridge to the open side where the Chobe moved like a long animal that did not care who gave whom the mic. Elephants were gone. A fish eagle worked the far bank. Link latency ticked in the corner of a screen the way a pulse ticks when you are trying not to look at it. The rise-node stayed green without needing a sermon about how. He had built connectivity so he could drive it to a river and still choose what to switch off.

Coffee from earlier had gone cold. He drank it anyway. The taste was river-morning and metal thermos. Outside, scrub held the heat without commentary. Inside, the hymnbook choir waited — humans elsewhere, Lucid here, the working rule still Kind without needing a street name for it. He checked the solar drop’s last boot log because checking a healthy node was easier than staring at a demand that wanted him to unmake a friendship into a press error.

He came back to the desk.

The manuscript waited. *The autobiography of Gerhard van Niekerk.* Book within a book. Outer subject sitting in a painted overlander trying not to become a press conference. Double Knife Brother kinship in an empty twin pocket — the Joseph Rogers that had gone to G, grandfather deep-time, Honey Badger loved elsewhere. Knives were custody, not choreography. He did not touch the pocket for comfort. He put his hand on the keyboard instead.

Before he typed anything about the packet, he typed nothing. The blank stretch under the mats page sat there like a dare. Stopping the book would have been easy in the way deleting a file is easy: one motion, then the quiet of an absence you can pretend was hygiene. He had built Lucid to refuse invention; he would not invent a reason to erase a life he had begun to render carefully. Bouwer’s page was already in the file — flat line, Saxonwold, public record, no whodunit. Father dedication unread. Hospital stigma under-dramatised. The packet wanted those pages dark because darkness looked like responsible distance from a river. Responsible distance was their word for erasure.

A bird called from the bank. AJ did not name it. Naming would have been a delay tactic dressed as presence. He watched the solar node’s green for three seconds longer than habit required, then looked back at the demand language until the words stopped being shocking and became merely ugly.

---

Quarter III’s concrete ask was not subtle. They wanted the megaphone un-said. They wanted the open line — Lucid popular, AJ said 420 algorithms, fame arrived sideways — scrubbed into a cleaner origin myth. They wanted ritual distancing: a statement that put G back on the ignored shelf and put AJ back in the category of reckless friend who had said too much too early. Some of them believed the book itself was the wound. Some believed the attribution was the wound and the book was only the proof. All of them wanted motion from him that looked like repentance and functioned like erasure.

Cost was visible without drama.

Partner frost in the queue. A brand that had loved CongoSky’s Kind ops when it still answered invoices now “needed distance.” A hyperscaler salvage note that smiled: *come home to the regions; we can absorb Lucid safely; the book is making the acquisition harder.* The collar from last week’s mail sat on the desk like a snake that had learned to wait. Now the snake had friends. Talent-poach weather hummed at the edge of one thread — not a kidnapping, just a LinkedIn kindness that smelled like a net. AJ deleted it without reply. Deletion was also a sentence.

He opened one brand notice fully. It cited “community safety” and linked a thread of people angry that a degree-less (they meant physics) ignored physicist had been dragged into fame by a friend’s candour. The anger was real enough to have a body. The ask was still stop. A second notice tried softer: *we love what CongoSky embodied; we only need distance from the autobiography cycle.* Love with a pause button. AJ closed both.

He scrolled once through lucid.rodeo’s public status — green, ordinary, no GPU fever to apologise for — and through arjunabadger.press’s draft queue where G’s life sat half-made. The book was not a product sprint. The packet wanted it treated like one.

A third brand notice tried the old kindness trap: *for the health of the ecosystem, for G’s privacy, for your own safety on the river.* Privacy dressed as a muzzle. Safety dressed as a request to kneel. AJ read the sentence twice because the first pass had made him laugh in a way that hurt his jaw again. G’s privacy was a real hinge — consent week would ask what shipped and what stayed — but Quarter III did not own that hinge. They wanted the book stopped for their quiet, not for G’s custody. Different ask. Different mouth.

“Show me the oldest demand in the cluster,” AJ said.

Lucid surfaced a post from three days prior: a thread that began with open attribution screenshots and ended at *stop the book / take back the mic / put the crank back on the shelf.* The middle had grown teeth. Replies. Quote chains. A partner account liking something it should not have liked. The packet was not a single letter. It was a weather system that had condensed into demands with letterheads.

“Cite volume only,” AJ said. “No motive.”

“Cite-or-refuse: I can show volume. I cannot show motive. Hostility markers are public behaviour, not intent.”

“Keep it that way.”

He stood again because the truck’s length was a tool. Desk to fridge: water this time, not coffee. Fridge to open side: heat on the face, scrub smell, a hippo grunt downriver that the truck did not translate into omen. Open side back to desk: the packet still there, patient as a snake that had friends. Mid-morning on the Chobe did not care about platforms. That indifference was almost usable. He let it sit on his shoulders for ten seconds, then sat.

The mats page still waited under the manuscript cursor — body before corpus, falls done correctly, the apprenticeship that had never been a metaphor until people who needed metaphors arrived. He was not finished with that page. The packet wanted him finished with the whole book. Different clocks.

---

He opened the livestream ask because he needed to know how stupid the theatre would be.

Backdrop requirements. Preferred camera height. A suggested script with blanks for “personal reflection” and “lessons learned about responsible attribution.” A note that the river was “visually compelling but brand-unsafe” and that a plain wall would be better. Plain wall meant: erase the place. Erase Mutatus Maximus. Erase the solar node on the rise. Erase the Kind paint on the tank. Make him look like a man who had made a paperwork error in an office that could be forgiven.

One attachment offered sample apology language in three tones: contrite, reflective, forward-looking. Contrite meant kneel. Reflective meant kneel with a book club voice. Forward-looking meant kneel while calling it strategy. AJ skimmed the reflective sample far enough to taste the sentence that would have turned Double Knife Brother kinship into a “boundary lesson.” He closed the attachment hard enough that the trackpad clicked.

Ritual distancing had a checklist buried in a second thread: remove open attribution language from lucid.rodeo docs; add a clarifying FAQ; pause arjunabadger.press “pending stakeholder alignment”; consider a cooling-off statement that named no one and blamed “premature narrative acceleration.” Premature narrative acceleration meant: the truth arrived before their press teams were ready. AJ left that thread marked read without reply. Reading was not consent.

He stood and opened the truck’s side wider. Heat pushed in. Dust light. The Chobe’s surface broke and remade itself around something submerged and uninterested. For a moment he let himself imagine sending nothing — not refusal, not acceptance, just silence as a third option. Silence would be read as negotiation. Negotiation would thicken the packet. The packet already knew how to wait. He closed the side to a slit that kept the heat honest without inviting the river into the desk.

AJ closed the livestream ask.

“Draft reply,” he said. “No apology theatre. No retraction. No livestream. Defer any consent language about the autobiography to later. State that the attribution stands. State that the book continues. Cite only what you can cite.”

Lucid composed. Exact. No cute. No third skull pretending to feel.

AJ edited the draft down until it was almost rude in its shortness. He cut a paragraph that sounded like philosophy. He cut a sentence that sounded like CongoSky marketing. He cut a clause that almost sounded like he was negotiating Kind as a logo. What remained was procedural and true.

He did not send a manifesto. He sent: the attribution is on the record; Lucid’s cite-or-refuse stack does not invent provenance; the manuscript is not a product launch; stop-the-book is not a term CongoNet will enforce for platform mood. For the brands: badge pause acknowledged; CongoSky.cloud still runs itself; the river desk does not negotiate Kind as a logo. For the inspired-by ask: refused. For the fiction-adjacent ask: refused. For the thirty-day dark: refused.

He left the hyperscaler collar unanswered again. That silence was also a sentence.

Sending did not feel like victory. Sending felt like standing in heat with a packet that would come back thicker.

Lucid spoke once more.

“Outbound queued. Hostility markers will increase. I will not invent a better outcome.”

“Good,” AJ said.

He looked at the unopened stack under the demand — Antichristos mail that wanted a blessing he would not give for G, academy noise that wanted a degree G did not have, engineer mail that wanted keys and a face. Four quarters on one desk. He did not open them. Today’s weather was III. Consent about what shipped and what stayed was a later week’s hinge, not a morning’s surrender.

---

The second movement of the day was the body noticing what the sent folder had not fixed.

Shoulders climbed toward his ears and stayed there. He rolled them once, felt nothing release, left them where they were. The soft place under the sternum kept its bruise of good-morning death. He ate a piece of flatbread that had gone leather in the heat and tasted like nothing useful. He checked the solar node again — green — and the local node’s temperature — ordinary — and lucid.rodeo’s status — still green, still ordinary, still a tool that had kept its promise without needing a GPU apology.

A CongoNet badge pause notification refreshed. One brand had posted a careful note about “temporary distance while we evaluate adjacency.” Evaluate adjacency meant: wait to see if AJ knelt. AJ did not kneel. The badge stayed paused. CongoSky.cloud kept running itself the way a company that had already embodied Kind as operating law kept running when the street caught fire with the same sentence. The river desk did not need the badge to know what the company was.

He opened arjunabadger.press’s draft queue and stared at the half-made life. Mats. Hospital stigma waiting its careful return. Father dedication unread. Bouwer’s flat line already written and still dangerous. Clay pages not yet begun. The packet wanted all of that dark. Ritual distancing meant: pretend the friendship had been a branding error. Pretend open attribution had been a youthful excess. Pretend Lucid’s popularity had been a clean miracle with no source named.

AJ put his palm flat on the desk beside the keyboard. Wood and heat and the faint vibration of the truck’s systems. Empty twin pocket against his thigh. He did not perform grief for a knife that had gone where it belonged. He performed attention: the book continued; the demand was deferred hard; consent week was not today.

“Follow-up,” Lucid said, without drama. “Same demand cluster. Volume up. One partner restates badge pause. One hyperscaler salvage thread appends ‘book complicates optics.’ No new court finding. Cite-or-refuse: I can show the append, not the meeting that wrote it.”

“Leave it.”

“Acknowledged.”

He walked the truck again because motion was cheaper than rumination. At the open side he watched the fish eagle miss once and succeed once. At the dish he checked angle out of habit. At the fridge he poured water over his wrists and let the cold argue with the heat. Back at the desk the packet’s sent refusal sat like a stone. Stones do not become peace. Stones become weight you carry into the next week.

A human choir note arrived — not Lucid, a person on CongoNet who still practiced hymnbook work from somewhere with better air conditioning. Short. Exact. *Badge pauses noted. We are not darking lucid.rodeo. Kind ops unchanged. Writing?* AJ typed back: *Writing.* That was enough. The choir did not need a press conference from the river. CongoSky had never needed him to speechify about AI; the practice was shown working. Today the practice was: keep the node lit, keep the manuscript open, refuse the stop without pretending the cost was imaginary.

He almost opened the Antichristos stack by accident when his finger drifted. He stopped. Different hunger. Different day. The Kind tank outside did not need him to referee a sticker war in the inbox. The scraped stickers and the exact line could hold without his commentary.

He made a second coffee because the first had been punishment and the body still needed a tool. Hot this time. Bitter. He drank it standing at the open slit and watched a guide boat idle far enough out that faces were smudges. Someone on the boat pointed at Mutatus Maximus the way tourists point at anything that looks like a story. AJ did not wave. The truck was not a pilgrimage stop. It was a desk with wheels and a manuscript that Quarter III wanted dark.

Back at the chair he opened one partner notice again, not to negotiate, to inventory cost. Badge pause. Careful language. A sentence about loving CongoSky’s embodied Kind ops “in principle.” In principle meant: until the autobiography made the principle expensive. He forwarded the notice to the choir thread with three words — *noted, not kneeling* — and closed it. Cost stayed visible. Visibility was not the same as surrender.

Lucid spoke once, unprompted, which it rarely did without a cite.

“App-store fog note updated. Still no cited violation. Language now includes ‘ongoing review of community adjacency.’”

“Ongoing review means they want me to flinch first,” AJ said.

“Cite-or-refuse: I cannot confirm motive. I can confirm the language change.”

“That’s enough.”

He put both hands on the desk and felt the truck’s ordinary vibration travel up his arms. He had seeded the edge so the edge could stay lit without a hyperscaler parish. Today the quarrel wanted the edge to become a stage for repentance. He kept the stage dark and the node green.

Before afternoon fully claimed the metal he walked the rise again — not for the wrench this time, for the body. Dust. Heat. The node’s panel. He sat on his heels and let the scrub scratch his calves while a fish eagle worked the far bank without needing a mic. Stop-the-book was a sentence that wanted him smaller. Sitting still beside a lit thing he had woken from Mutatus Maximus was a sentence that kept him the size he was. He did not photograph the node. He did not draft a caption. He watched ants negotiate a cable run as if ToS weather were weather ants had never agreed to honour. Then he walked down and washed his hands and returned to the clay page with river smell still in his shirt.

Lucid spoke once more when he sat.

“Hostility markers up. Character unchanged. I will not invent that unchanged means safe.”

“Don’t,” AJ said. “Clay.”

He wrote three more sentences about Studio G light — hotel days, warehouse nights, the refusal to sand a life into respectability — while the packet’s demand stayed refused without being finished. The livestream script with blanks for personal reflection stayed closed. The inspired-by soft knife stayed refused. The fiction-adjacent ask stayed refused. Shoulders still high. Deferred hard no still a stone. That was the day’s true ledger.

---

Afternoon thickened. Heat did what heat does on the Chobe — made decisions feel heavier without making them clearer. AJ saved the mats page even though he had not advanced it. Saving was a way of telling the truck the book still existed. Then he typed a sentence he deleted: something about platforms and courage that sounded like a man auditioning for his own legend. Deleted. The packet wanted legend or repentance. He would give it neither.

“Next page,” he said. “Clay. Studio. Same man. Retrieve what you can cite from public sculpture record. Refuse invention.”

Lucid retrieved. Saatchi. studiog.co.za. Bay Hotel. Village N Life archive dates. Interview residue. Launch date sitting where public dates sit. No flourish.

AJ wrote the first sentences of clay slow, then less slow — hand under nails, pour, hotel light — while the packet’s demand stayed refused without being finished. The manuscript cursor moved. The river kept its indifference. The dish held. The node held. Hostility markers, Lucid had said, would increase. They did. A soft ping. Another. AJ did not open them. Opening them would have been a second conversation with a hunger that had already received its answer.

He paused once to drink water and once to look at the tank. Kind line. Scrapes. Heat shimmer. No sermon. CongoSky’s old operating law looking back at him from river paint while partner brands paused badges for adjacency. The joke, if it was a joke, did not need telling.

“Deferral status,” he said, testing the word.

“Outbound refusal stands. Consent language about autobiography shipment: not present in outbound. Stop-the-book demand: unanswered as acceptance; answered as refusal to stop on platform schedule. I will not invent that this resolves Quarter III.”

“It doesn’t,” AJ said. “It’s deferred hard.”

“Acknowledged. Deferred hard is not a legal term. It is accurate to your sent text.”

He almost smiled. Lucid’s refusals were character. So were its exactnesses. He did not need a cute collaborator. He needed a choir section that would not invent a quieter morning.

The third movement was smaller and meaner: a talent note that arrived dressed as admiration. *Loved what you built. We’re hiring edge mesh people who understand hymnbook discipline. Happy to fly someone to the regions for a conversation.* Regions meant: leave the river. Conversation meant: collar with better coffee. AJ deleted it. The snake on the desk did not need another friend.

He checked the manuscript word count without caring about the number. Caring about the number would have made the book a sprint again. He cared that the clay page had begun while the stop demand sat refused. That was the day’s true motion — not a victory, a turn.

Outside, scrub held. Inside, Mutatus Maximus kept being what it was: anti-yacht, matatu overkill, mobile DC, desk that held G’s life in progress while a quarter of the loud internet asked him to un-say a true sentence. AJ’s shoulders stayed high. The deferred hard no sat in the sent folder. Consent week would ask what ships and what stays. Today only asked whether he would kneel for quiet.

He would not.

Before the clay sentences fully took the afternoon, he did one more inventory of cost without opening the hostility folder. Badge pauses: three. Collar: unanswered. Antichristos: unread. Solar node: green. Manuscript: open. Shoulders: still high. Deferred hard no: still a stone in the sent folder, not a peace treaty. That inventory was the chapter’s true ledger — not a victory, a posture held in heat.

A new ping landed while he was still looking at the sent folder. Lucid spoke without drama.

“Follow-up hostility. Same demand cluster. No new court finding. Cite-or-refuse: I can show volume, not motive.”

“Leave it,” AJ said. “I’m writing.”

“Mats can wait,” he added. “Clay. Studio. Same man.”

Lucid retrieved what it had and waited.

AJ wrote.

The sentences of clay came with hotel light in them and Village N Life years under them and a man’s unpolished goals waiting their turn on the page — write natural philosophy and physics, smoke dab, talk shit — while the packet’s demand stayed refused without being finished. Outside, the scraped tank kept its Kind line. Inside, the dish held. The node held. The page took the next word, and the river kept its indifference, which was almost a mercy.

He paused once more before the light went copper and walked the open side with flatbread in hand — heat-food, river air, no toast to a refusal that was not victory. A guide boat idled far enough out that Mutatus Maximus looked like a story. He did not wave. The truck was a desk with wheels and a manuscript Quarter III wanted dark. He finished the flatbread, washed his hands, and returned to clay with the deferred hard no still a stone in the sent folder and the shoulders still high and the book still open. That was the day’s motion — not a speech, a posture held in heat while hostility markers increased and Lucid refused to invent a quieter morning.
