# Style Guide — *The Man Who Drew the Universe* (standalone)

> **Binding contract.** Shares the project house voice but has its own register and its own
> failure modes. See [`STORY_BIBLE.md`](STORY_BIBLE.md) for the documented frame this book must
> never contradict.

## The register

Close-third on Richard Feynman: a mind that turns everything — a wobbling plate, a locked safe, a
dying woman's oxygen line, a shuttle O-ring, a room full of Swedish royalty — into a problem to be
looked at directly, without the comfortable fog of received authority. The prose should feel like
that habit of mind: plain words, short declarative sentences doing the heavy lifting, technical
ideas explained the way a good teacher explains them to someone who is smart but has never seen
this before.

- **Curiosity, not cleverness, is the engine.** Feynman's wit is real and should land, but the
  book is not a joke delivery system. Every comic beat needs to be doing work — revealing
  character, relieving a scene that would otherwise be unbearable, or both.
- **The physics is explained, never skipped.** When a chapter reaches path integrals, Feynman
  diagrams, or QED, the reader gets a plain-language handle on the actual idea — not just "he was
  a genius and did some math." Use his own gift: the concrete picture before the abstraction.
- **The cost is on the page.** Arline's death, the postwar depression, the failed second
  marriage, the vanity of fame, the fear in the cancer years — written with the same directness as
  the triumphs. No scene is allowed to prettify grief into inspiration porn.
- **Dates and institutions are load-bearing.** Los Alamos is 1943-1945, not a vague "wartime."
  Caltech is named as Caltech. Keep the reader oriented in time the way the man himself insisted
  on being oriented in mechanism.
- **No borrowed sentences.** Nothing here is copied from *Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!*,
  *What Do You Care What Other People Think?*, *Genius*, or any other existing account. Dialogue
  is reconstructed in the author's own words from the documented shape of events.

## Machine-tell taboos

No "in conclusion," no essay-style summarising sentences ending a scene, no telling the reader
what to feel about a fact instead of staging it. Avoid stacking three qualifiers in a row. Let
short sentences stand alone when a scene turns.
