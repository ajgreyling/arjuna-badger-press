The Yonaguni Monument: a submerged sandstone formation off the coast of Yonaguni, southern Ryukyu Islands, Japan, at depths of 5–25 meters. The site’s map: the Main Terrace (150m x 40m), stacked steps, right-angled “rooms,” the Trench/Road, and the “face” and “pool” features—all under shifting currents, often blurred by marine life. The chase geography: launch from Yonaguni’s harbor, descend at slack tide, navigate the terraces and fissures, racing survey crews and the sea’s time limits. The wonder’s texture: the chill of geometric edges in green-blue light, the hush of silt, and the pressure of unanswered questions in every current. The living world: Okinawan divers, local marine geologists, fishermen wary of outsiders, and the island’s undercurrent of pride and protectiveness. The chase moves between the surface town—where the survey team sets up—and the underwater monument, where the local lead’s knowledge of currents and stone is unmatched.
