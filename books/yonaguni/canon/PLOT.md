ACT I: ARRIVAL  
- The crew lands on Yonaguni, welcomed warily by the local diving community. The government survey team is setting up, their timeline tight. Dr. Saki Tanaka, the local, is introduced as both gatekeeper and expert.  
- OBSTACLE: Access is limited; tides are dangerous; the survey team blocks key windows for dives.  
- READ: The crew’s first descent is a sensory overload; the ND lead is overwhelmed by ambiguity—stone that feels both made and broken, lines too clean for comfort, but no tool marks.  
- COST: The survey team restricts access further; the crew gets only one more shot before the site is closed for “research.”  
ACT II: THE CHASE  
- The “potato” engine: the crew attempts to replicate the monument’s geometry in a controlled underwater test; their best efforts are clumsy, the stone chips wrong, and the result is a “potato.”  
- The local demonstrates how the currents and stone work together, showing features that could be natural or not.  
- The ND lead’s gift strains—some edges feel worked, others fractured; no certainty.  
- The survey team accelerates their schedule, boxing up samples and pushing the crew out.  
- The local and Jakobus debate the “what is it for”—function, not origin; the answer is not given but shown in how the site is used and protected.  
- The “hole”: the crew finds a feature (e.g., the Main Terrace’s impossible straightness) that neither side can explain or replicate; both Kimura’s and Schoch’s arguments are aired, both left open.  
ACT III: CLIMAX  
- The final dive: the crew, the local, and Jakobus descend together. The ND lead senses the limit of their gift—the ambiguity is real, not a failure.  
- The adversary locks the site; the crew must choose whether to push or respect the local’s boundary.  
- The KEY/HANDOFF: The local, moved by the crew’s honesty, shares their own reading—not an answer, but a function: the monument as a marker, a meeting point, a guardian of ambiguity.  
- The book ends with the site closed, the legend unresolved, and the reader standing in the “maybe.”  
SUBPLOTS:  
- The LENS’s struggle to lower the camera, to see the site as more than an image.  
- The ND lead’s reckoning with uncertainty as a gift.  
- The crew’s quiet respect for the local’s authority and the site’s ambiguity.  
BACK MATTER:  
- Every measurement, debated claim, and invented detail flagged and sourced, inviting the reader to chase the rabbit-hole further and find their own maybe.
