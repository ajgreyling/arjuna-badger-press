A small, neurodiverse crew travels the world’s rabbit holes, playing the official story dead straight, then finding the one unresolved fact—the “hole”—that keeps the wonder alive. In the Why Files register, they treat skeptics and believers with equal affection, refusing both credulous myth and tired debunk. The story’s engine is the honest “maybe”—the unresolved split between credentialed experts, held open for the reader to inhabit. The intended reader is a curious skeptic, tired of easy answers, hungry for the thrill of not knowing. The POV sits close-third on the crew (never omniscient, never local), with the local’s voice always out-reading the crew on what matters. The book’s bounds: only checkable mysteries; no supernatural or mythos-confirmed reveals; no omnipotent conspiracies; every invented flourish flagged in back matter. The creative driver is the tension between what can be measured and what resists closure: the “potato” moments where the official story strains, and the honest, rigorous wonder that remains.
