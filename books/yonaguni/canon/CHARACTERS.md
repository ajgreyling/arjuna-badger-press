The Crew:  
- ND Lead (texture/edge-reader): hypersensitive to tactile cues, can read the difference between worked and fractured stone by touch and sight; wiring is gift, not wound. Close-third POV sits here, their uncertainty and excitement driving the narrative.  
- The LENS: ex-war photographer, haunted, slow to lower the camera—sees threat and beauty at once.  
- Secondaries: a methodical recorder (logistics, samples), a dry wit (the fish-voice, never the narrator), all marginal in ways that make them outsiders who notice what others miss.  
Jakobus: the road, not the lead—weathered fixer, reads made things for function, always hands the moment to the crew or the local; never has POV, never home ground.  
The Local: Dr. Saki Tanaka (marine geologist/diver, Okinawan), owns the site’s currents and stone, out-reads the crew underwater, and holds the right to give or withhold the “what is it for.” Their voice is pragmatic, skeptical of stories, fiercely protective of Yonaguni’s dignity and ambiguity.  
The Adversary: The National Maritime Survey Unit—a slick, government-funded team tasked with “documenting and archiving” the site, polite but intent on locking down access and closing the legend.  
Voice contract: ND wiring is never caricature or wound; it is how the world is seen. The local’s camera is always heavier than the crew’s. All arcs turn on the cost of not knowing, not on solving the mystery.
