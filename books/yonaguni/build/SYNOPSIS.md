# Made or Not — SYNOPSIS

## Logline

A neurodiverse crew investigates the Yonaguni Monument, testing the official narratives and chasing the one intractable fact that keeps the mystery alive—refusing both myth and debunk, and leaving the reader in the honest, thrilling "maybe" between.

## Book Synopsis

A small, neurodiverse documentary crew arrives on Yonaguni, a remote Japanese island, to investigate the underwater monument dividing geologists and believers for decades. The crew—anchored by their ND lead, a tactile expert in worked stone—encounters both the skeptical government survey team and Dr. Saki Tanaka, a local diver-scientist who guards the site and its ambiguities. Access is restricted, and the tides and bureaucracy limit their dives, but the crew is determined to test the official story: is the monument a natural formation or evidence of ancient hands?

Through a series of dives, controlled experiments, and debates, the crew tries (and fails) to replicate the monument's geometry, finding only "potato" results far from the site's uncanny straightness. The local demonstrates how the currents and stone might produce ambiguity, and both the believers’ and skeptics’ cases are aired with equal rigor. But a final dive to the Main Terrace exposes the “hole”: a stretch of stone so straight and sharp no side can fully explain it, nor can the crew's own hands resolve the difference between made and natural. As the government survey locks down the site, the crew must choose whether to push or respect the local’s boundary. Moved by their honesty, Dr. Tanaka shares her own reading—not an answer, but an invitation to see the monument as a marker, a gathering point, a guardian of ambiguity itself. The book ends in the unresolved “maybe,” with every claim and flourish flagged for the reader to chase further.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Arrival at the Edge
The crew lands at Yonaguni, introduced through the ND lead’s sensory, tactile POV. They navigate the wary local diving community and meet Dr. Saki Tanaka, the local expert who sets the tone for access and boundaries. The opening establishes the crew’s dynamic, the ND lead’s way of reading the world, and hints at the obstacles ahead—limited access, tight timelines, and local authority.

### Chapter 2 — The Official Story
In the research tent, the crew reviews the official narrative: Masaaki Kimura’s conviction in a sunken city versus Robert Schoch’s geological skepticism. The ND lead, Mara, struggles with the photographic evidence—the Main Terrace’s unbroken straightness—and the chapter frames the central tension: two credentialed experts, two irreconcilable readings, and no clear referee.

### Chapter 3 — First Descent
The crew’s first dive is a sensory overload. Mara, overwhelmed by ambiguity, tries to read the monument’s stone—some lines feel worked, others fractured, but no certainty emerges. The dive is shadowed by the survey team’s presence and the ticking clock of restricted access, leaving the crew with the sense that their window to investigate is rapidly closing.

### Chapter 4 — The Potato Test
Attempting to replicate the monument’s geometry, the crew hauls a block of local sandstone and tries to carve it underwater. Their efforts yield only clumsy, rounded results—the “potato”—demonstrating how difficult it is to make such clean lines by hand or tool. The ND lead’s tactile gift is put to the test, and the experiment underscores the ambiguity at the heart of the site.

### Chapter 5 — Currents and Edges
Diving alongside Dr. Tanaka, the crew is shown how the currents and the natural fracturing of the Yaeyama Group sandstone can produce right angles and terraces. The chapter gives the skeptical, geological case its due, as Tanaka demonstrates features that could be natural or not, and the crew experiences firsthand the limits of interpretation.

### Chapter 6 — The Survey Team Closes In
The government survey team arrives in force, restricting access further. The crew observes the survey’s methodical, bureaucratic process and recognizes that their investigation is on borrowed time. The LENS, the crew’s cameraman, documents the shifting power dynamics, and the chapter ends with the sense that official closure is imminent.

### Chapter 7 — Stone Memory
Mara encounters the so-called “face” underwater—a classic case of pareidolia—and reflects on the difference between natural coincidence and human pattern-making. Her tactile reading of the stone finds edges that feel both too regular and yet lacking the “memory” of a worker’s hand, deepening her uncertainty and the site’s ambiguity.

### Chapter 8 — Above Water, Below Surface
The recorder, Pieter, and the LENS examine above-water analogs of Yonaguni’s stone, guided by Dr. Tanaka. They find similar right angles and terraces in the island’s cliffs, supporting the skeptical case, but the ND lead’s tactile comparison leaves the question open—above water analogs are close, but not identical.

### Chapter 9 — The Official Arguments
The crew gathers in the dive shed to lay out both Kimura’s and Schoch’s arguments, giving each the full weight of their evidence. Jakobus insists on building each case “straight” before looking for the flaw. The chapter ends with the recognition that both sides leave a “hole”—an unexplained, irreducible fact.

### Chapter 10 — A Question of Function
Jakobus and Dr. Tanaka walk the harbor, discussing not the monument’s origin, but its function. The site’s role as a marker, a meeting point, and a focus for both local protection and outsider fascination is revealed. The conversation shifts the crew’s perspective from “what is it” to “what is it for,” reframing the mystery.

### Chapter 11 — Pressure Drop
With the survey team accelerating their closure, the crew scrambles to plan their final descent. Mara and Saki discuss the politics and practicalities of site access and the looming shutdown, setting up the stakes for the last chance to investigate the Main Terrace.

### Chapter 12 — Descent to the Trench
The crew’s penultimate dive gives them a close look at the Trench and the Main Terrace. The ND lead’s tactile reading again finds ambiguity—the stone feels both natural and not, resisting any clear verdict. The crew’s frustration and wonder both reach a peak as the site’s edges refuse to resolve.

### Chapter 13 — The Hole
On the last allowed dive, Mara discovers the “hole”: a stretch of the Main Terrace edge that is impossibly straight and sharp, with a ninety-degree turn that neither the natural nor the artificial case can fully explain. Both Kimura’s and Schoch’s arguments are aired, but the feature remains unexplained—an open question at the heart of the site.

### Chapter 14 — The Adversary Locks Down
Surfacing, the crew finds the site formally closed by the survey team. The polite but firm bureaucracy of the Survey Unit enforces the shutdown. The crew must decide whether to push for one last illicit dive or respect the local boundary, with Saki’s authority and the ethics of investigation at the forefront.

### Chapter 15 — The Choice to Respect
Back at the dive house, the crew debates whether to risk a final, unauthorized dive to document the Main Terrace’s straight edge. The ND lead’s reckoning with the limits of their gift and the crew’s respect for the local’s wishes drive the decision. They ultimately choose to respect Saki’s boundary, accepting that some mysteries are not theirs to force.

### Chapter 16 — A Local Reading
At dawn, the ND lead meets with Saki Tanaka, who offers her own reading: the monument as a marker, a gathering point, and a guardian of ambiguity. She acknowledges the truth of both geological and human stories, refusing closure. The ND lead accepts the reality of the “maybe”—that honest ambiguity is not a failure, but a gift of the site.

### Chapter 19 — The Road Home
The survey team packs up, and the crew prepares to leave Yonaguni. They reflect on what was gained, what was measured, and what remains unresolved. The ND lead and the fish (dry one) debate the meaning of evidence and legend, with the honest “maybe” left intact.

### Chapter 20 — The Wink
On the last slack tide, the crew joins Saki for a final, permitted dive. The ND lead’s tactile reading finds the same ambiguity—no tool marks, no definitive natural process, just the flatness and the question. The book restates the two official stories and leaves the reader with the unresolved thrill of the “maybe.”

### Chapter 21 — The Ledger: Real, Debated, Invented
The recorder compiles the final ledger: what is real, what is argued, and what is invented. Every measurement, claim, and narrative flourish is flagged and sourced, inviting the reader to pursue the mystery further. The story ends with the ledger open, the “maybe” alive, and the reader invited to inhabit the wonder that remains.
