## 0. The one thing to get right (the hole is HOW + WHY, both honestly open)

Unlike Göbekli (where the lethal hole is mainstream and the *method* is largely solved), Nan Madol's two
holes are both about the **doing** and the **siting**, and both are genuinely under-explained — *not* a
claim against the textbook, but a logistics-and-provisioning gap the experts themselves leave open:

1. **The construction logistics.** No proven quarry-to-site **transport-and-lift** method, and
   **experimental archaeology has not reproduced it.** **[FACT]**
2. **No water, no food.** The stone city has **no fresh water and grows no food** — uninhabitable without
   constant canoe resupply. **[FACT]**

The honest "they can't fully explain it" here is **HOW (the method) and WHY-HERE (the siting/provisioning)**
— never "therefore aliens." The grounded turn (§6) re-opens the wonder without leaving the evidence: nature
*pre-cut* the stone; the human feat is moving and stacking three-quarters of a million tonnes of it, and
choosing to do so on a reef that could keep no one alive.

## 1. The headline facts (official story, straight — REVERE these) [FACT]

*All load-bearing; cross-checked against §D.11.*

- **What it is.** A megalithic **ceremonial-mortuary city** of **~92 artificial islets** built on a reef
  flat off the southeast coast of **Pohnpei** (Federated States of Micronesia), laced with tidal canals —
  hence "**the Venice of the Pacific.**"
- **The name.** **"Nan Madol" = "spaces between"** (the canals between the islets). *(This is the book's
  title and its through-image — the gaps, the water between the stone.)*
- **Who.** Capital of the **Saudeleur dynasty**, the line that unified and ruled Pohnpei, until **~1628**,
  when the dynasty was overthrown by **Isokelekel.** **[FACT]** *(Date as carried in §D.11; see §7 note.)*
- **Scale of the complex.** Core area **~1.5 × 0.5 km.**
- **When built (the megalithic phase).** Monumental construction began **~AD 1180** (radiocarbon).
  **[FACT]** *(I.e. the stone city is medieval, not "lost-civilisation" antiquity — a fact the book uses
  straight, and one the fringe consistently ignores.)*

## 2. How it was made — the materials and the numbers [FACT]

- **The stone.** Built from **naturally prismatic columnar basalt** — long hexagonal "logs" formed when
  thick lava cooled and contracted (textbook **columnar jointing**; the Giant's Causeway / Devils Tower
  physics). **Nature pre-cut the blocks.** **[FACT]**
- **The technique.** Columns and prisms **stacked dry (mortarless), header-and-stretcher, log-cabin
  style**, on a base of **coral rubble fill.** **[FACT]** *(This is Jakobus's made-thing read: header-and-
  stretcher is a real, legible building grammar — alternating a block's long face and its end, course on
  course, the way a bricklayer or a log-cabin builder binds a wall. He can read the **how-it-stands**; the
  **how-it-got-here** is the hole.)*
- **Total mass.** **~750,000 tonnes** of basalt over the whole site. **[FACT]**
- **Build rate (derived).** **~1,850 tonnes per year**, sustained over **~4 centuries.** **[FACT — derived
  in §D.11 from the total ÷ the span; keep as the calm, cold-eyed arithmetic the potato-prover runs.]**
- **Individual stones.** Columns typically **5–25 tonnes.**
- **The walls.** **Nandauwas** — the great royal mortuary enclosure — has walls **5.5–7.5 m high.**
- **The cornerstone.** A single cornerstone estimated at **~50 tonnes.** **[FACT]** *(The hardest single
  lift to account for — the scene-anchor for "now lift THAT, onto a wall, on a reef, by hand.")*
- **The source.** The basalt was quarried **~25 miles away**, on the **Sokehs** side of the island —
  and **there is no documented method for how it was transported** that distance over open water. **[FACT]**

## 3. THE REAL HOLE (mainstream-acknowledged, honestly unsolved)

**Both holes are real, cited, and left open by the scholarship itself — neither is reached past toward
aliens, neither is sneered away.**

### Hole 1 — the construction logistics [FACT]
- **No proven transport-and-lift method.** Rafting **multi-tonne basalt — up to ~50 t — some 25 miles over
  open ocean**, then **lifting** columns into **7.5 m mortarless walls** on a reef, **with no obvious
  crane, ramp, or surviving apparatus**, is **genuinely unexplained.**
- **Replication has failed.** **Experimental archaeology has not reproduced** the quarry-to-wall feat at
  scale. *(This is the potato made literal: the modern attempt to do the "easy" thing falls short of the
  thing that actually stands there.)*
- **The seabed columns.** A **University of Oregon survey (1988–89)** found basalt columns **on the
  seabed** between source and site. The prosaic, mainstream reading: **lost or dropped cargo** in
  transit — i.e. evidence of *the haul*, not of anything stranger. **[FACT — and the honest grounded read;
  do not let a character spin it into proof of a sunken structure without labelling that as their
  over-reach.]**

### Hole 2 — no water, no food [FACT]
- Nan Madol has **no fresh water** and **grows no food.** A colossal stone metropolis that was
  **uninhabitable without constant canoe resupply.**
- This sharpens the *why-here* question: **why build it on a foodless, waterless reef, and how was a city
  this size provisioned, at scale, every day, for centuries?**

> **The two holes feed each other.** It is not only *how did they lift it* — it is *why drag three-quarters
> of a million tonnes across the sea to a place that could keep no one alive.* The answer the book reaches
> for is a **function** (§6), and it is the local's to give.

## 4. The Pohnpeians' own tradition [TRADITION — render with respect; never the narrator's woo, never the crew's to "decode"]

- **The builders, in the oral history.** Twin sorcerers — **Olisihpa and Olosohpa** (brothers in the
  tradition) — are said to have raised the stones. **[TRADITION]**
- **The means, in the oral history.** They are said to have moved and set the basalt **with the aid of a
  flying dragon** (a serpent/dragon that levitated the stones into place). **[TRADITION]**
- **Why this matters to the book (and is NOT fringe).** The islanders' *own* account already concedes that
  **the logistics looked impossible** — that ordinary hauling could not, on its face, explain the city.
  That is not woo to be debunked and not aliens to be reached for; it is **a people's true memory that the
  doing was beyond the ordinary**, and it belongs to them. The crew defers entirely; the tradition is the
  custodian's to tell or withhold. *(Contrast sharply with §5: the dragon is the Pohnpeians'; the
  ancient-aliens framing is the outsiders' theft of it.)*

## 6. Grounded angle + the "what is it for" (re-opens wonder, never closes it)

- **The materials are fully natural.** The "prisms" are **columnar jointing** — nature pre-cut the basalt
  into stackable logs. The wonder is **not** that the stones are mysterious; it is that **moving and
  stacking 750,000 tonnes of them, by hand, onto a reef, is a feat we still cannot fully reconstruct.**
  **[FACT]**
- **The honest "what is it for."** The crew can establish *who* (the Saudeleur, a real medieval dynasty),
  and Jakobus can read *how it stands* (header-and-stretcher). The book turns on **what it's for** — and
  the answer is a **function**, grounded and physical, that re-opens the wonder rather than closing it.
  Strong grounded candidates the book can reach toward (the local hands the reading; the narrator never
  declares it proven):
  - a **ceremonial-mortuary capital** built deliberately *apart* — a place of the dead and the divine kings
    set on the water, off the living island, reached and provisioned **only** by those the protocol
    allowed; the foodless, waterless siting is not a flaw but **the point** (a city for the dead and the
    sacred, not for daily life). **[FACT-adjacent — consistent with the ceremonial-mortuary designation;
    the *meaning* of it is the custodian's to give, per §3.]**
  - **the sea as the road** — the canals and the open-water haul read as a **wayfinding/maritime** feat: a
    society whose mastery was the water itself, for whom rafting basalt 25 miles was an expression of the
    same Pacific seamanship that settled the ocean. **[ADDED — NEEDS SOURCE for any specific claim; the
    reconciliation memo logs Polynesian/Micronesian wayfinding as a natural *woven thread* pairing with Nan
    Madol — use as a grounded lens for the maritime read, not as an asserted construction method.]**
- The wonder gets **larger**, not smaller: not "aliens did it," but *a people whose sea-craft and whose
  devotion to their dead were great enough to put a stone city where no one could live* — and the precise
  rig that lifted the 50-tonne cornerstone is still, honestly, **a gap.**

## 7. Accuracy notes & open items (honour these; don't inherit an error)

- **The 1628 / Isokelekel date** [CONTESTED — soft]. §D.11 carries the Saudeleur fall / Isokelekel takeover
  at **~1628**. Scholarship gives a **range** for this transition (oral-historical, variously placed in the
  early-mid 17th c. and sometimes earlier). **Write it as "around the early 17th century" / "~1628" and do
  not assert a hard year as settled.** **[DO-NOT-STATE a single exact year as certain.]**
- **"~AD 1180" megalithic start** [FACT]. Keep as the radiocarbon-anchored start of the *monumental* phase;
  earlier, smaller occupation/use of the reef flat predates the great stonework. Do not let the prose imply
  the islets sprang up whole in 1180.
- **"~750,000 t" and "~1,850 t/yr"** [FACT / derived]. The total is the cited figure; the per-year rate is
  the **total ÷ ~4 centuries** arithmetic. Use as the calm cold-eyed measure (the potato-prover's
  function), never as false precision — it is an order-of-magnitude truth, not a ledger entry.
- **"~92 islets," "~25 miles," "5.5–7.5 m," "~50 t cornerstone," "5–25 t columns," "~1.5 × 0.5 km"** —
  all **[FACT]** per §D.11; preserve verbatim as the scene-ready numbers.
- **The University of Oregon 1988–89 seabed survey** [FACT] — the citable anchor for "columns on the
  seabed → read as lost cargo." Keep the prosaic interpretation as the honest mainstream read.
- **Replication failure** [FACT] — "experimental archaeology has not reproduced it" is the cited,
