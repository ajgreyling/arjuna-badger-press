## 1. The real place (geography, honest)

**Pohnpei** — a high, drenched volcanic island in the Federated States of Micronesia, deep in the western Pacific. One of the wettest places on earth; the interior runs to cloud-forest ridges and waterfalls, the coast to mangrove and reef. The modern island is real and present: **Kolonia** the small port town (corrugated roofs, a few streets, the FSM and Pohnpei State offices, the airstrip on a causeway), outboard skiffs, the smell of diesel and frangipani and low tide. This is not a postcard. It is people's home — fishermen, families, the church on Sunday, the kava (*sakau*) pounded at dusk — and the book locates its first act in that ordinariness so the reef can be extraordinary against it.

**Nan Madol** sits off the **southeast coast**, on a reef flat at the edge of the lagoon, fronting the district of **Madolenihmw**. You reach it by water, on the tide. It is **restricted, sacred, permission-bound** — entry is by right and by protocol, not by ticket. The book treats arrival as the *Nahnmwarki* order requires it to be treated: a thing you are *allowed* into, or not.

## 2. The site map (the through-image: the spaces between)

- **~92 artificial islets** built on the reef flat, laced with **tidal canals** — hence *"the Venice of the Pacific."* The core is **~1.5 × 0.5 km.** *(The book's title and its whole governing image: **"Nan Madol" = "spaces between"** — the canals, the gaps, and, underneath, the kept silences. The water between the stone is as load-bearing as the stone.)*
- **The stone:** **naturally prismatic columnar basalt** — long hexagonal "logs" from cooling, contracting lava (textbook **columnar jointing**; the Giant's Causeway / Devils Tower physics). **Nature pre-cut the blocks.** This must be shown clearly: the wonder is *not* that the stones are mysterious-shaped; it is what was done with them.
- **The technique:** columns and prisms **stacked dry, mortarless, header-and-stretcher, log-cabin style**, on a base of **coral rubble fill.** *(This is Jakobus's made-thing read — a real, legible building grammar: alternating a block's long face and its end, course on course, the way a log-cabin builder binds a wall. He reads the **how-it-stands**; the **how-it-got-here** is the hole.)*
- **Nandauwas** — the great royal mortuary enclosure — walls **5.5–7.5 m high**; columns typically **5–25 t**; a single **cornerstone ~50 t** (the hardest single lift to account for — the scene-anchor for *now lift THAT, onto a wall, on a reef, by hand*).
- **Mass and rate:** **~750,000 tonnes** total; **~1,850 t/yr** sustained over **~4 centuries** (derived: total ÷ span — the calm, cold-eyed arithmetic the potato-prover runs; an order-of-magnitude truth, never false precision).
- **The source:** basalt quarried **~25 miles** away, on the **Sokehs** side of the island — **no documented transport method** over that open water. Columns found **on the seabed** (University of Oregon survey, **1988–89**), read prosaically as **lost cargo** — evidence of the haul. *(A character may over-reach this into "a sunken structure"; the narrator never does — `[DO-NOT-STATE]`.)*
- **When:** the **megalithic phase from ~AD 1180** (radiocarbon) — **medieval**, not antiquity. Earlier, smaller use of the reef flat predates the great stonework; the islets did not spring up whole in 1180. Saudeleur capital; dynasty overthrown by **Isokelekel** around the early seventeenth century (carry **"~1628"** softly — a *range*, never a settled year).

## 3. The texture of the wonder (sensory, grounded — land it once, vary cadence)

The wall meets the water with no mortar line and no slip: hexagonal basalt laid long-face, end-face, long-face, climbing seven metres out of coral and tide. At low water the canals drain to black mud and the islets stand on their fill; at high water you pole a skiff *between* them, the stone close enough to touch, cool and rough and salt-wet, and the whole place is corridors of rock and corridors of sea, the spaces between as built as the stone. There is no fresh water here and nothing grows you could eat — a city the size of a town with not one thing alive in it that wasn't carried in by canoe. The silence on the reef is not empty; it is *kept*. Birds, the slap of water on basalt, the wind in the mangrove. And the cold fact under the beauty, stated plainly: **the brightest modern attempt to do the easy version of this falls short of the thing standing flawless and silent in front of you.**

## 4. The chase geography (where the thriller runs)

- **Kolonia / the offices** — the documentary-grain first act: the term sheet, the shell foundation's filings, the "cultural-tourism partnership" letterhead, the architect's renderings of boardwalks over the canals and infinity pools on the reef. Fluorescent light, a photocopier, a meeting that is a land grab in a linen suit. **Yusuf's ground.**
- **The lagoon and the tidal approach to Nan Madol** — the chase is **a thing you can only do on the tide**, by skiff, by permission. Timing, water, and right-of-entry are the obstacles, not gates and guards. A night crossing; the wrong tide; the protocol that says *not today, not you, not yet.*
- **The Sokehs side / the 25-mile water** — the quarry source and the open-water haul route; the seabed where the Oregon columns lie. The route the basalt took is the route the thriller retraces.
- **The archive / the survey record** — the season-spine whisper lives here: an earlier survey quietly bought up, a record gone missing, the resort outfit's options dated **suspiciously early** (the *someone-got-here-first* tell; plant it, never confirm it's one hand).
- **The Beast** — Jakobus's vehicle, shipped or sourced, far from home and useless on the water; *that he is far from his African mastery, and that almost nothing of it ports to a Pacific reef, is itself a recurring beat.*

**Constraints (honest, and they ARE the plot):** the tide governs everything; entry is permission-bound and protocol-bound; the place is sacred and someone's; the crew cannot dig, cannot take, cannot photograph without consent. The thriller's pressure is **legal and moral and tidal**, never a moustache-twirling guard. The adversary's pressure is **the clock on the options and the manufactured vacancy.**

## 5. The living people near the place (§3.3 — INVIOLABLE; not scenery)

Nan Madol is **not an empty riddle free for the taking.** It is a **living grave with living keepers.** Near it and of it:

- **The Pohnpeian custodian / oral historian of the Saudeleur sites** (the local lead — see CHARACTERS). The *Nahnmwarki* protocols of who may enter, who may speak, and what may be told to whom are **theirs.** The Olisihpa/Olosohpa-and-the-dragon tradition is **theirs.**
- **The *Nahnmwarki* order** — the living chiefly authority of Madolenihmw and Pohnpei, the structure of right and rank that governs access to the sacred ground. Depicted with care, never as quaint colour, never "explained" by the crew. *(In-culture read, FLOOR.)*
- **The fishing families, the skiff owners, the *sakau* circle, the church** — the ordinary, present life of the island, into which the crew arrive as guests, not as solvers. The grain of the place is people getting on with living next to their ancestors' impossible city.

**The wonder must serve the place.** It sends curiosity and money *toward* Pohnpei (the back matter points the reader at the real radiocarbon, the real Oregon survey, the real failed replications — *go and check; the record is open* — and at the **living custodians' right** to the rest). If the place reads as a backdrop for the band's epiphany, the book has failed (Bible §3, §8).
