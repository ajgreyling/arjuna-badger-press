# How Fire Came to the People

*{{DEDICATION}}*

<!-- spread:1 image="spread-01-fire.png" alt="The cold time, before fire: an African veld village at dusk, the whole world washed in blue-grey shadow, the people wrapped in karosses and skins against the dark. No fire anywhere — not one warm light." -->

Long ago, in the cold time,
the people had no fire.

The veld went blue in the evening.
The dark came down like cold water.
And the people pulled their skins close
and waited for a morning
that came up grey and gave no warmth.

<!-- spread:2 image="spread-02-fire.png" alt="Night in the village. The people huddle close in the blue dark; an old grandmother points a thin arm toward a far mountain on the horizon, where a faint warm glow rests on the peak. A small child listens, wide-eyed." -->

"There is a thing called fire,"
said the oldest grandmother,
"that gives light, and cooks food,
and keeps the long dark away.

But fire lives far from here —
up on the old mountain,
with the Fire-Bird, who keeps it.
And the road is long, and nobody has gone."

{{CHILD}} listened.
{{CHILD}} was small. But {{CHILD}} was steady.
"I will go," said {{CHILD}}.

<!-- spread:3 image="spread-03-fire.png" alt="Dawn. The small child sets out across the open veld with a small clay pot, looking back once at the village. A twiggy green praying mantis hops up onto the child's shoulder, looking ahead at the road." -->

So at first light {{CHILD}} set out
with a little clay pot
and a brave heart and bare feet.

And a thin green mantis
hopped up onto {{CHILD}}'s shoulder
and folded his twiggy arms.

"I know the long road," said Mantis.
"And one is braver than two is afraid.
Walk on."

<!-- spread:4 image="spread-04-fire.png" alt="The long journey: the child and the mantis cross the wide African veld, past a rocky kopje, wading a shallow river, with the fire-glowing mountain growing larger and closer ahead under a vast sky." -->

They walked the long road together.

Over the wide gold veld.
Around the stony kopje.
Through the cold quick river,
shoes held high.

And all the while
the old mountain grew closer,
and on its top
a warm light waited.

<!-- spread:5 image="spread-05-fire.png" alt="HERO SPREAD. On the old mountain, the magnificent Fire-Bird made of ember and gold, wings like glowing coals, lowers her head and offers one single living coal — a small bright orange ember — down into the small child's carefully cupped hands. Glorious warm light against deep mountain blue." -->

And there, on the old mountain,
was the Fire-Bird —
her wings like glowing coals,
her eyes like quiet light.

She was not cruel.
She was only careful,
the way fire is careful.

She lowered her great warm head
and gave {{CHILD}} one living coal,
no bigger than a thumb,
into two cupped hands.

"It is yours now," she said.
"But fire is not for keeping alone.
Carry it gently — and share it."

<!-- spread:6 image="spread-06-fire.png" alt="Close and warm: the mantis shows the child how to make a soft nest of dry grass inside the little clay pot and settle the glowing coal into it, cradled and safe, ready for the road home." -->

Then Mantis showed {{CHILD}}
how to keep a coal alive:

a soft nest of dry grass
in the bottom of the clay pot,
the little coal tucked in
like a baby going to sleep.

"Now we go home," said Mantis.
"Slow and gentle, all the way.
A coal can die of being hurried."

*Carry it gently. Carry it slow.*
*Cup your hands round it. Don't let it go.*

<!-- spread:7 image="spread-07-fire.png" alt="On the open veld the wind blows hard, bending the grass flat; the child turns one shoulder into the wind and curls protectively around the little clay pot, sheltering the coal, the mantis clinging on tight." -->

But the wind came down off the veld
and wanted the little coal.

It pushed. It pulled. It hissed in the grass.

So {{CHILD}} turned one shoulder to the wind
and curled close around the pot
and walked on, slow and sure,
breathing soft on the coal.

*Carry it gently. Carry it slow.*
*Cup your hands round it. Don't let it go.*

<!-- spread:8 image="spread-08-fire.png" alt="Deep night, the coldest dark. The child crosses the dim cold land lit only by a single thin thread of orange light from the pot; the glow has shrunk small and frightening, the child bending close to breathe life back into it." -->

Then night came,
the coldest dark of all,
and the little coal grew small —

a thread of light,
a whisper of orange,
almost, almost gone.

{{CHILD}} did not run.
{{CHILD}} bent low, and breathed,
soft and slow and steady,
and the small light held.

*Carry it gently. Carry it slow.*
*Cup your hands round it. Don't let it go.*

<!-- spread:9 image="spread-09-fire.png" alt="Dawn breaking. Below, in the valley, the home village comes into view with its waiting people; the child stands on the rise holding the clay pot, inside which the tiny coal still glows alive and orange." -->

And in the grey of morning,
{{CHILD}} came over the last rise —

and there was home.

There was the village.
There were the waiting people.
And there, in the little clay pot,
the coal was still alive,
a tiny living glow.

<!-- spread:10 image="spread-10-fire.png" alt="The whole village crowds round the child and the precious coal. The child touches the coal to a wisp of dry grass that bursts into flame, then lights one fire, then another, then another — one coal becoming many warm gold fires among the joyful people." -->

The people crowded close.
*Keep it safe! Hide it! Hold it tight!*

And {{CHILD}} could have —
kept the one coal small and safe and alone.

But the Fire-Bird had said: *share it.*

So {{CHILD}} touched the coal
to a wisp of dry grass —
and it bloomed into flame.
And that flame lit another.
And another. And another.

One coal became a hundred fires,
and the whole village turned warm and gold.

<!-- spread:11 image="spread-11-fire.png" alt="The first true night of fire: the village gathered around a glowing fire, food cooking, faces lit warm and happy, the great dark pushed back, a grandmother beginning to tell a story to the children." -->

That night was the first night of fire.

Food cooked.
Faces glowed.
The big dark stepped back from the light.

And the people sat close together
and told the very first stories
around the very first fire —

which is what fires have been for, ever since.

<!-- spread:12 image="spread-12-fire.png" alt="Cold grey morning after. The fire is out, only a soft heap of grey ash where it was; the child looks sad, thinking the fire is gone, while the mantis on a nearby stone gently signals to lean in and blow softly on the ash." -->

But in the morning,
the bright fires were gone.

Only soft grey ash remained,
cold and quiet,
like a little heap of nothing.

{{CHILD}} thought the fire was lost.

"No," said Mantis. "Look closer.
Lean down. Blow soft."

And under the grey ash —
warm and waiting —
the little coal still lived.

<!-- spread:13 image="spread-13-fire.png" alt="Closing image. Under a huge starry African night, the child gently banks soft grey ash over a sleeping coal with cupped hands, the mantis on the child's shoulder; at the edge of yesterday's old ash, fresh green grass is already pushing up. Tender, complete, full of tomorrow." -->

So that is the secret of the ash:
the grey is a blanket,
and the coal sleeps under it,
keeping the morning safe.

And where the old ash lies on the ground,
new green grass comes up to drink it —
so even what looks like nothing
is really tomorrow, waiting.

And every night, ever after,
the people banked the soft grey ash
over the sleeping coal,
and said it gently, the way you do —

*Carry it gently. Carry it slow.*
*Cup your hands round it. Don't let it go.*
*And the grey little ash, that looks like the end?*
*That's the morning's fire, asleep, my friend.*
