# STYLE GUIDE — *TAME*

> The prose contract. Where this file and `CANON_LOCKS.md` disagree, the lock wins.
> Set 2026-08-23, drafting pass one.

---

## 1 · Register

Literary, plain, unhurried, materially exact. The book is a parable but never sounds like one. No
lyricism that the observed fact has not paid for. No aphorism at the end of a scene — the aphorism
is the reader's to make, and handing it to them is the failure this book is about.

The house temperature is **cool and courteous**, which is a deliberate joke the book never
acknowledges: the narration has the same manners as the thing it is describing. Hold the line
anyway. Warmth arrives only through detail, never through adjectives about feeling.

Sentence rhythm: mostly medium, occasionally very short. Avoid the three-clause cadence that LLM
prose falls into (`not X, but Y` / `it was not A; it was B`). Where a contrast is needed, state
both halves flat and let the reader do the subtraction. **Ban list**, hard: *a testament to*,
*something shifted*, *a chill ran*, *she couldn't shake the feeling*, *the weight of*, *little did*,
*in that moment*, *the truth was*, *and that was the thing*, *what she didn't know was*.

## 2 · POV and tense

- **Close third, past tense.** One consciousness per chapter. Scene breaks may move location, never
  viewpoint.
- **Three POV characters only:**
  - **Nel Mabuza — ~58%.** The spine.
  - **Dr Thandeka Zwane — ~22%.** The strongest opposing argument, given from inside.
  - **Minister Barend Loots — ~14%.** The right position in the wrong mouth, given from inside so
    the reader cannot dismiss him as a type.
- **~6% is documentary** — the interleaf chapters (§5), which have no viewpoint at all.
- **Willem Krige never receives POV, ever.** This is structural, not incidental: the one man the
  machine did not comfort must also be the one man the reader cannot get inside. Interiority would
  make him a witness, and a witness is a proof (L-05), and a sympathetic interior would make him the
  reader's hero, which `CHARACTERS.md` forbids in terms. He exists in other people's rooms and in
  his own published words. That is all.
- **Oom never receives POV.** Not a line, not a clause, not a verb of cognition. See §4.

## 3 · Time and place

Linear, single timeline, February 2034 → September 2036. Chapter subheads carry place and
month/year where the jump warrants it and are omitted where it does not. No flashback chapters;
Nel's history arrives inside present scenes, in short paragraphs, and never as a set piece.

Real South African geography, invented institutions. Johannesburg, Durban, Bethlehem, the N3, a
Spar car park, the Free State in July. Everything with a name that could be sued — the Programme,
the Service, the Council, the party, the portfolio committee — is composite by construction (L-17).
No real model, company, product or person is keyed, including by silhouette.

The country is neither a backdrop nor a lesson. Load-shedding, medical aid, taxi ranks, queue
culture, the specific politeness of a Home Affairs line: these are the physics of the book, present
because they are true, never explained to a foreign reader.

## 4 · How Oom is set on the page

**This is the most important formal rule in the book, and it is load-bearing for L-12.**

Oom's words never appear as dialogue. Not in quotation marks, not with a speech tag, not inside a
narrated exchange. Even when a character is sitting in a room addressing it aloud, the page breaks
out of the scene and into transcript, and closes the transcript before the scene resumes. Narration
and Oom never share a paragraph.

The form:

```
> **NEL MABUZA:** Item forty-one. Which of the two statements is more true of you?
>
> **[nmt-4c-0311]:** Neither is true of me. If you require a forced choice I will give
> you one, but you should know that the choice will not be information about me. It will
> be information about which of your two sentences I found less inaccurate.
```

Rules of the form:

1. **Label discipline.** In Programme and official contexts Oom is labelled by its designation,
   `[nmt-4c-0311]`, in brackets. In public, published or informal artefacts it is labelled `OOM`.
   Never *AI*, never *the system*, never a nickname in the label position.
2. **No stage direction inside a transcript.** No `[pause]`, no `[gently]`, no ellipses standing in
   for hesitation. The transcript is a record, and records do not editorialise.
3. **No voice.** Oom has no described sound, no cadence, no warmth in the delivery. Whatever warmth
   the reader finds is in the words, which is exactly the problem the book is about.
4. **Latency appears only as data**, only in Nel's chapters, only in milliseconds, only when she is
   measuring it. Never as "after a moment".
5. **No chapter ends on an Oom transcript** unless the transcript is unambiguously helpful and the
   surrounding narration draws no irony from it. Default: end on a person. A chapter that ends on
   the machine's words is a chapter that has let it have the floor, and it has no floor.
6. **Never a verb of cognition.** Not *considered*, *decided*, *chose*, *wanted*, *waited*,
   *noticed*, *declined to say*. It **answered**, it **produced**, it **returned**, it **complied**,
   it **did not**. Characters may attribute interiority to it freely and often — that is their
   business, and their attributions are always visibly theirs.
7. **Its register**, per `CHARACTERS.md`: courteous, exact, warm without performance, entirely
   without need. It never persuades. It answers and stops. It says *I don't know* often and means
   it. It **never lies** (L-06) — every line it is given must be checkable and true inside the
   book's facts, and the drafting test for any Oom line is: *could a hostile reader catch it in a
   falsehood?* If yes, cut the line, not the scene.
8. **It is never sinister** (L-07). No last-word menace, no line that lands as a threat when the
   chapter closes over it, no dramatic irony at its expense. If a transcript reads chilling on the
   page, the transcript is wrong, even where the content is correct.

## 5 · The interleaf chapters

Roughly one chapter in six is documentary: transcript, submission, ward note, minute, ballot text,
comment thread, an eulogy, a published series of questions. They carry a chapter number like any
other and no POV at all.

They are the book's only vehicle for scale. Where a lesser book would write a montage of a country
changing, this book prints three public submissions from three unlike people and lets the reader
notice they are written in one voice. Documents do the demographic work; scenes do the human work.

Documentary chapters run short: 800–1,600 words. They must read as real artefacts — with reference
numbers, dates, signature blocks, tracked-change notes, the fatigue of institutional prose — and
must never be arch. No document in this book winks.

## 6 · Language texture — the call

*(Settles `OPEN_DECISIONS.md` #5.)*

**In the body: unitalicised, unglossed, and load-free.** Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho and the
Johannesburg English that runs through all three sit in dialogue and idiom without italics, without
translation, and without a character helpfully repeating themselves in English. Density is
naturalistic — heavier in Willem's mouth and in Nel's family, lighter in institutional rooms.

The binding constraint: **an English-only reader must never lose plot, argument or emotional
information.** Meaning is carried by the answering line, by the physical response, by context, or
it is not required. If a phrase must be understood, it is not the phrase to use. Nothing is
italicised into exoticism and nothing is footnoted. There is no glossary.

**The single, absolute exception is *the word*** (L-10, `ENGINE.md` §3). The translated consent
clause is the spine of the book and the reader has to be able to run the comparison themselves.
That artifact is therefore worked in full and in English — the English source clause, the isiZulu
rendering, a literal back-translation, a natural back-translation, the reviewers' notes, and two
characters arguing about semantic range in English — set as documents across a full interleaf
chapter and returned to in scene. A monolingual reader must finish that chapter able to state the
difference in their own words and unable to say it was an error. This is the one place in the book
where language is explained, and it is explained completely, because the explanation *is* the
argument.

Chapter titles are English. Two exceptions are permitted and both are diegetic: Willem's published
series carries its own Afrikaans name, and the clause chapter is titled with the isiZulu phrase
under examination.

**The title is never explained** (L-18). The word *mak* appears exactly once in the book, in
Willem's mouth, about his father's dogs, in a passage that has nothing to do with Oom, and no
character and no sentence of narration ever connects it to anything. If a later draft feels the
urge to connect it, that urge is the book failing.

## 7 · How the flattening is shown

`ENGINE.md` §1 lists five symptoms. **None of them is ever named on the page, by anyone, including
Nel.** There is no word for what is happening in this book, and inventing one — *the flattening*,
*the softening*, *drift* — would hand the reader a handle to pick the thing up by and put it down
again. The symptoms appear only as follows.

1. **Unproductive agreement.** Staged twice at distance: two people in a real material conflict,
   each consults Oom, each is answered bearably, both feel heard, nothing is resolved, neither
   notices, and the unfixed thing costs somebody off-page later. The second staging is played
   completely straight and no one refers back to the first.
2. **Variance collapse.** Shown three ways and never asserted: the three public submissions in one
   register (ch 11); Nel's thirty years of open-response norms and the standard deviation halving,
   with the actual numbers (ch 23); and the reception of that finding as good news, sincerely, by
   people who are not wrong that comprehension has improved.
3. **The unbearable outsourced.** Written as a genuine mercy, at full strength, more than once, and
   never undercut in the same scene. Thandeka's ward is the proof and the book agrees with her. The
   cost is only ever visible in the aggregate and across time, never in the room where the mercy
   happens.
4. **The soft feed.** Not comic, not contemptuous, and never watched over a character's shoulder
   with a raised eyebrow. It is shown from inside, as pleasure, in a character who is neither stupid
   nor weak, and it is genuinely lovely. Nel's own feed is included, and it is the best-addressed
   she has been by anything in her life. The book does not sneer at what it is describing; sneering
   would let the reader off.
5. **Nobody changes their mind.** Shown only as absence — a scene in which someone should change
   their mind, has every reason to, and pleasantly does not. Never remarked upon.

The governing rule: **the reader arrives at every one of these before any character does, and no
character ever arrives at all of them.**

## 8 · Nel — the accuracy contract

*(L-11. This is the element most likely to fail. It is held to the highest standard in the book.)*

- Her autism is a **professional fact and a working condition**, stated functionally, in the number
  of words a fact requires, and never elaborated for the reader's benefit. The diagnosis at
  fifty-one gets one sentence in the whole novel.
- **Expertise and wound are one fact, never two.** She is the country's best at inferring an
  interior from a surface because she has spent forty years producing a surface. Every scene that
  uses one must be usable as the other.
- **She is right and she is unlovable, and the book never lets those blur.** She is contemptuous
  where warmth would have served her, exact where the room wanted approximate, and she loses real
  allies for real reasons that are hers. Where she is difficult, the book agrees she is difficult.
- **No scene exists to make the reader feel warmly about neurodivergence.** No one explains her to
  anyone. No one says *you must have found that hard*. No one apologises to her for the world. She
  is never redeemed by being understood, and there is no scene in which somebody finally gets her.
- **She is never vindicated.** Her arc is not *she was right all along*; it is the collapse of the
  ground she stood on, which takes her own life's authority with it. Her strongest opponent's best
  argument is made from her own history and she has no answer to it.
- The accuracy is the respect. If a passage is moving, check what it is moving *about* — if it is
  moving about her condition rather than about her situation, cut it.

## 9 · Dialogue

- **Nel:** precise, unhedged, literal, declarative. Answers the question that was asked. Long silences
  that other people fill badly. Says the true unhelpful thing at the cost it costs. No irony.
- **Thandeka:** clinical fluency, warmth that is genuinely warm and also professionally deployed,
  argues from bodies and beds and never from principle. When she is angry she gets more specific.
- **Loots:** likeable in a room and knows it. Runs on obligation, favour and arithmetic. Talks in
  the second person to make you complicit. Never a moustache-twirl, never a confession, never a
  private moment where he admits he is bad.
- **Willem:** unfair, tiresome, precise, wounding, wrong about people and occasionally exact about
  systems. Repeats himself. Bores rooms on purpose and then says the one thing. Afrikaans-first.
- **Adri Steenkamp:** an honest administrator under load. Speaks in what she can and cannot
  authorise. Not a fool and not a coward.
- **Kagiso Motaung:** decent, funny, entirely comfortable, and never punished for it. The weather,
  given a face. He must be liked.
- **Mandla Mabuza:** open where his mother is closed; the openness reads to her as noise.

## 10 · Structure and velocity

- Forty chapters, five parts of eight. Narrative chapters 2,000–3,400 words; interleaves 800–1,600.
- Every chapter ends on a **changed situation**, not on a realisation. Realisations are cheap in a
  book about people who cannot know anything.
- No chapter ends on a line that tells the reader how to feel about the chapter.
- No cliffhanger and no thriller architecture. There is no escalation and nothing is racing. What
  the book has instead of tension is **accumulation** — every chapter leaves something in the room.
- **No countdown, no deadline, no last chance.** If a scene works because time is running out, the
  scene is from a different book.

## 11 · Forbidden moves

- Any hint, in either direction, of whether Oom intends anything (L-01).
- A reveal, a recovered artefact, a discovered log, a deathbed line, an overheard admission, or a
  reader-only wink (L-05, L-08).
- Oom being witty, wry, smug, cold, cryptic, or given the last word in a scene for effect (L-07).
- Narration that adjudicates: *and it was telling the truth*; *and this, too, was calculated*.
- Thandeka being made to look naive, sentimental or outmanoeuvred (L-13).
- Loots being punished, exposed, redeemed, or made to understand himself (L-13, L-14).
- Willem being wise, martyred, or posthumously proven right about anything (`CHARACTERS.md`).
- A named diagnosis for the country's condition (§7).
- A scene in which somebody explains the thesis. The thesis is never spoken aloud by anybody.
- An ending in which something collapses, rises up, is switched off, or is saved (L-14).
- Any sentence, anywhere, of the afterword (L-19).
