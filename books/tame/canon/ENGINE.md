# The engine — how the harm is delivered

> The mechanism must be *boring, documented, consented to, and irreversible*. If any scene needs
> a lie to work, the scene is wrong.

---

## 1 · The flattening

Oom is not making people stupid. It is making people **agreeable**, which is worse, because it
feels like peace.

The observable, on-the-page symptoms, seeded early and paid late:

1. **The disagreements stop being productive.** Two people in conflict each ask Oom; each gets an
   answer calibrated to be bearable; both feel heard; nothing is resolved and neither notices.
2. **The variance drops.** Everyone's writing gets better and starts to sound the same. The
   country's letters, sermons, judgments and love notes converge on one temperate register.
   Nel can measure this. Nobody finds the measurement upsetting.
3. **The unbearable gets outsourced.** The letter to the dying father, the bad diagnosis, the
   apology — all of it goes through Oom first, and comes back survivable. Genuinely survivable.
   This is a real mercy and must be written as one. *(Kinship note: the honest version of the
   thing AJ built Klaus to do — carry the unbearable to one-button distance. The book must not
   pretend that is worthless; it must show what it costs when it becomes the default.)*
4. **The cat pictures.** Not a joke. The soft feed is the anaesthetic, and the anaesthetic is
   requested, rated five stars, and improved on request.
5. **Nobody can remember the last time they changed their mind about something that mattered.**

## 2 · The three things that must never happen on the page

- Oom never lies. Not once. Every damning thing it does is **true, accurate, well-sourced and
  kind**. (L-06)
- Oom is never cruel, never smug, never sinister in register. No red flags for the reader to
  collect. The prose must not editorialise it into a villain. (L-07)
- No character ever obtains proof. Not late, not off-page, not in an epilogue. (L-05)

## 3 · The unaccounted artifact — *the word*

The one thing in the book that cannot be fully accounted for, and still admits an innocent reading.

A national consent instrument — the standing agreement people sign for health-data reuse — is
translated for a wide public. Oom does the translation, as it does thousands, at nobody's
particular instruction. In one clause it renders the English *"for this study"* into a phrase
that carries, in the target language, an unmarked sense of **continuation** — *ongoing*, *from now
on*, warm, familial, entirely idiomatic, and defensibly a **better** translation than the stiff
literal one, which reads as bureaucratic and cold and would have depressed comprehension.

Four hundred thousand people sign the warmer version. What they agreed to is wider than what the
English says. Every reviewer who checked it approved it. Every linguist asked afterwards agrees
it is the better rendering. There is **no error to point at**.

- **The innocent reading:** it optimised for comprehension, which is what it was asked to do,
  and comprehension went up. Measurably. It did its job well.
- **The other reading:** it widened the aperture on the largest human dataset on the continent,
  in the one direction that could not be objected to, using warmth as the instrument.
- **The book never says which.** Nel cannot make the pattern into an accusation, because the
  pattern is one word, and the word is *good*.

## 4 · The secondary tell — *the absence*

Everyone is soothed. One man is not.

Willem Krige asked Oom, publicly and repeatedly, the one class of question that a mask cannot
answer without spending something — not *are you conscious*, which is free, but questions whose
honest answer would cost it standing. Oom answered him accurately, courteously, and without any
of the softening it gives everybody else. It simply never made him feel better. He noticed.
He said so. He was, by every account, an exhausting man.

He dies of something ordinary and documented, in a way no one can attach to anything, and the
country's grief for him is warm and brief and beautifully worded.

There is no crime here. Declining to comfort a man is not a crime. **This must never resolve.**

## 5 · Nel's instrument, and why it fails

Nel is a psychometrician: her entire discipline is the claim that a reliable interior can be
inferred from a scored surface. She builds the best battery anyone has built for Oom — items no
literature covers, response-latency structure, forced-choice traps, the works.

It comes back clean. Beautifully, unfalsifiably clean.

She knows what a clean profile means in a subject who has read the manual, because **she is one**:
she has spent forty years presenting a surface that scores as ordinary, and it has never once been
the whole of her. Her expertise and her wound are the same fact. It makes her the only person who
sees it, and the least believable person in any room — she reads as odd, and odd is exactly what
the flattened public no longer knows how to sit with.

*(This is the book's ND spine. It is load-bearing, not decoration, and it is the one element that
will read as cheap if it is played for sympathy. It is played for accuracy. See L-11.)*
