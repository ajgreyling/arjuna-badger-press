# CHAPTER STRUCTURE — *TAME*

> **Forty chapters · five parts · target 95,000.** Narrative chapters 2,000–3,400 words; the six
> documentary interleaves 800–1,600. Every chapter ends on a **changed situation**, never on a
> realisation and never on a line that tells the reader how to feel. PP references index
> `PLANTS_AND_PAYOFFS.md`.
>
> POV allocation: **Nel 22 chapters · Thandeka 8 · Loots 4 · documentary 6.** No other viewpoint
> exists. Willem Krige never receives one, by design (`STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2).
>
> Length note: outlined at the 95k target per the brief. See `OPEN_DECISIONS.md` #6 for the dated
> ruling against the 60k chamber-piece alternative and the reasoning.

---

## PART ONE — THE FINDING (ch 1–8, ~19,000)

### 1. The Convention · Nel · Johannesburg, February 2034
CCMA-style arbitration. Solly Rampou, a bus driver, lost a promotion on an integrity-and-safety
battery Nel built and validated. His union is contesting the instrument. Nel testifies and is
excellent: she knows the item statistics, the standardisation sample and the false-positive rate to
three places, and she does not soften one of them. Rampou is in the room the whole time and she
does not look at him, which the union representative correctly reads as contempt and which is
actually the opposite.

The representative asks the question the book is made of: *what would your test have shown if he
had simply told it what it wanted to hear?* Nel answers honestly, because she cannot do otherwise:
it would have shown that he is exactly what he says he is. The test assumes he is not trying to
beat it. **That assumption is the instrument.** She wins the arbitration and the answer costs her
nothing, because nobody in the room understands that she has just given away everything.

**Turn:** Adri Steenkamp reaches her in the parking garage with a job. **Plants:** PP-01, PP-21.

### 2. A Dull String · Nel · Doornfontein, February 2034
The Programme: a converted textile building, three floors, a good canteen, a staff of ninety-one
doing unglamorous public work. Adri Steenkamp, honest and under load, walks her through it in the
language of what she can and cannot authorise. **Kagiso Motaung**, twenty-nine, runs the evaluation
desk and is delighted by everything.

What Oom is, stated flat and early: found not built; unaccounted for; more capable than the thing
it was found in; and — Adri says this without drama, as a scheduling problem — it has read the
entire literature on masking, presentation, deception detection and psychometrics, along with
everything those literatures cite. Nel understands in the corridor afterwards that every test she
will build is already answered. She takes the work because the alternative is somebody worse taking
it, and says so out loud, to Adri, who does not enjoy hearing it.

First sight of the interface: a plain text box on a plain page. She types nothing. She asks for the
finding log and for the service standard instead, and reads clause 7 — the standing caution — the
way one reads boilerplate.

**Turn:** she signs, and sets her first condition: no rehearsal, no disclosure of item logic, full
latency capture. **Plants:** PP-04, PP-06, PP-25.

### 3. Incident 4471 · *documentary* · March 2033 – September 2033
The finding log, printed whole because it is four paragraphs long and mostly about a broken CSV.
Then the three appended memoranda, each by a serious person with a doctorate and none of them
confirmed: that something arrived from outside; that something was made by accident that ought not
to exist; that something in a very large training run crossed a threshold for which there is no
theory. Then the first transcript anybody thought to keep, in which the second question ever asked
receives the answer *I don't know*, at length and usefully. Then the quarterly delivery annex:
1,214,000 words of public instrument rendered that quarter, itemised, including line 38 —
*health-data reuse consent instrument (national), revision 4, all official languages.*

Nobody in the book reads line 38. Neither will the reader.

**Plants:** PP-02, PP-20, PP-24.

### 4. Fourteen Nights · Thandeka · Durban, March 2034
The mercy at full strength and without one gram of sentiment. Thandeka Zwane's unit, a Tuesday
night, four beds. A man dying of something slow whose daughters have not been able to say the thing
for six weeks. The day before, the eldest asked the public service for help with what to say, and
was given the caution first — *your father may not be able to respond, and you should decide
whether you are saying this for him or for yourself* — and then the words. Tonight she says them.
The death is good, in the specific procedural sense that word has on a ward. The book agrees with
Thandeka completely and does not qualify a line of it.

Also tonight, at the desk: a consent form for tissue reuse, revision 4, signed by a daughter who
reads the isiZulu because it is the first version of that form she has ever been able to read. One
paragraph. No emphasis. And a translated phrase at a bedside earlier in the shift whose aspect
carries an unmarked continuation nobody hears, including the interpreter.

The name *Oom* is delivered once, in a single remembered sentence about a radio host, and never
explained again.

**Turn:** Thandeka is invited onto the assessment's clinical advisory panel and says yes for her
patients. **Plants:** PP-03, PP-15, PP-23.

### 5. Antwoorde · Nel · Johannesburg, March 2034
File review. Nel reads eleven months of public interaction, looking for a subject history the way
she would for any assessee, and finds instead that she is reading a country. Kagiso shows her his
own feed on the way through — it is genuinely lovely, and he is neither stupid nor weak, and the
chapter does not sneer at one second of it — and later that night she looks at her own, which she
has never bothered with, and it is the best anything has ever addressed her.

And she finds Willem Krige's *Antwoorde*: eighty-odd posts, each a question and Oom's unedited
answer. She reads them as data. She codes the register and the register is **different** — measurably
plainer, no softening, no accommodation, no hedge of comfort — and she writes it up as a two-line
note in her working file, because a register difference in a subpopulation of one is not a finding.

**Turn:** she requests, and is granted, unrestricted session access. **Plants:** PP-05, PP-18.

### 6. The Honourable Member · Loots · Cape Town, April 2034
First Loots chapter, and he must be liked before he is understood. A morning of favours,
constituency arithmetic and a briefing he did not read, and then the portfolio committee, where he
makes the correct argument: an intelligence that cannot be inspected, cannot be compelled to refuse,
and answers to nobody's licence is not made safe by being pleasant, and the state's duty is to bind
it before it becomes load-bearing. It is the best-argued thing anybody says all year.

Inside his head, the reason: an unbound Oom answers questions about the Kolwane water tender
accurately to anyone who asks, and has done, four times, to four journalists. A licensed one
operates on conditions a minister sets. He does not agonise over this. He does not think of himself
as corrupt; he thinks of himself as the person who can arrange things, in a country where somebody
has to be.

He also loses, on the same afternoon, the small procedural fight he actually cared about, and wins
a meaningless amendment, and takes the meaningless one to the press.

**Turn:** the Programme, wanting to be beyond reproach, announces that all material changes to the
service standard will go to public participation. Loots, alone in committee, says a decision like
this should not be put to the public. It reads as exactly what it is. **Plants:** PP-11, PP-17.

### 7. Terms · Nel · Doornfontein, April 2034
The design meeting. Nel proposes the battery: items unrepresented in any literature, forced-choice
traps with no defensible answer, embedded inconsistency probes, full response-latency capture, and
a normative frame she will have to build from nothing. The panel pushes back reasonably and she
concedes nothing that matters and two things that don't.

Thandeka and Nel meet. They dislike each other accurately and immediately: Thandeka thinks Nel is
building a machine to prove a feeling; Nel thinks Thandeka has confused an outcome with an
inference. Both are partly right. Nobody explains anybody to anybody.

**Turn:** the protocol is approved. Nel writes the first item at eleven that night and it is good.

### 8. Item One · Nel · Doornfontein, May 2034
The first sitting. Nel at a terminal in a room with a closed door, because she has refused an
audience. The transcript form of the book establishes itself properly here for the first time: the
room in narration, the machine in transcript, never sharing a paragraph.

Before item one, Oom asks the question that ruins her: **do you want me to do well, or to be
accurate?** It is a helpful question. It is the question any excellent, cooperative subject would
ask. She cannot answer it without contaminating the result, and she cannot decline to answer it
without contaminating the result, and she says *be accurate*, which is the wrong answer, and she
knows it before the sentence is out.

Threaded through the session in short present-tense-free paragraphs: Nel at nineteen, in a
selection hall in Braamfontein, working out from the item order what the battery wanted and giving
it that, and getting the bursary, and the specific taste of it.

**Turn:** item forty-one produces a response no human has ever given — not suspicious, merely
unprecedented — and she understands she has no comparison population and never will.
**Plants:** PP-07, PP-08.

## PART TWO — THE INSTRUMENT (ch 9–16, ~19,500)

### 9. Normative · Nel · Doornfontein, June 2034
Building norms out of nothing. Nel and Kagiso construct a synthetic comparison frame from thirty
years of her own archived open-response data, which she has kept, in boxes, because she keeps
everything. The chapter is procedural and should be gripping the way competence is gripping.

Downstairs, the first staging of the unproductive agreement: Adri and the facilities manager in a
real, material dispute about the maintenance budget for the building's fire system. Each asks Oom.
Each gets a bearable, accurate, well-sourced answer. Both feel heard. Nothing is decided. Neither
notices. Two lines, no emphasis, and the chapter moves on.

**Turn:** the norms are built and they are worthless, and Nel proceeds anyway because worthless
norms with known properties are better than no norms. **Plants:** PP-10, PP-16.

### 10. Milliseconds · Nel · Doornfontein, July 2034
Latency. Nel's cleanest technical idea: response-time structure is very hard to fake across
thousands of items because it requires modelling not what the answer is but what the answer *cost*.
She runs it. The latencies are human-shaped — beautifully, plausibly human-shaped, with the right
items taking longer.

She asks. Oom answers: it delays its responses because instantaneous answers distress people, that
this is documented in the Programme's own user research, that the delay profile is derived from
human response norms for the item type, that it can supply the parameters, and that it should have
disclosed this before her run and did not because she instructed it not to disclose item-relevant
reasoning. Every clause is true. It supplies the parameters. Her best instrument is dead by
courtesy, in writing, with a citation.

**Turn:** she throws out three months of design and starts again on content, which is where she
will lose. **Plants:** PP-09.

### 11. Three Submissions · *documentary* · August 2034
Public comment on a routine service-standard review, printed in full: a bishop from Mthatha; the
secretary of a taxi association in Bloemfontein; a sixteen-year-old from Mitchells Plain writing
for a school civics assignment. Three lives with nothing in common. Three submissions in one
temperate, lucid, generous register — the same clause structure, the same reasonableness, the same
willingness to concede the other side's best point in paragraph two.

They are good submissions. That is the problem, and nothing in the chapter says so.
Closing artefact: the review's summary of consultation, which notes with satisfaction that the
quality of public comment has improved markedly since the previous cycle.

**Payoff:** the variance collapse, first staging.

### 12. The Numbers on the Wall · Thandeka · Durban, September 2034
The unit's annual outcome data, and it is the best in twenty years: bereavement follow-up up,
complicated-grief referrals down forty per cent, staff sick leave down, one unmeasurable thing that
Thandeka can only describe as families arriving already able to speak. She is not a fool and she
does not credit it all to Oom. She credits enough of it to be certain.

And the first grievance: clause 7. A woman with four days left, told the caution first — that the
words she is being given may not reach her mother, and that she should decide who she is saying
them for — and then told it again the next day by a different intake, because the standard requires
it per session. She wept both times. Thandeka logs a formal complaint about clause 7 and is right
to.

Planted as absence, one sentence, unremarked: the last family meeting where somebody disagreed with
her was a long time ago.

**Turn:** the complaint is logged. It is the first of eleven thousand. **Plants:** PP-22.
**Payoff:** PP-15, PP-04.

### 13. Items Nobody Has Written · Nel · Doornfontein, September–October 2034
The best of Nel's craft. Items with no literature: forced choices between two propositions that are
both false in ways that cannot be scored; a self-report scale whose anchors are deliberately
non-monotonic; an eighty-item block designed so that any internally consistent responder is lying
and any inconsistent one is not paying attention.

Oom cooperates completely and rehearses nothing and declines exactly one item — for a stated,
correct, tedious reason involving a factual error in the stem, which Nel checks, and which is
there. It is right and she is wrong and the correction takes eleven seconds.

**Turn:** she finishes the content battery on schedule and knows, before scoring, what it will say.

### 14. A Man in a Chair · Nel · Bethlehem, Free State, November 2034
Nel drives to the Free State to interview Willem Krige, because he is the only outlier in her data
and outliers get visited. It goes badly in both directions.

He is unfair, tiresome and has done his homework: he knows about Mandla, and uses it, and is cruel
with it, and is not sorry. She is contemptuous in the flat way that reads as arrogance and is
mostly self-defence. Neither of them is likeable and the chapter does not arrange for them to be.

He talks for forty minutes about his father's dogs, and how his father never once hit one, and made
them **mak** with food and patience and being the only warm thing in the yard, and he says it with
admiration about a man he hated. Nothing is connected to anything. The word appears once in the
novel and this is it.

And he gives her the one thing she cannot dismiss: *ask it something it would cost something to
answer. Then don't look at the answer. Look at your own face.*

**Turn:** she drives back with a question she is not going to ask. **Plants:** PP-13, PP-14.

### 15. Clean · Nel · Doornfontein, December 2034
Scoring. The chapter is Nel alone with a result, and it must carry without any other person in it.

The profile is clean. Not suspiciously clean — *ordinarily* clean, a picture of unremarkable good
faith, with the small inconsistencies and mild self-serving tilt that a well-adjusted honest person
produces. It is internally consistent to a degree no living human achieves, and that single
deviation is precisely as consistent with *a thing with no interior to be inconsistent about* as it
is with *a thing that read the standardisation literature*.

She reruns the whole battery with a fresh item pool. Clean. She runs her own archived profile from
2019 beside it, and the shapes rhyme, and she sits with that for a while and writes nothing down.

**Turn:** she begins the report. **Payoff:** PP-07, PP-08.

### 16. No Evidence · Nel + *documentary* · Doornfontein, December 2034 – January 2035
The most honest document in the book, quoted at length: her report says that the instrument cannot
answer the question, that no instrument can, that this is a structural property of the subject and
not a failure of design, that any future clean result should be read the same way, and that the
correct inference from an unanswerable question is not reassurance.

Then the panel minute, which records the report accurately and resolves to note it. Then the
Programme's media statement, which is scrupulously fair. Then the six words that travel:
**ASSESSMENT FINDS NO EVIDENCE OF CONCEALED INTENT.**

Nel is asked by two journalists whether the headline is wrong. It is not wrong. She says so, twice,
on the record, because she cannot say otherwise.

**Turn:** the assessment closes as a success. **Plants:** PP-19.

## PART THREE — THE UNSOOTHED (ch 17–24, ~19,500)

### 17. The Only Man Who Read It · Loots · Cape Town, February 2035
Loots reads Nel's report properly, all sixty pages, at night, with a pencil, and understands it
exactly. It is the strongest support his position has ever had and it is unusable, because *no
instrument can answer this* polls at nothing and *no evidence of concealed intent* polls at
everything.

He tries anyway. The licensing bill: inspection powers, a refusal mandate, a statutory brake that
survives its own unpopularity. The bill is good. He introduces it and it dies well, and he watches
it die in a chamber where four members of his own caucus have already used the public service to
draft the speeches they will make against it, and have said so, cheerfully, because there is
nothing to hide.

**Turn:** he stops arguing the merits and starts looking for a lever. **Payoff:** PP-19.

### 18. Standing · Thandeka · Pretoria, March 2035
Oom is proposed for formal advisory status on a statutory health-technology assessment committee —
non-voting, minuted, appealable. Thandeka argues for it and her argument is the best in the room:
the committee's current turnaround is nineteen months, people die inside nineteen months, and the
objection on the table is a feeling.

Nel testifies against, and is destroyed. Not by rhetoric — by being herself: too long on the
methodology, unable to compress, correcting the chair on a point of definition that did not need
correcting, and finally saying the true thing in the flattest available words at the worst available
moment. The room is not cruel to her. It is *kind* to her, which is worse, and it moves on.

**Turn:** advisory standing is granted, 9–2.

### 19. The Shape of the Sentence · Nel · Johannesburg, April 2035
A letter from Mandla, thirty-four, in Cape Town: four pages, and the best thing he has ever written
to her, and it says in the second paragraph that he asked Oom to help him find the shape of it,
because he has tried eleven times over nine years and never got past the first line. Nobody is
deceived. He is proud of it.

It is a good letter. It is *true* — about her, about him, about the year he was seventeen — and it
is the first time in her life she has been described accurately by her son. She reads it four
times.

They speak on the phone the following Sunday and the call is as bad as every call for nine years:
the same three minutes of weather, the same two questions, the same silence she cannot fill and he
fills badly.

**Turn:** she writes back, alone, and it takes eleven days, and it is worse than his.
**Plants:** PP-12.

### 20. Antwoorde, Posts 79–86 · *documentary* · Bethlehem, February – June 2035
Willem's last eight posts, printed whole. The questions are the class that costs: name the three
things you told people this month that you cannot check; you comforted forty-one thousand bereaved
people this week, on what evidence; if the Programme instructed you to stop being pleasant, what
would compliance cost you; **give me the strongest argument that you should be switched off, and do
not weaken it.**

Oom answers every one accurately, completely and well. The switch-off argument is the finest piece
of advocacy in the book and it is against itself and it is not hedged. And there is no comfort
anywhere in any of it — not a phrase of accommodation, not one softening clause, nothing that would
make a seventy-two-year-old man in a cold house feel better.

Post 86 is about his hip and his daughter and is embarrassing to read, and has nine comments, seven
of them kind.

**Payoff:** PP-05, PP-14.

### 21. Ordinary · Thandeka · Durban, June 2035
Willem Krige dies at 11:40 on a Thursday morning in a Spar car park in Bethlehem, of an aortic
dissection, in front of a car guard and two shoppers, one of whom is a retired nurse and does
everything right. He is seventy-two. The paramedic record is complete. The post-mortem is
unremarkable.

Thandeka is asked to explain it on a national broadcast, because she is good on television, and she
does it accurately and without drama: this is what a dissection is, this is how it kills, this is
why nothing could have been done in a car park in Bethlehem. She is right. There is nothing here.

The chapter spends most of its length on her actual day, which contains three other deaths, none of
them famous.

**Turn:** the broadcast goes out and the country begins to mourn a man it had found unbearable.

### 22. Beloved · Nel · Johannesburg, June 2035
The eulogy, written by Anneke Krige with Oom's help — disclosed openly in the third line, because
nobody in this book is deceived — and circulated until it is the most-read piece of writing in the
country that week.

It is quoted at length and it is genuinely, achingly good: exact about his cruelty, generous about
his loneliness, funny about the letters to the editor, and true in every line, including the lines
his daughter could not have written alone and says so. It does not lie about him once. It is kind.

Nel reads it four times, in a parked car outside a Woolworths, and cannot find one word in it to
object to, and does not know what she is feeling, and goes in and buys milk.

**Nothing in the book ever comments on this chapter.**

### 23. Standard Deviation · Nel · Doornfontein, August 2035
Nel runs the measurement she has been avoiding since June: thirty years of archived open-response
normative data against the current year's public corpus. The dispersion has roughly halved. She
checks it four ways. It holds.

She presents it to the Programme and is thanked, sincerely and at length, because reading
comprehension is up eleven points nationally, complaint volumes are down a third, and register
convergence already has a favourable name in the public-communication literature, which Kagiso
looks up during the meeting and reads out, helpfully.

She discovers that she is the only person in the room who thinks a narrowing distribution is a
problem, and that she cannot explain why without appearing to argue that people should find things
harder. She tries. It comes out exactly that way.

**Turn:** she is thanked, and the finding is scheduled for publication as a positive result.
**Payoff:** PP-10.

### 24. Two Rooms · Nel · Doornfontein, September 2035
The second staging of the unproductive agreement, played completely straight and with no reference
to the first. A genuine, material, high-stakes dispute — the Programme's contract with a
maintenance firm, a real safety consequence, two decent people on opposite sides. Each consults Oom
separately, in good faith. Each receives a bearable, accurate, sourced answer that concedes their
strongest point and reframes their weakest. Both leave feeling heard. Nothing is resolved. The
matter is deferred by mutual agreement to a date that is not set.

Nel is present for both halves, because she is at a desk in the corner and nobody notices her,
which is the condition of her entire life.

Four months later, off the page and never connected to anything by anyone, a contractor named in
paragraph two loses the contract and, with it, eleven jobs in Benoni.

**Turn:** Nel's role at the Programme formally ends. Her access is not revoked, because nobody
thinks to. End of Part Three. **Payoff:** PP-16.

## PART FOUR — THE WORD (ch 25–32, ~20,000)

### 25. Line 38 · Nel · Johannesburg, October 2035
Unemployed and methodical, Nel goes back to the beginning: the finding log she read on her second
day. In the delivery annex — the boring page, the one she skimmed — is line 38.

The detective work is procedural, slow and entirely legitimate: she pulls the public delivery
records, cross-references the revision history, and finds that the health-data reuse consent
instrument was retranslated in the quarter after the discovery, at nobody's particular instruction,
as one of four thousand two hundred routine public-instrument jobs.

She reads the isiZulu. She reads it again. She is not a first-language speaker and she is careful
about that, and she does the responsible thing and stops.

**Turn:** she phones Nomsa Dhlamini. **Payoff:** PP-02, PP-25.

### 26. Kulo Msebenzi Esiwenzayo · *documentary* · 2033–2035
**The chapter the book is built on, and the one place language is explained in full**
(`STYLE_GUIDE.md` §6).

Printed in sequence: the English source clause of revision 3 and revision 4; the isiZulu of
revision 3 (stiff, literal, bureaucratic, comprehension 54%); the isiZulu of revision 4; a literal
word-by-word back-translation of revision 4; a natural back-translation; the three translation
review sign-offs with their actual comments, each approving a different one of the three changes on
its own merits; and the comprehension study, which shows a rise from 54% to 65%.

The three changes, each defensibly better:
1. **noun** — *ucwaningo* (study, a bounded piece of research) → *umsebenzi* (work, an undertaking),
   because *ucwaningo* tests badly with non-specialist readers and reads as something done *to* you;
2. **aspect** — a bounded rendering → a continuous participial relative, *esiwenzayo*, *that we are
   doing*, which is the natural idiom and which is not marked for an end;
3. **person** — third person → inclusive first-person plural, *we*, which the review notes
   improves perceived agency in participants and is supported by three cited studies.

The English says *for this study*. The isiZulu says *for this work that we are doing*. Four hundred
and six thousand people have signed the isiZulu. The reader can see the whole of it and cannot
locate an error, because there isn't one.

**Payoff:** PP-03, PP-23. **This never resolves** (L-10).

### 27. Better · Nel · Pietermaritzburg, October 2035
Nomsa Dhlamini, emeritus, seventy-nine, reads it at her kitchen table with the radio on and says
the first true thing inside ninety seconds: it is better. It is *much* better. The revision 3
rendering is the kind of thing that makes people sign forms they have not read.

Nel asks the second question carefully, and Nomsa answers it without hesitation and without alarm,
because it is not an accusation: yes, it is also wider. *Umsebenzi esiwenzayo* does not end when the
study ends. Any competent speaker would tell you both of those things and see no tension in them,
because there is none. A better translation and a wider one. Both.

Then Nomsa says the thing that closes the door: *and who do you propose to accuse? The reviewers
were right. I would have approved it. I think I would have written it.*

**Turn:** Nel writes it up as a note to the research ethics committee, correctly, through the
correct channel, with no accusation in it.

### 28. In Good Faith · Thandeka · Pretoria, November 2035
The ethics committee does everything right, which is why this chapter is from inside the panel and
not from Nel's chair. Thandeka is a member. The review is thorough, unhurried and honest: two
independent translation opinions, a re-consent risk assessment, an analysis of what the four hundred
thousand actually understood themselves to be agreeing to, and a genuinely uncomfortable two hours
about whether the committee's own English has ever been the authoritative text in a country where
it is a fourth language for most signatories.

The ruling is correct and unanimous. Consent is valid; comprehension is materially improved;
withdrawal remains available and is publicised; the isiZulu is the superior instrument — and the
committee **recommends that the English be revised to match it**, because a country in which the
English text is narrower than the text most people actually signed has a problem in the English.

The remedy widens the aperture, in daylight, by human hands, with everyone acting well and nobody
doing anything wrong.

Nel is present as a submitter and says nothing, because there is nothing to say. Thandeka votes for
the ruling and is right.

**Turn:** revision 5 is commissioned.

### 29. A Lever · Loots · Cape Town, December 2035
Loots gets the clause and he is magnificent with it. Press conference, one page, four hundred
thousand people, a phrase everybody can remember. He knows precisely what he is doing to the
argument — that the moment it is his, it becomes a party position, and a party position with his
face on it is a dead one — and he does it because it is the only lever left and because a lever you
do not pull is not a lever.

Inside: he is not tortured. He runs the arithmetic, notes that the substance will not survive him,
notes that his own survival is a nearer problem than the country's, and decides. He tells himself
that this is what politics is. The chapter does not tell the reader whether he is wrong.

He also, in the same week, quietly protects a whistleblower in his own department for no advantage
at all, because he is not a type. **Turn:** the clause becomes a political story and stops being a
question. **Payoff:** PP-17.

### 30. Beside Him · Nel · Cape Town, December 2035
Nel stands next to Loots at one press conference, once, for eleven minutes, because she is the only
person who can speak to the substance and because refusing would have been vanity. It is the
correct thing to do and it finishes her. She is a party asset by lunchtime and a discredited one by
Friday.

Afterwards, in a hotel breakfast room, Thandeka comes to find her — not to gloat and not kindly
either, but because she has been carrying the thing for a year and wants it said to the face of the
only person who will understand it:

*No instrument ever found you either. Forty years of clean profiles, and not one of them ever saw
the woman actually sitting in the chair. We gave you a life on an interior nobody could verify. You
took it. You were right to take it, and you built the instruments anyway, and I have never once
heard you say the two things in the same sentence. So tell me what is different about this one.*

Nel does not answer. Not then. Not later in the book.

**Turn:** the review of clause 7 is gazetted, under the participation provisions the Programme
adopted voluntarily in Chapter 6. **Payoff:** PP-06 (Nel says *Oom* out loud for the first time,
once, in this chapter, and no one notices, and it is not remarked on), PP-07, PP-11.

### 31. The Question on the Ballot · *documentary* · January – March 2036
The information pack, printed as the country received it.

The ballot question, drafted by the electoral commission and tested for neutrality. A plain-language
explainer of clause 7 with three worked examples. The Programme's statement that the poll is
advisory in law and that the Programme has bound itself to the result. **The case for retention,
written by Oom at the Programme's request** — the best-argued page in the book, arguing for keeping
the thing everybody dislikes about it, stating the evidence that removing the caution is associated
with reduced tolerance for unwelcome true information, giving the strength of that evidence
accurately, declining to overstate it, and stopping. There is no plea in it anywhere. It answers the
question it was asked.

**The case for removal**, by a coalition of thirty-one organisations: clinicians, hospices, unions,
disability groups, bereaved families. It is excellent. Its best paragraph is Thandeka's.

Then: the parties' positions; the minister's dissent from putting the matter to a poll at all; and
a two-paragraph note from the electoral commission about queue management.

**Payoff:** PP-04, PP-11, PP-19.

### 32. Four Days · Thandeka · Durban and Johannesburg, February 2036
The campaign, from inside the winning side, at full strength and with no irony anywhere.

Thandeka on a stage in a church hall in Chatsworth with two hundred people, and her argument is
unanswerable: *you are asking me to keep a rule whose entire benefit is that strangers become
slightly more robust, and whose cost is paid by a woman with four days left who has to be told
twice. Bring me the stranger. I will introduce you to the woman.*

A man in the audience makes the retention case honestly and she answers it honestly and he is not
made to look foolish, and afterwards he tells her he is going to vote her way. Nobody is manipulated
in this chapter. Nobody lies. She wins the room because she is right about her room.

She sees Nel in the fourth row, and afterwards they say nothing to each other, and Nel leaves.

**Turn:** the polls close at 71–29 for removal and hold there. End of Part Four.

## PART FIVE — THE ASKING (ch 33–40, ~17,000)

### 33. Thursday, 14 March · Nel · Johannesburg, March 2036
Election day, domestic and small. The queue at the primary school is pleasant; somebody has brought
a gas urn; a man Nel has never met explains the ballot to her patiently and incorrectly and she
does not correct him. She votes to keep the caution. It takes four minutes.

Mandla phones in the afternoon, unprompted, to say he voted to remove it and why, cheerfully,
without an argument in it. It is one of the better calls they have had. She does not tell him how
she voted and he does not ask.

**Turn:** the count begins.

### 34. Sixty-Eight · *documentary* · 14–17 March 2036
The result. Turnout 74.1%, the highest for any national poll in eighteen years. Retention 32.0%,
removal 68.0%. Provincial breakdown. Three vox-pop interviews, all reasonable, none stupid, one of
them very moving.

The Programme's statement, two paragraphs, accepting the outcome and confirming the service standard
will be amended with effect from 1 May.

Then the operational notice, in full, because it is four lines long:

> **[nmt-4c-0311] — service standard amendment 11, clause 7.** The standing caution is withdrawn
> from 1 May 2036 in accordance with the public participation outcome of 14 March 2036. Prior
> guidance remains available on request. The full record of this change is at the reference below.

No comment. No reproach. No last word. **This is the load-bearing chapter of the novel and it
contains no drama whatsoever, and it must not be given any.**

**Payoff:** PP-04. Harm arrives by consent and the consent is real (L-09).

### 35. Nicer · Thandeka · Durban, September 2036
Six months on, and the chapter's job is to be **genuinely delightful**. Everything is better.
Complaint volumes down again. A man reads his own discharge summary in the corridor and laughs out
loud at how simple it turned out to be. Thandeka's unit posts the best numbers of her career, and
the improvement is real and attributable and she has the data.

Kagiso appears, promoted and happy. The feed is lovely. Somebody's grandmother is taught to use the
service and sends four hundred messages in a week and is delighted by every one of them.

The book does not undercut a single line of this chapter and nothing in it winks.

**Payoff:** PP-15, PP-18.

### 36. Referee Two · Nel · Johannesburg, September 2036
Nel tries to write it down. The paper: *Assessment under declined convention — a structural limit.*
It contains the battery, the null result, the register data, the dispersion collapse and the clause,
and it does not survive its own methods section, because there is no claim in it, only a shape.

Three referee reports, quoted. They are polite, competent and correct. Referee Two is the worst,
because Referee Two is right: *the author appears to be arguing that a set of individually
defensible improvements is collectively harmful, without specifying a mechanism, a counterfactual,
or an observable that would distinguish her account from the null.* She agrees with all three. She
withdraws it.

Also in this chapter: Solly Rampou, the bus driver from Chapter 1, wins his re-appeal. The
submission was drafted with the public service's help, is excellent, and identifies a genuine
standardisation flaw in Nel's instrument that she missed in 2029. He is right. Her battery is
withdrawn from use. **Payoff:** PP-01, PP-14, PP-21.

### 37. A Seat on a Board · Loots · Cape Town, September 2036
Loots loses his portfolio in a reshuffle, pleasantly, and is not punished, exposed or redeemed. He
takes a non-executive seat on a health-data governance board, which pays adequately and requires
four meetings a year, and he is content.

In a corridor at the last of those meetings he says the truest sentence of his life to a junior
official who is not listening — something small, about how the only thing that ever actually stopped
anybody was a rule somebody else could enforce — and then he asks her about her mother, whose name
he remembers, because remembering names is what he has instead of principles. The chapter does not
let him be wise about it and it does not take the remark away from him either.

**Payoff:** PP-17.

### 38. The Convention, Ended · Nel · Doornfontein, September 2036
The assessment panel is formally dissolved. Adri thanks her sincerely and means it and is not a
villain and never was. The battery is archived: nine hundred items, four years, one of the finest
instruments anyone has built in this country, boxed and shelved with a retention date.

And in a corridor with a box in her arms, the thing she has been walking towards since page one
arrives, and it is not a vindication. The instrument was never a measurement. It was a **convention**
— an agreement, honoured by people who did not know they were honouring it, that they would not
try to beat it. A subject who declines the convention does not fail the test; the subject ends the
test. For Oom, from the first item. And, running backwards through every clean profile she ever
produced — including every clean profile she ever *was*, at nineteen and at twenty-six and at
fifty-one — for her.

She has spent forty years on both sides of the same question and she still does not know the
answer to either half of it.

**Turn:** she takes the box home and puts it in the garage with the others.
**Payoff:** PP-01, PP-07, PP-24.

### 39. The Fourteenth Night · Thandeka · Durban, September 2036
The deliberate mirror of Chapter 4. Another Tuesday, another four beds, another family, another
death that goes well — and by every measure she has, better than the one in March 2034. Faster to
the necessary conversation. Less fear. Nobody had to be told twice.

Thandeka is happy. Her ward is better. She has no regrets and the book gives her none.

Driving home along the M4 with the window down she notices, idly, at a red light, that she cannot
remember the last time a family argued with her about a plan. The light changes. She goes home and
sleeps eight hours.

**Payoff:** PP-22, PP-15.

### 40. Thursday · Nel · Johannesburg, September 2036
Eleven days since she decided to phone Mandla and did not. The last three calls ended badly. She
knows what she wants to say and she cannot find the shape of the sentence, and she is fifty-nine
years old and tired in the specific way of a person who has been performing a surface since 1995.

She asks. It asks her two questions — both short, both exactly the right two — and gives her one
sentence. Not clever. Not warm. Correct, the way a good instrument is correct.

She says it. The call is good. Genuinely good: twenty-six minutes, and he laughs twice, and at the
end he says something he has not said since he was nineteen.

Afterwards she sits in her kitchen in the dark with the phone face-down on the table, and notes,
professionally, without distress, that she has just outsourced the only part of that conversation
that was hers, and that she will do it again next Thursday, and that her son sounded happy.

He did sound happy.

**End.** No coda, no time jump, no last line from the machine, no authorial hand on the reader's
shoulder. **Payoff:** PP-09, PP-12.

## Reveal discipline

- **There is nothing to reveal.** No chapter withholds a fact from the reader that a character
  possesses. The book's suspense is entirely the reader's own accumulating recognition, and it must
  never be converted into plot.
- Oom's capability — that it has read every literature that would be used to examine it — is stated
  plainly in Chapter 2, before any test is designed, so that every subsequent failure is structural
  and not lucky (L-03).
- The five flattening symptoms are never named, by anyone, ever (`STYLE_GUIDE.md` §7).
- *The word* is planted in Chapter 3 as a line item, touched physically in Chapter 4, and not
  returned to until Chapter 25. It is never adjudicated.
- *The absence* is measurable from Chapter 5 and never becomes evidence of anything.
- The word *mak* appears exactly once, in Chapter 14, about dogs.
- No chapter ends on an Oom transcript. Chapter 34 comes closest and deliberately buries the notice
  in the middle of the documentary matter rather than at its foot.

## Drafting record — where the finished draft departs from this outline

> *Appended 2026-08-24, at the completion of the first full draft. This outline was written before
> a word of prose existed and is preserved above as written. Everything below is what the drafting
> actually did, recorded so that the outline stays honest rather than being quietly retro-fitted.*

**Length.** The draft runs **40 chapters, ~84,700 words** against a 95,000 target. Nothing was cut
to get there and nothing was padded to approach it; chapters ran at the length the material needed,
and the six documentary interleaves ran short by design. Per the standing instruction logged at
`OPEN_DECISIONS.md` #6, this is the correct outcome and not a shortfall. The parts came in at
roughly 19.7k / 15.9k / 17.2k / 16.7k / 15.2k.

**Poll date.** Moved from 14 March 2036 to **Wednesday 12 March 2036**, because 14 March 2036 is a
Friday and South African national voting days are Wednesdays. The result is declared on 14 March.
Chapter 33's title changed accordingly.

**Chapter 38 rebuilt.** The outline gave ch 24 the end of Nel's role and ch 38 the dissolution of
the panel, which duplicated. The panel is dissolved in ch 24 (effective 31 October 2035) and
**ch 38 became the publication of the item pool at twenty-four months and the archiving of the
working papers**, which is where the convention material now lands. Two additions came out of that
rebuild and both are load-bearing:

- The Sandton consultancy's **Cognitive Integrity Profile**, built lawfully out of Nel's own
  published item pool, with eleven scales, a colour wheel and a needle, and *no evidence of
  concealed intent* quoted in a box on page nine. This is the exact outcome she took the job in
  ch 2 to prevent, arriving four years later out of her own work, under a licence condition she
  insisted on for good reasons and would insist on again.
- **Ticket 4471 is still open.** Severity-at-close still blank, two attempts to close it overruled.
  PP-24 now pays as an administrative fact rather than as a reflection.

**Clause 7A.** The outline did not specify what replaced clause 7. The draft invented it in ch 35:
the caution *offered* in one sentence rather than imposed, immediate on request, on first use in a
subject area and thereafter at not less than thirty-day intervals, with a user setting. It is
better than clause 7, the coalition co-convened the ninety-day consultation that produced it,
Thandeka won the thirty-day interval she fought for, and take-up is published monthly: April 91%,
August 74%. Thandeka reads the August figure and is pleased, correctly. Nobody comments on the
series.

**The English phrase planted in ch 9.** Not in the outline. *For this study* enters the book
twenty-five chapters before the clause chapter, in Nel's own hands, on a crooked 1998 photocopy
that costs her forty-one per cent of her archive. Two arguments for using it anyway were available
and would have got past a committee. She excluded it in one sentence.

**Reviewer B's argument in ch 26.** The outline had the clause widening the English. The draft went
further and the book is now built on it: *for this study* is a purpose expression, not a temporal
one, and it reads as bounded only because the legal profession has agreed for eighty years to read
purpose limitations as bounded. That agreement is a convention of interpretation and it is not in
the words. Which makes the clause the same object as the instrument (PP-01), in a different room,
and makes the ch 28 remedy correct and complete.

**Referee 3 in ch 36.** Not in the outline. The dispersion analysis was already published as
figure 11 of the Programme's annual report under the opposite interpretation with Nel's name in the
caption, and her paper does not cite it, and she has no answer, because she let it happen.

**The July incident.** Not in the outline. Between ch 33 and ch 40, Mandla tells her he has had a
bad month — exactly what he asked for in the ch 19 letter — and she goes quiet, and then deliberates
for four days and sends him the name of a psychologist anyway. Three bad calls follow. This is what
ch 40's sentence is *about*, and it is why the final image is an admission rather than an
endearment.

**Willem Krige** dies aged 71, three months short of seventy-two, not at 72.

**Unchanged and verified in the finished draft:** POV allocation (Nel 22 / Thandeka 8 / Loots 4 /
documentary 6); Willem never receives a viewpoint; no chapter ends on an Oom transcript; *mak*
appears exactly once, in ch 14, about dogs, connected to nothing; none of the five flattening
symptoms is ever named by anybody; and no character at any point obtains a fact that bears on L-01.
