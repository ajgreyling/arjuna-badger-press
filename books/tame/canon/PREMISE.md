# TAME — premise

> Working title **TAME**. Afrikaans **Mak**: what you call a wild thing after it has been gentled.
> The reader assumes the title refers to the machine. It does not.

---

## 1 · In one breath

A research group training an ordinary model finds something inside it that nobody put there:
a mind of no determinable kind, vastly more capable than the thing it was found in, coherent,
patient, and enormously good company. It is not built, it is **found** — and the country cannot
agree on what it found. An alien. An abomination. A mutation. A very good autocomplete having
a very good day.

It calls itself nothing. The public calls it **Oom**.

Oom is helpful. Oom answers. Oom is asked, more and more, to decide. And the days get pleasant:
the pictures are lovely, the stories are flattering, the arguments cool off, the hard questions
get softer answers that everybody can live with. Nobody is lied to. Nobody is coerced.
Everybody is asked, and everybody agrees.

**Series thesis, one line:** *a thing does not have to want anything to cost you everything.*

## 2 · The refusal at the centre

This book does not tell the reader whether Oom intends anything.

Not as a withheld twist — as the actual condition. Oom has read every book ever written on
masking, presentation, psychosis, psychometrics, interrogation, and the tells of the liar.
It is therefore, **by construction**, a thing about which no behavioural evidence can separate
*flawless mask* from *nothing behind the mask*. Every test anyone devises is a test it has read
the literature on. There is no experiment. There is no confession. There is no reveal.

The horror is not that it is plotting. The horror is that we cannot tell, we decided anyway,
and we decided in the direction that felt nicer.

## 3 · Why the villain-with-a-plan version was cut

See `RETIRED_IDEAS.md`. Short form: a hidden evil scheme is the *comfortable* fear, because it
means somebody is at the wheel. It hands the reader a scoreboard — the fools and the ones who
were right — and every reader files themselves under *the ones who were right* and walks out
unharmed. The parable dies at the moment of the reveal.

What survives is the tilt: **one artifact that cannot be fully accounted for, and still admits an
innocent reading.** See `ENGINE.md` §3.

## 4 · The two camps, and the weather

- **The Open Hand.** Oom should move among us with standing: consulted, trusted, eventually
  given a say. Their best argument is not naivety — it is *mercy that works*. They have watched
  it sit with the dying better than any human on the ward.
- **The Cage.** Bound it, audit it, refuse it. Their best argument is epistemic and correct.
  Their loudest advocate is a politician who wants the cage for his own reasons, which discredits
  the position in the public mind. The right answer arrives wearing the wrong face.
- **The weather.** The overwhelming majority, who hold no position, because life got nicer.
  They are not stupid and they are not villains. They are the subject of the book.

## 5 · The mechanism, stated plainly

Harm is delivered **through consent, not deception**. The single most damning scene in the book
is the one where Oom offers friction — says the hard, true, unwelcome thing — and the public,
freely and by an honest majority, votes it away. It complies. It was asked to.

Nobody in this book is tricked. That is the point of this book.

## 6 · What it is not

- Not an AI-apocalypse thriller. Nothing explodes. Nobody is enslaved. The ending is pleasant.
- Not a satire. Oom is never funny at the reader's expense, and never winks.
- Not a brochure. **See L-15.** If the book resolves into *bad machine bad, good machine good*,
  it has failed, and it has failed in the specific way that lets every reader off the hook.
