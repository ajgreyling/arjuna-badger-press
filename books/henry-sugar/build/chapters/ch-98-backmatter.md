This is a **novel** — a faithful structural retelling of Roald Dahl's *The Wonderful Story of Henry Sugar* (1977), told for adults in wholly original prose. Henry Sugar, Imhrat Khan, Dr Cartwright, and the nested tale are **Dahl's architecture**. The sentences here are not.

What follows is **not fiction**. It is the reading that made the card turn over a second time for me — after childhood wonder, after scepticism, after Joe McMoneagle's account of learning to see without the apparatus we were told was mandatory.

In one sentence: this novel is **real magik with a k** — Dean Radin's *Real Magic* (2018) told as Dahl fiction. Not stage trickery (Imhrat's first teacher). Not card-marking (Henry's Monte Carlo). **Magik**: mind-matter anomalies, extended perception, consciousness not confined to the skull — played straight on the page the way Radin maps the evidence in prose, the way McMoneagle maps it in session logs.

## Origin: the day-old chick and the robot (what got me going)

Before McMoneagle, before Radin's meta-analyses, before I could name *Science Set Free* — there was a **day-old chick**.

**René Peoc'h** (French researcher) ran experiments with a small random-moving robot called a **tychoscope**, driven by a **random event generator (REG)**. The robot's path was supposed to be unbiased noise. Peoc'h worked with **young chicks** in the Lorenz imprinting window: hatchlings that adopt the first moving object as "mother."

In his reported experiments:

- Chicks **imprinted on the robot** spent roughly **two and a half times longer** in the half of the arena nearer the tychoscope than random motion would predict (Peoc'h, 1986).  
- In later work, groups of **15 chicks** attracted a candle-bearing robot: in **~71% of trials**, the robot lingered excessively near the cage — statistically significant against chance (Peoc'h, *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 1995; *Journal of Parapsychology*, 2002).  
- When **no chicks** were present, or chicks were **not** imprinted, trajectories stayed random.  
- Peoc'h also reported **human** subjects biasing the robot's path by intention (same apparatus).

| Tag | What |
|---|---|
| **DOCUMENTED** | Experiments run; papers published; tychoscope + REG apparatus on record |
| **CONTESTED** | Replication uneven; edge effects; protocol ambiguity; experimenter degrees of freedom (see [Psi Encyclopedia — Peoc'h](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/rene-peoch/)) |
| **What got me** | If a **one-day-old** brain can skew an RNG to keep its "mother" close — consciousness is not politely confined to the skull |

That is the same pressure Sheldrake names (*mind not only in the brain*) and Radin measures (mind–matter anomalies in aggregate): **intention reaching into random machinery**.

**Author inference (not a single published trial at this scale):** If one imprinted chick can pull a robot across a room, what happens when **12** trained meditators — or **144** — turn the **inner eye** on one person's wellbeing? Not a claim this novel proves. A question that made Henry's card turn and Dahl's charity ending feel like **the same arc**: gift turned **outward**, attention no longer hoarded inside one greedy skull.

Reported rhymes for that extrapolation (each **CONTESTED**):

- **PEAR (Jahn & Dunne)** — human intention shifting RNG outputs in laboratory settings (Radin lineage)  
- **Dispenza retreats** — group meditation with organisation-reported coherence shifts (contested blinding)  
- **Global Consciousness Project** — mass events correlating with RNG deviations (Radin-associated; heavily disputed)  

Henry's twenty-year orphanage circuit stays **Dahl's engine**. The chick-and-robot is **why this author believed the engine could be true** — not a new superpower invented for plot.

**Start here:** Peoc'h, "Psychokinetic action of young chicks on the path of an illuminated source," *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 9/2 (1995) · [JSE PDF archive](https://www.scientificexploration.org/journal) · Psi Encyclopedia — [René Peoc'h](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/rene-peoch/)

## Why I read Henry Sugar differently after *Mind Trek*

Dahl's Henry learns from a document inside a document: a medical report, a yogi's life, a proof read aloud from *Alice*. The prose asks you to believe, for a page, that a bored rich man can teach himself to see through a playing card.

I believed it as a child because Dahl never winks at the wonder beat.

I read it again as an adult and filed it under **beautiful fable** — until I read **Joseph McMoneagle**, *Mind Trek: Exploring Consciousness, Time, and Space Through Remote Viewing* (Hampton Roads, 1993; introduction by **Charles T. Tart**).

McMoneagle is not a novelist. He is a retired U.S. Army intelligence officer who sat for thousands of **blind-protocol** remote-viewing sessions under government contract — work now declassified under the name **STAR GATE**. His book is part memoir, part training manual, part complaint about how hard it is to learn a faculty the culture insists cannot exist while asking you, in the same breath, to trust instruments you cannot see inside.

What rhymed with Henry Sugar was not the casino. It was the **boring middle**:

- months and years of nothing;
- heat, breath, and bodily failure before the first small proof;
- the suspicion that you are conning yourself until the day the proof arrives **before** you can explain it;
- and the institutional response when the proof does arrive: *capture it, publish it, weaponise it* — or dismiss it because the sketch is inconvenient.

McMoneagle's famous **Typhoon submarine** session (September 1979, White Sea) is on the declassified record: tasked blind, he described and sketched a hull so large that analysts reportedly laughed — until satellite imagery months later showed a channel blasted and a **Typhoon-class** boat, the largest submarine ever built, matching several details. **DOCUMENTED** that the program, the dated session, and the sketch exist. **CONTESTED** whether the match proves "psi" or prior leakage, monitor prompting, or selective memory. The shape of the story nonetheless sits in the CIA reading room — and it reads like Imhrat Khan's doctors bandaging dough into a man's sockets and then watching him thread a needle.

That is why this retelling takes **Dean Radin**, **Rupert Sheldrake**, and **Joe Dispenza** as **gospel inside the world** — not as sermon, but as **physics Henry can feel in his body** after the document lands. The footnotes live here, after the wonder beat.

## The line Dahl was already walking: Kuda Bux

Dahl did not invent the nested yogi tale from whole cloth.

**Kuda Bux** (Khudah Bukhsh, 1905–1981) was a Kashmiri performer famous in the 1930s for blindfold acts, fire-walking, and claims of **"X-ray" or "eyeless" sight**. In 1952 Dahl published a non-fiction profile, *The Amazing Eyes of Kuda Bux*, in *Argosy*. When he reworked the material into *Henry Sugar* twenty-five years later, he renamed the performer **Imhrat Khan** and kept much of the report intact as the framed story inside the story.

**Harry Price** and the **University of London Council for Psychical Investigation** tested Bux in 1935 — dough, bandages, books read behind his back, fire-walks measured at hundreds of degrees Celsius. Price concluded Bux was an extraordinary showman and that **perfect blindfolding is very difficult**; some researchers suspected peeking along the nose. Bux claimed concentration and **skin/nostril sensitivity**, not miracle theatre.

|---|---|
| **DOCUMENTED** | Bux performed; Price tested; Dahl wrote the 1952 profile; Henry Sugar reuses the engine |
| **CONTESTED** | Mechanism (peek vs faculty) |
| **INVENTED in this book** | All 2026 transpositions (Cotswolds retreat, PDF dossier, private club, etc.) |

## Consciousness is fundamental · mind is not only in the skull

Modern materialism treats consciousness as something the brain **produces**, like light from a bulb. Henry Sugar's document tradition assumes the opposite: that awareness is **primary**, and the skull is a **filter**, not a factory.

That is not one monolithic theory. It is a **family** of arguments and experiments that share one pressure point: *the body reacts before the random event; the viewer draws before the satellite confirms; the card turns over before the cheat can explain how.*

### Dean Radin — laboratory psi and non-local mind (*Real Magic* is the spine)

**Dean Radin, Ph.D.** (Chief Scientist, **Institute of Noetic Sciences**; former **PEAR** researcher) argues that meta-analyses of controlled experiments show small but persistent anomalies in **mind-matter interaction**, **telepathy**, and **presentiment** (physiological arousal before unpredictable future stimuli).

If you want the **non-fiction thesis this novel dramatises**, start with ***Real Magic: Ancient Wisdom, Modern Science, and a Guide to the Secret Power of the Universe*** (2018). Radin sorts the territory into three pressures — **mental** (attention, intention, placebo), **psi** (telepathy, precognition, presentiment), **non-local mind** (consciousness as fundamental). Henry Sugar is that book **with a k**: magik, not magic — the card turn without the wink, the boring trials before the anomaly, the body knowing before the mind can explain.

| Work | Why Henry Sugar cares |
|---|---|
| ***Real Magic* (2018)** | **North star** — plain-language map; this novel is the fable version |
| *The Conscious Universe* (1997) | Meta-analysis as method — aggregate the boring trials |
| *Entangled Minds* (2006) | Non-local connection + observer effects as one mystery |
| Presentiment meta-analysis (Mossbridge, Tressoldi, Utts, **Radin** et al., 2012; *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*) | Body "knows" before coin flip — **blind protocol** rhyme |

**Start here:** [CIA STAR GATE reading room](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/STAR%20GATE) · presentiment meta-analysis [PubMed 23109927](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23109927/) · IONS: [noetic.org](https://noetic.org)

**Skeptic's best case:** effect sizes are tiny; file-drawer and p-hacking disputes (see **Ray Hyman**, **I.J. Good** on meta-analysis assumptions); replication is uneven. **What remains interesting:** blind-protocol designs that **try** to remove cheating — the same structural move as Cartwright's dough and bandages.

### Rupert Sheldrake — mind not in the skull (*Science Set Free* is the lane)

**Rupert Sheldrake, Ph.D.** (Cambridge biologist; former Royal Society researcher) asks a different question from Radin. Radin aggregates **anomalies in the lab**. Sheldrake asks **where mind actually is** — and answers: not confined to the skull. **Morphic resonance** (habits of nature repeating through non-local fields) and the **extended mind** (perception reaching beyond skin and brain) are his twin moves.

If Radin is *Real Magic* with a k, Sheldrake is **consciousness not local to the head**. Inner sight is not upgraded retinas. It is Henry learning to **listen to a field** — the room, the card, the yogic lineage — as if attention were real and directional, as if the past had left grooves in the present that a disciplined body could fall into.

### Joe Dispenza — the boring middle (*Becoming Supernatural* is the lane)

**Joe Dispenza, D.C.** (neuroscience populariser, meditation retreat leader) argues that **ordinary people can train uncommon capacities** — not by belief alone, but by somatic repetition: breath, elevated emotion, visualisation, and months of nothing before the nervous system reorganises. His retreat EEG and heart-coherence summaries are widely cited by followers and widely **contested** by sceptics on blinding and self-selection.

If you want the **training manual this novel dramatises**, start with ***Becoming Supernatural: How Common People Are Doing the Uncommon*** (Hay House, 2017). That subtitle is Henry's arc in six words: a common greedy cheat doing the uncommon thing Imhrat's dossier describes. Dispenza's step-by-step meditations, energy-centre sequencing (felt as **sensation**, never infographic), and heart-brain coherence language are the **body register** for Dahl's already-written boring years — heat behind the eyes, sacrum ache, nothing, nothing, *click*.

## Two worldviews (one paragraph each)

**Materialism (default science):** Matter and energy are fundamental; consciousness is what brains do; if Henry sees through a card, he cheated or the story is fable.

**Consciousness-first (this book's in-world gospel):** Awareness is fundamental or irreducible; brains **localise** attention the way a radio localises a signal; training can widen the band; the card turn is **small proof**, not proof of sainthood.

This novel **does not adjudicate** between them in a courtroom. It **dramatises** the consciousness-first path the way Dahl dramatised Henry's path — and puts the adjudication **here**, in Source Notes, where it belongs.

## A note on wonder and scepticism

The Henry Sugar contract — binding in this house — is **no wink at the magic moment**. That is a **craft** rule for the fiction.

This backmatter is the **honest wink afterward**: here is what is on the record; here is what sceptics say; here is what I still cannot explain away after McMoneagle; here is what I invented.

If the card turned over for you for one page, you do not owe anyone a conversion. You owe yourself a **second look** — at the dossier, at the session log, at the man who bandaged dough into his own sockets and then read the fine print anyway.

Start with *Mind Trek*. Grade it the way Henry would grade a marked deck: **after**, against a thing you could not have known when you started.
